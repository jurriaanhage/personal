Summary

After two demos this release here is the first full fledged by Medea, a psuedonymn for Dutch composer Henry Meeuws, recording in project form. Meeuws and three of the vocalists are members of Casual Silence.

The music

This album is a rock opera inspired by a visit to Florence, Italy by Meeuws. The bio compares it to Leonardo: The Absolute Man and Genius: A Rock Opera. Not without reason, one might say, since all three albums use a project form, with numerous vocalists, creating a rock opera all in a vein not quite prog metal, but definitely more beefy than regular progressive music. Where I find this album is different from its two predecessor projects, is mainly the vocals, which sometimes lack the strength to persuade, on the other hand are less bloated. Apart from that the compositions seem to incorporate some more classical influences, clearly heard in Fuga (but then again, that was composed by Bach), whilst at other times sounding a bit hard or harsh on the guitar sections. The more classically oriented sections do sound a bit artificial, because of the use of keyboards instead of orchestra. And then there's the dreaded drumbox. Granted, Meeuws masks the use of artificial drums rather well, a lot better than in general, but artificial the drums do remain.

Conclusion

Individual Unique has come out a pretty balanced album, sounding pretty good overall. As can be expected a lot of scores are played on keyboards, which especially in the more classically oriented sections gives an anachronistic feel. In its balance, though, the album lacks highs. From this point of view I must say that I can't quite see anything on this album drawing me into playing it again. Those into musicals and rock operas will probably consider this album an addition, those looking for a progressive album would do well choosing an album with a more song oriented approach.
