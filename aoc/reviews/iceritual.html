Summary

A Hawkwind live cover album from Finland, with Nik Turner participating. The album has been out for a while. Only track 14 is not live and it is not a Hawkwind song, but a Dark Sun song.

The music

Well, what can you expect? Space Rock in capitals obviously, since Ice Ritual does not change overly much. This is apparent already in the spoken Sonic Attack. Although the original version is not close to my mind, Watching The Grass Grow sounds more like punkrock than I would expect (although admittedly Hawkwind has fans in that arena). Nik Turner makes a noisy, shrill entry here. That the vocalist says Kiitos between songs in a telling fact that we are dealing with a Finnish band here.

Psychedelic Warlords has the same anarchistic approach as Hawkwind. Plenty of vocalists, nobody really singing in line with the rest. Kadu Flyer seems a bit older than the rest, with a bit of a sixties feel. The vocals are a bit mumbled, but the vocal melody comes out well enough. There is something of Bowie in this song.

Utopia '84 is another recitation track, followed by the flute of D-Rider. The sound seems quite bootleggy here, but the melody is good. I mean it is all perfectly listenable, but the sound is definitely a live one and it may be on purpose, but the guitar does not come through at all. The Right Stuff is a short (punk)rocker, followed by one of Hawkwind's most well known: Master Of The Universe. We rock on with Assault & Battery Part 1, followed by the more spacey The Golden Void part 2. Today I seem to have a preference for the quieter pieces, the rambling punk rock tracks, being too much alike.

Next up are three well-known Hawkwind tracks, Brainstorm, the fast paced, pumping Hassan I Sahba (fortunately not a lengthy dub version) and of course the hit single, Silver Machine. The final track is the cosmic Dream Circuit (bonus), which is quite good, lengthy, nice effects, varied. I'd be interested to hear what else they made.

Conclusion

Since Hawkwind made so many live albums and there are plenty of compilation cd's of this band around, one may wonder what the added value of this disc is. Is it the Dark Sun track? Well, it is a nice track, but in view of the fact that it is only one track against so many Hawkwind tunes... The Hawkwind songs do show a nice mix of up-tempo and punky versus soft and melodic, with many of the well-known tunes, but also some less well known ones (at least for me). It all sound very live, i.e. untouched by sonic improvements. The presence of Turner does give it an authenticity that another tribute cd would lack.
