Summary

A live album consisting mainly of songs (except 5, 9 and 10) of songs from the Voices From The Crowd album. It is also the farewell album of Dirk Bovensiepen and was recorded in the Netherlands for the most part.

The music

The album opens with the first four tracks from the Voices From The Crowd album. I liked that part of that album quite a lot. The songs were good and the lyrics sensible. The difference with the studio versions is that here Bovensiepen often slips into a Fish-voice and also that his vocals sometimes lack precision. First Contact is according to the remarks of the singer a party song, but the rest of the song with clavecimbel and some really striking melodies belie this. One of those melodies reminds me of something that Morricone could use for one of those westerns. On the whole the music is much more rough than on the studio album, and this difference it quite marked. Fortunately, this is compensated for by a higher energy level and also a higher emotional content: Bovensiepen may not have the best of voices, but he does bring over some emotion, emotion that I lacked when I saw Darius in the new line-up. Also, the songs continue to stand, for a large part by virtue of the good (vocal) melodies. Searching is a more ballad-like song, but it becomes more up-tempo later on with a good groove and plenty of organ. Alone is even more of a ballad, with the piano on the fore again becoming more forceful towards the end. Now we leave the Voices From The Crowd album for The Last Way comparable in style (but maybe a bit more epic) to the Crowd album. Snowflakes, dedicated to the father of Bovensiepen, is an active piece with some jamming in the middle on guitar and keyboards, thay they might have extended a bit. After the weak The Seven Signs, the band picks up again with One Of Them. This was also the last song from Voices Of The Crowd and now we take a step back to 1995 for the last two songs on the album, the first of which is a cover of Kayleigh. Not a bad cover, sounding a bit less slick and more playful than the original. The cover also indicates the roots of this band: Marillion, and in fact often I'm reminded of the Fish years of this band. However, the band is no copy-cat and most of the time it is more the semblance of the vocalist's voice to the voice of Fish than anything else. The closer is the title track of the first album of Darius also in an unplugged version.

The CD is nicely packaged in a blackish digipack.

Conclusion

The positive points of this album are the (generally) good songs and melodies and the quite emotional interpretation. However, the vocals are not as good as on the studio version, and I believe that also there the emotional content of the music shines through. So, I think I would suggest anyone interested in the songdirected, Marillionesque music of Darius to first try out the studio album before trying this one. I think that live album does add something to the studio album (energy and...verve), but I still prefer the studio album, mostly I think because of the steadier vocals.
