Summary

Kopecky is the only of the three band members I already knew, however, this effort should by no means be seen as the next Kopecky album: he only composed on one of the tracks, taking the back seat to both other band members.

The music

Parallel Mind is close to being a powertrio. Sure, the fact that there's three guys here helps in making the impression, but there's more: we get the hefty basses, the well fed organ (fighting with the piano) and heavy drums. On the other hand: there's no guitar here. A number of the tracks are longish and of a jazz-akin improvisational type structure. This further enhances the powertrio-feel of the album.

Opener Chromanic is based on a frivolous piano hopping around with the organ making tiny pushes into its sides at times. The other instruments play a supporting role in this one. Initially there is a bit of an avant prog feel about this track, but the strength of the instruments washes this away as it progresses.

Opposite Of Know opens almost as an etude, soon changing into something more up-tempo, almost funky in its use of piano and bass. The organ maintains its pushing role.

Into The Depths introduces female chanting, bringing with it a musical shift into something almost tranquil, featuring near Richard Clayderman piano moves. Against the backdrop of the other tracks on this album this track is something of an oddity I could have easily lived without. The somewhat mysterious end of this track can't fully nullify all that it's caused.

Underwater Cities is something of a flowing piano melody, in which the piano, later taken over by organ, plays such an important role musically that the track falls outside the powertrio formula.

Casa De Jig introduces some salsa elements on both piano and trumpet. Unfortunately this only happens halfway through the track, thus only further accenting the lack of spice in the early parts of the number. This salsa opening seems to be the signal for a barage of folk influences, including Japanese and Irish bits and pieces.

Conclusion

I often find that the powertrio formula can wear one down over the course of an album. The fact that this album shifts the usual course of things by switching to keys as the main musical benefactor brings a degree of freshness that one might expect to prevent the regular form of powertrio-fatigue. This should have been further helped by adding in some odd forms such as the piano or chant driven tracks in the middle of the album. Unfortunately the fatigue brought on by guitar is now introduced by the combination of the eternal emphasis on key instruments along with the pounding drums. The result of this is another powertrio album which is good enough in its songs, but tiring in its whole. However, those into the powertrio-formula will still find contentment with this release, I would expect.
