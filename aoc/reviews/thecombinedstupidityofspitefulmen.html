Summary

This album is a split album from the new London based House of Stairs label. It aims at releasing material from cutting edge heavy bands.

The music

First band up is American Heritage, a band making metal with gravely vocals, without ever grunting. The material leans heavily on guitar walls and the vocals. Even though it sounds pretty okay, I wouldn't particularly call this cutting edge, this sort of material is produced by a number of bands moving in this vein. The tracks easily flow into eachother. Best track is opener Dead Men Wear Tags (nice and morbid), which has some more complexity in the guitar lines.

Second up is Foe, who make more melodic instrumental material. The heavy guitar remains present, but due to the absence of oppressive vocals -most often complementary in this vein- and the strong melody the sound is pretty open, almost friendly. As the track continues it becomes more frantic and interweaved. Despite its significant length it has no problems in keeping the attention, a feat in itself for an instrumental piece. Good one.

The words on the vocals on Art Of Burning Water's material are pretty impossible to make out at times, but far better at others. These vocals oddly move between metal and a punky feel. The guitar sound is pretty clear, quite the opposite of the droning feel we often get, as well as melodic, giving this an open feel. A lot of guitar, but in no way guitar meistering: strong melodies, well adapted instrumentalists and a good total. This band has a bit of the feel of early Neurosis (first couple of albums). Thumbs up.

Conclusion

Let me be clear about one thing: there is nothing progressive about this music, that is, it does in no way come clear to the label 'Progressive'. House of Stairs objective, though, is to release cutting edge music, which is progressive in a descriptive way. It is amazing that with both multi-song bands the tracks pass into another without being noticed much, whilst the bands do have a clear identity
