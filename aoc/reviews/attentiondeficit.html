Summary

Another project-like thingy including session musician Michael Manring, ex-Testament Alex Skolnick and Tim Alexander of Primus. In the sideline I like to note that Skolnick also recorded a Crimsonesque album called Thonk, an album that I've never heard myself.

The music

The music on this album is instrumental (with some voices), rather freaky and free form. The guitars dominate ATM (that will later come back in two different permutations). On An Exchange Of Niceties we hear bell-like tones, a nicely bubbling low bass and Crimsonesque riffs. Again the band like to noodle around a bit and in all it doesn't sound bad, just a bit uneventful. The change into the more funky Scapula comes abrupt and unexpected (even to the mixer, I got the impression). Again the band is flirting with King Crimson, but trying to be a little looser and more avant-garde than said illustrious company. This song by the way is qute a tense one with nice rolling drumming and plenty of powerful chords. Still, the overall atmosphere is one that is "under the skin". After the short Snip we come to a more straightforward rock track. Fly, Pelican, Fly is by far the longest track of this album taking up almost 12 minutes. Lots of fiddling around on bass, guitar and drum often with musicians playing alongside instead of together. All very moody and relaxed. Later on the music becomes more involved with lots of ruffling and sharp dissonant guitars. Not very structured, but something's brewing here. MAT is a spooky track with some nice touches to it and quite a lot of tension in there making it one of the more succesful tracks on this disc. After the noisy Wrong we come into bassland with The Girl From Enchilada. Almost soundscaping on this track, but later on it becomes slightly jazzy relaxed kind of set-up. Ill Fated Consp ircayc sounds like the band is using synths or something, bu they are probably just loops (on this disc they promise "no synths". The groove returns on The Blood Room. The most striing song on the album with some heartrending guitarwork and tasteful bass work is Lydia. This song reminds me a in a loud way of Stu Hamm.

Conclusion

A hard disc to get into and although the character of the music can divert wildly within a few moments, the overall atmosphre is one of slow evolving improvisations. Lots of iddling qround and the way these guys play certainly is to my liking. The guitar parts are a little obtrusive and seemingly incoherent, but for instance the rhythm section does groove ( if they want to). All in all, however an album with a limited audience and although it is nice to listen to and the band does rock sometimes, they do not really shine. A line of music, where it is hard to separate the good from the bad, I have to admit giving this one the benefit of the doubt, but expect no songs on this one, but put it on if you want to have sound surrounding you and care for nothing else.
