Summary

Sonic Residue From Vapourspace is a remix album with Magna Carta material, made by remixer Mark Gage (aka Vapourspace).

The music

Don't think this is something of a high profile sampler, cause it's not. Due to the tracks not just being mixed individually, but also to a whole, this album sounds just as much, or maybe even more, as a trance album, than it does like a Magna Carta album. I can't say that I would be able to hum the original version of the tracks featured on this disc, but I should have been able to recognize some artists' styles or something. I didn't, apart from the vocal parts on Time Enough and Kansas. Now don't get me wrong, I'm not saying all tracks sound the same, after all, mostly the original music was used, still, there is less individuality about them then you might expect, especially when considering that not one album is represented with more than one song. This can be seen as Gage's accomplishment, but doing so would not make this CD any more interesting for Magna Carta fans. Vapourspace sounds a lot smoother than other rock remix CDs such as those of Marillion and Bush works, but it's probably helped a lot in that most of the tracks Gage took on were instrumental, and with that prevent themselves from sounding all cut up. But that's not giving Gage enough credits: he did make an effort to come up with a melodic track, without simply taking the original, thus creating a different view of it. Still, I can't help wondering whether albums like these aren't more scientific experiments than they are music albums. So far I haven't heard one: making danceable rock (like for instance The Prodigy and The Chemical Brothers do) creates material very different from remixes of rock music.
