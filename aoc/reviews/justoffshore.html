Summary

Electronic progressive or vice versa, who knows? Russ Mate is the mind behind Just Offshore, although originally a drummer and keyboardist and relatively heavy bands, this is something quite different.

The music

Ocean Storm is the melodic opener, holding somewhere between the orchestral sound of Vangelis and the sequencers of Tangerine Dream. But it is enough unlike any of them as well. There is a sense of foreboding and tension here, and the overall sound is spacey. A good EM track. The Possibility Exists is quite a bit different. It has classical elements, the synth strings, but also something akin strummed acoustic guitars to open with. Again, melodically everything is fine, and like the previous track there is an air of mystery. Some of the sounds remind me of Yanni, in his early years mind you, but never as bombastic.

House Of The Rising Sun is of course a well-known song, and one wonders why anyone should want cover it. The melody is still great of course, and it does fit in well with the other songs.

Meditation Medication is more rhythmic than the foregoing, calling to mind eighties TD. This song tends to wander a bit, the sound is rather tinny and it lacks the mystery of the opening tracks. The melody is slightly Arabic in feel.

Endless Journey is back to melodic EM and the sense of mystery that pervaded the first tracks. There is something melancholic about this one, although it all sounds rather friendly as well. There is actually very little change throughout the track: the moods are the same per track, the melodies are only varied upon slightly, an evolving of melodies. Only at two-thirds do we see a real change of character, adding what I would say is a Chinese note. The sounds here are rather sharp by comparison.

Galactic Construction brings fast and modern rhythms, a bit dance like in outset. The melody is not very important here. Rather nervous material this. Halfway, we get to have some melody, mainly by means of some cosmic sounding keyboards. Maybe the galaxy is taking form at this point.

From Here To There is a rather simple tune with the TD sequencers, again in their eighties form, as support for the music. There is not much solo work here, and the melodies remind most of Jarre this time around. Even though the main theme lies a`bit below the rest, you can hear it is a good one, although you have to pay special attention to it.

24/7 is the final track of the album. There is something of Patrick O'Hearn in here, and I get the feeling I have heard the melodies before on previous tracks. It does certainly breathe the same air as the other tracks do. In that sense Just Offshore is very consistent.

Conclusion

There is nothing here that would make me mistake it for progressive rock: this is simple electronic music, and it can be quite good EM at that. Especially the first half of the album was strong. The songs halfway did less for me, although Endless Journey is okay. The final tracks on the album were again in that mysterious vein that Just Offshore seems to be characterized by. In fact, only Meditation Medication and Galactic Construction are different, and could I appreciate markedly less.
