Summary

A neo-psyche band on the Think-Progressive label.

The music

At first listen this album seemed quite nice to me, but later listens revealed me that I had been too optimistic. The main problem I have with the music is that there is too little happening. One of the cases in point is Alone.

Spoon however is a bit more interesting. The name might make you think of Can and yes maybe the music will so too. The music has more ffects and ends in a long guitar solo. The melodies have something Arabic to them, the vocalist has a slight accent. Riff dominated psych for the most part.

Free sounds a bit standard again, but also wit ha bit of Portishead tucked away in there (but without the intensity). In Rain we hear quite a bit of percussion and more drive, also because of the keyboards.

Trance we hear in River II, dance and psych that is. A bit more complex than that maybe, but that was my first impression. The singing is a bit formless. Strange that it reminds me of Madonna in the more intimate middle part.

After a bubbly opening Time is psychedelic poprock with a focus on percussion, while Small is very moody again. Monroe might remind you of Floyd at first, rather typical space music, but ratehr boring. After a while an irritating guitar sets in squeaking away.

Dive sounds almost industrial, dark and with lots of feedback. Closer Dropout has intimate vocals and mesmerizing effects.

Conclusion

For some reason this album did not get to me after a first listen. The music is rather standard psych but possibly lacking in enthusing energy or on the other side, in subtlety. Hard to tell.
