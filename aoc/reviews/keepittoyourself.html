Mullmuzzler - Keep It To Yourself

Artist: Mullmuzzler
Title: Keep It To Yourself
Label: Magna Carta MAX-9039-2
Length(s): 52 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1999

Summary

Guillory of Dali's Dilemma and Mike Keneally of Zappa and various solo and other projects support Dream Theater's singer on his solo outing. It'd better be better than Winter Rose. Nice name for a band though, a bit like Poundhound. The music is written by Gardner, Guillory and a few of the guys from Shadowgallery.

The music

His Voice opens the album. In the beginning this is a rather dark and somber piece but towards the end the song becomes more metal-like. The vocal melody is very good and as such it is a very good beginning to this album. The next track, Statued, is quite funky in places. In the other places it is sometimes more melodic or more metallic. Personally I've never been a fan of LaBrie's voice, especially when he sings a bit higher. This is why I like his singing in the previous song better. However, this is a very personal thing. Still, the vocal melody is quite nice and the song becomes positively urgent towards the end. After these rather short pieces it is now time for something more elaborate with Shores Of Avalon. This piece shows some Tea Party influences in its Arabic stylings. In addition there are some heavy rhythm guitars (reminding me of Led Zeppelins Kashmir), but the vocal part is very melodic. This is actually one of the surprising positive points of this album: the vocal melodies are really good. Beelzebubba is something entirely different. The influences are from ELP here, with trumpetting sounds. I don't like it, it screams too much. Guardian Angel is something else again. A mid-tempo piece with the focus on the vocals and the instruments somewhat in the back. The song is very melodic and has a lot of variation. Of course, there's the mandatory guitarsolo but at the end the song becomes quite recognizable and tension mounts until the catchy chorus takes over again. The rhythm guitar part sounds familiar. A complex piece and not satisfying in all its details. Sacrifice opens as an acoustic ballad, but slowly picks up speed, power while the melody stays the same throughout. Although the song is a bit sugary, I like this track. Lace is a menacingly opening track with doubled vocals and is followed by the pianic ballad Slow Burn. The Gardner penned closer is As A Man Thinks. It turns out to be the case that I'm just not fond of this guy's work. I didn't like Age Of Impact and the two tracks I'm not fond of on this album are both Gardner tracks. Weird. And that while I do seem to like his Magellan stuff (as far as I heard it). The part of this song that I don't like is the again the screaminess of it. I do like the more melodic parts in it such as the mysterious, slightly percussive ending.

The artwork is distinctly ugly even if it tries to exemplify the name of the band.

Conclusion

Rather surprising to me, I like this album. I'm not overly fond of LaBrie's singing generally being either too cold or overly sweet, but I can't deny the quality of the songwriting on this album. Not to be missed by DT afficionados, but anyone interested in progmetal (I agree most of them seem to be interested in DT for some obscure reason) can surely take a listen. The music may be a bit little less metallic, but on the whole this is certainly not just the ballad side of DT.
