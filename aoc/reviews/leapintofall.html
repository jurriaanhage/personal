Summary

Poor Genetic Material originally was a soundscape duo formed by Glomb and Jaehne, releasing two albums. In 2000 they decided to team up with Alias Eye members Griffiths and Benedek. Due to the success of the album they recorded in this form, they decided to become a full fledged band, completed by Sturm.

The music

Rush Of Ages has a pretty open sound, belying none of the original disiciplines of its members: this is song oriented material, frequently reminding of Saga, not in the least because of Griffiths' liking to Sadler's voice, fused with soundscape like sections. The result is a rather longish track which is pretty ok, but doesn't quite live up to its try to epickity suggested by its five parts.

Thin Red Line is more a melodic track, bordering on pop, gently flow by as it does, this track doesn't exactly pose a challenge to the listener, even though it sounds nice enough.

Star Of Eden has a bit more spunk, being not only rockier, but also featuring some real nice changes in rhythm. The drums tend to sound a bit boxed, though. Still, clearly the best so far.

The title track is another non challenging one, with the flatness of the sound becoming even more apparent.

Antares is better again, having both gentle and stronger parts in it. The stereotypical drum roll by now starts to irritate me quite a bit, though, making me lean towards the negative on this one, still.

Fall is a gentle piano vocal outro, seemingly offering a truce after what's gone before.

Conclusion

The teaming up of forces makes a promise Poor Genetic Material can't quite live up to. The majority of the material is just okay, with only a couple of strong tracks, with the sound quality somewhat on the poor side as well. This is not a bad album, but somewhat below average none the less. It does float by in the background nicely enough, though.
