Summary

The music

We definitely have an odd one on our hands here. Opener Hi features dissonant vocals on an almost hypnotic melody. If you like your odd bit of off keyness, this should work for you. The drums in the back have a sort of art dancy quality, like we saw in the eighties with The Associates. After a still arty start, Yume is pretty much an avant dance track, the sort of stuff you'd expect from Front 242 or Front Line Assembly, made in the late eighties or later The Shamen. Neurotic and a tad repetitive. What is most striking, though, is the difference from the opener, although the rhythm in that one was present too. There is a driving feel to the track. Sou keeps the beat, adding some industrial sounds and guitar, sounding more like early Ministry. The vocals are now pretty raw and ragged. Kokoro is more experimental dance, featuring many samples and featuring B52's happiness. The interspersed sax sounds cannot hide the dance feel. Ai brings us yet another genre. There's a bit of waltz and a bit of nursery rhyme in this one, resulting in a sound that is a bit droll and silly. Tori is pretty odd, sung with a strange voice (sort of like Bono in Lemon, high, almost piped) and gentle strumming accompaniment, including toms. Katari adds in a rather dominant Hammond organ, the vocals now pretty dissonant again. The beat is eighties, something you might find with A Flock Of Seagulls. Nan has piano, synths and strong enough rhythm. The eerieness of the vocals shoos any thought of this being a normal track, though. Irodori is led by acoustic guitar and piano that has the drifting still quality found with Not Drowning, Waving. Despite the presence of residues of strangeness, the track sounds strong. Ame has a hard rock 'n' roll intro, turning out fit enough for the track. Sort of B52's without the women. Odd, but pretty okay. The final track, featuring just a Japanese title is something, possibly this album, played with the fast forward button pressed down. Quite. As a ghost track, or afterthought track 11 also features another of the dissonant tracks featuring toms and keys. Pretty repetitive, though. As the piece continues acoustic guitar loops start drowning out the vocals, which only return in hearing after the loops reach their climax.

Conclusion

After a tranquil-ish opener which could be put in the avant genre, we move into a realm that's dominated by various kinds of late eighties off the road dance music. Luckily this realm soon crumbles, deviating us back to avant land, via the nursery. This album contains a number of worthwhile tracks, from a progressive point of view: opener Hi, and Tori through Ame. The dance stuff is too far out, although interesting, whilst the closer is possibly intended to add weirdness. I think that these people can come up with a good album, experimental, and have to say I almost think they did with this one. For anyone to feel so, (s)he'd need a pretty broad taste, since we do cover a nice bit of ground on this album, genre wise.
