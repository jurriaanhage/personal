Summary

A new band from Germany with their selfproduced debut.

The music

This is a pleasant surprise: the music opens as a dark Hollywood soundtrack (a la Elfman), somber and dramatic and mostly string like synths. The sound is quite clear. Oddly enough, this first track was performed and recorded by the bass player. Kongfrontation is the continuation of the opener, The Inauguration Of King Kong. This song has a long up-tempo intro with loud guitars and keyboards. The vocal part is not that strong, the vocals are rather accented and well the lyrics are not really sung either. The band is more at home in the instrumental parts, that range between progressive metal and neo-progressive. After the vocal part we get a long up-tempo keyboard solo with a powerful driving rhythm guitar as a basis. Kindergeburtsdag is again an instrumental. The music on this track is quite tasteful and somewhat in the style of the opener. The music however is more varied here, with some rather unexpected breaks like an alternation between hard driving passages and some bouncy parts. During the bouncy part, the guitar comes to the fore with a strong melodic solo. Then the guitar turns acoustic and the driving passage returns. Because of the rather dark sound and the atmospheric passages I'm reminded of Cliffhanger on their Not To Be Or Not To Be album. This band does have more progmetal influences. Good track. Maze Of Despair is a vocal track, opening with a musical box. Again, the music has strong film music influences, before it moves into rather offbeat neo-progressive rock. The vocal part is again quite different: moody and in the Floydian spirit. Subtly we are moved into some akin to a tango. The vocals do sound better here. The song is concluded by an emotive guitar solo accompanied by piano. Before we come to main dish of the album, we first visit Der Mann, based on Aram Khatchatourian's Toccata. Like the other instrumental tracks, there is a rich variation of passages, being alternated. The band however does seem to have a knack for stringing these passages together and by themselves as well, the passages are quite nice. This song is characterized by a rather driving part in which the bubbly keyboards lend extra menace. I like those rolling drums. The guitars yield a sense of panic of some kind. When Darkness Turns To Light is a the main dish with a length of over 26 minutes. The opening part, The Hill, of the in total seven parts is a vocal part. The melody is okay, but not striking, the overall mood is sad. The high pitched guitar is very good, full of emotion, but all too soon it seems to give up and a lush carpet of keyboards takes over. The music here can be compared very well with Aragon and their Mouse. I think overall the music has that same darkness and somberness to it, but not dreary, just dark and potent. The keyboard solo is rather typical for prog and does not do much for me. The next instrumental part features some marimba, while Depression has a nice vocal melody. Again, I can't shake off that Cliffhanger reference. Musically, this band is less complex, but especially the atmosphere and the vocals are quite related. The following guitar solo is again very good as we move into part 4, Leaving Darkness. Lots of soloing here on keyboard and guitar. The keyboard solo's do not sound very fresh though. In Don't Believe In Miracles I'm reminded of Spocks Beard's The Light, because of the vocoded vocals. These are alternated with poppy but enthusing keysolo's, punctuated by the guitar. Metamorphosis might remind some 'The mist crawls from the canal like a primordial phantom of romance to huddle under a cascade of neon pollen'. I like the keyboards here, sounding a bit more nineties. Now we actually come to the Light, the last part of the track. This one opens hazy, a bit in the style of Shine On Your Crazy Diamond, but just a bit. The vocal part does not have that extra, that could lift up this long track by its hinges. The song ends with a long guitar solo, melodious, but again lacking that little extra. The album closes with a filmic bonus track for which I have no name.

Conclusion

Well, you've read it: quite a nice album this one, althogh by the strenght of the first tracks I think I did expect more from the epic. The vocals are the weakest part on this CD (but sometimes they are quite okay) and the style varies between progmetal, Aragon and Cliffhanger with also some more moody and filmic passages. The guitar is very prominent, the keyboards much less so. Although the music may not sound that new, the band has added some interesting new features such as the film music and some, more minor, details.
