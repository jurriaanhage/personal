Summary

Hailing from Atlanta, Georgia, this is an American foursome.

The music

New Year's Eve opens with pleasant piano and cello and then moves into a pacier part with rolling drums. The music is definitely not prog, a bit like a rockier version of Agent Cooper with catchy harmonies in the chorus. The vocals of Crocker are rather low in in the verses, but stronger and higher in the chorus. I really like his voice there. Toward the end of the song becomes rather chaotic. Premature Speculation opens in complex fashion, a bit in the style of the chaotic parts of Faith No More. Again Crocker shows himself as an imposing vocalist and this time there's quite a lot of variation in the vocal lines, some parts reminding me of Be Aggressive by Faith No More. Again the drums have a powerful drive and the guitars have a supporting role. Westernrise opens in mid-tempo with nicely bass-piano interplay. For the rest this is quite a catchy track with backing vocals in various places. These vocals are not always integrated well into the music, but they do introduce variation. Haitian Holiday is a driven rock track with a very abrupt ending going over into the more balladic The Art Song, a possible single track of accessible grungy rock with a memorable chorus. Except for the la-la-la's maybe. Terminal is a heavier rock track with melodic interjections by the keyboards and the somewhat wavery backing vocals. The vocals of Crocker are hasty approaching rap speed. Most Likely To... brings back a bit of chaos into our lives, while the last and longest track on the album Half Of Nothing opens with a tune familiar from childhood (or something). The song itself is plodding rock with rowdy vocals and a varied rocking middle part. The piano punctuates the guitar riffing.

Conclusion

A grungier version of Enchant, a less wacky version of Faith No More I guess are the best approximations of this band in terms of bands you might know. The music is definitely in the rock vein, but also definitely not what we generally consider prog. However, like Agent Cooper, I do think that lovers of the accessible side of progressive rock, can be charmed by the energetically performed songs that tend to be well-written and good melodies. And let's not forget the varied vocal styles of mister Crocker right there.
