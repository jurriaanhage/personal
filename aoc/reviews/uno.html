Summary

The bands hails from Spain, and Juan Olmos seems to be the band leader.

The music

Asesinato En Orient Express & Desenlace is a mysterious opener, with a filmic kind of tension. Slowly the music builds up as drums, guitar and synth strings are added. The drums have a rather harsh sound, but this might even be on purpose. The organ sounds gives the music a authentic sheen. This rocks quite well, with a bit of playfulness added via some of the guitar melodies. Then the music drops out, and we come to rather melodic and peaceful piano plus electronics. This is only for a short while, before we enter the up-beat part again, in which piano dances and the guitar drones. The guitars by the way have elements of progmetal. The rather loud drums could have been a bit more varied. This is automatic pilot. If it had not been for the rowdy guitar bits, then Alan Parsons Project would not have been that wide off the mark. The acoustic guitar also gets its say toward the end, so all in all this is very much a mixed bag, something for everyone. Something of a showcase. So I wonder, what could be up next?

Merlin opens with violin, followed by tranquil flute playing, seemingly based on a folk melody of some kind. There is very much a live feel to this song; did they actually do some engineering here? A rather sleepy making affair, this. The drums sound really crappy here. I wonder how the music would have sounded with a bit more in the way of production and such. Difficult to say, but as it is I tend to see a lack of versatility.

With Mite & Mita we move into acoustic territory for a short while. Viaje A Escocia is however one of the longer tracks. A folky tune, played in a somewhat stately style, gives way to interesting sounding keyboards with vocoded vocals in the back. This is really well-done and distinctive. The continuation on guitar is a bit of a disappointment. Melodically a bit too trite. The style can be compared to a folkier version of Camel, Harbour of Tears-era. In line with progressive expectations there are plenty of passages here strung together to make up the tune. The transition between these takes place naturally enough, although at the end of the tune, I think the band overdoes it. Too much is going on, there is too little line in the music.

With Notre Damme the pace comes back into the music. This is a rather steady tempo rocker, which tends to plod on. Tull influences abound here with some sharp guitar work and of course the flute. Not particularly distinctive this one. Recorriendo Mundo is in a similar vein, although a bit more frolic. Again, the lack of dynamics and decisiveness in the drums makes my attention waver. And I can not listen to the music without hearing them, they are too much on top of the music. This is certainly a drawback.

Zyclopea is not much different, although the violin and flute tend to lighten things up a bit and adds some variation. The electric guitar is in blues mode this time. Locura Temporal is a nice one, a sort of Karda Estra feel pervades with a string and wind orchestra playing it, actually the flute, the violin and some keyboards. Works out really well here.

Elektrickal Night is a total surprise. This is vocal hard rock with a dose of strings and flute. The end result is quite nice, although somewhat straightforward in pace. The flute and strings do help it make more interesting. Fantasia Del Rey Arturo is back to the style of Locura Temporal although a bit more playful. If only they had not put in that lame part, in which the drums come to the fore again. The guitar is present, but only somewhere in the back, below the 'orchestra'. A very melodic tune. Final verdict: okay.

En La Corte brings back in the vocal, this time in Arabic style. And he seems to be singing from the cellar. Piano is the main instrument here, at least melody wise. Then the music picks up beat, the piano calling the tune and the guitar meandering along. There are some vocoded vocals here again, which give the song a very distinctive flavour. The vocals seem more improvised than sung. The sad violin and the nice pacey melody on guitar add to make this a good tune.

Feeling Your Breath is the first vocal track in which I can also follow the lyrics. There is some accent here, the vocals are indeed in English. The song is an acoustic ballad, with an excursion into Jeff Buckley territory (that and the voice of Damian Wilson is what I am reminded of when the notes go up). Surprising.

Conclusion

I think more dynamic and versatile drumming could do the world for this record. As it stands now we have a number of folkyish tunes which sometimes remind of Camel, sometimes of Tull. Almost all of it is instrumental, with only a few voices thrown in (including the really nice vocoded ones in Viaje Escocia). However the drums preclude me from enjoying the music. At times, the rest of the music is good enough to let me forget about the drums, but the level of the music is not that consistently high. Besides the opener there are not many tracks which stand up. Taken together with the lesser production (important for this style of music) and some doubtful melodies (a bit too trite for my tastes), I can only say better luck next time.
