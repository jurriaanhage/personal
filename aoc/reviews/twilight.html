Tracks

1) My Ivory Soul 8.40
2) The Theme 1.31
3) The Fisherman 8.42

7) Flower Of Innocence 3.51

Summary

After their much heralded concert at iO Progheaven 1 in November 1998, I had to write something about these guys, so here it is. The following message is for organizers of prog festivals around the world: invite these guys.

The music

The album opens with My Ivory Soul, that reveals the Genesis influences of the band: vocals like those of Gabriel and the general atmosphere is also of those band. The music is emotional and easily carries you away. The name of the band derives from King Crimson and in fact this more or less explains the dark side to the music. After the powerful first three minutes we come to a whispered part and the music stays slow and dark, reminiscent in some way of Cliffhanger, with a large bass presence, but as always the ghost of Genesis is not far off. Gosebumps aplenty during the last few minutes where various shards of melodies are combined into a potent brew. The Theme is a darling short instrumental interlude mostly acoustic, but also featuring some keyboards as a dressing. It leads up to The Fisherman the first part of which is thoroughly in the seventies Genesis style: a great vocal melody, the lyrics a story, percolating acoustic guitar and some Hackettish electric guitar. The bridge is all-out drowned in organ and more powerfull vocals. Then we return to the melancholy of the first two verses and the thematic chorus. After the interlude featuring acoustic guitar (a la Hackett's acoustic solo albums) and seagulls, the music builds up again to slowly fade away after a rather noisy Crimsonesque eruption. Then the following acoustic build-up is cut short by a heavy plodding rather aggressive passage. Tomorrow Happened is a more menacing track with like the previous tracks many different passages linked, each of them with its own atmosphere featuring wailing ethereal guitar, flute and washes of keyboards including mellotron, but also some more modern rocking parts. The lyrics are at times very weird and sometimes I really can't tell what is meant. This track is a little too fragmented for my tastes, although it does have its moments such as the bombastic build-up at the end. The Black Cage is the next one up, opening with what seem soundscapes. After percussion sets in the vocals commence. The guitar plays some very ethereal tones here, while the bass lowly bubbles and gurgles. A sweeping chorus (yup, those goosebumps again) and a strong guitarsolo round of this rather dark track. A Game With Shifting Mirrors is a longish instrumental, quite complicated in part with even some Discipline-like guitar work and varied percussion. This is the most Crimsonesque track on the album and it can be quite estranging featuring keyboards that remind more of ELP. After the dissonant violence of the previous track Flower Of Innocence is the almost acoustic, almost peaceful closer.

Conclusion

A good album in the spirit of the old Genesis (and sometimes not just the spirit), but darker, revealing hints of King Crimson. It also reveals that in some places the band could do better: in some of the tracks they seem to want to put in a little too much into their songs making them sound fragemented. Still, the emotion, the playing and the melodies are there. For those who already own this disc: live they're even much better. In fact to any one able to see these guys live: do it!
