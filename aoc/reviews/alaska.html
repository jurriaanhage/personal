Summary

Another self produced album from the States (the States have many of these it seems or probably they have a higher ratio of e-mail accessing bands).

The music

The first track Icespirits is a slowmoving track with relaxed, varied drumming like the drummer think conciously about what he will hit next. The music is mostly keyboards for the rest and of course there are the vocals. They are a mix between Jon Anderson and Pete Nicholls of IQ: high and a bit light. The vocal melody is quite nice and the composition is quite nice as well. I do think however that the vocals could be better. For my tastes they are both a bit too high and selfsame and as often happens with recognizable vocals: it makes the music sound selfsame. Museum Dreams is quite comparable to the previous track with much keyboards with some Asian overtones added and again the music can be compared to a quiet version of IQ, but lacking a certain quality, liveliness or dynamics might be that quality. The vocals are also quite slow usually and it is when Nicholls/Menel sing like this that I hear the correspondence (i.e. compare with Common Ground, but without a guitar eruption at the end). Two Shades Of Grey is even slower with soft percussion and a peaceful world music like atmosphere. The melody is quite memorable. Anyman's Tomorrow is the first longish track. After a somewhat classical opening we come to the relaxed vocal part that has a certain western quality because of the (synthetic) harmonica. The notes on this song seem a little too high for the singer at times. After the middle we get a nice intermezzo after which the waltzing merry part sets in. After the electronic Bardanes we move right into Reason To Wonder. Two strange tracks with strange vocals. The music comes close to cosmic electronic music but with live drumming and a little vocals. Very atmospheric, with good and intimate singing. After the nice classical Mesa Extrana (with live string quartet), and mostly piano and percussion, we come to Tiananmen Square. Over 10 minutes long the track features a quick opening with neurotic drumming and quite a lot of variation melodically. Typically progressive. The bassdrum adds a distinctive menacing note to the music. The song is quite up-tempo at times, but the echoed vocals have no really striking vocal melody to work with. The intermezzo is a rather complex keyboard solo with Oriental stylings. Wellsbridge like Tiananmen Square contains quite a load of lyrics. Again the song is not striking enough to warrant my full attention during the over 11 minutes that it is long. Unfortunately the cd began to buckle during this track. It also refused to be played during the next tracks until I had tried it in another cd player. And then it played well again in my own cd player. Very strange, but never mind. Caring is a very quiet track, a melancholy soothing ballad. The last track is Forest Of Heaven and again I'm not that satisfied although the electronic rock in the middle is quite nice (a bit louder may have been better. If you want to rock, rock). The problem is probably that starting from Tiananmen Square the sameness crept into the music and if then the compositions are a bit longer it is hard to really enjoy it anymore

The album has very nice, colourful, computer generated artwork.

There are some religious and humanitarian sides to the lyrics and in spirit this album can be compared to Jon Anderson's (solo) work, but more down to earth.

Conclusion

Quite a long album for a first one and full of rather nice compositions, overall friendly and keyboard dominated. One of the problems I have with this album is the fact that there are many vocal parts and also that there is little variation in them. Musically the band should be compared to a less rocking and complex version of Yes with some echoes of the softer side of IQ. The length of the album and the length of the composition make that I tend to like the album less towards the end.
