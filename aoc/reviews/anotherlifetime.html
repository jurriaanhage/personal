Summary

Simon Phillips is a highly regarded drummer who releases here an album by his first self formed band.

The music

Nine compositions written by either Phillips himself or together with Ray Russell. After a rather laid-back opening with the saxophone being the leading instrument we move into the straits of jazzrock. To my feeling, the jazzrock is more of the American kind (if that means anything to you). In the following more relaxed part the guitar plays the prominent part with rather rolling drums playing along. The song now sounds quite ethereal. Then the riffing part returns. And then it is plain alternation between these various parts with slight variations. Towards the end the drumming becomes more involved. P O V opens with percussive and a dark sound like the drowsy nodding of a large animal. As always I might say the music tries to be tasteful, but also cannot help to be a little sterile because of it. What does help is the often quite ethereal and open guitar sound. Freudian Slip is a bit bouncy in a relaxed kind of way, but stays in the jazzrock realm. Mostly guitar here. Eyes Blue For You goes in a more funky direction, but the open guitar sound stays. Sometimes during this disc Holdsworth comes to mind, but I still feel the music is more American, less involved than aforementioned guitarist. Kumi Na Moja is with slightly over eight minutes the longest track here. It is also up to now the best I think. Especially the more important role of keys in this track and the quite active drumming throughout make this an attractively dynamic and at times even somewhat bombastic track. Very nice. Past halfway now, we come to Mountain High, which is rather like the earlier tracks with its alternation between the short rocking and the longer laid-back parts. Some piano and acoustic guitar here as well. E S P opens with heavy guitars and an organ sound. On the whole a rather active track. As happened more often, the music on this track sometimes reminds me of the more active, instrumental, almost jazzrockish pieces of Toto. Euphrates opens with some extensive drumming, but like the following title track adds little to what we have already heard, except maybe that there is some definite fiddling around in the closer. The melodies on the whole are not bad, but they can hardly be called memorable and are typical for the line of music.

Conclusion

Very little showcasing which shows that this is an album by a band which happened to be formed by a drummer (who is also responsible for much of the composing). Anyway, the music is very much in the jazz rock style and not experimental in any way. Just plain jazzrock and if you like that, you will find this album well constructed and well executed and you need not look further. Toto in its more jazzrocky instrumentals comes to mind and another reference might be Allan Holdsworth. The music however, unlike bands like Finneus Gauge, cannot really be considered progressive. A problem with this kind of music is that it is often a bit too sterile, but I can easily enough listen to it. Best track: Kumi Na Moja.
