Artist: Ageness
Title: Imageness

Summary

From Scarab to Ageness, these people have been at it for some time. I was not satisfied with Rituals, but I heard they had improved so time to take a listen.

The music

The opening of the album is rather quiet, and stylistically is rather like the opening of the Seasons End album. Then the guitars set in and it turns out that this is a rather poppy track with American style vocal harmonies (all by Eriksson, who is still the main figure in this band). I'm still not impressed by the vocals (they are similar to those of Nicholls, but for some reasons I like Nicholls better), I would like the singing to be a bit lower, like he does later on Fear. This song at first reminds me quite a lot of older Genesis (Wind and Wuthering). There even seems to be a video of this song. It includes a rather jazzrockish part in the middle that turns into a nice guitarsolo later on. The song ends anthemic. Chain Reaction opens quite up-tempo with a nice riff and a layer of keyboards in the back. The organ drenched chorus is very good on this track with nice vocal melodies. And a great guitar solo rounds it all off. All in all a catchy prog rock song with quite some variation, but still very accessible. The ten minute Metamorphosis opens with piano, and turns out to be quite a plodding piece with quite some organ in there and a sound that reminds me again of Genesis. The dark atmosphere of the latter part of the track is rather IQish. Plenty of mood changes such as the one at the end where we move into a rather relaxed Pendragon style passage with a soaring guitar. Sequels is takes up slightly more than half of this album. The opening is quite strange and might remind some of Peter Hammill in his weird vocal days. The second part, Shadowplay, is acoustic with that old-Genesis atmosphere. Some very nice vocal melodies on this one. Philandering is an instrumental part that could have come (and might even) straight of a Genesis record with some typical Banks-like keyboards, while the lyrics of the next part, Royal Suite, are in the Foxtrot style. The track up to now has been a kind of nostalgic trip, you might think and indeed it has been, but it certainly is not a bad trip. The music becomes certainly less like Genesis afterwards, but there's one rather strong reference to Yes in there still and the organ/guitar solo at the end might well remind some of the Flower Kings at their most active. At the end the opening of the album recurs.

Conclusion

An album I could appreciate quite a lot better than Rituals. The references are IQ and Genesis for the most part, but as long as the music is good, I won't complain overly much. I'm getting a little more used to the vocals, but I would like them to be somewhat lower. Still, a very nice, consistent and accessible album with appreciable melodies and good compositions and quite little fiddling around.
