Summary

I've assumed these are the track titles standing behind the Japanese ones.

The music

Welol, it's quite easy to characterize the music on this album: tiring. The signatures are often changing and quite hard and also hard to follow and as a whole the music on Friends is very disconcerting and disorientating. Also, the music doesn't really flow, but seems to pounes more and more into rocks just below the waterline and then continues again, against all else. The saxophone plays a prominent part on this CD as well, and some synth solo's can als be admired and once in a while an occassional melody pops up. I'm a bit overreacting here, but the listener should be warned that the tone set in this track tends to be followed up by later tracks and the music is bouncy, quirky and well, I just ran out of descriptive resources.

The next one up is a little less disjointed maybe and especially at the end becomes quite a powerful track, high on marimba mostly.

The same holds for White Collar Worker vs. Black Rubber Man. Although the music is quirky and it flows very little, there's a certain tension that is build up here, using a saxophone (that sounds it is being wrung out) and organ and later on treated keyboard sounds. At the end some more recent KC influences enter upon the stage, but Tipographica is far more busy and seem to have placed their bets more on variation then raw power (e.g. compare Thrak).

The next track shows more openly the fact that this music is very music jazzrock based and also that I like them better loud, otherwise the band starts like just any jazzrockband without anything going for them and I wouldn't want that. Still, the music has a sense of humour that is found more often in jazzrockish versions of progressive rock (Cantebury) and this is also found in the nonsensical titles (because if they really do have something to do with the music, I'd like the writer of that music to explain it to me, pronto). This sense of humour is of course not limited to titles alone, but is also found in the happy sounding parts of the musi, although the long guitar solo at the end of the track is very serious indeed.

The other tracks are all similar in style, more or less although there are some excursions into plain jazzrock and jazz.

Funny that I like something like this, but can't be too fond of say Boud Deun, while they are comparably inaccessible. Also strange, because Boud Deun is a little more original, because what Tipographica does is to make plain jazzrock less obvious sounding by interjecting strange breaks, weird sounds and so on. The taste is a strange thing.

Conclusion

Not for the timid and to be honest, not for very normal people either. If you pride yourself on being fond of hard-to-get-into-music and you have a partner to get rid off, you might try playing this over a romantic dinner (I'm not liable for hospital expenses). Anyway, I have to admit to liking it after being a little careful at first, and it does take getting used to, but it's a nice experience when you're into it, although if you listen close (especially at the end of the CD, a lot of it is plain jazz rock). Strong inaccessible fusion for the experienced listener. (Does that make me experienced too?)
