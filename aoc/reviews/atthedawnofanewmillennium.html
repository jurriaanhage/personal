Summary

Singer Gary Savoie was leaving so the band thought it a good time to close down that period from 1992-1999 collecting here songs from their three albums released in the nineteenhundreds. To my surprise Destiny? was also released on the Musea label in Europe.

The music

I could do this album easily by pointing you for most of the songs to my reviews of Destiny? and its predecessor Theatre Of The Mind. In fact only Cinderella is not on those albums. However, I'll give you a quick rundown on what I happen to think of the tracks now several years after my encounter with them. We open with Destiny? from the album of the same name. With five tracks Destiny? is the most sampled disc and by far the one that takes up most of the minutes on this compilation. Mystery is a band that can be likened to Journey and Styx, mostly because of the great emotional vocals of Savoie, but also because of the compositional approach of the band in its entirety. The music of Mystery is more symphonic (hence more Styx than Journey) than either of these bands as exemplified by the bombastic opener. The vocal parts at times also have strong AOR leanings. Another title track follows, The Theatre Of The Mind, opens subduedly, but with a strong undercurrent. The vocal parts are very accessible in places, maybe a bit too, but the instrumental parts are very good with some sonorous electric guitar lines and melodic acoustic guitar. The metal guitar solo in the middle is punctuated by some melodic keyboards and the bass is plucked audibly. Both songs serve as a kind of introduction to the album, with Destiny? being the stronger of the two tracks. It seems to me that on Theatre Of The Mind sounds a bit reserved. Before The Dawn opens with some French spoken vocals (remember the band is from Quebec). The song opens friendly and melodically and has a terrific melodic intermezzo right in the middle with a great twist at the end. Dominated by acoustic guitar, In My Dreams is a sad melodic ballad with dreamy synths. Black Roses also opens in a dreamy way with a large roll for acoustic guitar. However, the song erupts during the chorus, which consists simply of the title. The easy intermezzo consists of flute, and is maybe a bit too laid-back. The second instrumental passage is more involved and includes some sharp guitar playing. In The Inner Journey - Part II, Savoie has some goosebumps to inflict on his listeners. Cinderella was the only track I was not familiar with as yet. It is a pumping rock track with some nicely audible bass playing and bombastic keyboards. It shows here that the songwriter is not as mature as yet, but the elements of the later sound are here, such as the tension building middle part and the vocal parts. The final three tracks taking up almost 28 minutes are all from Destiny?, in fact they are the final three tracks of that album. The Mourning Man takes a little time getting underway, being a bit les concise than we are by now used to. As a composition it seems a bit unfinished. Submerged opens with underwater echoes. The almost 15 minute closer is Shadow Of The Lake, a major undertaking with Floydian atmospheres and a slow but sure build-up.

Conclusion

Professional production, great songs, great melodies and a great vocalist make this an important release. The music owes a lot to Journey, Styx and has elements of progressive metal (in the sometimes heavy guitar work) and also of AOR (in the harmonies). Even more notable is the use of the acoustic guitar which is present in many tracks giving the song a kind of medieval/folk feel. Accessible material, but recommended nonetheless, or is it because?
