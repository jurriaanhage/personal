Summary

This very productive band (five albums in three years) has released their second one for SI (I have heard neither of the previous four though).

The music

This band isn't like any of the other SI bands, this is no neo and it doesn't even sound like Tracy Hitchings or Sally French. This band makes music in the somewhat obscure haze of This Mortal Coil and the Cocteau Twins. The vocals are very pronounced, but to be honest I've never been too fond of female vocals, I just don't like it high. Still, I can get over Lorna. The music is very dark and moody and at times dissonant, maybe a bit of the softer Univers Zero being evident here. The guitar when used sears somewhere on the background and isn't noticed very often.

On this album there songs which are slow and on which the presence of the vocals is an overwhelming factor. These songs have a lot in common with the Cocteau Twins' Garlands or less noisy, Bel Canto. Lorna's voice moves in higher registers than Twins' Elizabeth, who sounds a bit like Siouxsie. Many of the songs have quite an accessible and easygoing vocal part, but the instrumental parts can be quite menacing and quirky. Best song on the album is Wired to the Moon in which Wileman can perform all the tricks he knows with he himself in the picture. The other tracks are what you might call mini soundtracks to thrillers or horror movies, menacing and strange, yet not too. Sick of Ordinary is a bit between the too classes, because the vocals are beside the usual even here.

Conclusion

Very acceptable album. Not very uplifting or varied and at best menacing, repetitive (in the good sense) and intriguing. The drumming is programmed so you'd better not pay attention to those. Points of fixation are the vocals and the menacing keys. This is not something I'll listen to a lot (what is?), but if you're into this kind of music, give it a try. I would like to hear a solo album by Wileman, especially when he takes all of the above even further. Might turn out to be very interesting. Best to my liking turned out to be 1, 4, 9 and the instrumentals.
