Inquire - The Neck Pillow

Artist: Inquire
Title: The Neck Pillow
Label: Self produced
Length(s): 66 minutes
Year(s) of release: 2000
Month of review: 07/2000

Line up

Dieter Cromen - guitar, vocals, bass
Robert Koehler - keyboards
Thomas Kohls - drums, percussion
Uli Mueckenheim - bass

Tracks

1) Die Bettwurst Part One 20.44
2) Circles 17.39
3) The Death Of Ase 2.28
4) Swidwin 7.09
5) Die Bettwurst Part Two 16.22
6) Die Berliner Bettwurst Part One 1.57

Summary

Second album by this German band. Very professional artwork on this one. Now for the music. The bass player has been exchanged for a new one since the debut.

The music

Bettwurst is literally bedsausage. I guess this is what a neckpillow is. After an introduction to the story in German the music starts with acoustic guitar and the some spaceous electric guitar. The 20 minute track quite naturally takes some time getting underway with highpictched keyboards. Quite abruptly we move into a more strident part with menacing tones, low organ work and sharp melodic electric guitar. All in all rather in Cliffhanger style, quite diverse and complex and dark as well. The nthe song takes a turn for the more epic. In these more driving, heavy parts, with a strangely frolic part inserted into it, the lyrics do not always seem to fit very well into the music. However, the music continues to be interesting, yet typically progressive rock but with progmetal leanings in the heavy plodding passages. In addition to English lyrics there are also some German and Latin ones, as in the final part of this first track Schwere Jungs Und Leichte Maedchen. This part sounds a bit classical because of the fugue like organ. The vocals in Latin are spoken in dark tones and the music is very much progmetal at this point, not fast or overly loud, but the impression is there. The track ends abruptly, a bit too maybe. Circles is the next track and with over 17 minutes also not a short one. According to the booklet it is inspired by Ralph Waldo Emerson. It opens quite relaxedly with melodic, slightly repetitive guitarwork and dark vocoded spoken words. After a short classical interlude with synthetic clarinet the solo guitar accompanied by slow drums and low bass takes the fore. Percussively then the music gains momentum with sawing guitars, washes of cymbals and well-timed keyboards to take a break for the progmetal once again. Especially the drummer gets awfully agitated here. The final part is filmic and solely keyboards. After Grieg's The Death Of Ase, which fits in very well with the album and is mostly guitar, we come to oddly titled Swidwin. A darkish track. De continuation of Die Bettwurst is less than the first part. Some of the ingredients are somewhat cliche and the melodies in this track (especially the vocal ones) are not particularly enticing, while the guitarwork in the middle lacks in imagination. I'd have hoped for more notwithstanding the driven playing. The final part is better in that it tends to hold the attention a bit better. The Pasadena Porno Casanovas indicate at the end that they want to go to Berlin. In this respect I can also mention that Inquire supports the WM 2006 (world championship football) campaign for Germany. The album ends on a humoristic note with typical German music. The kind you do not want to hear.

Conclusion

This band has grown. On their debut the band was doing very well in moods and such but on the long epic they weren't as good as I hoped. The typical filmic keyboards are gone for the most part, but in return we get a good album full of rather typical prog with powerful moods and passages, but also some more classical and subdued passages. In addition to the artful approach to the music and the more complete packaging this is certainly a large step forward and I have the impression this band with its combination of Floyd, Marillion, progmetal and more typical progbands such as Cliffhanger, Aragon and Ageness they ought to find many a fan.
