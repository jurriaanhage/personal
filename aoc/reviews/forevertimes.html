Forever Times - Forever Times

Artist: Forever Times
Title: Forever Times

Length(s): 45 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1999

Summary

After many a problem with releasing their debut album they finally did so on the new Dutch Zizania label.

The music

Six tracks in 45 minutes, opening with the eight minute In The Eye Of Compassion. The opening is not bad, but I'm not very satisfied with the vocal part and the song does not really get under way as it were. The driving chorus is a lot better, with a good recognizable melody. Their are slight Arabic tendencies in the melodies. The most easy references can be made to the Gathering, but the music is somewhat more 'progressive'. After the second chorus we get an unnatural feeling transfer to "strings" on the keyboards, that wind the track down. The bass playing is nice here, with a feeling. System Of The Heart is the second track. Some of the vocals parts on this one are not to my liking and the lyrics also sometimes do not fit well making the track sound a bit unnatural at times. But the chorus is again quite memorable, the guitar solo is nice and the song is enlivened by a saxophone (but I did not miss it in the live version). The drummer is quite a busy bee during this track. Silence Of The Mind opens ploddingly and is, because of the heavier guitar, a more metallic track. The "violin" of this track is a nice addition, but the vocals have the same problem as the previous track: unnatural phrasings. Instrumentally Ill...Or Perhaps Distorted at first calls Yngwie to mind with its slightly classical tendencies. Later the saxophone sets in again, and the songs turns more moody (Stuart Hamms Flow My Tears?), but not without some pretty heavy drum passages. We come now to the better two of the six tracks. First is Flashover. The chorus on this one is great. The vocals of Laura is somewhat wavery, but they carry all the more weight for it. The electric and acoustic guitar of round it all off. The epic track of the album is Skyang-Khang-Ri which is already a fairly old track (I have the tape with a predecessor of this song from 1996). The lower regions are not as I hear not where Laura feels most at home. The theme is then introduced on guitar. The double vocals are not really a success, but the instrumental parts are nice, for instance as when the military drums set in. The second part of the track, Crosswinds, is instrumental and has plenty of variation. The vocals of the first part return in the last part, but then the vocal parts becomes a little more poppy. The song closes with the part D, And All The Gold Has Turned To Stone, which is an instrumental part and a good climax with great melodic electric guitar.

Conclusion

The difference with the band live is quite strong: the music is harder, but more importantly the singer goes deeper making up in inspiration where lyrics or melody may fall short. This inspiration does not come over on this record and one gets to here all these lyrics that just don't fit, the not so great harmony vocals and such. It seems that this is one of those bands with plenty of ideas, but not yet the know-how or maturity to also make this work in the studio. A better producer or just keeping up the hard work will certainly help in this respect. Promising, but plenty of room for improvement.
