Summary

Taurus are a Dutch band that started off somewhere around 1976. They released one fairly well-known album called Illusions of a Night and there have been more releases like Works 1976-1981 and a live album See You Again. There are some tapes and singles as well, but I think most of it has been released on CD now. Even on Illusions of a Night, the sound was very of themselves and not entirely progressive in the strict sense, but more in the pop direction.

The music

This line has been followed on this new album, with a somewhat changed line-up, on which some old songs are present as well (this can also be found by looking at the writers of the songs).

Unfortunately the progressiveness has just about left the music and we are left with a kind of medium tempo pop and a few instrumentals. I don't think this album will interest anyone interested in progressive rock, except for completist purposes. This means that for prog fans, the already mentioned live-album and Illusions of a Night will be of more interest, because these do contain some original progressive material.

What about the music itself then? Well, the production isn't very good especially the drums (or is it a computer) and the drumming itself is uneventful. The vocals are thin and not overall pleasing. The songs are of the standard type: intro, couplets, choruses and the occaisonal bridge. The only difference that you might see with real pop, is the sometimes prominent keyboards, but still they can't prevent the music from getting stuck in a morass.

Looking for reference, most of the songs have no reference within the progressive area. Some songs are in the poppy Kayak vein, like 6 and 10 that remind me of Periscope Life. A song like 12 reminds me a of late era Alan Parsons Project.

Conclusion

Not very interesting for prog fans. Lots of short songs the most interesting of which are comparable to the song directed Kayak (6 and 10 for instance), while the rest is not interesting at all.
