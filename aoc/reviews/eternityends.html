Summary

Time Machine is an Italian band that already has two full length albums to its name. All of them are about some historic story and in this case it seems to be about Jesus.

The music

In twelve tracks and 55 minutes we hear Time Machine's ideas about the story of Jesus. The album opens with End Of Darkness which is a short acoustic track. The following track Falling Star is also short, but is thoroughly in the progmetal vein and this I think should be regarded as the intro to I, The Subversive Nazarene. There quite a lot of pace in this song, with the lyrics passing by quickly. The vocals are slightly accented, but Fortarezza is not a screamer. Quite a powerful track with rolling drums and good melodies, but the "chorus" is repeated a little too often. Hidden Pain opens with keyboards and later on also guitar. With a nice quiet bridge to the vocals, which are rather soft and slow. The song is a bit plaintive, and resides somewhere in the Queensryche corner. Past halfway, the band plays its first trump when the keyboardplayer plays a short sax solo. Eternity Ends is rather poppy up-tempo track with lush keyboards. The accent of the singer is much more notable here. The song features a Latin intermezzo with dito percussion and Spanish guitar, alternated with furious guitar. I Believe Again is a ballad with some cool sax. Another version sung by Andre Matos exists on the Secret Oceans Part 2 single. The quality of a song like this falls or stands with the quality of the singer, since the other instruments do not feature overly much. Okay, we have the mandatory guitar solo here, but still. IMO, the vocal rendition of Fortarezza does not make it shine. No time is wasted as we roll right in to the thoroughly rocking Desert Of Souls, which enjoys good vocal melodies. Behind The Cross is with just under ten minutes the longest track on the album. It opens very melodic, but then it turns out there's place for quite a lot of playful percussion and Spanish guitar. A strange effect, since the singing continues nonetheless. Later on the song becomes more typical progmetal. The chorus is nice with a good melody and a late nite sax closes the track moodily down. Again no time is wasted as we move into Sphynx. In some way the German hardrock band Scorpions comes to mind at times, although admittedly Time Machine is much more progressive. Maybe the vocals. A driving track this with a busy drummer. When The night Surrounds Me is a like one might expect from such a title, a soothing track. Like often on this disc, the melodies are nice, but not particularly striking or easy to remember. The music is nice to listen to, but does not strike a chord. The last real track is the mysteriously opening Pilatus. Real menace in the opening of this track. One of the nicer tracks with again plenty of melody and always an eye out for composition.

Conclusion

Compared to Time Scanning: Galileo it seems that the band has now the tendency to put their energy more into composition, more than atmosphere and the fiddling around that they did on their older albums and this album is clear advancement with respect to their previous studio album. Still, many of the songs on this disc are compositions such that one gets what "one might expect" and thoughts like "I've heard this before, but can't remember where" pop into my head. This is not to say that the music is bad or something, but that it lacks some kind of vision to really captivate. On the whole the sound of the band is more progressive hardrock than progressive metal, which might also be an influence of friendband Angra and a result of the personnel changes during the Galileo. The latin percussion and the soothing sax are nice additions to the overal sound and the band ranges from soothing to powerful, but like I said, it is not very original. But what is?
