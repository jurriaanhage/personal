Summary

The first disc of the Moonchild label undergoing scrutiny by yours truly. More are certain to follow. Over 72 minutes long, this album is a way to sample 11 of the more obscure prog bands in the world. In fact, it is MY first encounter with any of them. Not all these bands are actually on the Moonchild label, but who cares. The label was initiated by Brian Hirsch of Indeed and Andrew Robinson of Magus.

The music

The album opens with the Danish Fig Leaf. A strong composition with leanings to the prog rock of the seventies. Especially powerful is the organ in this song, but the vocals are powerful as well. Although the song is called The Humble Poet, they might as well be talking about themselves. They do not care for chains of gold, all they want to do is to make you think (and enjoy their music). According to Vidales of Cast the best prog band of today is Crucible. Lots of Mellotron and organ in this track that is also very much in the seventies vein. The singing is not an imitation of Gabriel, but the song does have a strong Genesis feel to it. Deluc's Identity contrasts strongly with these tracks. First of all it is instrumental, it is much more modern, but although fusion seems to come around, it is thoroughly melodic and his a few nice themes on which it is built. Acoustic Alchemy comes to mind with Pruett & Davis Group's Ascension. If you like those or like fingerquick acoustics this might be something for you. It isn't really anything for me, although they do go about it in a subtle enough way. Smokin' Granny makes music that is a very free of form of jazz rock but with a large rock component. Rather like a band such as Conglomerate. The longest track on this album is New Sun's Cyrus Gore. The track opens with soundscapish sounds, the first part of the track being rather ethereal and spacey sounding. The song stays quite spacey and the singing is also done in a very loose way bringing across a sense of chaos, but also a weird sense of urgency. Considering the length of the track and the style of music a worthy attempt. Indeed = Brian Hirsch and on his Poindexter's Army Of Hissing Android Alien Spiders. The title reflects the title very well. The music is very synth based with rather mechanic drums. The opening of Jeremy's Earthquake is very (recent) Crimsonesque. Then we get a rather driving part and an ethereal guitar sets in. At the end we get a very quiet part as the earth slightly rumbles on. Very nicely done. Rome is by Blue Shift. A tottering track with vocals that lie between Jon Anderson and Pete Nicholls. Although it is obviously based on a song by Yes, there are also some influences of IQ in there somewhere. One of the more accomplished tracks notwithstanding the obvious influences. Magus is the penultimate track opening very quietly and soothingly and it turns out to stay this way, although later an ethereal electric guitar is added to the spare sound spectrum. The last track on the album is by Truth In Advertisement and is a sensuous, ambient like track with female vocals. Very dreamy.

Conclusion

Most samplers in progressive are worth having since the music sampled is very hard to listen to in any other way and usually they are quite inexpensive to obtain. This also holds for this sampler (it is cheap I hope) and a number of interesting bands have been introduced to me now: Fig Leaf, Crucible and Blue Shift, but to be honest, there's nothing really bad on this disc, some are just better than others.
