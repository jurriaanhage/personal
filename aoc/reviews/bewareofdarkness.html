Spock's Beard - Beware Of Darkness

Artist: Spock's Beard
Title: Beware Of Darkness
Label: Giant Electric Pea GEPCD 1018
Length(s): 58 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1997
Month of review: 05/1997

Line up

Alan Morse - electric guitar, cello, vocals

Ryo Okumoto - hammond organ and mellotron

Summary

Already a big name in progressive rock this is the first album by them that I heard. I thought Meros was a typo at first, but it turned out that the thank you part contained more Meros people so I figured it wasn't.

The music

Beware of Darkness is a cover of George Harrisons track from the triple box All Things Must Pass. The song has been subjected to many a change as for instance in the very Yes like opening. The bass work is also pronounced like with Yes and the lyrics have been changed and shuffled around a bit. The Hammond organ of Okumoto is also very much present and the song has got a heavy progressive and more powerful sound also through the powerful raunchy vocals.

The opening of Thoughts contrasts happy and playful melodies with dark brooding sounds and we hear a Gentle Giant in the harmonies. The verses are sung by diverse voices and the music is played on mellotron, while the choruses are kind of heavy. Some parts even manage to remind me of the more "progressive" tracks of the Beatles or beter yet the more progressive variant ELO. Energy, diversity and melody have melt into one.

A pianic introduction to The Doorway gives rise to a rocking verse with a rather pleasant sounding harmony chorus reminding me of CSN. For the first part the song is mostly acoustic with a small ELP-like keyboard solo. At the end the song is flooded with organ and the energy level increases and becomes more bombastic and after the music has disappeared it comes back to end in crescendo.

The next one is an acoustic track. A soft reminder that there's music besides progressive rock and also serves as a breather.

Walking on the Wind starts aggressively with organ, while the verse is mostly vocals with bass. The chorus on the other hand is rather poppy. The middle section features a nice guitar solo while the end is again climactic (a shame they couldn't close down with that climax but chose to add some more).

The next one up starts acoustic again and although I can't really pinpoint it the band sounds very American besides sounding like a lot of English progressive bands and this might be due to the acoustic parts and the rougher vocals that you find more often in rock bands then in progressive bands. A change for the better.

The closing epic Time Has Come starts powerfully and continues with a soft sung verse reminding me of ELO while the next verse sounds distorted. The heavy bass and raunchy guitar contrast nicely with the pleasant vocals. After 8 minutes or so we get into an acoustic part (Kansas?) the song becomes rather poppy, but still very much progressive.

The lyrics also help to add to the music being rather indirect and vague, but they stay away from love and the more standard subjects of progressive rock.

Conclusion

Combining the harmonies of Gentle Giant, the synths of ELP, the pop melodies and songwriting of ELO and the overall sound of Yes (and including along the way some acoustic music), into one organic whole these guys have delivered one of the best album thus far this year. Seventies influences with nineties skills, sensible melodies, powerful playing and singing and plenty of breaks and mood changes make this album worth the attention of anyone into progressive rock. Only one question remaining: when will they be playing live here?
