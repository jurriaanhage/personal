Odyssice - Moon Drive

Artist: Odyssice
Title: Moon Drive
Label: Legend Records LR-801
Length(s): 25 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1996
Month of review: 06/1997

Line up

Bastiaan Peeters - guitar
Bart Kuhne - drums
Jeroen van der Wiel - keyboards
Pascal van de Pol - bass

Tracks

1) Frustrations 8.14
2) And So Am I 5.09
3) Different Questions 5.35
4) The Final Decisions 6.21

Summary

Known to few by their early demo Track One that I do in fact have and also their presence on the Exposure 88 album, the band Odyssice has reentered the stage. Since the last time we heard of them van der Wiel and van de Pol replaced Andre Hubbeling and Erik Aafjes, making Peeters the only original member of the band.

The band might have let someone look at their English bio though; it's full of errors (and don't start pointing fingers at me).

The music

A party, the car door of a Porsche, footsteps on the shingle, the Porsche drives away. That is opening of the instrumental mini album by Odyssice. While the car drives away, the drive has started, a wailing, sad guitar enters the stage, keyboards figure lowly in the back. Then the music changes as the drums fall in and the organ takes the leading role. The later part of the track is more up-beat with a large role for keyboards surprisingly (Odyssice used to be guitar dominated). At the end also, the song turns into something of a jamsession with various types of keyboard and guitar on the fore.

The introspective and sad sounding And So Am I has quite a lot of Camel echoes has a slow build up and features good guitar work. The song, as indeed is often the case with Camel, is rather laid back, but still brings over some of the emotion, especially, because some of the melodies are sad, while other somehow give hope.

Different Questions opens with a spacious guitar solo, is rather slow and again reminds me a lot Camel. Only after over 3 minutes does van der Wiel get his chance to play his keyboard solo, but in my opinion it might have sounded a little fuller.

The last track already, the car still drives on, is The Final Decisions, that starts out as a ballad with piano. This track is a very symphonic one with very nice melodies indeed. The end of the track reminds me very much of Camel to the point that I want to start singing "In reply to your request, I hereby protest". Ah well.

The car reenters its parking space and we leave the car.

Conclusion

A melodic instrumental CD based for the most part on Bastiaans guitar work, but with a surprisingly large amount of keyboard as well. The playing is alright as is the sound of the album. The compositions themselves are not always to the point, but for the most part these are sensible melodies and I can forgive them their occassional outburst easily. The only problem left might be the price as compared to the amount of music one gets in return. Important reference is Camel in their Nude period. Encore, but then a full length CD, please.
