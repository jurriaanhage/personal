Summary

Hmm, first review for Musea, so better make this a good one..okay, serious now. I saw these guys in Almelo and they were actually very good. Very varied and expressive and with interesting instruments and all. Before the concert started people already warned me that this would be good and so indeed it was. Naturally I wanted to review heir CD and they pointed me to Musea a source and to my surprise Musea did send me one (and one other). Well, then here it is.

The music

What struck me directly were the good vocals. They tend to be rather high, but still with some muscle. The vocals have bite and are expressive throughout. Besides this important instrument, a lot of other instruments are involved on this album, which as whole is both folky and borders at times on the hard rock. At once we have the jig of The Way of Things, which tends to be a little jolly and also the very heavy Solitary Man, which one way or another reminds me of Gentle Giant. Also a band with some folky tunes in their music. The sixth track on the other hand is more medieval and acoustic mostly, like much on this album. Still you will not find it boring, because what is introduced here is a form of poweracoustics and the playing can be very heavy. Some more quieter tracks are Dependence Day again with a Gentle Giant ring to it(mostly because of the vocals) and also Seasong for the Moominpappa, which sounds for the most part rather like Entangle,d although at the end its more like a sailor's song (Queen's Seven Seas of Rhye).

More references can be found in Typhoons Decide which reminds me of Led Zeppelins Kashmir in a good way: heavy rock, but not too plain. Also, I'm reminded of the more majestic and also acoustic parts of Yes in their earlier (Fragile) days. The tenth track also reminds me just a little of Yes, with a lot of bombast but also with some veyr nice piano playing at the beginning.

The closer has some Greek undertones and is quiet throughout.

Conclusion

A lot of references, but I only use them to give a few pointers where music itself is missing. As a whole an extraordinary album from a dito band. Having a style very much their own, with good melodies, variation across the compositions and interesting arrangements and vocalizations make this a worthwhile acquisition for just about anybody. And if you have the chance: go see them live.

Can I have more CD's now?
