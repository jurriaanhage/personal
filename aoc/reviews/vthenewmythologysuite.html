Summary

After my review of their second album The Damnation Game I never got anything of them again. I guess I was too negative. It seems that in the meantime they released a magnum opus The Divine Wings Of Tragedy. This is their fifth album (yes, the title is V like of the just release Spock's Beard album, but this album has a subtitle that makes all the difference) and let's see how far they've progressed.

The music

Well the beginning is good, a very melodic bombastic opening with classical choirs and also some powerful ingredients. In Evolution the rhythm guitars and double bassdrums set in full force. The vocals of Allen remind me of Malmsteens earlier records, but the music is just that bit heavier, quicker but also more varied. The chorus for instance, is just that bit quicker than the verses. The fantasy based lyrics are written for quite good melody lines and Allen is a good singer, flexible enough to give the nuances. Although the music is rather complex, the vocal lines are quite accessible. Main reference is to Rhapsody and like I said Malmsteen. Menacingly tingling keyboards open Fallen. The rhythm guitar are come out through the melodious music bit by bit as this driven song unfolds slowly but surely. The keyboards, at this point somewhere in the back, behind the rhythm guitars, remind me of the tense parts in the music of Aragon. The chorus has a memorable melody line, very good. Some harmony vocals in this one as well. In Malmsteen style there are some quick guitar scales being played on this one. Now this is not really my kind of guitar playing preferring a bit of melody. It does seem to me that there are some Arabic influences in this solo, melody wise. The album has a number of shortish interludes, Transcendence being the first of these. This part is a dark atmospheric piece, very much in the style of contemporary film music with all kinds of orchestral ingredients. Communion And The Oracle is compared to the other major tracks rather relaxed. Mostly acoustic guitar with some more driving passages as well, but for the most part the ingredients are quite friendly and at times accessibly melodic because of the vocal lines and piano. Vocally, quite a lot is happening here with lots of harmony singing. The Bird Serpent War/Cataclysm is a driven guitar riddled piece, up-tempo with hasty vocals. On The Breath Of Poseidon is another orchestrated piece in the style of contemporary film music (in the style of Danny Elfman and the like). Maybe not a bad idea to give these guys some work in this direction. Of course I wonder if people would like the guitars that are present in this part. I do like them here. Egypt is a bouncy piece with orchestral linings and the sound somewhat Middle-Easternlike. The vocal melodies on this one are not always great, but the vocal melody in the bridge in the style of Kansas is very good. The chorus is a bit vaguer vocal wise. The instrumental intermezzo is rather relaxed, but by adding guitars gains more momentum and power. The Death Of Balance/Lacrymosa brings us another great orchestral piece and then powerfully Absence Of Light continues with some Queen like vocal harmonies. A Fool's Paradise continues along the same heavy lines with singalong choruses (think Malmsteen again) and church organ to give the song a bombastic lining. Uh-oh now we move into baroque music, a type of music I'm not fond of at all. Ah well, let them. After a short introductory segue we move into the twelveminute subtitle track. Heavy guitars dominate this track for a large part, somewhat meandering guitars at times, but often feeling their way towards something more melodic as well. The vocals are of the masculin type as mostly on this album, and the piano adds a nice classical touch to it all. In this track the metal parts are intertwined more thoroughly with the classical, orchestrated parts and I guess we can also hear some Ayreon influences here. Of course in a tracks such as this there is plenty space for signature changes and the band makes full use of this. Allen also shows himself in various disguises, from the relatively "normal" vocals to the more aggressive vocals of the verses. The ending is very melodic, quite an abrupt change from the previous parts to this very orchestral ending.

Conclusion

Quite heavy, but especially in the vocal lines very accessible progmetal. The guitar solo's are usually of the scale running type and therefore not so melodic as I would like. However, the band has made sure that there are a lot of bombastic, melodic interludes, that are sometimes of the singalong kind. Compared to The Damnation Game a great step forward, but I am not sure this is due to this record, or whether this was already apparent on the two albums in between. Main references are Rhapsody (but heavy instead of speedy and more atmospheric/tension driven) and Malmsteen (because of the solo guitar work), but also flim music in the seques and various other places. A good progmetal album for the classically inclined, but also the people interested in more accessible material are free to taste this one.
