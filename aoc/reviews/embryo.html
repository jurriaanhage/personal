Kraken in the Maelstroem - Embryogenesis (Bioregular Heat Emissions)

Artist: Kraken in the Maelstroem
Title: Embryogenesis (Bioregular Heat Emissions)
Label: Mellow Records 165
Length(s): 51 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1993
Month of review: 02/1996

Line up

Marco Signore - synths, backing vox
Valerio Riccelli - bass, lead & backing vox, electric guitar on 1
Giacomo Ciavatta - guitars
Davide Schioppa - soprano sax, percussion
Dario Longhi - drums
There's also a choir consisting of Anna Fiore and ... that's it.

Tracks

1) Menuet 2.57
2) Sunwise 8.35
3) 79 A.D. 9.19

All by Ciavatta & Signore except: 1 by Bach, intro and finale of 3 taken from Liado Is Sick by Khacaturian.

Summary

Don't know anything about this band, but I thought they might be interesting. That's why I review them here.

The music

Well, the album starts out sober enough with Menuet by Bach. Just acoustic guitar and something sounding like cello are in evidence. At first may sound a bit lame, but it turns out to be a good appetizer for what comes next. Nice touch with the twin guitars.

The second track and actually the rest of the album is an entirely different cup of tea. Frantic guitars, offensive rhythms, bubbling basswork, shards of sax a la VDGG and a keyboard sound a la ELP at their most inaccessible are thrown at the listener, taken him unawares.

There's also some singing abound. The vocals are not very well tended, but I don't think anyone will mind who likes this kind of music. Anyway, not to put them down, but to me they seem like a rowdy bunch making music not too pleasant for untrained ears and in my case it take quite a few listens to appreciate this lot.

I've already introduced you to how the music should sound. The sound is rock, but with a jazzy feel added at times by means of the sax. Some influences of VDGG thus become apparent, but VDGG is more organized than this. After a while the melodies seem to seep into your mind, becoming aware of something going between also the various noises.

Naturally, there are moments in which I can see no way out of the meandering morass trying to catch on to something meaningful, but usually they pull it off quite alright, with their no-nonsense approach not caring for whether what they do is beautiful (although in the end they turn to somewhat mroe delicate stuff), as long as it works and the most part of their work they try to convey a march of monsters and vampires onto the listener and well, this is just what you get for it.

Conclusion

So regardless of the amateuristic vocals and loss of focus once in a while, they are something to experience. But mind you: only for the experienced listener with an ear for the "ugly". (Does that mean I'm one now too?)
