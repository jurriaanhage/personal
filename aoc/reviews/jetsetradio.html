Summary

The third album of this original duo, excluding the spin-off Pineapple Thief.

The music

The longest track, 11 plus minutes, opens the album. The music is easy going and a bit bouncy with sparse percussion, slight psychedelic keys and mainly acoustic guitar. The vocoded vocals of Hugo Drax feature on this first track and although he his hard to understand, it fits in well with the music. In the middle of the track the music becomes rather harsh and chaotic, but soon after we are left with percussively played piano. The melody here is very subtle. Then the music becomes more catchy with lots of pa-pa-pa-ing. The guitar continues to be rather noisy and psychedelic. I'm strongly reminded of King Black Acid here, a band who is also succesfull in combining prog, psych and alternative rock. Admittedly, KBA still does it a bit better than Vulgar Unicorn does.

It Didn't Used To Be This Way consists in fact of two parts being alternated. One rather up-tempo with acoustic guitar and the occasional synth solo and one more relaxed part. The music is still in the line of the previous track. Not really prog, not really alt, not really psyche, but somewhere in between. Most likable reference continues to be King Black Acid, but a band such as the more recent Porcupine Tree is also not that far away.

Weird keyboard effects, easy going drums and soft spacey/vocoded vocals dominate the opening of the short Powder, Groom, Hardpack. Notwithstanding the unnaturalness of the vocals, they do fit in well with the music. The rhythms are rather modern, this immediately gives the band a less proggy sound.

For a guy like me who types out all the song titles, the titles such as this band uses are no fun at all. This one should be called I Saw The Messenger Of The New God There. This song has a seventies feel over it, but not the prog seventies feel, I am afraid. The song has a strongly funky feel to it and could by some considered to be a weird undanceable kind of disco. The vocal part reminds me of The Blue Nile (remember Tinsel Town In The Rain?) and later Gino Vannelli (in his older days). A drum computer is used to good effect in places. A rare occurence.

Secret Spot opens spacily with bleeping and burping keyboards and radio sounds. The song gets a little bit of pace later on, but continues to be an excursion into noisy space rock with a vibrating sound. I'm Beginning To Think I Can Cure A Rainy Day is 'nother one of those long titles. Another noisy track, with again a groovy seventies feel lying over it. The music is psychedelic and rather "full". Lots of things happening. The sounds on the keyboards give the music a rather light and sunny feel.

Quick fingered melodic acoustic guitar we find on Diabolical Scheme. The influences are various: Portishead in the slow parts, Jon Hassell in the trumpet parts. For the rest: little or no change from the previous track. The track that was sampled for the 4th Cyclops Sampler, More Money Than I Know What To Do With, is one the more melodic ones (especially the keyboards).

Tierce De Pacardie introduces an easy going pace of acoustic guitar and vocals, but it is alternated by some vicious sounding keyboards. The final part is again of the type we have come to expect.

The closer Scherzo In C Minor is a minimal classical piece. Very nice. Later we get piano and drums, the classical influences stay.

Conclusion

Well what to say about this. I guess these guys make a mix of modern music that is based on older music (Beck, Portishead, Jamiroquai) and psychedelic rock (Porcupine Tree, but mainly King Black Acid) obtaining a noisy and experimental mix of pop and rock that is quite good to listen to. On their older albums, there were usually some things I could not particularly dig, but this time it seems the ingredients are balanced. I do have to admit liking a comparable band such as King Black Acid more. The band does have the tendency to float around a bit at times, but that is not disturbing. It does help if you set aside your expectations of a prog band on Cyclops. In that sense the band is comparable to Parallel Or 90 Degrees although they take their influences at least partly from elsewhere.
