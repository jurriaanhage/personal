Summary

In the field of electronic music (among others), Paul Speer's name is not unknown. I myself know him for his collaborations with David Lanz. Here he is teamed up with Queensryche drummer Scott Rockenfield for the menacingly titled Hells Canyon. An interesting combination and their second album so far. The album is out in two versions, an American one and a European one. The European version has one extra track and nicely painted artwork by Matthias Noren.

The music

After a stormy introduction with loud and interesting sound effects we come to a strangely repetitive pattern. The music here is quite laid-back instrumental prog with repetitions. However, many things are going on: a bubbling bass, spaceous percussion, loud ahhs on the keyboards, quick fingered guitar work and busy drumming. The sound of the record is great and energetic. The music is in the style of Liquid Tension Experiment or better yet Bozzio Levin Stevens, but more composed and also more electronic.

Chant Of The Fathers is in the same style, but with a darker guitar sound, a better melody and something I would indeed typify as the Chant Of The Fathers, low sounding voices in a choir. A bit tribal. Again, the song is short, compact and has quite a lot of repetition. The music is continually varied though.

Snake Dance is quite a percussive piece and at first I'm reminded of older Patrick O'Hearn material. But then the music becomes more up-beat, although on the whole it is the most relaxed, meditative piece so far, based on a catchy tune.

After Crossing To Freedom we come to the dark spooky Coyote. Again the empty spaces of the U.S. are evoked in this driven dark track which has a powerful theme propping up once in a while.

Red Torrent opens with percussion and fast bass playing. A catchy track is what follows in which I hear some echoes from the past (unfortunately I can not tell you whom's past). The track also has plenty of breaks so that the music obtains a more "jammed" character. River Of No Return has spaceous guitars, a bass bubbling under and like most of the tracks here a strongly repetitive character.

China's Last Stand has a subtle weirdness in its stereo effect, a bit as if the recorder was wobbling all the time. A relaxed soothing piece with a lonesomely sounding guitar. Again, good melodic material, but I do get the impression that the duo could have done more with it, it all tends to linger around a bit. The oohaahs from the keyboards give the music a slight Enigma flavour.

Buffalo Eddy is a bluesy flavoured style, but still in the fashion of the rest of the album. Carved In Stone is somewhat aggressive and strongly percussive, with them nice rolling drums. It does sound a bit sharper than the other tracks, especially in the beginning.

The European bonus track is called Epilogue. A low, audible bass and a open jazzrockish slight spacey sound conclude the album.

Conclusion

Comparable to Gordian Knot and Bozzio Levin Stevens this is certainly a worthwhile album. The music is not improvised and hence has more of Bozzio Levin Stevens' Situation Dangerous. The music of Rockenfield/Speer is less flashy and more laid back and repetitive. Because of that I tend to rate them below BLS, but then again, that is and was a really great album. Although the production is very good and clear on this album, I would have hoped for some places where the music REALLY rips. I get the feeling at times to listen to rock songs produced by an electronic musician. A very pleasant instrumental album, especially to listen to, with melodies that do stick, but because of the lack of a real bite is better suited for late in the evenings. In fact, for that it is wonderfully suited. What stands out most I think is that notwithstanding the occasional outburst and the number of styles incorporated on the album, the album is fully coherent.
