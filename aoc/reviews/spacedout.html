Summary

On the same label as Mystery this is a jazzrock outfit coming, as does Mystery, from Quebec.

The music

Green Teeth opens with jazz rock on the basis of rather cheesy keyboards and a very active electric guitar. The melodies are of the meandering kind and the music is groovy. The meandering quality of the music stays on Toxix. The music has a Latin tinge here, because of the percussion. The music may be compared to many a French band risen from the ashes of Magma (Zao, and so on) with a strong jazz rock vein and much less Zeuhl. Plenty of breaks here in the middle with some nicely disjointed piano playing right there. Then the drummer and guitarist play in tandem, with the one reacting to and with the other. Very nice, but the continuation is less than fluent. A Freak Az is a rather freaky track, getting rather chaotic toward the end, and spiced with eerie sounding keyboards. The music is rather dark here and reminds me a of combo's like Presence and Univers Zero. Groove returns in Magnetyzme, which also features some very nice melodic lines on guitar. Then the groove returns with some prominent bass playing, to subside again into an extended guitar solo. The music might be compared to such bands as Scott McGill's Hand Farm, but I do feel that Spaced Out is both more varied sharper and more adventurous. Fafard has the tendency by the rather sharp sound of his guitar to be more "present". The dissonants also help to keep the listener awake. In Delirium Tremens we experience some nice atmospherics: sparse drumming and soft keyboards accompany a bass solo. The Fifth Dimension again continues on the rather straightforward, yet meandering, jazz rock basis. Weirdly enough, there is a part that reminds me a lot of Twelfth Night's Creepshow (must be accidental). Lots of notes fly by, notwithstanding the rather low pace. Lots of rhythmic variation as well. Soundtrack music opens Pensestuax and then a slow guitar sets in. The music is quite fluent. However, this is not for long, because then the music moves into a rather easy-going pace. A thoughful bass solo interrupts the track in which the filmic aspects of the opening return accompanied by some well-dosed drumming. Really percussive the music becomes in Futurosphere, which is almost a subtle kind of drum solo. The music is not just drums/percussion thuogh, because the moody filmic atmospherics of the previous tracks are continued. Furax also has a strong rhythmic component, but the guitar has returned. A very disjointed track, this, showing again that the music of Spaced Out is not run-of-the-mill, not even within such a constrained line of music such as jazz rock. For that they introduce too many estranging aspects. The albums ends with the bubbly Glassosphere. Very nice fast playing on this song, which still hae a subdued edge to it, because the sound is not so sharp. And there's even some melody in there too!

Conclusion

This is an album for lovers of jazz rock. There is a lot of variation, but I feel there is little chance people other than that can appreciate this music. Ah well, maybe the ones that are more experimentally inclined can take a peek. The music can be groovy, is well played, has some nice melodies, but for the most part, the music is of the meandering kind with the keyboards providing either a somewhat cheesy accompaniment or a nice atmospheric backdrop. Towards the end of the album I felt a departure from straightforward jazzrock to more interesting and varied approaches to the style including what amount to be a drum solo and the nice bubbly closer leaves a good impression.
