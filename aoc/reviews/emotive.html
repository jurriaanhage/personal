Summary

Knowing The Great Deceiver by King Crimson I got an idea about the music on this album. Now I could be wrong, but the fact that the album cites a statement by Fripp tells us something. The only person in this band I'm familiar with is Jay Tausig, who recorded some tracks for the Mellow Records tributes and also features on Louie Mastro's Mermaid In The Mist.

The music

Opening dreamily with Waking In Dreams, the song slowly gets underway. The guitar playing has something of playing Spanish guitar on an electric one. The doubled vocals are quite nice, a bit "far away". The vocal melodies are good, but production wise the music doesn't sound as well as it could. The instrumental parts are rather fiddly, but certainly not overly so. All in all I get an impression of haziness and vagueness, but the chorus is to the point. Schtzorhythmia opens with piano and then moves into a pumping passage with busy percussion and organ/synths. The music here goes back to the seventies and has a good drive with some powerful chords. This instrumental is well- crafted and notwithstanding the looseness of the composition, the track does have focus. With flute, talking guitar and some quick piano this is very nice piece especially when the piano takes over. The music is quite hard to pin down, some King Crimson floating around, but also some UK I would say and I feel some Spanish/Italian undercurrents and a general likeness to Echolyn. However this band sounds a bit less modern. Love And Death opens with a lot of percussion and then a sensitive piano takes over. The piano part has something of a funeral march (reminds me a specific one, but I can't recall the title). Then however we move into a bouncy part with its lyrics on Death, Life and Love. A bit of a playful piece with varied drumming and references to Van Der Graaf Generator in the vocal part. A jazzy interlude is featured at the end. The first of four improvisations is next up: flute, double bass drums and guitar work with shards of piano in a rather disjointed one-minute package. For Me opens with keyboards in the ELP style, quite fast paced, with also a nod to Eddie Jobson. The fast paced piano part that follows leads into a slow vocal part, that reminds me of seventies pop, a bit on the mellow side, but good nonetheless. One of the best tracks so far. Another track, another improvisation, this time slow and with didgeridoo and subtle guitar and percussion. Later the guitar becomes more pronounced and a kind of gurgling Hammond sound is used. The opening of The One Who Grieve is chaotic to say the least. In a way I'm reminded of Genesis' I Know What I Like. Chaotic vocals, meandering guitar, varied percussion and a zooming bass. The percussion/keyboards intermezzo is more in the style of the Italians of the seventies. The guitar melody sounds a bit familiar. A Gathering Of Days opens with acoustics. This track is a bit like a psychedelic ballad. A great vocal melody, the music a bit on the hazy, ethereal side with the necessary mellotron. In a way the song also reminds me of earlier singersongwriter music in the style of Hammill, but without the harshness of the vocals. Still, I feel that Tausig may have been influenced by the man. The improvisation When Grandfather Gets Flatulent, We Kick The Dog, is the next one up. Flute, varied percussion, low bass work and a meandering guitar make up this track. In the style of the first album of King Crimson we now get "In the wink of an eye" excerpts (in three parts). The first of these has crashing guitars and a move into the direction of recent King Crimson, compensated for by the vintage keyboards. Locked is the vocal second part. This is a rather accessible piece, with rather chaotic vocals in the chorus. The third part is a more up-beat playful part. Reality Is Mine alternates twinkling piano and keys with a funky-rhythmic passage. Like In The Wink Of An Eye, I'm not altogether satisfied with this song. The guitar part is alright as is the twinkling part, but the vocal part leaves osmething to be desired and the song is also a bit too jazzy and improvised. It seems that all the instruments get their share of soloing here giving the song a somewhat improvised character. After the final improvisation, We Came For The Jam, We Stayed For The Explanation the album ends with Mozarts First Movement Symphony #25 in G Minor. The improvisation is rather repetitive in the piano part. I'm not a Mozart fan at all, so this wasted on me. Okay, it's not that bad, and varies between King Crimson goes tango and organic Ekseption with some pretty busy percussion thrown in for good measure.

They should pay more attention to the artwork. People often feel the artwork to be a reflection of the music, and I'm afraid they will not be attracted to this front cover.

Conclusion

Vintage progressive from the States. The production is not top quality, but especially for those of you interest in pure progressive, this is a inventive, original album, with good melodies, great playing and songmanship. Toward the end of the album, I noticed a slight drop in my appreciation of the album.
