Summary

See any familiar names? You should, because 1, 2, 3, 5, 11 and 13 are tracks that have been reviewed on this web site in the context of an album. The third compilation of colleague Markus Weis of DURP (I'm afraid I'll never get round to that MP3 compilation, Markus) and all in a well done package like the previous compilation disc.

The music

The album opens strongly with an excerpt of the song of the same name on the Yoke Shire album Masque Of Shadows. This is yoke Shire at their most epic and adventurous. The band owes a lot generally to Jethro Tull, but this is something different, atmospheric, balladic and very well-crafted. Most of their music is more rocky though. Wasting Away by Agent Cooper is really a great catchy track. In fact, this band has quite a knack for writing interesting melodic composition, listen to that guitar opening. The singer is very pop, but I like really his voice. A concise track, not really prog as most people see it, but the band has some tricks up their sleeve on the full album. Light music, with a climactic passage toward the end in which the organ gives the music is really groovy feel. Nangyala is the only Dutch band on this record. Their short demo Born Gifted/Paragon was a leap forward from the preceding mini album. This track is an atmopheric Floydish edit of the original 12+ version on the cd. Good guitarwork, good vocal and otherwise melodies and good atmospheres. The singer has a pleasant voice with a raw edge and the song works up to a nice full crescendo. Zenobia is the first track I did not know yet. The vocals are rather accented and in the music refers a lot to early eighties Eloy. The singing is very hasty, and the music is rather light on the whole. Not as good as any of the previous ones, melodically okay, but lacks a spark. Scythe is again a band I'm familiar with. This is the short pacey opener of the album with a seventies feel. The sound quality is not as good as one might hope, but the music is sharp and notwithstanding the small length there's a lot of variation. After this we come to the AOR/metal of Tunnelvision and their Reach The Moon. Vocally the music is quite accessible (which gives me an AOR feel), but the guitars are heavy and the keyboards play a strong role on this track. Harmonies, rhythm guitar, driven drumming and good playing and singing throughout, this an anthemic track with the singalong chorus the high point. TellTaleHard plays Tip The Scale for us, an accessible heavy prog piece. This is not really metal, for that I find the music too soft. However their music might be harder generally, because they are described as a progmetal band. The song is quite good with two different melodic parts alternating. Main influence that I hear is Rush with a hardrock tinge. Body Full Of Stars is something different altogether. The music opens with African percussion and a really weird atmopshere. The singing reminds me of a band such as Hawkwind with the dialect accent shining through. Very atmospheric music, not really rock with weird interjections by the vocalists and a rowdy guitar solo at the end. Closest to psychedelic rock, but because of the tribality quite unique as well. Platos HalO's In The Pink is one of the surprises on this album. A unique guitar sound on this waltzy piece with the very distinctive vocals of Marija Rawlins. At times she does remind me of Kate Bush, but of Kim Wilde as well. A sensuous track. Not progressive maybe according to some, but very enticing. Transcendence's Step By Step is catchy progmetal featuring violin, vocals in the style of Chandelier and rather extensive backing vocals. The music is rather straightforward, except for the nice addition of violin. Sfumato I already knew with their excerpt of Take 2. Their instrumental with the dominant bass sound, plenty of (melodic and temporal) variation still lacks a bit in productional quality, especially when compared to most of the other selections on this cd. However, the music has originality and drive and I guess that's what counts. Prototype is a new band to me. Progmetal of the very heavy kind, with a strong rhythm guitar presence, but the music is quite poppy nonetheless. The production leaves a bit to be desired, but the composition is a good one. Paranoise concludes the album with their worldprog music. Wonderfully melodic including many influences from around the equator. After a wailing kind of opening the music does get more pace along the way, slowly but definitely so do not be alarmed.

The first of the bonustracks -- they are called such, because these bands need not be sampled being quite well known already -- is Mystery with their Beneath The Veil Of Winter's Face from an unreleased demo. A lightfooted opening with acoustic guitar and a kind of folky outview. The music is not really typical for the Styxian music of Mystery. A melodic ballad, but with very tense overtones. This I guess chose the maturity of theis band: the possibility to play with these moods in an effective way. A very good track. According to the booklet the band has a third cd out, which I didn't even know. Salem Hill is a preview of the upcoming album Not Everybody's Gold to be released on Cyclops. They also impressed me with their Robbery Of Murder. This rather long track is ratehr melodramatic piece with good melodies.

Conclusion

After the somewhat disappointing second DURP compilation, the website comes back with a vengeance. Very interesting new bands on this one and for the ones that I already: good choices throughout. For anyone into progressive a worthwhile disc to try and sample the bubbling under bands of progressive rock and related styles of music, but never overly difficult. Not forgetting the bonus tracks by two good, already more well known bands: Mystery and Salem Hill. By far the best DURP compilation so far.
