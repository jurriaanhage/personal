Summary

This is this year's sampler of the Finnish Wolfgang Records.

The music

Opener Alamaailman Viserat comes up with something that could be described as progressive ethnic humpa: the Indian kind of flute that gets snakes out of baskets, cello, some brass and a stomping rhythm as you would find with any American powertrio. Nice one.

Track 2 is a bit spacy and bubbly, sort of in the Ozric Tentacles vein, although guitar and bass are a bit more dominant here.

Track 3 starts with flute and a ha-ing lady, to lead into what could best be described as ECM jazz, the kind you'd expect of a Jan Garbarek (sax). I like this kind of stuff.

Track 4 displays a jazz quartet, piano, bass, drums and later on sax. Piano and later sax going off a bit, but sort of a nice atmosphere.

Track 5 has a guitar over keys, with the kind of bobby bass you'd expect with dub music. And just when you think you've heard it all, a Robbie Robertson wannabe starts his vocal bit, later joined by sweeping sounds that could almost be from a jet fighter. Sort of an odd hodgepodge that may not even sound so bad, but that still doesn't do it for me.

Track 6 is an instrumental piece, a bit of Camel and a bit of of gypsy leads to a rather average result.

Track 7 sounds like pretty traditional lap like music. First solo female, later with choir, always accompanied by a violin played without fluent strikes, making for a nice effect. Not very progressive, but nice in itself.

Track 8 features sax with running water and piano in the back. Makes for a nice effect. Sounds pretty good, although the synths later on seem to smooth things over a bit too much.

Track 9 starts with honky sax and neurotic bass. As the track progresses the neuroticness dies down, and a more straightforward jazz feel emerges.

Track 10 is a quiet and well controlled piano solo piece. Pretty nice.

Track 11 has accordeon and mandolins giving something of a Venetian feel, which is strengthened by the tenor voice of the singer.

Tracks 12 to 14 are pretty reggae-ish. Mmm, Finnish reggae. 13 moves even towards dub, with a Finnish toaster.

Track 15 is dreamy, almost still, quiet key carpets with ditto accompaniement.

Track 16 is led by Sheryl Crow like guitar, but the electro voice doesn't fit with it, feels sort of Parliament. As the track opens up, it becomes more, but not quite, poppy.

Track 17 is pop with a twist, with Divine synths. Reminds me a bit of such American female pop singers as Billie Myers or Erikah Karst.

18 is an American alt pop track, with a nice twist in the bridge. Good one, this.

Conclusion

A varied bunch.
