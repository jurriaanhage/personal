Summary

Again a Finnish band. And me thinking nothing much was happening up there besides Ageness and Hoyry-Kone, but this is their second album already. This band features two guys Timo and Esa from the gothic metal band Black Crucifixion. They decided however to change style and to play somethin that was not metal, but retained a dark mood. According to the accompanying letter this music is supposed to be "Progressive FOLK music with a dark mood". Wij zijn benieuwd.

The music

Opener Rat Dance is indeed a rather dark track. Especially the vocals are kind of down (quite typical for gothic) and, as usual I would say, accented. The vocals do not always fit well within the music. The song has a predominant riff, but also a nice atmospheric intermezzo. After this, the (space) rock returns with the addition of a flute that plays its solo. The song ends abruptly and we continue with a beautiful theme in It's The Light. The prominence of the bass adds to the depression of the track as well as the large, slow organ. In a strange way this music evokes older Camel, probably because of the melodies. I'm especially fond of the drumming which is very varied. After having come to a stand still, the dreary vocals pick up again. Then the song turns psychedelic with vocoded vocals and distorted guitar. Another more urgent theme enters the stage at the end reconciled with the first theme. Blazah opens soothingly with flute (again that Camel). Quite dreamy this track in the Italian way if you know what I mean. The folkiness that was already slightly felt in the previous songs, becomes more prominent in Self-Portrait. This is quite an up-tempo track, still dark, but the flute yields an optimistic note. The keyboards lay a nice layer of sound for the rest to build upon. The theme as in the second track is beautiful. Note that in the booklet this song is called Somber Regards and the next one is called Self-Portrait. I'm not sure which is which since the titles themselves are not mentioned. Somber Regards, then, is an acoustic and again thoroughly melodic song. In some way, the music is indeed quite folky, but the guitar sound is quite feeling and bluesy and the theme as played by the flute gives me goosebumps. Spiderland opens with soothingly again, but after a minute and the song is fired up. Then the song quietens down again and the flute plays again a great solo. Quite loudly the song afterwards winds to an end. The next two tracks Pennywhistle and Attack Of The UEberwermi are combined into one. First up is the very folky Pennywhistle that, before becoming quite frolic, opens with vocoded vocals. The song becomes more involved later with the addition of electric guitar and keyboards. Military drums and echoed acoustic guitar then lead up to some the vocoded vocals and then I guess we are taken into Attack... This is quite a rough track with some great breaks and this track destroys all that comes before it. Wild percussion, disjoint piano chords and spacious guitar. At the end the lyrics of PennyWhistle returns, so I'm not so sure now whether Attack is actually on this disc or whether it's lacking it.

The recording is good, it is quite easy to hear all the instruments, but if the album lacks one thing, it is power. This is mostly due to the singer, who sings like a dark pond, no ripples visible at the surface.

Conclusion

A surprisingly good album with indeed quite some folk influences. However do not think you will get jig after jig or even something in the vein of Tempest since the gothic influences are to be felt in the vocals and musically there's a lot going on. These guys really have both a terrific nose for a good theme and the flute is really impeccable. The downiness of the vocals might put some people off, but they will miss some great music doing this. With slightly over forty minutes, not a long album, but a thoroughly enjoyable one it is.
