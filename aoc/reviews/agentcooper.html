Summary

Well, I had never heard of them before. I have now.

The music

All songs on this shortish album are quite short. This should not mean that the music cannot be progressive, but it has to said that the music on this album also has a large pop (in the vocals) and rockcomponent. The first track, Wasting Away features Hammond, a somewhat acoustic approach, somewhat highpitched vocals, the beginning of a nice keyboardsolo and a great openingriff on the guitar (repeated later). The somewhat bouncy Don't Get Up has a striking vocal melody. It can sound quite poppy, but there also some heavier features. A very good song with plenty of variation. The beginning of A 13 Year is as catchy as that of the opening track, alternating between a soft acoustic/vocal part and somewhat psychedelic guitarwork getting quite orgastic towards the end. Little Town opens with acoustic guitar and a somewhat sad vocal melody. Lots of longing in this track, which ends with a choir. The vocals sound a bit fluttery (this is good, in case you wondered). Disinfect Your Mind is a rowdy riffbased track full of distortion. The Desolate Supreme is back to acoustics and is with 4.58 the longest track on the album. Again, the vocal melody stands out, and halfway we go over from acoustic guitar to piano and then back to guitar. Helmet Head is a somewhat bouncy track with plenty of organ and harmony vocals that sound a bit like Queen. Good For My Soul opens in the King Crimson Discipline style. A fast-paced rock track with plenty of organ and lots of energy and some nice breaks. After a rather extensive and varied instrumental intermezzo we close with the fast-paced opening part. The last track on the album is the percussively opening The Drive Song. The song has something tribal to it, evoking the empty freeways and large plains. Again the singing is quite longing, but the chorus is more poppy.

The artwork does not speak for the contents in my opinion. And although the singing is quite understandable, having the lyrics would also be nice.

Conclusion

If you call Marillion's Radiation progressive then I would suggest calling the music here such as well. The band combines pop, rock and prog and writing great songs and thinking of great melodies in the process. The vocal parts are often rather quiet and melodic, but the band can also kick ass when they want to. One could compare this band to Castanarc for instance, but the songs are even better and the variety is larger (louder when loud). Maybe the Urbane, a band on Verglas, is also a worthwhile reference, although my guess is that The Urbane is a bit more "alternative". Great music. Recommended if your horizon extends beyond plain prog.
