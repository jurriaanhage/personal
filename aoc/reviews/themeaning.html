Rivendel - The Meaning

Artist: Rivendel
Title: The Meaning
Label: Musea FGBG 4178.AR
Length(s): 51 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1996
Month of review: 01/1997

Line up

Tono Cruz - guitars
Juantar Perez - drums
Oscar Bello - keyboards
Josemari Aguirrezabala - bass, wind instruments
They all seem to sing a bit.

Tracks

1) La Telarana (6 Parts) 13.26
2) The Meaning (12 Parts) 28.47
3) L'art Brut (3 Parts) 9.02

Summary

A fondness for spiders (with 10 legs!) is without question, that's what I get from the cover. I know one track by them from the Exposure '88 compilation so they have been around. Never heard any of their albums though.

The music

The first track starts out very much in the Italian lyrical vein and is a bit tame at this point. Important instruments here are the acoustic guitar, the flute and the female and male vocals. In the last part the song picks up a bit and the female vocals are rather good (although very high) especially in comparison with the vocals of the regular bandmembers. All in all there's a little excitement for such a long track.

The next track, the title track, is a lot better with after a melodic start, loud guitars and a often repeated keyboardmelody. Often there are some funny and ELP like intermezzo's, but also the guitar is allowed to play in the rather typical Spanish style, a bit whining and over and over the keyboardtheme returns. The third part of the track is more peaceful with keyboards and flute. Then a bouncy part starts with some vocals, that are spoken. The music here is rather rhythmic and almost minimalistic and spooky sounds. The next part is one of the better ones: there are some dual vocals here and that works very well, because then the accents of the singers are masked and the way the vocal melodies complement each other, although they still sound a little amateuristic. I do notice some emotion here. The next part is a part that combines the German school of EM with a quiet but clear guitar sound. Rather slow and tension is now building and after a while a noisy, heavy guitar (a vacuumcleaner?) takes over the song. Then the guitar becomes even more pronounced, sounding clear and high and sharp. Also the next two parts are dominated by the guitar. We're at part 7 now and the guitar in this part is very repetitive, minimal almost while in the next part, who would have guessed?, the guitar has a blues rock sound. Part 9 is starts slow and low again, with the atmosphere of an approaching thunderstorm and although we might expect the guitar to take the lead again against the background of hard drums, it is the keyboard this time that does so and the guitar is condemned to the background as a tension builder. Part 10 has a spacey sound to it, with pronounced bass, keyboards in the back and a clear guitar and the singer asks himself: "Why am I here? Where am I here? When am I here? Who am I here?" after which the music erupts (they might have done that louder) and after some slowing downs and breaks we feel the quickening pace in part 11. The last part of the track is peaceful with acoustic guitar and rather accented vocals. I prefer that they sing in Spanish. The song ends on a Twelfth Night note with a good guitarsolo and ends in peace.

The third and last track is sung in French (!) and starts with vocals introducing the song after which the piano takes over. The song again has a definite Twelfth Night ring to it: both vocals, atmosphere and guitar. The second part of the song begins acoustic and the singing is again dual, but in this case it only makes the lyrics less audible (for most people this will not make any difference since it is French, but to me it does).

Conclusion

Well, you might have gathered by now that a lot of variation is present on this album and also that although I very much appreciate The Meaning and also the last track is rather nice the first track is a bit boring. The vocals are not very good and they might do better to sing in Spanish or use the trick of dual vocals more often. If you need references I'd say a little of he lyrical Italian bands with some Twelfth Night echoes but all in all I can't really hear them imitating anyone in a big way.
