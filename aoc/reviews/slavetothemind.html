Summary

Started in 1991, this is their third album and the first one coming to my ears.

The music

Access Denied opens with neurotic voices all speaking at the same time. Quickly the music starts and the beginning is good. Good melodies on the opening. The vocal part following it, is slightly less melodic and at first it seems that the band cannot hold on to the quality of the opening. However, later on more is added to the vocal part and the song comes out better. There is plenty of variation here, but within the context of a good composition: driving rhythm guitars, breaks, a bridge and some nice melodic material. The music is definitely songdirected, but the music is at times quite bombastic and powerful so I think the term powermetal is well-deserved for this music. The Distant Call is the follow-up. The intro is a great one, a very memorable melody. Then we come to the more tension building part, somewhat dark, but powerful. Before the minute is over the music speeds up and like a fast streaming river that ends up in a still lake we come to the vocal part. What often goes wrong with progmetal bands is that they can make instrumental parts, but the vocal parts leave to be desired. This powerful track does not have this problem. The vocals are accentless by the way. The song goes over neatly into the longish A Matter Of Mind. Again, the song is a great one with plenty of keyboards as well and a prominent bass sound. It seems at first in The Sense Go Blind that the band takes a little rest, but no, hell breaks loose soon enough again. In fact the ending is the most powerful part yet. Wind And Light is the first quiet track, with acoustic guitar and although the song is quite ballad like in approach, it is not all quiet and serene here, but it never becomes fast. A soaring piece with great melodic guitar and symphonic keys. Manic is a rather short track, but is not quite so manic as I expected. It is rather fast, but not chaotic or anything. Electronic Eyes opens in a complex way with lots of breaks. At times the music sounds a bit plodding, but then speeds picks up again. The melody sounds a bit familiar. The rhythm guitar is more prominent here than before, but there is also something else sounding like acoustic guitar, but quite loud. The opening of Now And Again reminds me a bit of Eye Of The Tiger by Survivor. Again one of those memorable melodies in this varied track which contains both plodding passages as well as great swerving guitars. We close with the driving Live In The Light, which features some quick piano playing.

Conclusion

Well, progmetal isn't dead as has been shown to the fullest by this record. Once in a while a record comes along that deserves everybody's attention. The sound is full, the songs are well-structured, the melodies are great and the playing and singing are impeccable. All in all a must have for progmetal fans. And if I may add: this should be interesting for anybody interested in prog music of the nineties.
