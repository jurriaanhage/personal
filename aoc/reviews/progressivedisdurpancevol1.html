Summary

A review page (ah, the competition!) known by the name of DURP released a sampler of various German bands and when he asked me to review this disc, I easily complied.

The music

Esthetic Pale opens the album with The Approach. The intro features soft, slow guitarwork and tingling bells, then the military drums set in and with a nice keyboard riff the short instrumental winds up to an end. A good choice for an intro. Joy Of Colour is featured with Claire (A Ghost Story). The vocalization is audible German, but not disturbingly so, the music is rather dark, but although the band tries to create a tension enhancing with some nice spooky soundeffects, the song tends to plod along a little, as if the writer and players have to think a little too much on the song. A little more fire during the quicker parts, please gentlemen. In atmosphere the music seems quite progmetallic, but certainly on this track it's not all quick and hard. A nice track, but there's room for improvement, improvement that should come with more expertise in recording (I hope). The Planet's Pulse of No Inner Limits opens as a synth track. The following passage is rather muddy with no clear line in the composition. However we emerge from this noisy passage into a rather typical up-tempo progmetal. The guitarsound is rather varied alternating between heavy rhythm guitars, quick Fripp-like neurotism and more typical progmetal guitarsolo's, but in a slight Arabic style. The vocals are certainly typical of (prog)metal, and rather high. The first half of the track is quite bouncy. The ending is softer part with long chords and for me, more appreciable, muscled singing, even something like The Tea Party creeping in. Witchboard is up next opens with acoustics. This recording is not the best one, the drums being rather dull. The vocals are also not great, but the lyrics are also not great ones to sing. Melodically this is the most recognizable up to now. Maybe that holds a promise for later; for now there's still a strong smell of amateurism. Track number five already with Sunblaze and their selftitled contribution, which is also the longest on the disc clocking at over 14 minutes. The opening is promising, quite bombastic and very melodic. Then some progmetalhead starts knocking on the door and rhythm guitar sets in, but then a surprise: a violin. Then, weird things going on with awkward time signatures, but the violin does not pay attention to all of this, it just continues. The more aggressive part returns. Although not a bad track, actually it's quite fun to listen to, it is one of the those track that feature a lot of nice things, but do not ring entirely well yet. It might be too fickle, too fragmentary, but a lot of nice ingredients do not make a good dish. Maybe next time try to keep the song shorter and put less things in, since some passages are really nice. I am especially satisfied with the emotional vocals, the accompanying piano and the Oldfieldian guitarsolo's appearing later. With the tasteful, but sometimes quite sweeping additions on violin this is the best track so far, even though it is quite fickle. re-Vision is a progmetal track with some soft acoustic verses. The vocals are quite strange on this track, it is hard to describe, but the singer not only speaks more than he sings, but he also does it in a strange way. The opening is more typically progmetal, lots of melody, lots of things happening and powerful chords. As often happens, the song sinks in with the first vocals, as usual because the vocal melodies are not up to par. The "fall on my knees" part is better and the guitar solo sounds okay, but the band has little new to offer. Estethic Pale does not feature with the longest track, but of all the bands they contribute most. This second track is over eleven minutes long and opens with ethereal female harmonies, quite nicely arranged actually; the vocals stay good throughout and might remind some of the Gathering, but more varied. The first few minutes the song tends to plod on a bit, with little change. Then the band starts to add things on: a playful piano here, some folky electric guitar there and a merry tune on the keyboards. Is is hard to tell whether band actually has two singers, since the line-ups are not included. If this band usually plays progmetal and this is just one of their non-metallic efforts, I wonder how the metal sounds, since what this band does here is quite distinctive. Strong folk leanings with great vocals and even some... glockenspiel? At the end there's even a strong synth solo. Asarja's Time Of Illusion opens with ecstatic keyboards and although the keyboardsound is quite varied, the song is quite straightforward. Especially the chorus is quite up-tempo with lots of echo in the vocals, making it closer to neo-prog and AOR than anything else yet on this disc. Closing shop is Spyhole with Losing. This song is probably not serious: the sound quality is lousy, the vocals are muddy and the music is very punklike, although with quite a few breaks, quite melodic and ending in piano. The song is not bad, but the sound is and very much so. Btw, you might think always writing about progressive that punk is not to my liking: wrong! In fact, the other day somebody asked me to review for a punk webpage and I might just do it.

The album features some info on the CDs the bands released, usually demo CDs, but more importantly pointers to the websites. Since the outside of a disc often determines quite strongly whether people will buy a disc, I strongly urge the maker of this disc to pay a little more attention to this aspect next time.

Conclusion

An all German compilation disc. From the outside you might think: bah, lousy artwork, not a really ringing name and probably full of bad progmetal. The latter is certainly not the case. If you compare his with mid-eighties progressive from the amateur progbands of this time, most of the music comes out favourably. There's a slight tendency towards amateurism by Witchboard ..., but the rest of them come out well with Esthetic Pale and Sunblaze coming out on top. Also, if the songs played here do say something definite about the bands, then the compilation doesn't lean that much toward progmetal at all and the more progmetallic songs are also quite balladlike. By the way, I bought mine for $5-6 during a festival from the guy himself, so I guess there's no reason not to try out this disc.
