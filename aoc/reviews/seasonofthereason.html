Summary

Although the name made me categorize this album as electronic music, it is clearly neo-prog, although a lot of electronics are used.

The music

Opener Second Man shows what this band is about: accessible music with a more or less progressive feel. Clear keys and riffy guitars, and obvious artificial drums. Bonnell's voice is about as high as and somewhat reminiscent of Jon Anderson's, but seems to miss out in gusto.

It's The Same All Over The World stylistically is mostly the same, accept that Bonnell's is now regularly off key. This is pretty awful.

Things don't generally improve from here on. The drumming sounds highly unnatural. The best moment for Bonnell's vocals is when they're on key. The organ has its moments, but the synths tend to get tacky. The guitars create some momentary relief, but not enough by a margin.

Positive exception on all this is the instrumental sequencer with guitar track Thirteen.

Conclusion

I didn't like the first track much, and not far into the second Digitalis started to irritate me. Frankly, sitting this album out is something of an accomplishment. I, for one, am totally in the dark as for why this material was ever released.
