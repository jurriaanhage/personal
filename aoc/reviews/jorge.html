Summary

From Spain hails solo artist Jorge. This seens to be his first walk on the electronic pathways.

The music

Lots happening in the first track. Synths, (percussively played) piano, bass like sounds work together to form a tapestry of melodies and patterns, all intertwined and juxtapositioned. The melodies and patterns are quite repetitive with slight variations. The music can also be quite soothing and cosmic with beeps and squeaks. All in all, Jorge incorporates a lot in his music. In some of the rhythmic parts I hear some later TD (Hyperborea). The intro to track Two owes quite a lot to classical music. The rhythms have so little sustain that it seems somebody is tapping you on the ear when you hear this with headphones. After a few minutes the music becomes mellow, too mellow for my tastes. I'm thinking of a slow moving Procol Harum here, but others might think of the sweeter side of Kitaro here, but it is not as good. Then comes a groovy bass-rich part with some jazzy piano work. Quite playful, with a nice melody. The following synth work is really quite strange. Very groovy, and with very strange sounds. Track three opens quite pastorally, but also quite menacingly. Visions of haunting forests dance before my eyes here. The theme seems simple but is also very striking. Then the music fades away somewhat and we get a rather simple and straightforward tune. The (s)low beat behind the music doesn't really help either. Quite a lot of different melodies here, but as a composition it seems like the wind blowing from all directions so you do not only not know where you're going, but also where you are meant to go. A strange composition this since quite little of what has occured reoccurs, leaving me with an idea that this is just a musical tapestry. Also I'm not fond of the sweet parts, that are really a little too sweat. The closing section however is quite dissonant and I like it. Four opens with what seems to be a children song melody, but it is also vaguely fugue like and later it becomes more "classical". On the also rather short Five some pointers exist to the more rhythmic TD, but the music is like previous tracks quite groovy, but my feeling is that music like this, if played at all, should be played by a whole band. This jazziness/grooviness continues in Six, but it also contains some parts in which the swinging aspect of the music is put to better use, where the music sounds like a swinging version of eighties TD. The melodies on the seventh track point into the direction of the East, Japan or somewheres. The melody wanders around constantly. Later the music becomes more atmospheric. Eight closes the album; it opens rather cosmically with good melodies and a nice build up. The music goes slightly into the direction of moody TD here. This moodiness in fact pervades the album that contains some strangely wailing sounds as well. I have to give that to him at least: he seems to have his own sounds and that is very important in his line of music. That I do not always like what comes out may in some cases more of problem for me than for him.

Conclusion

is that I'm in two minds about this album. On the one hand I find it too fragmentary, taking on too much on a debut CD. On the other hand, this is quite courageous and I also admit that Jorge has some nice touches and good, new sounds (to my ears at least). Compositionwise it could be a bit stronger, and later on he might borrow still less from TD and find his own niche in the electronic world.
