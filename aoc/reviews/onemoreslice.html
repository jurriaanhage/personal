Artist: Fruitcake

Summary

After two albums and line-up changes so that Pal Sovik is the only one present on the previous one, Room For Surprise, the band is back with

The music

The Joke enjoys a long intro with military rhythms and mellotron. Soviks vocals aren't without accent, but he still has that intimating quality and although there are vocal melodies, the vocals sound in some sense spoken. The song seems lyrically, and also in the mood it evokes, like a sequel to Time To Go of RfS. The keyboard playing is very typical and although we have a different keyboard player now, the sound is identical to that of RfS, organlike and a bit ehm woolly? and very slow. The song is not slow throughout and contains some powerful vocal parts and a majestic guitarsolo with a slow bubbling keyboardmelody.

The classical acoustic opening of The Spirit Of This Day is something else. This balladlike evokes a medieval mood and the wavery bass? in the back is nicely subtle. Then we get a melodic guitar solo with a rather unexpected break and some keygboard and organsolo's are thrown in for good measure. After this the ballad part returns.

Hell's Kitchen opens with keyboards that sound a bit like Morse code and a wailing guitar. We get a mid-tempo, rocking continuation on keyboard and guitar. After the sparse almost spoken vocals we come to a terrific powerful part reminiscent of King Crimson. This terrific part is sometimes replaced by a straightforward rock guitar and sometimes by a meandering keyboard solo and the opening section returns in some form at the end.

In Between Reality And Dream the intimate vocal section alternate with more euphoric pieces and again that squeaky keyboard is there. The sound of the song is rather reminiscent of Camel. Have A Slice starts as a typical neo-progressive up-tempo track with prominent basswork and organ. In the vocal parts the tempo is lower, but this is a rather straightforward track and certainly not their best.

Somewhat Late For Supper is a more melodic and varied instrumental track with nice melodies and dark keyboards. The drums sound, like earlier on, a little militaristic. In addition to the fine guitaristic parts I like the tension building in the second part of the track.

Final Signals tells a story. The theme of the song is one of the best on the album but I'm not too happy with the chorus (like the presence backing vocals, but I'm not fond of the vocal melody either). After the first vocal part we get some very subtle piano and guitar playing and a terrific bombastic part, after which the vocals return.

The album closes with Just Another Weird Vector. This is a typical Fruitcake track with a prominent high frequency melody on the keyboards and a good vocal melody. The tempo of the song is low and the overall atmosphere is moody and gloomy. After an isntrumental part the vocals return as they often do on this album.

Conclusion

I cannot help but be captivated by the music of this band. Although I can't like all of it, the mood the band evokes in their music is rather unique and although the rather woolly organlike keyboard sound will remind some of Camel, especially the vocals are unique. Sometimes, by building tension or the use of mellotron they also evoke King Crimson and on the whole the electric guitar seems more prominent here then on the previous album. Have A Slice is sort of the bad apple in the basket and does not really fit in with the rest, but still a very nice album. I wonder however whether they can keep this up. Hard to classify, but, for me, easy to like.
