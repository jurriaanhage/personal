Summary

An important rerelease from SI. Ed van Otterdyke is a multi-instrumentalist who recorded an album for major WEA in 1982. The album was well received and sold quite a few copies. Little was heard after that from the man due to problems with his label (he had a contract for three albums). It seems that these problems have come to and end and that he can release albums in the future.

The music

Let's talk about this album first. A lot of bands always yell that all the songs on their albums are so different from each other (while this is usually only the case, because they, being the makers of the music, look at their songs with a microscope and every note that is different is interpreted as being a world of difference between two songs). Ed doesn't say anything (I've never heard him anyway), but here it is: the album contains songs that range from melodic symphonic to classical to pop and to jazzy songs. It's immediately recognized that Ed knows how to play his synthesizers well and on this forms the basis for all his songs (there's a lot of hobo as well, played by Frank van Kooten). His own style is a little subdued and some themes reoccur throughout the songs, most notably the washing on the shore. Not that Eds music is that sweet, because his music is more like mysterious than romantic and at times majestic.

Going by the songs one by one, You and She's Gone, seem to be 'regular' popsongs, though You is the strangest love song I've ever heard. For instance, in the lyrics: 'You told him -- you told him -- you're in love with me -- he beats you -- he beats you -- beats out all the love you feel -- until only pain is real' She's gone is the song I like the least on this album. It's very up-tempo and I do not really like the lyrics. Still, even this song has it's moments. The popsongs are also the up-tempo ones, but usually the music is very quiet and moody. The vocals are quite good I think, his voice is a bit fragile and high (not squeaking). A jazzy song on this album is Sad Affair with Camel influences and horns.

The last category of songs, that I call the Syrensongs, contain the songs that have yet to be mentioned. The songs all share an atmosphere that borders on the romantic (love songs like Someone else) and the classical and is typical of this album. Syrensong, the beautiful and majectic pinnacle of this album greatly exemplifies this category. Another highpoint of this album, Nobody loves you is also to be found in this category.

Concerning the artwork, it's different from the album. Now included are some poems concerning the syrens and the drawing on the front is a lot better than the cover on the album. It looks a lot better this way.

Conclusion

All in all maybe not the progressives favourite because there are some very accessible songstructures to be found on this one, but the album cannot be easily dismissed especially if you like classical, keyboard dominated, diverse, at times majestic quiet melodic progessive for the early hours. Try it.
