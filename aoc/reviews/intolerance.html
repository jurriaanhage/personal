Summary

MarryAnn is a Slovenian electronic musician of the more accessible kind.

The music

Intolerance opens with a rhythm box followed by moody synths. The rhythm box tends to dominate the scene, as do some poppy themes that remind most of Jarre. There are also some tinkling bells, a bit like a Glockenspiel. I wonder if that is synthetic too? Halfway the power sets in, and we move into Jan Hammer territory.

Primal Instinct contains the rather simple melodic style, which does not mean that the songs are not well-composed. The brassy keys sound very synthetic, but I do not really mind. The melodies have something of an oriental ring to them, at times (between the brassy parts). The rhythm box is not really quiet, but the other keyboards outdo it, this time around. In the middle, we get a quieter part before the beat sets in again.

Nature's Hysteria continues in this vein with a strong percussive beat (but less nervous), and a very digital choice of sounds. There is some pan flute here too (digital). Lost Dreams opens in scratch mode, as if the sounds slip. There are plenty of things happening, also at the same time, but the music does keep a careful air, as if the composer is not so sure of himself.

With Earth's Revenge we get dancing electronics following a percussive/bass opening. The music is again very much in Jarre style (of the seventies), but a bit thinner in sound. Halfway, MarryAnn moves into moodier, atmospheric things, but not for long. All in all, a bit too danceable.

Ignorance Or Belief is even more poppy, if you can believe it. This is very much in the synthetic poppy style of eighties. It reminds me of Simple Minds' Theme For Great Cities, but certainly not up to that level.

Overheights is a beat rich piece, a bit like very early Depeche Mode, but the synths are a bit more prominent. Still, overall the feel is mainly eighties synthipop, but without the vocals and not so strong on melodies. From Now To Eternity is a slower, more thoughtful piece, with occassionally tinkling bells among the slow percussion. Then the beat becomes more prominent, as well does the bass line. It all stays a bit too light. The second half is more moody again, reminiscent of eighties TD. Finally then, Night Generation, which is a nice and catchy affair.

Conclusion

A very digital affair this, with accessible melodies all relatively straightforward. The songs sound mechanical and melodic at the same time, and the chosen sounds sound a bit cheap. Still, if poppy electronics is your thing (Jarre and Jan Hammer), then why not?
