Summary

Owning only The Pendulum I saw a gig of this Japanese band when they appeared at the Pul. The presentation was very different from their album, which for instance featured a vocalist. It seems now this person is no longer with and the parallels with Ars Nova were quite strong. At the stand of the band Mr. Numero Ueno was selling records of the band including a limited edition carton box with seven tracks and which now seems to be some kind of best of/leftovers album. After talking to me a little he gave one to me for reviewing. I'm not sure how many are left and whether there are also ordinary CDs of this title. Find out yourself.

The music

Opening with a special version of Meridian, at first I believe to be in the grasp of TD/Schulze, with some sequencerlike stuff, but then the bombasm of ELP enter the stage with all kinds of euphoric themes and music that also parallels that of the Australian Cybotron, residing between electronic and progressive. But the heavy drumming with all kinds of fill-ins tell us that this is really over-the-top progressive, and the band can hide quite well that what they are playing is mostly a collection of synthsolo's strung together with a few themes, the most interesting ones, recurring. On the next track, Orpheus Part 1-3 the introduction shows to be the missing link between Yngwie Malmsteen and ELP. The vocoded singing adds an nice dimension to the mostly instrumental music of Gerard. After a long bombastic part, the moving main theme recurs in a sad way (as in the beginning) in a violin-like voice and the song ends with some classically styled keyboards mimicking horns and piano. The Act Of The Apostles bristles again with energy and the opening is dominated by fluttering keyboards that are played in the seventies style. A very busy piece. The instrumental version of Prelude, which as might be expected is, a throughly melodious, semi-classical piece that one might compare with Camel's Raindances, but a little more powerful (and less beautiful, but that goes without saying). Empty Lie, Empty Dream is a vocal track with a glorious melody. The vocals of Suchy are very open and loud and this is probably his last appearance with the band since they parted ways. Sad, since vocals usually add something extra to hold on to. Again lots of pyrotechnics on this song with busy playing towards the end. Bombasm has no end on the relatively short Revenge and this is more of a play track with little soul, but lots of things going on at the same time. A jam. here's some part in it that evokes something of Genesis quite clearly, although the style of the music is thoroughly jazzrockish. Fiddling about is also what they do quite a lot on the closer Melting Time, but again a great theme underlies it all. Still the impression stays that the songs are not really compositions as such, but just very good themes strung together hidden beneath instrumental prowess. This is not necessarily a negative thing, and I must admit liking the music a lot.

Conclusion

The energy bristles of this album that combines the best of ELP and UK into a miasma of themes and busy playing, but such that emotion is not right out of the picture. The melodious are always nice and sometimes very good, but along the album some of the melodies seem to recur a little too often ( between songs instead of within). Certainly a band worth searching out, especially if you're into the bombastic side of progressive.
