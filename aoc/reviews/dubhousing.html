Summary

An album from the seventies reissued on CD. The band came out of the Rocket From The Tombs band and was quite experimental.

The music

The album opens quite typically with Navvy. This song shows the very distinct vocal style of David Thomas. His high, somewhat panicky voice. On On The Surface we hear Doors organ, while the music is somewhat reminiscent of Talking Heads (Stop Making Sense), but with samples, effects and plenty of bass. Dub Housing is a relaxed piece with talked like vocals. Again, Thomas gives a panicky note to the music and quite a lot of shrill sax. Instrumentally the song is quite meditative, but also a bit chaotic and with some less obvious guitar playing. On Caligari's Mirror we open with bass while the vocal part is a schizo version of What Do We Do With The Drunken Sailor? Shards of music make up Thriller! (long before M.J.) and the music is even less tangible than on the previous songs. We come to things more danceable with the short I Will Wait and also Driking Wine Spodyody is weirdly funky and jumpy. Again the Beefheartian meanderings of Thomas lead the song on. Slide guitars enough on the jumpy/noisy Ubu Dance Party. In fact, the band use a slide guitar sound quite a lot on this album with the vocals giving the album a kind of helium feel. Again, quite frolic stuff. Dark and noisy is Blow Daddy-O (what about those titles?). The opening of the closer Codex reminds me of the American Mid West. Then we get an interlude with some kind of soft cosacks quire and the opening returns. Rather a subdued and melodic track with some weird playful guitar twanging. Then the repetitive vocals set in. The melody, by the way, is remarkably familiar.

Conclusion

Experimental, that is for sure. Definitely a sound of their own (both then and now one might say). The vocals of Thomas (recently resurfaced with Mirror Man) are the main focal point. The music is not loud and not even that inaceesible, but the band has a knack for turning out some might quircky songs. I'm nmot that fond of songs like Ubu Dance Party (thought I can understand the "humour" of it) and prefer Thriller! or Caligari's Mirror. Whatever way you look at it: something for the avantgarde/RIO people and of course people who like Red Crayola or Captain Beefheart.
