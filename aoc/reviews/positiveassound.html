Silent Lucidity - Positive As Sound

Artist: Silent Lucidity
Title: Positive As Sound
Label: Music IS Intelligence WMMS 078
Length(s): 46 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1996
Month of review: 08/1999

Line up

Andreas Jaeger - bass, sequencer programming
Jens Christoph Maurer - drums
Joachim Reichl - vocals, guitars, sequencer programming
Violker Zeeb - guitar

Tracks

1) A Different View 10.45
2) King Of Paris 7.34
3) Walls Of Silence 10.45
4) Atmosfears 7.58
5) Restraints 9.40

Summary

Already three years old when I got it, this album is unique in one respect: I had already bought it myself before I got this promo copy. The album is on the Music Is Intelligence label, but the letter I got was from Lucid Deception Music from a member of the band (I remember now, that this is also the first WMMS to be reviewed by me). The second album of this band is planned for 2000.

The music

A Different View opens with bells, and after some somber vocals and piano, the rhythm guitars set in, but quietly. After a few minutes, the song gets underway, with mid-tempo rhythm guitars, but this part is not really striking, both melodically as well as vocally. On the whole this is a rather dark track, not very fast, but more on the heavy side. There's plenty of variation on the vocal side and also compositionally, but the song also lacks a bit of coherence, but also the fire and energy that generally comes with progmetal. It seems that the band believes more in a kind of subdued progmetal. This is again the case on the next track, King Of Paris. If I heard correctly the band even uses a banjo here or at least a guitar instrument from folk music. Afterwards the progmetal burst loose again. Again, the songs fails to captivate me. It might be the vocals, it might be the composition, it might be the melodies, I'm not sure. It gets a bit better on Walls Of Silence, which like the previous tracks has some folk influences. The vocals ary very relaxed, a bit hazy in fact. The guitar intermezzo features both quick acoustic guitar and also some quite heavy rhythm guitars, after which we come to a spacey guitar solo. A good atmosphere is now being build on this somehow willowy sounding track. The best track so far, alhough the ending sounds a bit too popular. Atmosfears is an instrumental which after a gloomy beginning, the song is transformed into riff-driven progmetal with plenty of variation and some nice keyboards as well. Again, I wouldn't have minded to get it a bit louder, but this might be a simple question of budget. A satisfying instrumental for which the band saved some of the best melodies. The closer is Restraints, quite a bit longer than was specified. Here, the singer shows the back of his tongue in a somewhat Roger Waters like eruption. The verses however sound a bit dreary, but might be meant to convey exactly that. Then follows a driving part, that could have been a bit more driving, if they would have hightened the pace a bit more. The last few minutes of this song seem like an afterthought and maybe the reason that this song is longer than is noted on the CD.

Conclusion

Not a bad album, but on the whole not as satisfying as I would have it. The band includes some interesting things such as a little folk and it seems they do know atmosphere, but in many of the song I find something lacking. One of the things is that the music does not have that energetic driving feel to it that most progmetal has. On the other hand, the atmospheres tend to compensate for this. The vocals are not great (but not disturbing either) and I hope they will be a bit more powerful next time (like in Restraints). I have to say however that the song get better during the album, with some good melodies especially in the instrumental and Walls Of Silence being another good track. Reason enough to be curious as to their next album.
