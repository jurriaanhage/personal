Summary

A short history with this quickly penned and recorded album (16 days). Like LTE it seems all those DT guys want some time off and Morgenstein and Rudess are also at quite a lot lately. Magna Carta started off the tributes and now it seems their project idea also caught fire.

The music

The Platypus is that weird animal from Australia that combines aspects of fish, birds and mammals into one strange whole. Maybe that is also what the members of this band thought of the band at some time. Opening energetically with the organdrenched rocker Standing In Line, with vocals reminding as always of King's X, the album continues with the slightly bluesy Nothing To Say. Tabor's voice slightly whines here, but it IS a somewhat sad song with Morgenstein freewheeling on the organ. Also a quite Beatlesque intermezzo a la LSD in here, after which the guitar violently tries to break the fitting monotony of the track. Rock Balls/Destination Unknown is a JazzRock instrumental with lots of things happening and plenty of variation especially on the drumming side. Then the track becomes moody and I guess this means we have entered Destination Unknown. Great build-up here and on the whole a very strong instrumental track. Platt Opus is a slightly classical, but still rocking track that thus reminds me of Kansas although the soaring guitar is certainly not typical of said band. Again, great melodies throughout here, and making Tabor stop singing for a while is I think a good idea, since his voice is so distinctive that songs are bound to sound the same. Also note the fact that in this instrumental track it is explained what the Platypus is. I'm With You is back to rock and with the slightly vocoded vocals I'm first reminded of Lenny Kravitz, but this love song also holds those typical King's X harmonies. Plenty of organ again. Blue Plate Special is a relaxed instrumental, a bit bouncy and like many of the tracks here, definitely in the blues direction, but without the limitations. The synths play a strong diversifying role here. Chimes contains...chimes...and some very nice piano. A flowing instrumental and this is fitting before the rocker Willie Brown. This is the song that comes closest to a straight rock song and is also selected for single release, but even so it is not bad. Bye Bye is a moody, slow moving ballad with an atmosphere that reminds me again of Kravitz, that sixties atmosphere like in Let Love Rule. What About The Merch? is a slightly freaky track, a bit bouncy and quite some leanings to jazzrock.

Conclusion

A coherent and likable album. Not that technical, but for me the right balance between instrumental and vocal tracks with diversity, good playing, especially on the keyboards, and strong, thoroughly melodic compositions. Strong blues leanings and some classical and jazzrock influences are involved. Sometimes quite straightforward, but also quite enjoyable.
