Summary

Von Daniken consists of a member of the English band Haze and one of their roadies. Two of the tracks were already present on a demo tape by the band from 1990, while the middle track is featured in a different version on the McMahon solotape The truth about Flying Saucers (which also contains The Liquidator by VDGG and Shot by both sides by Magazine). Haze was an outfit of neo-hippies making all kinds of music really, whatever they felt like. They had some bad mishaps in their time and made quite a few demotapes, two albums and a few singles. Recently a compilation of the best Haze recordings has been released.

The music

They were rumoured to sound a bit like Oldfield, making three 'side' long suites with low tempo melodic synthesizer/guitar music.

It does indeed start our very subdued with layers of keyboard and mid-tempo acoustic guitar. In atmosphere comparable to the likes of Gandalf. Not actually New Age music or synthesizer music, but not progressive ROCK either. It's just being plain beautiful, like describing the sun up on one of their new worlds. The second part of track one reminds me of Vangelis' China.

An interesting track, although not that good, is Closedown, the last part of Suspended Animation, a track about the demise of Haze, with a lot of references to VDGG. This track is the most rocky of the lot and is comparable to Haze material.

The second suite contains tracks with names such as Amphibious Aardvarks, Syncopated Cephalopod Scurry and Plank's Ton, while the track Eartlight used to be called 'Chopsticks through the neck pinned him to his Sushi bowl'. This track is also quite good and indeed a bit in the Oldfield vein with the parts Seasoned Rivers and Fish Finger Frenzy/Plank's Ton being the best parts.

The third one contains the like of From the Submarine Canyons of the Eigenmannia... Well, anyway, this one starts out rahter mysteriously, like having wandered off into some dark forest. It calls to my mind the old Tangerine Dream. Another track that is more progressive than synthesizer is Funky Dog, the second part of the third suite. Quite a busy, jamming track with highpitched keys and meandering guitar. The following A Pleasant Interlude is no more than that and no less. The closing track is another one of those very good build-up tracks that I so thorougly enjoy.

Conclusion

The title does cover the load. Very atmospheric music and beautiful melodies, but never dark or moody. In a sense quite Oldfieldian, but also a lot of synthesizer and piano and less guitar I think. Especially quite little electric guitar. I would therefore dub this release as more of a synthesizer release than one by a progressive rock band. Recommended to anyone interested in good synthesizer stuff, not always mellow, but varied with some guitar (mostly acoustic) and at times great melodies.

For Beatles fans: track 18 contains a part of George Harrisons 'Only a Northern Song'.
