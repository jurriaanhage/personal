Summary

The second album of the Italian band Eldritch after the debut Seeds of Rage. This progressive hardrock band should be in the vein of Savatage, Angra and Dream Theater.

The music

After some spooky sounds and screaming people the album opens full blast with aggressive guitars, fast paced drumming and biting singing. The lyrics seem to be somewhat in the horror area, a kind of subject that I'm not very fond of, but to each his pleasures. This song is very up-tempo in the instrumental parts and can be likened to the Magna Carta metal bands, but seemingly more melodic. Naturally in this the keyboards, but also the vocal melodies play a decisive role. Usually I'm not too fond of the vocalist, but the vocals fortunately do not all sounds the same on this album.

In the next track we get a long intro with interesting signatures and where the keyboards are also quite important. The song has double vocals: he singer himself, but also a whispering demonic voice. The singing and the vocal melodies have something typically Iron Maiden over them. Most of the tracks are similar in approach as the first one with energetic and fast paced playing, guitar solo's, keyboard lacings, typical progressive metal vocals, signature changes, but and that is very important, interesting melodies and more importantly different vocal melodies. Some remarks about the heavy tracks are that the lyrics do not fit well in At The Restless Sea that also has a rather playful intermezzo, the bombastic Salome's Dance which has a good vocal melody, the extremely fast-paced Erase and the complex The Quest and the impressive closer Dawn of the Dying that has a great melody.

Of course the album has its typical ballad. In this case Sometimes In Winter being slow moving, but still heavy with piano and a question/answer game in the vocals. Especially the piano adds an interesting note. Kind of a desperate song that works itself to a climax.

A point of criticism that I will not delve into deeply is that sometimes I get the impression that sometimes the musicians seem to make a decision to do something, like playing a guitar solo and then the guitarist thinks better of it. Well, it may be my imagination and at times, but this is very subjective, I would have used some of the melodies more to the fullest and would have withstood the need for change.

Conclusion

I'm not very fond of the type of lyrics they spew, but musically the band is at okay, with real compositions containing quite a number of breaks but without seeming too artificial. The melodies are generally quite alright and the music contains a few other features that make the songs not sound all alike. I'm sure the lovers of progressive metal of the more melodic, but not compromising, kind will like this and in fact I like to drum along with the music which is always a good sign. I do recognize the inherent limits to this kind of music, but within its kind it stands above average.
