Summary

The band the Enid is rather a strange duck in the progressive waters with their very orchestral, classic approach to music. Especially their earlier albums are interesting, but IMO the later Enid work is not so interesting. Here we have a very recent album of this long lived "band". Also, they have a very dedicated "following" under the name The Stand, that have helped the band to record their music and they seem to be more active in the music then an ordinary fan-club.

The music

Not many progressive acts can be "accused" of being romantic, but this song tells us that there's at least one. However, this is not the only face the the Enid turns to us, because the rhythms in the beginning of this track reek of ambient house, while later in the track there's some heavy guitarwork and also some ethnic percussion. On good listen one hears rather straightforward electronic music, which is then turned into some entirely different by use of electronic strings.

The next one is rather easy going and a little too uneventful, while the third track starts out very bombastic with a good synth intro. Then, quietly the theme is repeated and the intro comes back, heavily. For the rest of the track, a soloing guitar is at the fore, not very structured, but more or less meandering its way through the notes.

The long titletrack is in the classic style, but there are some trite, romantic parts to it that I really don't like, but it's not all like that and in some cases the guitar actually roars on this still seemingly relaxed track.

The next track I like better. Again, very classic, but also rather emotional and melodic.

The sixth track starts out with keyboards, a bit of a bubbling crawling sound, subdued but menacing. The percussion starts to set in, and we wait for "it" to happen. It then is the meandering solo guitar that moves somewhere in the back of the track. In the middle the sound becomes dark and moody and I think Hitler is allowed to speak his word (in other words, to rant). Especially in this part the title is to the point. Very dark stuff. Then the music abruptly takes a turn for the bombastic. Good melody. The end of the track is rather spooky although it ends on a symphonic note.

Actually i'm not very familiar with classic music, and so the closer can remind me of something that doesn't actually sound like it at all. Anyway, the last track is overal very melodic and friendly and reminds me of those melodramatic Hollywood movies. Moving towards the end, the music gets more powerful.

Conclusion

Musically this album contains both ethnic influenes, Tangerine Dream like synthesizer and more importantly classic song structures. All of these have been integrated well and especially the more important role for the synthesizer playing itself as it were adds to the music. The music is not as structured as it used to be, but sometimes the instruments play for the sake of playing and again this is not a bad idea. Best tracks for me are Gateway, Freelance Human and Dark Hydraulic. The orchestra is of course programmed and some people might not like that, but I guess that is their problem, not so much The Enid's. I think that I like the music better then I might have, because I've listened to quite a lot of classic stuff lately (don't ask me where I find the time, cos I don't). When it gets romantic however it tends to make my skin crawl.
