Finisterre - Storybook

Artist: Finisterre
Title: Storybook
Label: Moonjune Records MJR002
Length(s): 77 minutes
Year(s) of release: 2001
Month of review: [07/2001]

Line up

Fabio Zuffanti - bass, vocals
Stefano Marelli - electric and acoustic guitars, vocals
Boris Valle - keyboards
Sergio Grazia - flute
Andrea Orlando - drums

Tracks

1) In Limine 7.19
2) Orrizonte Degli Eventi 15.29

4) Altaloma 10.12
5) Macinaaqua, Macinaluna 8.12

7) Phaedra 13.51
8) Canto Antico 11.28

Summary

Although Zuffanti did the Merlin Rock Opera, I lost touch with this band after In Limine (oh wait, they did do the Fiinisterre Projetc album Hostsonaten, that I also reviewed). Still, I used to wonder where the band itself went: did it dissolve or not? This disc does not answer any questions, but does give us some "new" Finisterre music.

The line-up of this live disc, as it was recorded in 1997 in the States at Progday, is more similar to the one of Finisterre, than to that of In Limine. The tracklisting however shows many tracks from In Limine, three of them in fact. The others, except Altaloma, are from the self titled album. The album is actually a rerelease of the album Live at Progday 1997.

The music

In Limine was the title track of Finisterre's second album. It opens in a style reminiscent of Focus with a flute playing a memorable theme. The electric guitar also sets in early and the music becomes driven. Then the music takes gas back time for some atmospheric mellotrons with more modern keyboards running in the back. Soft bass lines, fast but light drumming. Time for some jazziness then with playful fast piano. The music does continue to be moody. Then the main theme returns in a subdued fashion and the music then reminds me of Barclay James Harvest at their proggiest.

Flute opens the long track Orrizonte Degli Eventi. Like PFM at their most lyrical the first part is filled with easy going acoustic guitar and wonderfully melodic Italian vocals. Then we get a strong dark tense passage with tortured vocals. Time for some more typical progressive rock with meandering guitar and keyboards both supporting each other, non-standard rhythms and quite a lot of repitition in the melodies. Just before the tenminute mark we have another jazzy interlude, jazzy mostly because of the use of the Fender Rhodes. The Italian lyrics continue here, the bass and guitar interject an occasional note. The music then stops, and when the audience thinks: ha done, the music starts all over with a bass lines and the repetitive riffs of the middle part. Nice driven drums line the melodic guitar solo followed by a melody taken from Van Der Graaf Generator. Plenty of breaks here, as we are waiting for the final triumphant vocal part. First however the drums and Fender Rhodes play some nicely dissonant and tense music. The triumphant guitar that takes the lead and the vocals then begin the final build-up. Goosebumps here, although the vocalist seems almost out of breath.

Phew. Time for something less conspicuous. This is Hispanica with its playful opening on flute, somewhat moody, mellotronic sounds and tinkling acoustics. Along the way alternat5es back and forth between somewhat the flamenco of the acoustic guitar and the playfulness of the flute. With respect to the acoustic guitar one might also think of Steve Howe and Yes on this track.

Altoloma is a cover of a track by PFM. Actually what strikes me most about this song is the Jesus Christ Superstar inspired riffing in the middle (remember, when Judas runs towards his death). A really dramatic and driving piece of rock there for you. The music gets accompaniment from some meandering dazzling keyboards by Valle. Then the music dies down a bit and the flute makes its entrance again, with the guitar repeating its lines.

The starts to play around a little on Macinaaqua, Macinaluna with its classical intermezzo's, heavy rocking guitars and Fender Rhodes piano. The vocal part sounds a bit operatic.

Asia is a rather short track. It opens with fast acoustic guitar and rather standard neo-proggish keyboard runs. The flute is something else again and actually the music is quite tense here. Later the music becomes more anthemic with fast played guitar.

Phaedra features rather wild progressive rock in the beginning, but also a bluesy and sensitive part with an anthemic guitar. Then we get a lullabyish passage with the dreamy Fender Rhodes and flute. The great theme is repeated on keyboard with echoey spacey guitars soloing. Time for a short drum solo and we come to the band introduction. Boris Valle's name is followed by a part of PFM's wonderful and emotional Impressioni Di Settembre. Heavy guitars and fast flute playing follow. The pace has gone up quite a bit now. After Zuffanti's introduction, we hear another of the bands influences: 21st Century Schizoid Man. Marelli's name is followed by a citation from Firth Of Fifth. This part is quite long. We move back then to wildly meandering pacey keyboards. The band is freaking out now, somewhat.

The final track Canto Antico has a melodic and somewhat somber first vocal part. Playful drums take the song from there. The music has quite a bit of the Italian bands form the seventies with percussion punctuated flute and somewhat Arabic melodies on guitar.

Conclusion

Actually, the music on this live album is much less daring than their two studio albums. This does not mean that the music has become boring. Far from it, because the quality of the themes is high, the playing active and dynamic and the vocalists show great diversity as well. The album has a strong live feel (also because the sound is less than perfect), but hey I am not complaining. If you found their first two studio albums just a bit too complex, sterile and prefer your music more melodic and easier to take, this is an album for you. If you have these albums
