Summary

The music

This album is filled with the sort of brooding progressive rock made in Sweden and -to a lesser extent- Norway. So no surprise the band should come from Sweden. References are bands like Gazpacho, Carptree and Drahk von Trip, the latter for the seventies rock atmosphere. Not that the music is always akin stylistically, but the mood is. A lot of the tracks have the structure of regular pop music. The longer tracks are more an elongated form of this, building up to a climax, than they are epics in the traditional progressive sense. Added in are some Americana influences. Greed, for instance, has the slightest hint of Neil Young in both guitar play and vocals. The short Zero also hints of the American, but less identifiable. Alongside with the American influences are simple rock influences.

The vocals generally spoken are quite firm, and often take the lead. Combined with their sound this steers the music in the direction of late sixties, early seventies psychedelica, whilst moving away from the intimacy of more modern prog styles. The fact that the guitar may introduce some lingering licks, but just as often stays in the background falls in with this. We are already in track 5 when the first lingering guitar comes along.

Conclusion

I like the new style of progressive music, since I don't consider steering away from the focus on long songs with odd signatures and umpteen sub melodies a necessarily bad thing. Sure, the structure of the tracks is simpler, but the music often gains in sincerity and with that intensity.

This album signifies that DarXtar, even after being fifteen years around, still manage to develop themselves. The mentioned Americana influences were not an enrichment from my point of view. They bring in an aspect that doesn't enlarge the concept. Sure, it makes the sound somewhat different from others moving in this vein, more raw. But I don't consider that to be an addition necessarily. This, of course, is in the ear of the beholder. Good album, but not at the top of the stack in its genre, not the band's best either.
