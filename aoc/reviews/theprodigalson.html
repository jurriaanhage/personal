Summary

The come-back album of Nektar. It is already a few years old, while I write the review. In fact, a new one is close at hand.

The music

Terminus/Oh My is the long opener, although in fact these are two songs. The first of these opens with bouncing guitar, the vocals, with good vocal harmonies added at times, are distinctive. The main feel I get is that of a proggy bluesrock band, with elements of Magnum. But the approach is less slick than Magnum's. The Magnum is strongly apparent when the music relaxes somewhat, and we move into more balladlike territory. This is a good song, a good melody, and also quite a bit of atmosphere and variation. Don't come looking here for complexity though.

With Now we come to ballad territory, careful acoustic guitar, somewhat shy even. The vocal melody is a good one again. Then the Kashmirish guitar drone sets in, and we a good quality rock song with a bluesy undercurrent and good melodies and bombast in vocal harmonies. I Can't Help You is a bit too catchy for my tastes, but it is sung well. The organ is prominent, but still the song itself has strong AOR influences. The vocal harmonies are nice again, bringing in some more variation. The final part is a bit of a singalong affair.

The Drinking Man's Wine opens with acoustic guitar, again a good composition with a distinctive melody, but keeping it calm. Well, not entirely though, because at times the guitar barges in for compensation. On Shangri-La, the guitar simply barges on, although in the case too the vocal part is acoustic and friendly. The vocal is plaintive.

Salt And Vinegar And Rhythm And Blues is what you might expect. Think of guitar dominated blues, and not really anything else. The Prodigal Son then opens catchily, but soon dies down to a sparsely vocal passage. The harmony vocals are soft and subdued underlying the music. There is something of the soft side of Saga in here.

Be Tonight is a weak opening catchy tune. This is too cliche for me. It reminds me strongly, and in fact this happens more often, of American AOR and radio rock from the eighties. The blues undercurrent and the keyboards are the only countering elements.

Day 9 opens quietly with acoustic guitar, did I not here this song yet? Yes I did, this part comes from Drinking Man's Wine. But then we get some more proggy guitar, nicely melodic and driving, also because of the strong bass presence.

Conclusion

A mixed bag of rootsy blues, Magnum/eighties AOR, with songs which are generally quite accessible, the evident progorientation of Nektar is long gone. However, the songs, especially in the beginning of the album, do include sufficient variation to, for me at least, stay on the right side of good. In fact, the melodies and execution make for some well-remembered music, and the warm organ and the large keyboard presence as well as good vocal harmonies do bring in an element of bombast which ought to be liked by a progressive rock fan. Note though that this becomes less and less the case as we proceed along the album, where the sameness can wear me down a bit. All in all though, this one contains some good songs, which can be heard in contrast with, but never instead of Tab In The Ocean or Journey To The Center Of The Eye.
