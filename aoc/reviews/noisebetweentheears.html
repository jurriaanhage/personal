The music

Notwithstanding the rather terrible artwork on the front cover this seems quite an appealing album at first: The Dark Tower opens menacingly with keyboards after which the drumpads set in. In a way the music reminds me of Jarre here, but with live drums and the music comes close to synthesizer rock. But the music is more varied and has some rather good atmosphere changes as well as appealing melodies. The composition as a whole may still be a little too monotonous, one part is repeated a little too often with too little variation. Moody is the opening to the next one, The Rage Of Achilles. Then the music slowly picks up momentum with helicopter sounds and a strong piano riff. The melodies stay good throughout. The music is quite powerful, but it could be more so I feel and the song does end a little too abruptly for my tastes. There is a certain melodic similarity with the next track, Facade II. The atmopshere is a brooding one and damn that I can't remember what they sound like. In the music there is some of the darkness present in the music of TD, but the music is a little too rocky for this and contains no sequencers. The melodies are generally quite warm and the band has a tendency to repeat their melodies quite a lot. Other people that come to mind are Jobson with his dark Theme of Secrets, but more so the more obscure band Rejoice and Chris Hillman. Again a nice track, but notwithstanding the good melodies something seems to be lacking. It might be the fact that on the whole the music does not bristle, the laidbackness, or something is missing in the compositions (they are structured, but maybe a little too slowmoving and contain too few surprises). The sound becomes raunchier on Drift with nicely rolling drums and a noisy guitar in the back. Later on this track becomes more or less like the first three tracks. Redival is a little too easy, and nothing much happens in this track. It bounces around a little and reminds me sometimes of Yanni. Canvass is with over ten minutes the longest track. Opening with weird soundscapes and jungle sounds the music on this track is quite powerful. Quite a poopy song though notwithstanding the breaking glass, the Art Of Noise samples and the rather rocky sound. This song does come closest to what we call progressive, so far at least. The samples are used as part of percussion and not just as a random sounds. Grace (Preferred) is also a rocking track and not really what you would expect from the title. Again, it shows that the drumming grooves quite well on this album, since my hands want to drum along instead of typing this review. The guitar is quite prominent on this track with an extended solo.

The soundquality and production are reasonable, but especially the latter deserves more attention. This however is something I assume will come with time.

Conclusion

An album with nice music, but unfortunately in some way little to say. The style is reminiscent of Cybotron, Chris Hillman and Rejoice and as such lies somewhere in the synthrock corner (although they say they are a progressive instruMENTAL - ROCK jazz trio. On the whole it turns out the the melodies are really fine, the drumming is also quite good and the music is very keydominated (except for the closer contains little guitar). Still, the music lacks something and that is mainly because the band does tend to repeat it's melodies quite often making the music sound like a long string of repeated tunes. This sells them a little short at least since the compositions are not that bad, but I do feel that a certain meaningfulness is lacking here. Still, all in all quite a nice album that will certainly appeal to a number of listeners and a hope for the future, if, and I hope they do, they have one.
