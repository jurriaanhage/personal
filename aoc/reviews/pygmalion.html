Square the Circle - Pygmalion

Artist: Square the Circle
Title: Pygmalion
Label: self produced
Length(s): 35 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1997
Month of review: 08/1997

Line up

Roland van der Stoep - guitar
Jan-Willem Verkerk - bass
Paul van der Zwaal - drums
Martijn Paasschens - vocals, keyboards

Tracks

1) Chasing The Shadows 6.01
2) The Saddest Show 10.08
3) Bright Horizon 8.32
4) Pygmalion 10.31

Summary

I got this demo CD from the band. I'm not sure whether it is for sale. In any case, the band is now working on a regular CD. The band started in 1993 and has released another demo CD "Nature's Window". You can find them at http://www.reborn.nl .

The music

Although the band still has some amateurist tendencies (most notably the sometimes rather trite drumming (compensated elsewhere) and the vocals still sound a little too Dutch to me (like Voyage by Differences and Cold Steel by Cliffhanger)) I was positively surprised by this demo. The sound of the band is very melodic and might remind some of a band like Differences, but although the vocals are very different, the band reminds me a lot of Twelfth Night (for instance the spoken part of The Saddest Show reminds me of Creepshow and in fact the lyrics are also rather indicative of that same song). The sound can be very dreamy, but can also be biting or create tension in the music. In atmosphere the music can be likened to Lands End. The four songs are rather similar in style with strong melodies and lush keyboards and varied guitar playing and off-beat rhythms that are lacking a bit in the groove area: the rhythms can be anything you like, but they should add dynamics to the music, making you want to tap along and I'm sorry to say this isn't often the case here. The intro of Bright Horizon calls Collage's Moonshine to mind and also the guitar work in this song in particular reminds me of that same band. The instrumental part is a bit too long for taste and again the drumming isn't very exciting.

Conclusion

An enjoyable demo CD that tastes like more: very melodic but not too accessible symphonic rock that should be interesting for lovers of Twelfth Night, Collage and Egdon Heath (the level of these bands is never made, but The City of Egdon Heath is also a far cry below their later work especially in execution). The vocals are okay and often work well but they do not shine. Some parts of the music sound a bit lame, because the vocalist is singing and the drummer doesn't seem to want to interfere overly much, although this also happens to be the case in some of the instrumental parts.
