Summary

This band has had some positive reviews, so I was very curious about their album and here it is.

The music

The album opens with some quick piano work. Chuck's vocals are very good and sympathetic. They remind me a bit of Jens van der Stempel of Egdon Heath. The song is quite up-tempo, well played and with solos on keyboard and guitar.

The vocals on the second track are somewhat higher and a bit thin. I think Ray is singing on this track, but after a while Chuck takes over again. They have taken the trouble of inviting a trumpet player for this song, which like the previous one is up-tempo (Sussudio?). There's even some sort of choir.

The third track starts out very melancholy, with piano and acoustic guitar. The track is comparable to Embrace by Landmarq, although sadder, and very well brought with the obligatory guitar solo. The Well Lit Place returns later on.

The next track reminds me of the likes of neo band Abel Ganz: up tempo and melodic with a very good pianolike keyboardsolo in the middle. The background vocals are a little weak.

We move directly into The Search. This song like many on this album has blaring horns on it, reminding me of Fish's Big Wedge. Don't think that makes it bad, after Phil Collins has scared the proggy wits out of everyone with tracks like Sussudio. I think Fish a.o. showed that it can be done. The melodies are good, that's what counts here.

In track 6, The Well Lit Place returns with keyboards and trumpet. Yes, trumpet again, but now more plaintive and subdued. Again we move directly into the next track. This is a good ballad with well-dosed backing vocals by Michelle Grenier.

The next rack again reminds me a lot of Egdon Heath, for instance a track like Stand Straight, while Mourning of Spring reminds me of For Absent Friends.

The End of Forever is not that impressive, and the same holds for the next track which is a bit uneventful, although the melody is alright.

The intro to The Closest I've Come sounds very familiar and the subsequent piano riff reminds me of Bruce Hornsby. This song is also not quite a success.

Porn Jazz is not mentioned on the outside of the booklet or the CD, but it can be found inside the booklet and what's more on the CD itself. It's a tiny jazzy instrumental, probably to be used for adult movies in time to come (pun not intended).

Conclusion

Comparable to Egdon Heath musically but less epic and more into lovesongs and the shorter songs like for instance Abel Ganz or For Absent Friends. They also use the trumpet and other hirns quite a lot, and sometimes quite obtrusive at that, but it's easily forgiven. Very good melodies and that's what counts in neo prog. Of course, the album is well-played, they have some good vocalists and there's emotion here.

The lyrics are mostly about love and that might be a bummer for some of you, I know, because you want to hear lyrics about other people and countries, not about things you can relate to directly. Well, tough luck.

This album should be something for the (romantic) proggers among you that are also interested in the likes of the alreayd mentioned Egdon Heath, For Absent Friends and Abel Ganz to name but a few. This album could very well turn out to be an album worth of attention for people outsided the progressive realm, without offending the people in the realm itself. On the negative side I have to note that the album is a bit too long, because at the end the songs sound a bit weaker.
