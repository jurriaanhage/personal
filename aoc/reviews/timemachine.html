Summary

Well, everybody should know that Capt. Sensible is former guitar played of the Damned and has also had his solo share of fame. For the rest the guys keep themselves shrouded in obscurity although I have the impression that Dr Spacetoad is in fact Paul Cheetham.

The music

Should we take this band seriously? Okay, the Tom Jones cover indicates they do have a sense of humour, but their approach to English psyche, with most notable aspect the totally ludicrous SF story that connects the songs on this album, also indicates they might just be parodying that scene. Nothing wrong with that of course, but it tends to take the focus off the music.

Musically the band combines Hawkwind, because of the keyboard sounds, but Dr Spacetoad rocks less, Gong, and an improvising version of Doors. The latter because of the vocals and also because of the often present organ.

The first part of the album contains prototypical English space rock with lots of guitar and whistling and peeping keyboard sounds that are interjected at will. Very cosmic stuff and although the music sounds improvised rather than composed they do contain some very nice parts, most notably the first one, Introduction.

After this we get a change of mood, in the sense that Trajic Nature and also the long don't Push The River have a jazzy and subdued atmosphere with lots of piano. They both profit very much from the fact that Dr Spacetoad really uses his voice here.

Carnival Town is much like Doors, but they tend to fool around a little here without getting anywhere, while the rather straightforward Trying To Be Wierd is not very interesting.

The last two tracks of the album were surprising in that the penultimate one, My Old Mate The Coypu is almost like a sailors song and the closer Dance Of The Toad is actually a jig.

A track that is interesting for all you psych heads out there is the cover version of Set The Controls ... by PF. It's a nice cosmic rendition and a long one at that.

Conclusion

I'd say a varied but not consistently interesting album of English psych, but with a few grins added to it.
