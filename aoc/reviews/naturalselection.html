Summary

After the unreleased songs compilation An Older Land that lacked coherence, but that did contain also some of the best things they've ever done, this is their new full length effort.

The music

If average length of a CD would be a good measure for progressiveness and more importantly the quality of the CD, I wouldn't bother writing anything here. On the other hand however I like this new Lands End very much. As if An Older Land never happened the band continues the line of Terra Surranum with soaring ethereal guitars, introspective and somber vocals, and organ like (but not exactly) keyboards. The drum work is nice, being rather loose and at times even on the front (intro to Love Through the Winter and Blood in the Spring (pfui and all those titles are like that). Still some changes have been made (as can be seen from the following) so we're not at stand still here. I had been promised by some a CD that was to be in the same style as Terra Surranum, but better because of the fact that it was to be more coherent and, in fact, this happens to be true. On the whole I also find that there seem to be more emotional outburst on the album (most songs contain one) and in this way the album sounds a little less aloof and distant than its predecessor.

Going over the tracks: After the short instrumental intro, Strictly Speaking in Geographical Terms, we come to From the Ruins of a Fallen Empire, which is in fact a great track and of course very much in the bands own style, but that in the percussive intro reeks a bit of Marillion's Assassin. I've already said something of Love Through the Winter and Blood in the Spring, which is another track passing the ten minute mark and that enjoys becomes quite emotional after a percussive start, after which we get a dark interlude with a lot of humming sounds and which then moves into a wildly meandering typically progressive keyboard solo. Two layers of guitar then try to take over and we get a conversation going between them. We end with a quiet atmospheric part consisting of seemingly unrelated synth sounds. An Emptiness That's Never Filled... is with six minutes the second shortest song and is an acoustic ballad with a good vocal melody and a prominent bass. After a wailing guitar solo, the song ends like it started. In whisper mode. The song moves right into My Home the second but last track on the album. A great melody and build up on guitar with keyboards out to annoy in the back (I'm not saying they do, but they are well not inline wit the guitar). The song is about a trek made through Europe making america a different place. The song contains, surprisingly enough, some groovy guitar playing and on the whole is guitaroriented with solos that sound like jams rather than being composed. Then the pace picks up until it erupts into thunder and on a friendlier note percussion continues. The song ends majestically (although I have the impression that they don't go all the way yet).

The half our closer Natural Selection is actually a track in what seems to be seven parts of which one part is live improvised and sung in German. After a froggy intro and some spanish guitar evoking a nightly desert atmosphere, the somber monotonous vocals of McFarland and soft percussion dominate. The practice part that is done in German sounds rather menacing and disjoint and helps to build tension. Slowly structure evolves and and I would especially like to note the high energy outburst at the twenty minute mark that really stand out on this album.

Conclusion

What don't I like by these guys? Little it seems (I'm not familiar with their first one and some of their less easily found material). Seventyfour minutes of homemade progressive with soaring guitars, somber vocals, mellotron, meandering keyboards, just like on their previous one. But a few listens learn that the album as a whole is more succesful in the end than Terra Surranum and as such a step forward. I wonder where the next step will take us.
