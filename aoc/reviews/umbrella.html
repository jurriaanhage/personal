Summary

I recently visited the Pul in Uden for a prog festival and there was an Englisg guy there selling CDs and tapes, the latter containing collected UK prog material. I didn't really feel like buying, but he did mention that one of the bands on them, Vulgar Unicorn, was a great band. To my great surprise I got a CD from Roughtrade of that same band and am now in a position to see what they are worth.

The music

Well, any prog fan encountering this in the shops would buy it, based on the lengths of the songs alone (and look at the titles, such weirdness). This weirdness also becomes apparent in the detective/spy story with a twist that seems to be the subject of the first almost album length track. The story reminds me in a way of Foucault's Pendulum: a detective/spy story with a pretence. On the other hand, we might have a regular parabel or allegory here and I was just too stupid to notice. Names like Brecht, Boell and Bartok and Stravinmsky fly about, while a lawyer office in England is called Wright, Goodfellow & Fairman and their Russian counterparts are called Ripemow, Stelin and Turiski (pronounce these names and you'll see what I mean). The lyrics are a jumble of quotations and names that should be leading somewhere I guess and I would be interested to know what. Maybe they are just pulling our legs, but it does have a certian Cold War ring to it. If you buy the CD and find out, please e-mail me about it.

Musically it's progressive material to be sure. The long tracks already indicate it, but it's confirmed at first listen. There is of course a large variation acorss the songs, but not too much, meaning that a guitar riff can be held on for a minute or so. The keyboards are not too important on this album, but there's not really an instrument that is present a lot, although there's relatively a lot of acoustic guitar and more trumpet and saxophone than you would expect. The latter do give a jazzy feel to some part of the songs. References to the music are a bit of Marillion (some not just like, but are), but less song oriented than them, more directed towards atmosphere.

The atmosphere is usually quite relaxed, the durmming being non-obtrusive, but not too standard as well, the vocals are subdued and very possibly used to give the tracks an anchor, something for the listener to hold on to. Although the vocals aren't very important they do in this way have an important task: making the music listenable.

Still, I do think that some people may find this music too longwinded and boring, but I guess that that also depends on the mood you're in. Sometimes you might break it off after some 10 minutes, because it doesn't seem to lead anywhere and at another time, you just eddy along on the current on the music and I guess the latter holds for me, because although the album doesn't contain a lot of highpoints (the guitar riff in the 3rd part being a notable exception) it is pleasant to listen to, a bit like Tangerine Dream and Klaus Schulze are pleasant to listen to: to meander along with the melodies. I do think that most of this music has been written by just jamming along and see what we come up with (I mean, you don't really plan a drum solo, do you? Even if it is a small one). A good help they have from the wind instruments and the violin that give just that little extra flavour to make it interesting. I do wonder how they will handle this live.

The second song on this album starts out slowly and quietly, after a while the tempo picks up with a very good vocal line (in this part I also here a modulation in the volume on the CD, an error I think). The sound is quite rocky now, with the guitar screaming, while the keyboards start to play along as well. After a while the music dies down a bit, and the moods becomes a bit Floydian, only to pick up again entering a jam of some sorts.

If you're looking for a reference than I think it would be hard to give you one, but I'll try to give you an idea. The only definite thing I can say is that they sound very English. I will not put the neo-prog label on this thing, because I always refer to neo-prog as being similar to Saga and Rush. Because of its atmospheric content, I'd rather say Floyd, with which they also share the fact that they like to introduce wind instruments although for instance in the last song, they do let it scream once in a while, moving more into the realm of band like Jadis or maybe Hawkwind because of the jamming nature of the music.

At the end of the song the train stops and it's the end of the line. But not entirely, because...

The clever people among you might have noticed that this CD contains more minutes of music than the timing of the songs may implicate. This is indeed the case, as there's more music in the last track, but this can only be heard after silence (John Cage being another reference here) and I ask myself if it is accidental that Grey Lady Down have done the same thing?

After three and a half minute of quietness, we get another 2 minute jam, with a few Hawkwind influences.

Conclusion

A pleasant album, mostly instrumental with obscure lyrics to hold on to. Within each track enough moods and variation to keep the attention, although there's little fire to be had, although more to the end of the album you get more the feeling that they are just jamming along, than playing there songs from sheet. This is also where the Hawkwind influence is to be found, while most of the album is in the Floyd vein. I even heard some faint Marillion echoes BTW, usually riffs that were vaguely familiar.

It is hard to say what parts are best, because of the well, eveness of the music. I think I liked the second track better then the first one, because the strucutre was more apparent. This is music that you have to get to know well to enjoy, but there's a chance you will.

As always I'm always interested to find out what they can do in the short song format.
