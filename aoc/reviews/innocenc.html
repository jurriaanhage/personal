The music

This album starts off quietly, but after some time the guitar takes over, screaming. This is really a song. The vocalist has a good voice, with a throat like sandpaper, but pleasant. The song is quite on the heavy side.

Forever is a ballad, in the Janus vein. Flowing melody and vocals with cello and violin. Very romantic, but good as well.

Gravedigger II owes some to the orginal Gravedigger of 20+ minutes. Some melodies have been taken over wihtout modification, but when they are good, it's no use whining about it. The credits of this song go to all Janus members from way back, the ones who all composed the original version. So it might really be a rehashing of the old song, a little shorter and they rerecorded it, so it isn't just a new mix.

Ovine (one for the...) has a classical start and flows by gently with some guitar and all. At six minutes the music stops (it often does this in Janus songs, making you think there's more than one song involved. I am not sure whether this is a bad thing) after a small but noticeable pause it starts again on a different track this time, with more guitarwork. At the end the music moves back a bit and we here sheep, yes sheep bleating. So that's where the titles from.

Track 5 is back to the heavier side of Janus, but it never goes over the top. The song never really gets underway.

Dark Christmas a ballad and it somehow reminds me of something, but can't seem to figure out what. It is quiet, but has a threatening undertone. Very interesting and well done. the last part of the song, well I don't know, is a bit like those American songs from the 50's with vocal harmonies. Strange combination. I would have hoped that the more menacing part of this had been worked out a bit more and the last part maybe have been left off. I wonder what would have come of it.

Sacre Blues is what you might expect a French variation on the white Blues, right from under the Eiffeltower, or better yet directly from Montmartre. A bit of organ on the side makes it sound even more early seventies. Derek and the Dominoes, Ten Years After and Groundhogs time I'd say.

The intro to track 8 is promising, but it turns out to be plain hard rock. Pity.

Harvester is a bit in the Gravedigger II vein. Very good melodies, with cello, piano and all things combined. At four minutes we get an abrupt change, the bass becoming dominant.

Didn't Whitney Housten have a song called Hero, or was it Mariah Carey. Might be the same song, played like Clapton would do it. I'm not overly fond this song, to say the least.

Conclusion

This album was meant for people who like Gravedigger. Gravedigger had lots of guitar, although the sound of Janus ranges from seering guitars to acoustic rock (of which the might be the inventors, listen to the song Gravedigger) to orchestral pieces with lots of melody and strings (no horns, but usually cello and violin).

Rather an inconsistent album with some weak spots on it, most notably 7, 8 and 10. They just do not fit into it. Really different form Free Fall, which was World and New Age combined with some added, while this is more rock in it's more elaborate form.

The songs are quite alright, but do not approach the sincerity, majesty or completeness of Agnus Dei. The songs sometime undergo abrupt changes, leaving the listener a bit wondering where the hell it went. The songs do seem to sound a little fragmented at times, because of this. Best tracks are 1, 2, 3 and 6 (the last part left of) with 4 and 9 as runners up, having the potential, but not using it all.
