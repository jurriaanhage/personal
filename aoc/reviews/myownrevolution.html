Summary

The album I have on the Brennus label are generally rereleases of French hard rock and not very interesting. It seems however they do have some new material from new bands as well, this being one of them. The promotion for this album was done by Omega in Germany.

The music

The album opens with Intro. After some keyboards, the drums start a a military anthem and some powerful chords are struck on guitar. Notwithstanding the fact that this is an intro, the singer does sing on this rather epic sounding piece. I'm not oo fond of his vocals since they are of the very high type (almost like Geddy Lee's). If you know me, you know that I have something against this kind of vocals, and I like the music generally less for it. Most metal/progmetal lovers do not have this problem so they should just disregard my complaints. With My Last Wish the tempo is upped considerably and the boys use backing vocals, but its more shouting than singing. Plenty of breaks in this one. During the classically oriented guitar solo the drummer puts his kit to good use. During the vocal parts he tends to hack away quite monotonously and audibly. As to being progressive I think the bio is right in introducing also the name of Helloween to show that this is in some sense more "metallic" than "progressive". However, Helloween is also a band that is generally of interest to progmetal lovers. Never More is the longest track on the album with 8 minutes. Again, the music is quite loud and abundant with quite a nice opening. The drumming plods on a bit, but melodically this song is quite alright, as was the previous song. Paying a little more attention to pronounciation would not be a bad idea (the way voices is pronounced) and this applies rather generally. However it is not really disturbing. The oh-oh at the ending I could have done without. During the intro to the title track I get the impression that a number of fragments were mixed together since this is really an inaccessible passage. Lots of breaks and changes in 15 seconds. I'm not sure what is wrong here with the drumming. Maybe I'm missing a good groove here or possibly I just don't like the prominence of the beat being whacked almost continously and I would have put it further back in the mix. Lights And Dreams is a rather poppy sounding track. The guitar work is as always quite powerful and there's even quite a sensitive part. If I have to choose my favourite instrumentalist here, I would choose the lead guitarist, of the keyboards I heard quite little after the intro. Of course the band the following track contains keyboards. Again, the drums sound the same all over, but I am getting used to the vocals and the melodicity of the music stays throughout. The chanting backing vocals are put to good use here and the long guitar lines are very nice. Fallen Angels is also a good, driven track. Maybe a bit metallic for some. This is mostly because of the chanted backing vocals, they sound a bit too much as if sung by soccer fans. Bad Memories starts out as a piano ballad. The French accent is more apparent here. Later on the song becomes more emotional and the guitar adds some power and the drums stay subtle. Of course the next tracks is energetic enough. This is quite an accessible track, although the guys put in plenty of variation to make it seem complicated. It is in fact quite catchy, but does not really bring anything new. The closer opens as a track with some atmosphere. One might think of Queensryche during the intro. The backing vocals sound quite angry and in a way this song has a somewhat more epic quality than the previous tracks. The chorus however is again quite catchy.

Conclusion

This is not something that I can recommend outright. The positive points are the songs that are certainly not bad, the vocals melodies and the often good lead guitar playing. Negative points are the accented vocals (but maybe you are used to those), the stadium metal backing vocals and in large part the drumming. The music is not simple and hence there IS plenty of variation. However, I cannot get into the drumming and I have the impression that this plodding, flat sounding pounce is always there and maybe it's only that it is too audible. However it does tend to irritate me after a while. With me remains the impression that this is just metal, ordinary metal but made a bit more complicated by the breaks and the like. Is this the essence of prog metal? My idea is that in progmetal there should be more depth to the songs, more a building up and a breaking down, playing with atmospheres and trying something new. Those things I tend to miss here.
