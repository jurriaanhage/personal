Artist: Third Voice
Title: Reflections
Label: self produced
Length(s): 45 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1998
Month of review: 05/1999

Summary

A self produced disc from the States. It is striking that so many solo produced discs from the States have such extensive biographical material included with the CD. I like this and I would hope the European bands take to this as well.

The music

The album opens with the titletrack. The music opens rather friendly and the surprise comes when the guitars set in. If you think of the fact that there are two people playing on this album, you do not expect something akin to progmetal. The music however is not run-on-the-mill progmetal, is more songdirected and owes a lot to Enchant. Unfortunately, the drummer is a tin one. A live drummer might have given a more organic sound to the music. The next track is also available on the second DURP sampler. A good track with a great melody on the piano and rhythmically playful. Jeff Kearney shows that he can sing with soul. Again I'm reminded of Enchant. However, this is only the first album of Third Voice and in production and sound quality the music on this album the band still has some way to go. We continue with Mission Of Time in a sense rather similar to the previous one. The drums are military now, but the piano is still there. The vocals are now more typical of progmetal. I like the vocal melody, but to my feeling the music is sometimes a little too fiddly here. As if the composer tried to put too much into it. The guitar is the foremost instrument (or do vocals also count), but the keyboards lend a nice flavour to the music. What We'll Be opens as a rock song. It features double bass drums, screaming backing vocals (very nice in the chorus) and heavy rhythm guitars. Melodically it is not so interesting and I'm also not fond of the lyrics. Mystery is a bit of weak one as well, a bit too mellow for my tastes. Never Know is a ballad, not very appealling I'm afraid. It's a bit too long and the melody is not particularly striking. In the rather longish Innocence an epic quality creeps into the music. Again the piano in the intro and then the song bursts loose. The nice vocal melodies are back, but the song is a bit repetitive. The album closes with the aptly titled How It Ends. Although I like the song in outset, there's something here that was also present in some of the previous songs and that is that it does not really flow well. My feeling is that this will dissolve with the use of a live drummer, the mechanical drums sometimes sound too much as an added layer and not part of the song.

Conclusion

After a good beginning (the first two tracks) and an okay trakc, the third, this album sank in a bit and the three songs in the middle were not really to my liking and on the whole a bit weak. With Innocence the quality rose again, but this already goes to show that this duo isn't there yet. They do something of an own style (although they do remind me a lot of Enchant in vocal sound and style) with the piano and the heavy guitars together and the vocalist expresses himself well. Room for improvement in the drum section (please add a live drummer here) and it might be overall a good idea to extend the duo into a more complete band also with the view of live performance. I'm sure time will take care of an increase in recording/production quality. Notwithstanding my negative remarks I'd like to stress that this is a decent effort and growing maturity will surely give rise to some interesting music. I hope they will hold on to their focus on the song format (which btw they share with Enchant).
