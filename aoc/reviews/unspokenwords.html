Summary

The music

The Aurora Project make a firm type of progressive. Their guitars tend to be on the heavy side, bordering on prog metal, as do other sounds. Still, I wouldn't quite dub them prog metal. They also have some of the riffy approach bands like Rush display, apart from their symphonic side. This results in the sort of blend you hear more often from Dutch bands, such as Cliffhanger or Marathon in the past, and Novact nowadays, the latter being closest stylistically. The Betrayal uses this sort of approach. The Untold Prophecy is more melodic, almost fully dropping the prog metal idiom, only pulling away from ballad-hood near the end. The Gathering, on the other hand, has some pretty heavy bits. Nocturnal Lament has an instrumental break that builds towards a climax in a rather nice way.

One of the interesting features is the melodic development in the band's compositions: even in vocal sections we regularly hear melodies on especially the guitar. This adds warmth and depth that is far from usual. Having said that, some of the vocal section lack strength, which is particularly striking because they contrast with the many that dó have that sort of strength.

...Unspoken Words is based on a concept by Gooys inspired by the dictum "I feel, therefore I am", resulting in a science fiction type story. Apart from the fact that this can be found in the album's lyrical themes, we also see it reflected in the song structure, including short narratives (for instance parts of the Unspoken Words tracks) and shorter intermediate sections. If these were brief sections only, I wouldn't have minded them much, however System Log is almost only narrative telling a section of the story focusing on the Gnarus Unit. No, I don't know what it is either. This has something of an arresting effect on the musical flow, no matter how well done. These narrative bits have background music that lies somewhere between EM and esotherics.

Conclusion

The sort of professionalism and unity displayed on this album belies the fact that is only the band's debut. Maybe the fact that they have worked several years on it helped. Generally speaking the balance between story elements and music is very good, better than normally seen with rock operas, but the System Log track is a blight. Skipping this solves the problem (although this might hurt the story). The result is a very strong concept album.
