Summary

A concept album about nuclear arms and such inspired it by Revelation, Chapter 9.

The music

The album opens bouncily with Isotope 239, where the meandering clarinet of Schultz is the main solo instrument. Later on the guitar also enters into the picture. The drumming is rather flat, being automatic, the soundspectrum is rather full and in some way noisy, probably because of the many cymbals. There is some variation in here, but it tends to go and on. Everything We Know is quite a long track, over eight minutes, and more driven than the previous one. Again the clarinet takes the melodic lead, but the vocals of Ray this time also figure in the music. Like I stated in my othger reviews, his vocals are a bit hoarse. However his vocals are not on top of the mix so it is possible to focus more on the music. Remember though that production is not high quality so expect overall a flattish sound. The music can not be considered prog, I am thinking more of rock with more variation than usual and in particular many meandering solo parts. This track has some variation in that some keyboards give the instrumental intermezzo a kind of spooky atmosphere, notwithstanding the thrilly clarinet that continues to play and play. Later on we get a kind of question and answer session between the clarinet and the guitar. Quite a nice track. This does not hold for the bouncy easy-going follow The Einstien Blues. One More War is better, in the typical Rick Ray riffing style with the focus on guitar. Power In The Empire continues this line, but with reversed cymbal sound and it seems a somewhat clearer sound and also more telling features such as the menacing guitar lines and pronounced bass playing in the instrumental middle part. The vocals sound quite far away. The title track is a long one, having a rather fast paced first part with some nice guitar but unobtrusive guitar work in there and some nervous clarinet solo's to boot. The nice guitar work takes on different forms: the long drawn notes on electric guitar and the melodies on the acoustic. This title definitely has more of an epic quality with quite good buildup and although there's plenty of soloing, there are some parts where structure and melody are more important, giving one a handhold for the composition. This is also one of those track where the poor production wreaks itself, because although the song has plenty of parts that really rock, the production makes sure it doesn't do so fully. I don't mind the vocals of Ray here: they are subdued enough and only help to latch on to the music. Around the ten minute mark the music even gets a bit orchestral after which the bouncy rock sets in again. The melodic vocal part reminds me a bit of Floyd. The song ends with nuclear explosions. Fortunately the Violent Gentlemen sign peace treaties later on (okay, maybe a bit too late). Some weird vocals (the gentlemen I presume) in this track in addition to Ray's own. The guitar solo is quite extensive and there are some rather loopy things in here. Rip Snorter is a short one and the short but varied The World's Requiem closes the album. A rather discordant piece this ending in a quick finger yet melodic guitar solo.

Conclusion

A few nice tracks, quite a few weak ones, but the nice ones are by ar the longer ones on the album. These tracks are the ones where most variation is encountered and it is not only variation of "we must vary" kind. These longer tracks seem to have more interesting melodies, being less song structured and also have some atmosphere and tension building features.
