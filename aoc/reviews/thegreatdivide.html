Summary

Unknown band on well known label.

The music

The album opens with fervour in Perpetual Child. The verses are quite subtly sung on this track, the chorus on the other hand is quite forceful, however in both cases, the vocal melody is good. The musical intermezzo is very energetic and might remind some of Cairo (without the ELP influences). A great sweeping track. Sleepwalker is a more song directed, up-tempo piece. The guitar is quite prominent here, and the vocals sound slightly swollen. This is not meant in anyway deragotory, in fact I like these slightly operatic vocals (as long as the vocal melodies are good). Then we come to quieter waters, although the fast pace i kept up. Join alternates between full-blown bombasm and more atmospheric passages. Again a good vocal melody and great singing in all and even a church organ in the middle for good measure. The song is quite accessible for the most part and the vocals remind me of Dennis Deyoung when he puts a little more force into his voice. Epic stuff. On Spare Chicken Parts the bandmembers are allowed to have their go without having to align themselves with any prespecified song pattern. The song opens in Arabic style. At the end the musicians disregard all speed limits as they go full speed ahead towards the march like ending. After this selfindulgent excursion we return to the vocal tracks with a funky guitar and a not so interesting vocal melody. Towards the end it becomes better, but I think these more straightforward tracks are not their strong point. Fortunately it becomes better with the heavy The Bottom Line. The second epic of the track is named after the band, Ice Age. The song opens frostily, but later on drifts into more known waters and at the song becomes positively bombastic and anthemic at the end. One Look Away is a piano ballad with a few louder passages. Not the best of ballads but better than Because Of You. In Miles To Go we first hear the spirit of Hendrix passing by, but then we are back on track with flashy progmetal. For a while only though since this song features a bouncy interlude on keyboards after which we return to the full blown progmetal of before. The vocals are quite aggresive. To SDay Goodbye is split up into two parts. The first of these is Worthless Words, which is in instrumental, at first quite rowdy, but later tastefully pianic. The piano continues into On Our Way, but here also acoustic guitar is added to the tasteful melange. Of course, having said this, the music is sure to erupt into bomabstic progmetal. In all its diversity, this track has something of the progmetal equivalent of a musical song. By far the weakest of the three epics.

I found that the music comes out much better using headphones. A strange thing for progmetal.

Conclusion

Although I have tried to avoid mentioning their name, this band should be interesting for DT fans in the first place. This is great sounding, well executed, but also well-penned progressive metal with a tendency to the bombastic. The vocalist is very good, can be both aggressive as well as subtle and the music is generally good. The band comes out a bit less favourably in the balladic parts however. Especially in view of the length of this album, a very satisfying debut and something that fans of DT and Cairo will certainly want to hear.
