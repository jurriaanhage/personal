Summary

If one should believe the bio coming with this album, then this Californian band must be the band of the coming decade. Let's just check to make sure.

The music

The album opens with This Golden Voyage. The song has many references to Yes, especially in the guitar sound (both electric and acoustic), but also in the compositional style. The vocals are quite different though (yet the style is sometimes similar), which takes away much of the similarity between the two bands. The music is quite complex with many breaks and somewhat chaotically combined intermezzo's, but also enough attention to melody. Actually, the music is quite tuneful, not really build around a theme, but more tunes that are pasted and concatenated together into a rather fragmentary whole. The fragmentariness is strangely enough not disturbing, a bit in the way that also Spocks Beard has, but less slick. Tracer begins well with repetitive guitar runs, melodious Yeaheah... and easy going drumming. During the vocal part the bass is very pronounced and the chorus mirrors the intro. The ethereal guitar solo is also a good one, and after the nonobvious break into a piano passage we return to the verse and then back to the "chorus", that because of the harmony vocals sounds grand. The drumming is really loose on this piece and tends to give the impression there's "not much going on". A weird track, but it works. Seeing Eye God opens funkily and has a strong rhythm guitar backdrop. I'm strongly reminded of King's X here. Again a large role for the bass, this seems a characteristic of this band (and also of course of Yes). The difference for me between Yes lies not only in the vocals, but also the more modern sound that leaves room for more modern rhythms, heavier guitars, but there's also something that makes this band American, something a band such as Kansas also has. Seeying Eye God as a whole is not as interesting as the previous two tracks, possibly because of the lesser melodiousness. The bass still rumbles on the follow-up Garden Of Autumn. Dazzling brassy keyboards on this one and a good chorus as well. Again, many things are going on, maybe a bit too many. In the bridge I'm reminded of Kansas. All the references mentioned so far come back in the partly catchy Perfect World, mainly the Yes reference and some of the harmony vocals remind of Gentle Giant. Reflections opens reflectively with keyboards that sound as they are in fact rays of light reflected by water. For a relatively short track quite a lot of (doubled) vocals. Again the drumming is quite loose and the bass is pounding notwithstanding the fact that this a rather sober track. Breakwater opens with bagpipes, is longer than the previous track, but has far fewer vocals. Military drums continue, and we are in for a very Scottish track it seems. After the short vocal part it is time for some freaky soloing on organ and guitar, but the jazzy/pianic vocal part at the end is quite catchy again. The Time Being is the longest track on the album. Again lots of things are happening, especially on the guitar front and the vocals are quite loud on this one. There's also some space for keyboards soloing, but it seems that this band wants to put as much as they can into a single composition being able to contrast heavy, distorted vocal parts with an almost folky intermezzo and continuing within a few bars with a high pitched guitar solo. The intermezzo is vocally quite naked, with only piano to accompany the emotive vocals. A highpoint on this album. Dream Catcher is a more rocky track with plenty of harmony vocals. Again the chorus is quite catchy. There is plenty of organ. A powerful track with some nice atmospherics in the middle with sampled vocals. The music then takes a rest coming to an almost unproduced vocal part with just acoustic guitars and the track finalizes with an emotional guitar solo. The album closes with From Henceforth Now And Forever (PS124). This is a rather quiet closer with mostly keyboards and bubbly, echoey bass/guitar. The lyrics are a bit on the vague/mystic side, but that happens more often on this disc. In that sense also Yes/Jon Anderson is not far off. The album closes down with loud thunder and lightning.

Conclusion

A band often coming close to Yes, sometimes Kansas, but that also has some connections musically with a band such as Spocks Beard, probably because of the many interludes and intermezzo's. I do feel Spocks Beard has a more commercial, slicker sound, while this band tends to be a bit more chaotic and has less "mannerisms" in their compositions. But then, this is only their first album. I hve to say that this band really had to grow on me. The first few listens I wondered what everybody was giving up about, but these guys do have talent and I expect a bright future for them. I certainly expect them to do even better than this.
