Summary

I already knew their self titled album from 1997, and only know do I hear a second sign of life from this American instrumental trio, and rather surprisingly on the Dutch Mascot label.

The music

Creepy Spider opens spookily, schleppend and a slow guitar. Then the bands starts to rock with, as is usual these, a rather rough guitar sound. The overall feel is psychedelic with strongly present percussion alongside the drums. This fickle instrumental rock can be best compared with say Attention Deficit, although the band shows to be more jazzrock oriented later on. The melodies on Jam For Jason (for Jason Becker, the guitarist) are much more pronounced and likable. This song also has soundscapes and some distinctive effects. In the more straightforward parts the bass is very much present, the guitar I found at times to be a bit too fiddly. However, the "crying" guitar solo later is really quite good, as the band shifts into Latin gear with Santana and Spanish guitar. A long and varied track.

Road To Tanzania/Tribal Trance is a two parted track. The first part is for the most part moody jazzrock subtly recorded and relaxed. The production shows itself to be very clear here. The second part is percussion and soundscapes. With Winter In Innisfail we encounter some bouncier playing. Maybe a bit too straightforward at first, but later on the music gains in power and momentum with drums ánd percussion. The rhythmic side is in order, but melodically the music falls short.

In Loving Memory not surprisingly the music is not too happy or rocky. In fact, on this romantic track the music is quite sad with a sobbing guitar. Later the rock gains influence, but still not much.

Creepy Spider Part II is really quite nice with the drums coming on slowly and the overall claustrophobic feel to the guitar. The drums are varied on this one. The echoey Sea Broth features a Crimsonesque guitar but more in a jazzrock vein and always a bit distant. Godzilla's Revenge is only a short track with raw keyboards.

With Cauldron Of Evil the darkness creeps back into the music. Although the music is not that intense, the vocal samples, the harsh wah-wah guitar make it all not too pleasant for your ears, but in a pleasant way. Hendrix is the main influence on Chinese Eyes while Tribal Trance is reprised and at first vague, but later really swinging.

Conclusion

Not bad, not bad, if you are into jazzrock with a more progressive tinge (i.e., the Tone Center releases are a bit too jazzy) and bands like Attention Deficit did catch your attention, then this might be something for you. I can appreciate Attention Deficit a bit better, but hey, the threesome is doing alright: they have variation, they can play. Only wish they would have a bit more melody and a little less fiddling around.
