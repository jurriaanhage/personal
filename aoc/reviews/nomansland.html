Summary

Well aftre many a solo album and also many a live album, Wetton came back with what seems yet another live album. Things however will turn out a bit differently.

The music

After a short intro on acoustic guitar and the introduction of the band the first track continues with The Last hing On My Mind. One of the slickest pieces on the album, it has a very nice touch in the chorus, where the vocals of Wetton sound "not-so-slick' in the Ohooooooh-Wraw. Also the metallic guitar playing of newcomer Kilminster grows a few more rough edges on this song. Sole Survivor as most of you know is an Asia track and one of the more well known ones. Playful bassplaying in this optimistic sounding piece with Christy hacking away and again Kilminster playing in a somewhat metal style. The music does not necessarily breathe a live atmosphere, but the energy, the spark is surely here with Kilminster soloing if his life depends on it. Taking some gas back with Book Of Saturdays is a good idea. Acoustic guitar and Orfords flute playing make this into a quiet faeric piece. Another quiet piece on acoustic guitar is Emma. A bit of a mellow track, but the vocal melody is certainly not bad. And if limited to just one or two of these songs, it is no problem for the album. A number of these songs would quickly be too much. Tatras is an Orford piece. Especially in the beginning I'm much reminded of My Baby Treats Me Right Cos I'm The Hard Loving Man All Night Long on the first IQ album. This piece is a bit longer and less varied and I guess I still prefer My Baby, but this is a good piece nonetheless bringing us to the end of the quiet interlude. Lots of piano here (although the booklet doesn't imply this might be a piano). Now we rock right into a hardcore version of In The Dead Of Night of the first UK album. The guitar solo against a backdrop of keyboards, sounds a bit coldish, alone and I guess this is just the spirit for the song. Bygosh!! features acoustic guitar, soft drumming and lush keyboards. This seems to be an improvised affair that moves right into Easy Money (here Kilminster varies considerably). The freewheeling interlude is quite an adventure with the solo guitar setting in after a while and ending in a somewhat rock/reggae style finale. Another classic then: UK's Rendez-Vous 6:02. Quickfingered Orford on the keyboards and backing vocals and Christey punctuating with percussion. The Birth Of Igor is an instrumental piece on acoustic guitar and keyboards. After All is a Wetton/John Young song. This is one of the more recent tracks and can't be called progressive. This is plain pop music. Of course, this doesn't mean it can't be good. However, it seems to me the weakest track on the album, althought the bombastic afterbirth does a lot to compensate for this. The 10 minute rendition of Starless is surely the highpoint on this album. The playing of Kilmninster is phenomenal on this track, with a strong reference to the darkest of blues and the tense build-up with also Christey playing a terrific leading role and Wetton pumping on the bass, finally erupting into gutsy chaotic guitar solo. What Would Robert Have Said? I wonder. The Night Watch closes down the album, in its Spanish fashion and riddled with acoustic guitar.

Conclusion

The inclusion of a guitarist such as Kilminster seems to me a gamble of sorts if you happened to know how most of the recent Wetton material sounds. However he and the others do bring it off in a fresh, adventurous sounding long live album with a perfect sound and an energetic performance. Well worth your attention.
