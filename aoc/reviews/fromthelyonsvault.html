Summary

Not for sale as far as I know, this album is a sampler given along with the French magazine Acid Dragon featuring a number of bands from the 'environs' of Lyon.

The music

The sampler opens with over 21 minutes worth of Cresudi. The first of the two tracks, Potion, opens with piano going into acoustic guitar. The song takes some getting under way and only after over five or so minutes does there come any fire into the music. After 6 and half minutes again it seems we come to a new song altogether and here are finally the echoes of Pulsar present with some dark keyboards. Afterwards we return to the piano and acoustic guitar after which comes a very compelling part with long lush keyboards and later a nice Arabic part on guitar. Notwithstanding some nice themes this track goes under due to a lack of direction. The next track Ombrage stays under ten minutes. It opens with acoustic guitar in a rather somber mood. I'm reminded here not of any prog, but more of people like Ralph Towner. Then however, the bass and electric guitar set in and the songs becomes all the more menacing (instead of plain dark). In the middle the band starts to fiddle around again, but the song ends fairly well. Track three, October, is for Indelspleen which has in the meantime transformed itself into Silver Lining. The song opens with violins and is typical progressive one might say: keyboard solo's, lots of variation with lush, melodic sweeps of guitar, but also parts in which the guitar is more prodding, more driving. The military part in the middle of the track reminds me of the Dutch band Earth & Fire. After some more nonobvious choices, the song seems to get back on track after six and a half minutes or so with a strong build-up with keyboards and heavy guitars. Here however they turn out to be a little too greedy, pumping up the volume a bit too quickly. Still they manage to maintain it to the end, so there's no real harm done. The next one is a solo track, I guess it is solo, by one of the members of Indelspleen, Pascal Indelicato. Although it sounds in some way rather dated it is more concise and less arbitrary than many of the other tracks on this disc. Besides being rather full of all kinds of keyboards it features also some medieval folk. A dreamy track. The next one up is by Gandharva. The music of this band harkens back to old times quite a lot. The old organ sound is mainly responsible for this, but the recording quality as a whole is not great and the singing is quite unsteady. The composition is not bad in itself with some very definite sixties Floydian influences and the full-blown ending is worth its while. The last and longest track is for Marco Dolciami, a member of Gandharva. The song, The Shades Of Night, is very Genesis like with that typical kind of acoustic guitar (and electric guitar). The atmosphere of old-Genesis is also very much evoked and the word carbon-copy comes to mind. Marco's voice is not great, but that is not much of a problem here. The electric guitar is more blues rock than Hackett's (yet you might be reminded slightly of the Musical Box). The song has its moments and the composition is quite okay in my opinion. It lacks in the rendition however.

Conclusion

A charming little thing and hopefully will make these bands somewhat more known than they are (I had never heard of any of them). The music, the playing, the production, it shows bands in development. Problems are as often with the singing, and that seems to me a harder problem, yet production should help also there.
