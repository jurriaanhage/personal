Summary

Like A. Molotkov, Pamela Zero is a member of Discord Aggregate. This is her solo album with songs written between 1997 and 2005. She does everything on this album, but as we shall see

The music

Endless is the opening track, and provides a good idea of what we may expect later: Pamela's voice is the only ingredient here, so we have an acapella record on our hand. She does not sing in a single voice, although there is a definite first voice. But in the back, we also hear her voice reverberating in a somewhat eerie way. It all sounds very melancholic and spiritual too. On Mercy, we move to more folky territory. If you really need a reference, I guess one can hear echoes of Tori Amos in there somewhere, but still it does not come close. It is a fact however that Pamela visits mainly the higher vocal regions. I do like Mercy more than the opener, there is more melody. She continues the ascending line in Physics in which some of the backing sounds are used a instruments, a bit like Only You by The Flying Pickets (remember them?). The leading melody is very good this time. Interesting that Pamela has this many styles with such a limited palette. Velvet for instance moves to the vocal blues, sung in smokey bars.

In Entrance there are no lyrics, just hey-hey-hey's and variations thereof. Again, this gives a rather spiritual feel, with the vocals coming close to what I would call 'Indian' (as in the original inhabitors of the U.S.). The vocal wailings take a long time, this time around. The Journey Home is back to folkiness, while The Yearning is a fragile piece with heavy breathing in the back. Pamela takes the idea of wailing rather serious on this track. Folk is well suited for acapella singing, which is probably why Gallica is the third such track. A friendly dancing melody dominates it.

Living Backwards is one of the longer tracks. Pamela is going tic-toc in the back, to signify the passage of time. The main vocal line lies more on top of the music, freer of interference. The arrangement is more complex here, with various Pamela's singing similar lines starting at different moments in time. The short closer is Present Tense (hey, I wasn't planning to consider song by song this time), the vocals are solo this time.

Conclusion

This a-capella album is not for everyone. However, the music itself is not even that inaccessible. Endless was, the opener, but later songs reveal more melody and good ones too. The styles vary, if you take the time to listen closely, making this quite an original and sustaining listening experience. Not something I'd like to do every day, but still...
