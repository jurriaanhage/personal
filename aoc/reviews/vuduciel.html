Jean-Pascal Boffo - Vu Du Ciel

Artist: Jean-Pascal Boffo
Title: Vu Du Ciel
Label: Musea FGBG4244AR
Length(s): 20 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1998

Summary

I almost never get anything from Musea to review, only when I ask. This time, surprisingly, something fell into my mailbox and more surprisingly it was a CD single, probably to promote Boffo's new album of the same title. Although I have his first three albums, I have not followed him since and have to be honest in saying that I'm not fond of all he has done.

The music

The very first track is the single. Soft music mostly acoustic guitar can be either something that sounds like New Age are be something appropriate for a quality label like ECM (Towner for instance). Although the song seems to progress somewhat slowly, the percussion is two sided: very quick and light percussion and an occasional rumble on the drums. The melody of this track is not very obvious and one has to play this song a few times to hear it. At first you will find it boring, but there's a chance that this will change. The TyPe Man reminds me of Bruford latest attempt (If Summer Had Its Ghost), slightly jazzy, pronounced bass and on the whole a bit boring and unsatisfying. A bit too frolic. Parfum D'Etoiles is a very melodic track with vocal meanderings. The guitar is electric here. A nice track, a bit in the Oldfieldian style, but the vocals are certainly different. Adios not surprisingly has a somewhat Spanish sound and the name is also very good to close down this single with (is it also the last track on the album). The song is the longest of all of them. The percussion is rather lazy and sparse, with the focus on the acoustic guitars and the electric guitar that sets in after a while. A well-dosed melodic and emotional solo. The female vocals are also present on this track. Plenty of variation in the guitar playing here, although the mood of the song does not change. One way to describe this song: pastoral (okay, the vocals get a bit too loud toward the end to warrant this).

Conclusion

All in all a pastoral single that at first sounded boring to me. One might not call it progressive, but after a few listens the craftmanship of Boffo shows and especially the closer Adios with many melodies is a very nice track. The TyPe Man however falls short. No rock on this album, but it does not fall short of melody and emotion. Last remark: if this single is to hope for air play, that might have taken catchier music than this.
