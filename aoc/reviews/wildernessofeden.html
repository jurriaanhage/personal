Summary

World Turtle came from Haze, a neo-hippy progressive band that gigged in England in the eighties (mostly) and recorded numerous tapes and two albums.

The music

The opening track of the album starts out as a plain rock track, but fortunately it has a nice bridge and subsequent guitar solo, and they manage to save something of the track. The next track suffers from the same problem: the music is somewhat progressive with some of the ingredients, but much of the music is plain melodic rock. Paul McMahon really shows the back of his tongue on this track, which by the way also has some bluesy undercurrents with heavy guitars and organ accompaniment. The Wilderness Of Eden is a peaceful track with percussion and jungle sounds. From the lyrics of this track as well as many of the other tracks speaks a kind of critical view on the world. It moves into the sad Oldfieldian ballad The Last Unicorn.

Kickback is back to the plain rock approach. Very energetic and featuring tenor sax. Not bad, although I don't like the chorus. Queen's Gambit contrasts very well being a small piano Stalemate is soft ballad with wailing violin and not very interesting. The next song Vertigo reminds me of a song by Frank Boeijen Groep called Ik Mis Je. Being a great track I admit the mysterious opening of Vertigo as well. After this we get a more typically progressive part with loud basswork (a bit too) keyboard and guitar playing together and prominent percussion. The middle part is a little bouncy with lots of horns (Sussudio!) and the basswork stays too loud making fiddling with the equalizer necessary. After another bridging instrumental (a scary guitaristic one) we get to Rip Van Winkle, which is a rather straightforward rock song with horns. Wolf about the gulf war starts off very well with church organ and machine gun drums and guitar afterwards. Very heavy track, but not very melodic. Wave is an acoustic singalong with violin and a bit too mellow for my tastes. Human is sung from what seems to be a cellar. The song is ostentatiously a love ballad and rather mellow like the previous one. It is comparable to an Asia ballad with orchestration. Closer is One Day that opens as an easy going pop song. The uplifting chorus is quite nice, though and the build up in the song is well done as well.

With regards to sound quality, I got quite an amount of noise out of my loudspeakers. Not too disturbing, but it's audible.

Conclusion

The album is not a bad one, and harbours many styles. Towards the end the music becomes progressively less progressive. Generally the band is a more accessible form of Egdon Heath on Nebula with more straightforward rock but similar but less powerful vocals. Horns are prominent on some tracks. The lyrics are often quite nice. However, for the most part the music is not very progressive in the strict sense and the sound and recording quality leaves to be desired. Best tracks are the interjected instrumentals, Wolf and the first part of Vertigo. Compared to Haze, the music has moved from hippy progressive to hippy/critical progressive pop with a few heavy notes.
