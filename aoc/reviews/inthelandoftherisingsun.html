Summary

Like so many greats, and not so greats, from the seventies, Renaissance have rebanded to do a new album (last year's Tuscany) and a tour, resulting in a live album, which now turns out to be the swan song of this Renaissance incarnation.

The music

As could be expected this live album mixes the old with the new (including four Tuscany tracks, and some Haslam solo stuff). As could also be expected not all the new can quite keep up with the old. The break with the past is further augmented by Dunford and Haslam being the only ones remaining of the traditional Renaissance line up(s). Annie's voice hasn't lost one bit of its pitch. It isn't as steady and strong as it used to be, though. Aside that the instrumentation on the older tracks isn't as full and luscious as it used to be, the approach being somewhat more utilitarian. All this sets us up for, well, a bit of a disappointment, really. The second disc, which has a bunch of the older longer tracks is better than the first, of which only the first three tracks are any good. Still, most versions aren't all that inspired.

Conclusion

The quality of older Renaissance material can't be hidden, and some of the new stuff is pretty nice, too. However, I can't help feeling that this live album doesn't really add anything to their seventies live album Live At Carnegie Hall.
