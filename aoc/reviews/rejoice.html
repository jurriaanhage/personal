Summary

The music was composed by Ziegler in 79-82, but recorded only in 95-96.

The music

This is typical instrumental progressive rock, generally played in high tempo and according to my ears it is not always devoid of noise. Although there are only two people playing here, the "band" has a rather full sound with different keyboardriffs thrown at you all the time, lots of melodic variation as well and various melodies are played intertwined. The sound of the album will be dubbed dated by some of you, but I'm sure the music on this extremely optimistic first track will appeal to lovers of early Yes and Wakeman. It is particularly nice to have a live drummer here and he's really at it, plugging away. Sahara opens stately after which an off-beat played organ takes over. This part is rather hard to follow. After an ELPish part the song starts out anew, building up to a speedy part with fingerquick playing and souble bass. I'm not that fond of the theme, but Skygazer owes a lot to old Genesis, like the guitarintro. After this the song at first slows down a lot after which we get another uptempo part, quite dark with again the double bass. Later on we get some more ELPish stuff. The same holds for the euphoric Welcome, with lots of Emersonian keyboards, but also some great guitarwork. Some of the lines are not to my satsifaction, but these guys really know how to enthuse while playing. Good thing though that the album is only this short. Tick-Tock not surprisingly opens with just that. The next few minutes are filled with typical progressive bombast after which the ticking returns, neurotically. Lots of recurring themes here and some Arabic stuff thrown in for those in want. The we move bouncily to the end of the track. The closer, Prophecy, is the only track containing "vocals": one Lili Plieninger squeaks her way through a number of ahhhhh's here and you might have guessed it by now: I am not too fond of this, also because she often doesn;t make the note. The song has some nice moments though and is not entirely lost, but it does disfigure the track.

Conclusion

A pleasant surprise from the seventies and I think the recording, though late was deserved. Of course, one cannot expect modern music here, and lovers of Marillion and whatever came after are hereby warned to stay away. The recording is not perfect and I think I would not have let Lili sing on the last track, but for people still into up-tempo, keyboard-dominated, seventies symphonic rock with hints of ELP, Yes and Genesis (and who isn't?) this is a very worthwhile addition to your collection.
