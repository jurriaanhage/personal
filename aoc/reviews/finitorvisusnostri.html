Summary

Coatl M. Evil is from Italy, but this mini album reached me through Brian Hirsch of Moonchild.

The music

Rich keyboards introduce us to the album in Nightmare/Lost Souls. A bit darkish and pompous, but certainly not empty. A typical introduction to an electronic prog album. The music did remind me a bit of Danzigs solo piece, Black Aria. (In you case you didn't know he made a terrific classical music based instrumental mini album). The next track is Dawn and is seemingly the first part of the twenty minute Dawn. The song title is very well chosen. Horse Carriages and a somewhat more subdued tone we find on Victory. A bit on the percussive side with heavy percussion rumbling under the keyboard melodies. At the end the keyboards take on the guise of a church organ. The Castle: Arrival, Feat And Departure has some clavecimbel like parts. It tries to evoke the atmosphere of a medieval castle with a folky tune. I'm not that fond of it myself, although the change is welcome. The last part is mostly piano, but tends to plod on a bit. The weakest piece so far. Deep Blue Waters features accordion like sounds, while the short Revelations focuses on a strong theme with military drumming (unfortunately not real ones). The closing part of the main track is Towards Orion (a Gentle Giant). I don't really hear any Gentle Giant here, so I guess I just don't understand the reason for this undertitle. A nice melodic piece though. The closer of this mini album is Epilogue with recited vocals that add just that extra meaning to the nice thematic piece. This track also features some Floydian guitar work.

Conclusion

Bjorn Lynne, Janus' Agnus Dei, Glenn Danzig's Black Aria should hopefully indicate what this music is about. The music can be quite bombastic and powerful and in development I think Agnus Dei by Janus (a tune also well worth searching out) comes closest. The music is always high on melody, but never trite. Only weak track is the fourth, The Castle, but for the rest very appealing music (but no rock).
