Summary

Seemingly the debut album of this man.

The music

The album opens with Dao Dermo and it is in this song quite clear where it is about: jazzrock with the guitar in the lead. Fortunately it does not just go on and on and on, but there are some world-musiclike passages as well, complete with exotic sounding percussion. The more jazz rock/fusionist parts on the keyboard are of the meandering kind, melodies winding their way. Not very structured, but keeping you trailing. The next one La Violoncelliste Contemporaine is a somewhat slower piece with ponderous bass work and a jazzy piano. Although the guitar does enter the stage, it is played in an acoustic way. Blue Ipsis continues with rather disjointed jazzrock. On the whole the music is quite melodic on the surface, but as often happens in jazzrock, lots of things are going on and in this case there's this looseness to what happens, making it hard to catch on to the songs and even more to remember them in some way. Cobaltine is again a more moody track with the keys playing the rather soothing melodies (yet the tones are high) and the drummer keeps on filling in and playing his rolls. The guitar is more acoustic on this track. A number of tracks still follow, but from the above you should get the picture.

Conclusion

Plain and simple: this is jazzrock and only meant for those interested in the style. This is not a hybrid like UK (which to me sounds more easily liked in the purely progressive corner), but a Holdsworthian type of album, with plenty of energy, but also its more soothing moments. Although the boss of the band, La Greca, is the guitar player, the keyboards and piano of Dao Dermo also plays a prominent part and all along the drummers make pretty sure they are heard.
