Summary

The second album following the previous concept album Legendary Tales. The promotional material speaks of barrierbreaking prog metal. See if there are any grounds for this.

The music

The opening is nothing short of okay: operatic vocals, a classical and bombastic taste foreshadowing what is to come. We continue with the single Emerald Sword. Again the classical influences are strong in this fastpaced and double-bass riddled track with its violins. Bombasm reigns supreme. One part of this track I don't really like and that is the "for the king for the land ..." chorus. It still strikes me as somewhat idiotic and overdone. Wisdom Of The Kings opens melodically and soothingly with flute and acoustic guitar. The peace does not last long however as the music again picks up pace and jolly tunes are concatenated with fine melodies. The short Heroes Of The Lost Valley consist of two parts: the first is a medieval sounding one with clavecimbel. Then there's a spoken part and we continue with the swirling keyboards and string section of Eternal Glory. This song contains certainly some references to film music, in fact was it not for the fast metallic parts one could think this to be a soundtrack for some fantasy feature movie. You might judge from the all those titles that the story told is here is a fantasy story and you are right in this. In the faster paced parts it is as always hard to sit still since the music is really driving and whatever people might say, called it cliche or fake, one thing stands out: these guys know how to play, but also to transmit some of the energy over to the listener. As such one might have the tendency to underrate the music on this album, since what they do sounds "overdone", but on the other hand one cannot deny that it works. Beyond The Gates Of Infinity is a varied track: again those fastpaced passages with the good vocals of Fabio Lione, but also some slower bombastic churchorgan, and some menacing piano and cello and all glued together and never seeming unnatural. Wings Of Destiny is the first ballad. The vocals are slightly desperate and are sung with feeling. The song ends with some nice recorder and oboe. After a classical, baroque opening based on Vivaldi the pedals are pressed down. The vocals are this time accompanied by a choir followed by a filmic interlude. The quieter parts of this album, like the opening of Riding The Winds Of Eternity, reminds me Klaus Doldingers score for the The Neverending Story, because, I guess, of the optimistic tone to the music, notwithstanding the not always very optimisitc lyrics. The tear in the voice of the narrator as he says goodbye to the dragon Tharos is plainly audible. It is followed by a sensitive piano part. The second part of the, closing, title track, is The Hymn Of The Warrior, which features church organ and is bombastic and majestic throughout. The third part is slightly more fast paced and has some gypsy and folk influences and later also features a choir and even some soft piano and lyrics sung by Constanze Vaniyne. In the last part we have some bagpipes. In this way this great and varied track, not as metallic as most of the previous ones, comes to an end and therewith this album.

It did strike me that not all words in the English lyrics are pronounced in the way they should be. Might be something to remember for next time.

Conclusion

A very strong album showing great technical ability, but also a great sense of melody and it works well in transmitting the energy but also feeling in the music to the listener. The band is succesful in combining progressive metal and classical music into a whole, that is both appetizing and energizing.
