Summary

The music

In the ever continueing story of Isildurs Bane's Mind we now find ourselves at volume 5, an ever so fashionable DVD-release of a 90 minute concert recorded autumn 2004, with -how could it not be- added extras.

The opening track Under The Wind is accompanied by something that might be entitled a video clip, with vague-ish and atmospheric images of people out on the beach. Not before the second track do we switch to concert footage. What immediately strikes is the -whatshammacallit- enormous number of people out on stage: apart from the band's eight members we get an added total of nine guests. Granted, not all at the same time, most perform on a very limited number of tracks, but the fact remains. What also strikes is that the band seemingly have no trouble setting down their rather dynamic (as far as both volume and style go) brand of music quite well, at no time in danger of seeing their sound drowned out in the so often perceived sausage of live mix, especially with this many people. Whereas your regular band might look rather uninteresting after some minutes (who cares about looking at a quartet of homely blokes for an extended period of time), this large ensemble makes for a pluriform sight to say the least. This is further aided by the bits of outside footage, sometimes documentary, sometimes atmospherically, displayed during the intermittant musical sections.

Due to the high quality of live performance, the musical sound of the main concert can be compared quite well to the band's more recent studio efforts. We get a mixture of strong melody driven sections, mixed male and female vocals (of ample quality), a large variety of musical instruments and elongated atmospherical type sections. Adding up to the sort of progressive music the more modern groups make.

Apart from the main concert we receive 26 minutes worth of extra video concert material. The first of these tracks has been recorded with a limited audience in what appears some sort of studio set up at a Portuguese art rock festival. This material is instrumental and somewhat more technical and experimental, less atmospheric, than that of the main concert. It even includes some jazzy intermezzo meandering. This track is pretty good still, but when listened to after the original material it clearly appears limited. The other two do include the vocalists, but are less complete compositionally, and feel different as well through the dominant use of trumpet. Still worthwhile, though.

To top it all off we receive 23 minutes worth of new audio material. This material takes the style of the most recent studio release as a starting point. The shorter tracks seem to fall somewhat short in identity, but the final eleven minute track Thoughts Stand Still manages to return the sort of moody atmosphere, so typical for Swedish bands, we heard in the main concert. Nice one, easily floating by.

Conclusion

The pluriformity of Isildurs Bane as a band creates a base that might make the images offered through a DVD (such as this one) worthwhile (the fact that the band doesn't consist of men only might factor in here as well). The combination of technique and atmospheres has worked out very well, creating a DVD that definitely deserves listening to, it even constitutes more than just the polite standard first view a new owner might give such. Or, to phrase shorter: good one, this.
