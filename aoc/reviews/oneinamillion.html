Summary

The fourth album of this band. The band, I was told, shuffled thelein-up around, but there was no info on who is playing what in this band.

The music

The short The Stolen Eye opens the album. A rather balladic piece, which gives way to the mounting tension of Ancient Lines which then opens full-force with washes of drums, heavy rhythm guitars and quite a bit of keyboards as well. The vocal melodies are quite distinctive, and the vacuum cleaner of the sound stays throughout. However, the keyboards are also quite imporant: after the first vocal part, they take the fore somewhat with some nice soloing. The first bridge is quite unmelodious, but the second compensates (could have done without the Yeah! here). As always Poverty's No Crime is focussed on strong compositions with good memorable melodies. This song is certainly of that type with plenty of alterations of tempo and quite an epic feel, but it also has its weak points, although they are few.

Incognito continues the line of accessible progmetal. With powerful strokes the band plods its way through quite a long composition. Again the melodic quality of the music manages to appeal. The music is rather full. The ending is somewhat classical in style with only keyboards. A small reprise.

Heavy plodding drums and rhythm guitars ring out on Just A Dream. Mostly riffing on this dark track with sinister verses, but the band can't stop itself: the chorus is quite accessible. Again the music sounds a bit overly full. The keyboards go on in Arabic style as the nightmare unfolds.

The Stranger Within is by far the longest track on the album. The first part is full of bombast with all the necessary melodies including. Again a very full sound, but this time the intermezzo takes a little gas back. However the electric guitar comes back with a sweeping vengeance. The drummer has plenty of room on this track, playing percussively some of the time. Nice.

The percussiveness also pervades on the power instrumental Logan 5, that after a fast start back downs to jazziness after one and a half minute. Some lazy piano here. On Open To Attack we hear the now familiar accessible melodic material with grinding rhythm guitars in between and quite a bit of piano. The transitions are rather abrupt, but the pumping piano is a nice addition.

Point Of View finds us in another up-tempo (prog)metal song. Lined with orchestral phrasings and rhythm guitars the song moves, in pace, in the direction of bands such as Rhapsody. The sound of the song, something I noted earlier on on the record, is that there is lots of "high" in the music giving at times a shrill impression. A hasty track this one.

Before we come to the Rush cover which is the bonsutrack, we close the album itself with Dare To Fly. The middle part features some fast acoustic guitar making this the closest we come to a ballad. The middle part also has some percussion that does not really feel well there. Its purpose escapes me. An epic closer with some furious drumming.

I did not feel it necessarty up there to make note of the fact that the music seems like one or another progmetal band. I feel that this band is more poppy and catchy than most, but in a good way. The album really closes with the "limited" bonus track, which is a cover of a Rush song: Distant Early Warning. The well-known melodies shine through as is natural, but the vocals are quite different: much lower and also in a way more relaxed. In a way it is not strange that the band covers this song, because it fits well with the rest of the album. Most notable is the very audible bass and varied drumming (is there only one drummer at work here?).

Conclusion

Actually, I had my misgivings after a first record and although I can still not put it down as a classic it has to said that the band consistently knows how to write a song and infuse it with energy and live. For that I forgive them that they tend to make their music a bit too full, too much of a blast to my poor ears. All in all a satisfying album with likable catchy progmetal songs, but without falling into the pitfalls of triteness and melodrama. For some reason I think however, that this band will find it hard to make it really big.
