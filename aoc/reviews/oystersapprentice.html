Summary

The music

Despite being recorded this year, the tracks were written over a range of thirty years.

Opener Ghost Reporters is a track that is rather open which adheres in style to for instance a Koinonia. Melodic, and with a structure that is close to pop music. As the track progresses it becomes more experimental, straying from the Koinonia style.

That Strange Plaza is more the sort of mood track we often heard from Taylor. The use of sitar-like sounds and the steady and slowly building key and drum background make for a firm atmospheric base. During the second half the sax creates some more straight jazz oriented variations.

Joe Hill's Recorder is atmospherical once again. Track four lost its title over the 29 year span to its recording. Like its predecessor it's built round mesmerizing key sections on which minor musical variations are built, both electronic and on acoustic piano. Both pieces are quite minimalistic in style, although their enchanting quality is less commonly found in minimalism.

Vue opens very quietly in the back with slow keys. Only after some two and a half minutes it erupts with moog-type sounds and sharp guitar, creating a rather symphonic sound. The interchange between more quiet and more symphonic parts continues the tracks length.

Aiolos combines a progressive background on keys with interchanging jazzy solos on sax and more rock oriented solos on guitar. This results in a track that keeps its balance in the middle ground, although the sax does have its Koinonia moments.

Iron Wood slowly builds on a theme played on assorted keys and other instruments, all with an atmospherical feel. After some two minutes this cracks open to reveal Vogel's sax sound, which has a back drop of rather rhythmic and filling drumming, apart from some low keykeys.

Closer The Arrangement -the other track composed this year- starts out with the Koinonia sound of the opener, which fades into what probably is Variax, which sounds sort of like an electronic mix of harmonium and accordeon. The sharp sound of the sax is now gone, moving us gently towards the end of the disc.

Conclusion

The previous Taylor discs I heard were without Vogel. Vogel's sax draws the music in the direction of more regular solo-based jazz music. The majority of the music (although not by a large margin) is still the sort of atmospherical material that would fit well enough on the ECM label, but a sax oriented jazz sound can also be heard. This makes this release of less interest from a progressive point of view than those without Vogel. It does remain interesting, though.
