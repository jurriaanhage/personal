Summary

The music

Opener Nepsis has a pretty aggressive feel. Quite opposite to the early Tangerine Dream sound of previous Roz Vitalis material, this track has something of an Heldon feel: electronic and militant. Light Of The Ice is more open and melodic, bordering on traditional EM. Never enticing or irritating, but leaving me disinterested. Sakura In Bloom is a bit more TD-like, but with some semi-Japanese sounding twinkling dropped in. The opening section of the track seems to hang somewhere between focusing on a melody and focusing on atmosphere, leaving it in musical no-man's land. Halfway through it starts working towards a climax that is intense and emotional, to wind down with more experimentalism. Still, after the indifferent start this works out surprisingly well. Transspace Express is very twinkly and off on both melody and atmosphere. Thomas's Doubts has a more pastoral sound, once again close by EM. As it progresses a distant piano and humming lady add in a bit of the fragility known from Not Drowning, Waving. From this the band settle into the new style a bit more: despite experimental, the tracks are a tad more fluent, thus finding a better balance between experiment and listenability. Becoming The Poor is a rather long track Which has a bit of a fragmentary feel. It does move on, over and through whatever it may come across, but not exactly in a way that makes you want to kick back and enjoy. Towards the end the track becomes quite tranquil and near Japanese again. Something of an odd conclusion for a track such as this one.

Conclusion

Where previous Roz material might be on the derivative side, it did work to a nice result. This material is a lot more original, but at least part of this originality is too far out of bounds to work to a good result. The end result is an album that is more interesting than it is listenable, even though there is some improvement towards the end. Those into the less melodic experimental material on Cuneiform might want to give this a try, other's better play safe with earlier Roz releases.
