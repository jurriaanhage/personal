Summary

It is rare to find a real neo-progressive album on Musea, but according to the bio this is one. For those interested, the rhythm section is that of Priam.

The music

The album is eight tracks long, the first of which is Good To One Another. Although the bio states that the vocalist sings excellently in English, comparable to Gary Chandler of Jadis, I can tell you right now they are wrong. Okay, the English is fine, but if they mean that there is no accent then they are wrong. The accent is recognizable as French. Also, the vocals of Serge Barbaro are more easily compared to Ruud Stoker of Timelock then Chandler. The music itself is also quite close to the melodic progressive rock as made in the Netherlands by bands such as Timelock, Ywis and the like. SI Music stable music hence. Also fans of a band such as Jadis, might hear something he likes here. The song structures are not too difficult of course, and plenty of attention has been paid to come up with interesting vocal melodies and the middle part of the first track at least features plenty of high pitched keyboard solo's alternated with a rowdy guitar solo. The transitions are not always well executed, but the end result is fine as far as I am concerned.

The Last Word continues with a more sinister feel, the music is still very melodic with pointers to a band such as Saga, especially the rhythm guitar work. The vocals are quite different. The vocal melody is again a memorable one. Melodically the music has some Arabic influences, the pace is usually rather easy-going as is the keyboard solo smack in the middle. The triumphant keyboards that follow remind me of...ehm...yeah what do they remind me of. Pay attention to that bassline running through the song. The end of the song had a good guitar solo. Trustworhy Man continues the line of the album with plenty of soloing among the catchy vocal parts. The music is a bit more up-beat on this one, but continues to appeal.

On Chrysalides the music moves more into the relaxed Floyd vein, with also a bit of the relaxed side of Marillion and Clepsydra mixed in. The music is in fact quite relaxed, with a great guitar solo right at the end. Neo-prog fans will love this. Eye Of The World has some funky guitar parts, but also some powerful rhythm guitar parts. The vocal harmonies sound much like Timelock and Dreamcarnation: the more accessible side of neo-prog hence.

Statues is an even more catchy track. Again, the vocal harmonies in the chorus give the music its very catch sound. The vocals have been transformed a bit, the overall gait of the music is a plodding one. Not bad, but a bit too light for me. Halfway the song gets moodier with fast low keys and some tension building. Then the all-out keyboards set in. A rather standard solo this. At the end the vocal part returns.

What A Night is a moodily opening ballad. The vocals are quite emotional at times, the rhythm guitars get their share of attention, but we also hear an acoustic guitar solo. I was thinking a bit of the band Darius here. Again, the music is maybe a bit too mellow.

The closer is a bit more powerful and driven. Compared to the previous tracks it is certainly more varied and proggy. The variation is not always to the point however. The music has hints of Jadis, but the choruses are poppier. The end section takes the easy way out. A bit too standard.

Conclusion

A rather nice neo-progressive album with pointers to the Dutch neo-prog bands: Ywis, Timelock and a bit of Floyd, Clepsydra and Jadis thrown in. The vocals are at times quite accented (comparable to or more than Clepsydra). The music is often quite catchy, but within its genre this is really a nice album, although the quality seems to go down a bit towards the end. Playing and production are fine.
