Abel Ganz - Deafening Silence

Artist: Abel Ganz
Title: Deafening Silence
Label: MSI CDMS 1168
Length(s): 43 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1994
Month of review: 11/1994

Line up

Robert Wilson - guitars, backing vocals
Stuart Clyde - keyboards, vocals
Hugh Carter - bass, backing vocals
Colin Johnson - drums, backing vocals.

Tracks

1) Look At Me Now 6.14
2) The Radical Departs 5.04
3) The Deafening Silence 5.41
4) Hold The Moment 5.07
5) Serendipity 7.55
6) Stranger In Your Heart 6.27
8) It's Different Now 6.43

Songs written by Wilson, Carter, Forsyth and Hew Montgomery or some subset thereof.

Summary

Having released three demo tapes in the past that have all been released on CD as well, here's a new effort by this Scottish band, usually known for having had Alan Reed of Pallas in their lines. Hew Montgomery used to be the driving part of the band, and in a way he's still part of it, though he isn't part of the line-up. I guess he didn't have anymore time to play and tour, but did to record with them.

The music

The band is obviously neo-prog, song directed, refrains, but with an emphasis on keyboards, while most neo bands are definitely guitaroriented. In most bands the vocalist is a guy with a pleasant voice, but rarely sings out of line, being too rough or rugged. Forsyth is an exception to this, because he likes to raise his voice once in a while, making his performance a bit more intense than usual. He pronuniciation is a bit slurry at times though, but I like his voice. The sound is open, but not always very balanced. On the other hand I have to admit that while keeping on their own territory, the album is quite varied. A song like Look at me now can be called a song following neo-prog standards, but the second has some early Kelly tones, a good melody and even some organ from way back. The third song is a ballad, moody and atmospheric, a bit like Dire Straits' epic Telegraph Road. The fourth song is more of a melodic up-tempo rocker, with some Ark and AOR influences. The hitsingle you might say. Serendipity (yes, the word exists) is a varied enough song containing a lot of meandering keyboardriffs and also some guitarwork. Still at times it's all a little too obvious and mediocrity comes rushing in. They do manage to save the song. Number six is a ballad with good vocal melodies and the expected slow guitar- solo, but it just takes a bit too long, especially with the 90 seconds repetition of 'I don't want to be a stranger in your heart'. In the last song, the organ is back and the song is something of a mid tempo rock song from the seventies, if it wasn't for the trickling keyboardparts that have been inserted into it. Very anachronistic.

BTW the artwork is quite good and beautifully reproduced in colour on the CD itself.

Conclusion

A CD with quite a lot of deja-heard riffs, that keep you wondering where you heard it all before. Still it contains some good melodies and while the playing is satisfying, I'm not too fond of the production and recording quality. This should have been clearer. References are Pallas, Ark and some of the Dutch neo bands like Marathon and FAF though the latter are more guitar oriented. For the ones who are alreay familiar with this band, they haven't changed much except for the vocals, which have become a little throaty and less clear, but does have its charms.
