Summary

From Japan, Six-North is led by Hideyuki Shima and it will not be too difficult to obtain this album, because it is also released on Musea.

The music

Meandering panflute opens the album the prelude Ajikan. The keyboards that later accompany it have that Danny Elfman mysteriousness. A rather vague, but somehow pleasing soundtrackish piece that becomes sweeping towards the end.

The title track is the next one up. Fluttering flutes, soft snare drumming and tense notes on guitar and saxes all against a backdrop of a low easy bass. The style is now jazzrockish, but of the more throughtful kind (comparable to say Tony Levin's work). The music becomes quite a bit freakier later on with a strong focus on guitar. The thrilly sax part is maybe a bit too far out for me. Notwithstanding the meandering aspects the mood is the same throughout. Rather relaxed and in the style of world music. At two points in the track the band uses a tune from a well-known tune but played in a very distorted form.

Inner Crystal opens with piano and familiar melody. Again the style is jazzy and reminds me more of Keith Jarrett and various other bands on ECM than anything prog. The playing is elegant and subtle and this time has even more room for the bass.

Less introverted is the funky Silence, Darkness. Again a large role for the sax (tenor this time) and also plenty of room for a seventies funky guitar and the drummer is also allowed to play his fill. In addition the guy on the keyboards paints some soundscapes a la Fripp. Intense. Later on the groovy jazzrock becomes a duel between squeaking keyboards and an electric guitar (and the drummer plods on). Rather experimental sounding with all these meandering instruments, but still pleasant to hear.

Circular Pinx opens with peaceful piano and has a some willowey improvised vocals by Chizuko giving the music a fleeting impression. Then the music becomes more "normal" with clearly ringing vocals in Japanese, again by Chizuko. The mood of the music is now different, moodier.

The moodiness extends into Speakless where I hear two singers but only one is listed. Again, the music progresses without being in any kind of hurry. Even during the long jazzy keyboardsolo. Then the melodic vocals return accompanied by some thrilly sax. Again a very nice track which ends with a melodic guitar solo (think Metheny).

Kundabuffer explodes into heavy drums and is the ultimate marriage of King Crimson and jazzrock. Hectic, frantic and neurotic, the opening is great. Then we get some fingerfast piano playing (think Jarrett again) and not without dissonants mind you. Not for the faint of heart, but not without its appealling melodies either. The later part is more thematic and melodic.

The closer is Where No One Has Gone which is a rather sleepy ballad on acoustic guitar, bass and percussion. A very intimate sound.

Conclusion

Professional playing abound on this record, where the music takes the jazz of ECM and guys like Keith Jarrett and Chick Corea and turns it into something soothing and relaxing, but also experimental and in some refreshing. Certainly not music for all readers of this website, because a fondness for jazzrock and fusion is recommended. The beginning of the album also features some music reminiscent of guys like Tony Levin solo. A charming album and at times very intense.
