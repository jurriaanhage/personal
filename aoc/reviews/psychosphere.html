Summary

Their debut arrived in 1994 and only now in 1999 have they managed to have a new record out. Plenty of things happened in between of the record company and management problems kind so I won't bother you with this. Fact is, that this band was pointed out to me as a very promising one even at the time of their debut. Lets see what five years of experience brought us.

The music

Quite a number of rather short tracks in just over 46 minutes of progressive metal. They open loudly with Take Me To The Other Side. Then the singing starts. Olaf Billic has a somewhat throaty voice when he sings low. He doesn't really sing high, and his voice can be described as masculine, but not of the broad and hairy type. An important aspect of the music of House Of Spirits are the harmonies which make for a rather American sound. This is especially notable in the catchy opener which besides being catchy also shows a varied approach: heavy rhythm guitars, vocoded vocals and catchy choruses. Back On My Own sounds rather similar, but with wandering melodies on the rhythm guitar. This is a more riff based song, with less attention to melody it seems. Throughout nice and driving drumming a prominent bass sound. Comparisons can be made with the recent Threshold disc (but more concise) and later Dream Theater (hence, not very epic) and possibly King's X (for the song directeness) and related projects, but without the Beatles influence. History Is Repeating brings a change in the opening: some nice "marimba". For the rest, the song is a bit monotonous. World Full Of Pain is with slight over five minutes the longest track. A moody opening and a rather ballad like continuation. Voices is a very short track with strong "breathing" and on the whole very atmospheric. Good thing they put this here, since the strong song format will eventually bore as they probably know. Nice repetitive guitar playing. The music continues into Voice Of My Heart where the rhythm guitar sets in. The vocal part is not very melodic, vocoded and slightly chaotic. Some Arabic stylings in here as well. Safe The Secret tells a story. The music is quite nice: the vocals are varied and the vocal melody is striking. Additionally there are some nice effects and even a guitar solo. Unfortunately it is one of the kind I do not like. Time Is Drawing opens moodily with bells sounding, and brings with it a sense of foreboding. Strangely enough, the rock that I was waiting for does not come and so the song winds down to an end. Melancholic. Oblivion's Night is a hard driving song or so it seems during the opening. However, for the vocal part, the band takes a step back, which in my opinion is somewhat like putting the volume down and making the music seem pale in comparison. Dark & Light is a plodding piece with some noticeable keyboards. Back At (?) The Double is a vocally chaotic and not very interesting track. The closer is the instrumental title track which opens with acoustic guitar and later on the electric one takes the fore. A nice end to this album, winding it down as it were.

Conclusion

Like the bio said: melodic metal, not very epic or "progressive", but just varied metal with a strong melodic component. Lovers of King's X might give it a try since the guys also use harmony vocals and do have a comparable sound. The music though has definitely no link with the Beatles. Not really my cup of tea (as was the latest Threshold), since there's a bit too much rock and too strong a song format and not very appealing melodies. Still the band does try to brighten up the songs a bit with some nice motives such a ringing bells, vocoded vocals, and marimba.
