Summary

To me they were an unknown duo from the U.S. until they sent me this CD:

The music

the opening of the album is close to New Age: bells clinging, a low flute sound and later on some soft sax. Half way through we get a happy theme on flute and more pronounced percussion and stick work and the music has a South American sound to it. The music up to now reminds me of Paul Horn in style.

The next one, Jesse James, also contains prominent percussion and is rather bouncy because of the stick and the saxophone plays the melody, of the cowboy riding into the desert.

Hale Bopp Astronaut is a somewhat darker track with heavy stick presence (this time for the melody) and heavy drums. The flute lends a soothing atmosphere to compensate the darker tones of the other instruments.

Ancient Sea Scrolls is a rather simple tune with very low bass. The sound is very clear as always and surprisingly the song breaks into a very melodic and easy-going track, as if riding on the back of a desert animal.

The next track features all the guitars of this album, five no less, and is as such quite a loud track with a rocking and jamming character. Lots of solo's and interplay between the various guitars, that are all played by different people.

Capture The Queen is slow instrumental with various melodies on flute intertwined and Dave playing his stick as an acoustic guitar. 204 Scotland Road features the return of percussion and is an off-beat track with the flute seemingly improvising along the stick and bass rhythm section. In the middle we get a tempo change, yielding something folky reminiscent of Jethro Tull but of course without singing and guitar so it's not all that similar. Then we come back to the more free form part.

The sax returns on Comfortable Shoes which is a more swinging track which is the reason that I'm not that comfortable with it. What follow is the very short Saturn Sunset with John playing the Orbital flute (?). The track is dreamy, almost hazy.

Bite Of The Monkey is the first track to feature the watery sound of the didgeridoo. This track is supposed to evoke a jungle atmosphere with typical percussion and melody played on pan flute (?).

Spy Island then is a rather simple track with cymbals and melodies played on both stick and flute. The closer Manhattan AD starts with radio sounds and noise and stays rather free form with the dark didgeridoo sound and the saxophone and flute on top of it all.

The artwork is tasteful and a sure eye-drawer for the right audience.

Conclusion

A rather soft spoken album with interesting instrumentation. Not something for the average prog lover, but if you're interested in world music, New Age (but not the synthy kind) or musicians such as Paul Horn, then this might be something for you. It remains to be said however that the albums contains little in rounded songs, and I regard them more as tunes and a lot of the music seems to be just played instead of composed giving the music an air of freeness. The danger of these albums is that the album easily becomes a morass of tunes, and in this case it seems they have tried to compensate for this by varying the instrumentation. Rightly so.
