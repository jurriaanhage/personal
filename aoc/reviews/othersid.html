Sally French - The Other Side

Artist: Sally French
Title: The Other Side
Label: SI Music Simply 39
Length(s): 44 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1994
Month of review: 09/1994

Line up

Sally French - vocals
David Etheridge - keyboards, programming, orchestral harp, mellotron
Clive Osborne - guitars
Alan 'Sticky' Wickett - drums and percussion.

Tracks

1) Beyond The Sky 4.36
2) Inside The Tower 7.29
3) Ophelia 5.14
4) My Time Has Come 4.11
5) Lifting The Veil 5.16
6) Golden Leaves 4.15
7) The Last Time 7.34
8) The Key 5.19

All songs and lyrics by Sally French.

Summary

An album on a symphonic label with a woman, being the driving force. Quite a rarity, but it's true. What I gather from the biography, Sally (I'll call her Sally and hope she won't mind) is a self-confident and busy type trying to take care of everything herself.

The music

The style is easily placed: some Bel Canto (with the icy atmosphere and vocals), some Dead Can Dance (though less ethnic) and some Strangers on a Train to which it is closest if only the SI releases are taken into considerations. The atmosphere evoked by the songs is comparable to that evoked by Bel Canto, a bit cold and with much light (say a snowy landscape somewhere near the poles). The synthesizer and Sally's vocals are most prominent (hardly anything else is heard). A song that falls somewhat out of the considerations above is My Time Has Come, that is up-tempo and had better been left out of the CD. Golden Leaves is not very succesful either. It comes to close to a 'sweet musical tune' for my tastes. In the Last Time she takes to hoo-hooooing which she might have left out IMO. This song is also a bit too melodic and accessible for my tastes. The last song is quite subdued and I like it a lot more than 4 through 7.

Conclusion

So, concludingly, I'd say we have a companion to Strangers on a Train here, but less classical and less piano, more song directed and containing a hint of Tracy Hitchings. Until the first three songs I was quite taken in, but with that fourth song and all that happened afterwards, I can not be very impressed.
