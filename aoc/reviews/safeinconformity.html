Summary

The music

I have one of these CD caroussels in my car, so that you never know exactly what you get. I heard this disc for the first time through that and thought I was listening to an Italian Genesis clone. Quite sure no candidate for that was in the caroussel, it took me some twenty minutes to figure out I was listening to the second Hamadryad disc. Eh? This is Hamadryad? But stylistacally this is where we are. We hear the heavy vintage synths, the vocals quite alike to Gabriel's with dito guitar. The rhythm section is slightly different from the Genesis one of the mid seventies, but them being very much in a supporting role their influence on the overall sound is limited. And sure, the sound is more modern, so that maybe you could compare it to The Watch, but.. Genesis it is, so there you have it.

Compositionally you would find the same kind of diversity as on a Foxtrot type album. Some bits and pieces that sound tongue in cheek, others that are more epic, or poetic, including the smooth changes of theme. All pretty good. Just derivative.

As the album moves towards the end, we hear some material that lies more in the direction of the debut. The highly rocky One Voice, has its (unplanned) dissonants and heavy guitars. Not an asset. Polaroid Vendetta also displays the more prog metal oriented style of the debut. Omnipresent Umbra has something of Yes intro, although the organ is more Genesis. This track maintains its style of both Yes and Genesis influences. The climax has a pretty majestic feel, but any sense of originality is kept at bay in this track too.

Conclusion

If your voice happens to sound like Peter Gabriel's that can't be helped. But there is a certain odd convenience in the fact that pushing forward the bass player as the new vocalist made the band change from a Yes meets prog metal style to a Genesis style. Having said that, and leaving no doubt that this album is more derivative than it is original, it does not sound bad. If people go out (in large numbers by prog-count) to see Genesis cover bands, maybe this album deserves some credit too. Because, after all, there's nothing wrong with what the music sounds like. If you feel like a new album in mid seventies Genesis style, give this one a listen.
