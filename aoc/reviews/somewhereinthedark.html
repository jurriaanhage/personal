Summary

Deep Thought's self released debut Shadows Of The Past contained tracks 4, 5 and 7 of this full fledged album.

The music

Opener Clock is an interesting hodgepodge of ideas. Smooth synths, experimental sections, but mostly somewhat insecure sounding vocals. There is something nice about this track, on the other hand, it's not very consistent.

Changing The Rules is a more consistent track, initially led by heavy bass and somewhat coarse vocals. The bridge takes it into a more mellow section.

Waiting For Darkness is a shorter somewhat poppy track. As opposed to the previous tracks, this contains just one idea, just not one of the greatest.

After a start up of 3.5 minutes, Simple Man moves into the happy Marillion keyboard sound we know from the mid eighties, with even happier sounding vocals and guitar section. As the bounciness passes, we move into a more serious section, which gently leads us towards the end of the track.

Shadows Of The Past is dominated by strong guitar and synths, and more of Merz' insecure vocals. From here on the highpoints are the instrumental section in Ice (although the lyrics of this track about the growing diffidence towards other people are quite okay too, if somewhat artificial), and closer Mud On The Hill. Latter track has some pretty good sections, some with raspy vocals that suddenly have lost their wavering quality, nice instrumental sections, and a decent enough percussive intro. Despite still not being a wonder of structure, it is an unexpectedly good closing for this highly mediocre (at best) album.

Conclusion

Galileo is a label of which I thought as pretty strict in their releases, focusing on quality. That made this album even more of a disappointment: though some of the tracks might contain ample ideas, simple as they are, those ideas haven't, in general, been joined into songs, making for rather unstructured material. Most tracks seem to just start, go on for a bit, and end, without too much having happened in between. Then there's the fact that none of the instrumentalists, let alone the singer can carry the weight of a track. The pretty good closer just manages to make up for a bit. Still this is a very disappointing album, in a light neo-progressive vein, that would have fitted a lot better in the early nineties. By far the worst release on the Galileo label.
