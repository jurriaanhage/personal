Summary

This is Dificil Equilibrio's seconds release on the French Musea label. Remarkably enough, this release is a digipak, the first released by Musea I know off.

The music

Well, this band wants to let the listener know from the start where there loyalties lie: opener Vidas Son Horas immediately starts with a very crimsonesque bit of guitaring. And Dificil Equilibrio appear to have no intention of changing that impression. Sure, this album is not pure King Crimson, but I would describe it something like "king Crimson through Dificil Equilibrio's eyes". The range is pretty wide, Penumbra for instance sounds like a Red-era track, a bit more distorted and extreme in its use of guitar. Aforementioned opener sounds more like an eighties King Crimson track. Al Destino Devenir is more a late nineties, early twentyfirst centure type track. This album pretty much covers all the more instrumentally directed KC styles, leaving the vocal side (Court, Poseidon, Belew stuff) out. Ruptura3 is a bit out, really. Not only is it not KC, it's also sort of hanging inbetween, missing the intensity of the tracks around it. This does mark the start of some less Crimson directed material, or at least material that can be less easily pinned on a particular period, although we safely return to the master as the end (of the album, mind you) nighs.

Conclusion

If you don't like King Crimson, you can safely let Dificil Equilibrio be, if you do, though, you should see this as a great addition. Despite the fact that the band so heavily leans on the Crimson style, they do manage to come up with their own compositions and interpretations of the doctrine, mind you, a lot better than the big KC itself has been doing over the last couple of years. You might say that Simetricanarquia is the album KC could not make. Good stuff.
