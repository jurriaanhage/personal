Summary

A little late, but I guess better than never.

The music

The Flower Kings should be counted among the most promising and interesting of the current progressive bands, together with the likes of Spock's Beard. Their reputation is good and it seems they're getting better and also live they are certainly worth seeing.

Opener Eyes Of The World shows already what Flower Kings stand for: a combination of orgiastic music, thoughtful intermezzo's, crazy and often rather playful inserts (for instance the Inspector Gadget like part in the intro) with leading musicians, Rione Stolt with his screaming or soulful guitarplaying and the versatile Tomas Bodin on keyboards. Add to this the Wetton-like vocals and also the very strong vocals of Hans Froberg and a tight rhythm section and I hope you get some idea what the band is about: a melting pot of musical styles (the drummer is actually a reggae drummer) lyrically and largely influenced by the positivity of Yes, but musically I wouldn't call them close to anything. A Room With A View is one of the seven short tracks on these albums. After the violence of the opener, this is a very soothing track, with a slight jazzy undertone. Just This Once opens with a collection of rather weird sounds, while the track itself plods quite nicely along. At the end we get a slight Genesis touch. The organ sound that this band has can be very well compared to that of Spock's Beard on Beware Of Darkness. Differences are not only in the vocals, but also in the fact that Spock's Beard's music is more consistent and The Flower Kings tend to take there influences from just about anywhere, making the music maybe somewhat more fragmented, but fortunately the band is good enough to deal with its selfimposed richness. The Church Of Your Heart is a mid-tempo track with a beautiful fraternizing chorus. Compared to the previous tracks it's somewhat less fickle, but appropriately contains a passage on church organ. After the acoustic ditty Poor Mr. Rain's Ordinary Guitar we get another instrumental: The Man Who Walked With Kings which features some soprano saxophone, the all-familiar mellotron and is in fact a very captivating track with a worthwhile melody and strong build-up. After some circus music we move into Circus Brimstone, the longest track on this disc. After a spooky, Crimsonesque beginning, the song becomes thoroughly menacing to change character after a few minutes, becoming a rather happy track. Not surprisingly the menacing part returns after which we get some backwards sampled vocals and a bouncy intermezzo and the song closes with some Arabic sounding melodies. After another ditty we come to the close of the first disc, Compassion. A slow track with little music and treated vocals. The song sounds almost industrial at first. This track reminds me of some Asia track of the Arena album, especially the harmony vocals. As an afterthought there are some spacey meanderings on the keyboards (not part of the track).

The second disc opens with the organic Pipes Of Peace and contains the main theme of Stardust We Are, later to come. The End Of Innocence is a mid-tempo track with a jazzy feel to it, that contains a stretched out bluesy guitar solo. The Merrygoround is an uplifting track to drive away the depression of the last track. After some busy drumming and a soothing part the song ends (or should have ended) with a fantastic guitar solo. Don Of The Universe is another instrumental with some sitar as a way of introducing variety with also the saxophone giving some input. A little free form this track and again the theme of Stardust We Are enters into the picture. After the short A Day At The Mall we get what seems another instrumental, but the fact is I just can't find the lyrics to it. Notwithstanding the presence of the sitar and tabla, not one of the best tracks; a bit too bouncy for my tastes. Kingdom Of Lies also has something to do with flies. It is sung by Froberg and the song is a bit monotonous with loud bass and drum, but not overly complex. The chorus is nice. The theme of Stardust We Are also occurs in the moody pianic If 28, while Ghost Of The Red Cloud reveals the roots of drummer Salazar. Not one of the shining moments of the album. After the last ditty we come to the title track, Stardust We Are, a whole 25 minutes long. This is a majestic song containing, as you probably would have guesses, everything the band stands for. Being so long, I wouldn't want to call it the best composition of the album, it is a bit too fickle for that, but on the whole a nice compendium of what the band is about.

Conclusion

Some 130 minutes of outstanding colourful progressive rock, uplifting music, versatile and varied enough for three bands. Heartily recommended.
