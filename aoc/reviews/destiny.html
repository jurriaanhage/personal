Mystery - Destiny?

Summary

These guys know how to do it: include my review in a booklet accompanying the new release of place my name in the thank you's is THE way of getting a positive review. Seriously now, some time ago this band released their first album (also reviewed by me) which revealed a strong AOR/Styx influence. Their drummer left and they had some more problems so it took some time. As they say Theatre of the Mind, the debut, was a great success and with the promo of Destiny? was enclosed a new pressing of this album.

The music

Legend is the overture of the album with dripping water, piano and a softly wailing guitar. Then the sound becomes bit more rhythmic and at the end of this short track a little disjointed. The vocal part of this track is not very succesful and sounds forced. On the bext track, the title track, it is the rather high clear voice of Savoie, a voice that reminds me of Dennis DeYoung. Nothing wrong with this track. The composition is well-built with introductory sounding vocals, lots of varied guitarparts in the middle and recurring vocals at the end. Basically it is a rather straightforward rock track with a reference to later Rush, but sounding more sensuous and less modernistic. Slave To Liberty opens with lush keyboards, spaceous guitar and balladic vocals. Then rhythm is inserted as the story of Daniel is told. At the end the song could have used a little more power. They try, but it doesn't really work. On Before The Dawn the lyrics of Legend are repeated but now in French. Before The Dawn starts almost the same as Slave To Liberty and structurally I hear some parallels between the songs. Not a very good idea. The vocal melody is quite okay fortunately since a lot of lyrics are sung on this song (in fact a lot of lyrics on the entire album). There some power in this song and also subtlelty (what a strange word) with the Greek sounding guitars in the middle. Certainly a captivating track, but also at the end I feel there's some restraint, that the band does not really go all out. Queen Of Vajra Space is slightly folky and like the previous tracks opens like a ballad. This time some violin is used. The break into the guitarpart is rather typical of Rush and in fact I'm onstantly reminded here in some way of older Rush (2112). Some nice counterrhythms here, but again the music does not really erupt from the speakers and I think sometimes this is called for. Fortunately at the end we get some more powerful playing and we move right into the next track: The Mourning Man. Submerged features some nice fretless bass, good melodies, some heavy bass, and it really rocks later in the track. Closer of the album is the almost 15 minute long Shadow Of The Lake. Because of its length it has a slow build-up, slightly orchestral with again some nice thoughtful bass lines. All in all a ery nice track with plenty of variation and all kinds of features that keep on building up the tension on this bombastic track that really show what this band can do. Not that I'm asking that the band put three or four of these songs on an album, since that would not make for a good album, but it is certainly a moving highpoint on this album.

Conclusion

Well crafted music, good melodies, but something seems to be missing here. The music is mostly based on the varied guitar sound of St. Pere, with some background additions on keyboard. The vocals are good, but you'll have to like the sound of them. Some negative points are the fact that the music is rather self similar in structure and that the band not always gets fierce when they should, at least in my opinion. Not very difficult music, very songdirected, so some lovers of prog will not want to hear this, but all in all, the music sounds professional and should be varied enough to please most and the closing epic is really a great one. References are AOR (but more varied), Styx, but harder-edged and Rush (but less technical) and some symphonic ingredients added for good measure. Hopefully this will give you an idea.
