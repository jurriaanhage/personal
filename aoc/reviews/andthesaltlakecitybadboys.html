Summary

The second album of Alfy Betz, last time with the Imaginary Orchestra, now with a "new" outfit from around Salt Lake. The album is for sale at Kinesis.

The music

River Of Fire is a bit of a vague opener. At first the music seems to be rather unrelated, but after a minute or so the violin and keys take on a melodic theme. The piano plays in the back, and a late nite jazzy mood is created. Not very adventurous, and melodically not very enticing. All a bit on the cheesy side. In the middle the guitars sets in, and the keys show a bit more daring. The music does not have a real lot to do with progressive rock though.

Waltz For Nobody is a light piano piece, the melody being quite nice actually. Rather in the vein of David Lanz and similar artists. The music might well be used for a romantic movie of sorts. The violin playing is quite sensitive and reminds me of Ponty.

With The Fool the organ sets in giving the music immediately a proggy feel (Spock's Beard even). The theme itself is also quite nice, although not very distinctive. However after this start, the music mellows down taking the form of acoustic guitar. The song has nice elements but my impression more could have been done with these. The various fragments seem as so much loose sand, very little cohesion here. Too bad.

Letter To Karumo is one of the solo piano pieces. Likable stuff, classically styled. I get the feeling there is an air of Chopin running through it. It is quite somber. The pace goes up on the beat quirky Blue Rider. The violin is also back again, giving the music a Ponty feel, yet again. Strange how dominating such an instrument can be. The song itself is more of a blues tune, but one with plenty of variation. There is also some soprano sax here, meandering throughout the middle part. Halfway, the Hammond starts to gurgle, and a live feel is accomplished.

Lucy in The Sky With Diamonds as you probably expect is a Beatles cover, here played instrumentally on harpsichordish synths. The melody is grand of course, while Alfy plays his pipe organ. The chorus I like less and it comes out rather cheesy, percussion and acoustic guitar with organ (but the Hammond at the end is nice).

Fantasy in C# minor is the major epic running at over twenty minutes. This is the ensemble at their most avant-garde with the piano on the foreground. Both melodic and more repetitive lines intertwine; the drumming on the other hand seems a bit too obvious. I like the piano work here, strongly classically oriented. Later the music becomes more 'band' like with the usual instruments setting in for a laid back continuation and some eerie guitar work. Halfway we get some minimalistic tape-loop influences, after which we move into somewhat psychedelic territory. A rare combination of styles, resulting in something less than coherent. But it has to be said: it has its moments.

Sunday At The Pool is a cover like Lucy... but a hidden one, that is until you here it. This is one of Satie's Gymnopedie and yes that music may well describe a sunday at the pool (or sundae if you prefer to be at the pool on other days as well). For those not familiar with Satie, think of Baddalamenti instead (Twin Peaks).

Conclusion

This is an album of tunes, mainly in a fusion vein, with the piano as leading instrument. The main epic is more classical in outset and quite likable in fact. However, all in all the music has relatively little to do with progressive rock in its various forms, unless it should be the mixing of styles and the slightly avant-garde leanings. I do also feel a family ressemblance with the less bombastic work of ELP.
