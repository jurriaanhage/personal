Pallas - 25/04/98

Artist: Pallas
Title: 25/04/98
Label: self produced?
Length(s): minutes
Year(s) of release: 1998
Month of review: 06/1998

Line up

Alan Reed - vocals
Mike Stobbie - keyboards
Graeme Murray - bass
Niall Matthewson - guitar
Derek Forman - drums

Tracks

1) Beat The Drum
2) Blood And Roses
3) Refugee

Summary

Many like me will probably have tried to get hold of the complimentary tape of Pallas introducing their new album, Beat the Drum. Here are my thoughts. Also, this tape was given away a present to those who were at the Zwolle festival where Pallas because of an injury on the part of the drummer could not perform. The accompanying note does tell that people who were there are entitled to visit a specially arranged concert in Zwolle for free. The band also took the liberty of advertising the fact that Arrive Alive period material will be released on CD soon.

The music

Judging by the title Beat the Drum might not seem such a high flyer, but it certainly is. The song opens very moodily with sad singing, and only after a few minutes does the beat come into the music. The vocal melody of the chorus is especially good and easy on the ear. The drums on this version, in which I hear some slight references to older Genesis, are still electronic, since the drummer was out of the running. Hopefully this will make the final version a little more dynamic. Striking about this song are the slightly classical passages, maybe something left over from the film/tv work Stobbie has been doing.

Blood and Roses is a subtle track with again synth strings and piano. The band states that this song is still only a framework, but already works for me. Goosebumps. It might be even better if Reed would sing another vocal part during the more bombastic passage in a more emotional voice.

More typically Pallas is Refugee, an older song. It is quite catchy, but IMO the least likable song on the album, mostly because of the screamy verses. The ending is a great blistering finale.

Conclusion

I'm truly impressed with the music on this prerelease tape. Especially with regards to songwriting and integrating the various aspects of their being Pallas, they have succeeded in delivering the goods this time, with great melodies throughout.
