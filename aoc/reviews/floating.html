Summary

An English one-off on Cyclops.

The music

An album by this Cyclops band, a label that has been quite active the last year. Not all they release is that good. The CD I'm no listening is quite alright. It's definitely neo with a singer that sounds quite English (a bit like the singer of Castanarc though less female and dreamy). The song might need a little getting used to.

The album starts out with soft keyboard as an introduction to the quite up-tempo Heaven. The song is very melodic, but it's not very varied and the drummer is really hacking away. 'Will you follow me, will you follow me..' does remind me of Market Square Heroes. The guitar is the prominent element in this song and this only adds to the Marillion influence (more after than before Fish though). This song might, if shortened somewhat, be a song for the radio, a real party stomper. Friend is quite different and reminds me of All About Eve somewhat because of it's stillness and beautiful melody (especially the refrain). Strangely they also remind me of For Absent Friends during the stanzas. Some people might think this song somewhat boring, but I can't agree. I mean the rendition isn't quite like firing someone up, but the idea is very good as well as the melody and that also counts for something. The intro to Slaves definitely reminds me of something, but what? This is a very good song, a bit meancing and driven. The song is well-balanced with all instruments getting their share and varies quite a lot in tempo as well. One Way Man is a again very melodic, but not very progressive, it's getting more pop than prog now. Maybe Icehouse might make a song like this some time. It's midtempo by the way. Shame does start out to prog-promising either. The rhythm guitar starts it off with, again a good melody (and a RHYTHM guitar at that). The song is again very accessible and has no surprises. Not a very strong song. 11-11 starts out a bit more promising. The melody is again quite accessible. You might say that this song has the complicatedness of the New Marillion. The guitarwork is very prominent, specifically the last part of the song contains quite a long guitarsolo, that just manages NOT to rip. Hypnotic is an instrumental and has a good build-up. The leading instrument is the guitar (yet again) and the song is in a way quite repetitive. You might even say that it has an instrumental chorus. Keep On Running starts out quite heavy, but after a while the For Absent Friends melodicity (a new word?) comes floating up again. Circles is more like a rocker, a bit up-tempo and all. Not too interersting. Triludan was supposed to be the 3-piece masterpiece of this album. It's the longest song on this album and for prog albums this always includes a promise. The first part is quite heavy at times, the vocal melody isn't very interesting. The second part if very up-tempo and heavy on the guitar. Between the second and last part, it becomes silent, after which the song moves on quietly until the end.

The problem with bands like these is that you have to ask whether it's you that think that songs get less complex all the time, or that the musicians make the songs less complex. It's quite difficult to answer for yourself, I know. In this case, i've made up my mind that it's the musicians. This doesn't mean that this CD is not to be tried. First of all the melodies are very worth- while and well above AOR and say stadiumrock. Still, it isn't GG, KC, UK or VDGG and some think that that's a problem. Well, that's their problem. I take my chance and play Slaves again, because they do know how to write a good melody. They might include some surprises for their next CD.

Conclusion

Rank under Marathon, For Absent Friends, and more of those Dutch neo-bands. The vocals are a little flat, but the band has promise because of the melodies. Needs something extra though to keep the listener captivated. Let's hope that when they compensate their 'shortcomings', they won't lose their ability with the melodies (some bands do, sadly enough).
