You might have noticed the strange titling. The idea is that some of the tracks are multiparted, but the first part is always the name of the whole. Now go figure it out yourself.

Summary

Hillman is a multi instrumentalist with hi third album to date. The older albums were all a bit in the up-tempo synth rock style a la Cybotron/TD. The bio says now that he has moved into more progrock directions.

The music

The album opens with Wheels Within Wheels part 1 in four parts. The first part has the thematic introduction. The theme is a strong one, but the trumpetlike sounds are not really my cup of tea. However the music seems more varied than ever, with acoustic guitar and also some clavecimbel like passages. The second part is much more up-tempo and it is unfortunate that the music sounds a bit cold and well devoid of life is putting it too strongly, but notwithstanding the pace and energy in this piec of music it all comes off a bit artificial. Maybe its just the electronic percussion, I don't know, but the music does not groove very much in my opinion. On the other hand the music is still riddled with good and memorable melodies. This second part ends rather quietly and mysteriously. Compared to the previous albums, Hillman takes a more varied approach instrumentally and the music is less and less of the sequenced type. The links with for instance Tangerine Dream and the space synth rock of Cybotron getting further and further away. After the soothing acoustics of the third part, A Time That Was, we return to the up-tempo Against All Odds. This is more typical for Hillmans previous albums. A telling melody and I'm reminded a bit of Mastermind here with all its earlier bombasm. Moongate is a track from 1989 and brings Camel to mind, a bit like Spirit Of The Water, with flute. Flying High comes in three parts. The first is a catchy one with the typical spacey guitar playing of Hillman. Again, melodically I'm thinking Camel here in their more up-beat period. The Earth Sleeps Below is the rather repetitive second part. New Horizons is also a three parted affair. It opens with long slow sounds and takes quite some time building. Mostly synths here and of course the mechanical sounding percussion. Pulsator is a track that puts itself very much on the foreground, because of its insistent repetitive melody. Obelisk is a mysterious piece with flute that evokes a kind of Egyptian atmosphere. After the short Upon The Hill we come to the pumping rock of the title track. I'm not too fond of it, like Pulsator it is a bit too insistent. On the other hand it is a rather striking track with its carnival like melody, pounding drums and strong organ presence. The second part of the track is very spacey. At the end the rock of the opening returns. A Time That Was was also the second part of the first track. This is a bit of a Hackettish track on acoustic guitar. Then we come to the four parted Wheels Within Wheels (part 2), with lots of thematic repetition (we might also call this parts within parts, because one needs a degree to handle all the various parts and their connections in name alone). In Tiwst Of Fate there are some nice melodic transitions. Upon Reflection is not only reflective, but also a bit bouncy at times. I'm not too fond of that myself and the music sounds a bit too synthetic here. Wheels Of Change is a bit of an odd track: reminding me of Brian Hirshes later solo work, this is a bit of a technocratic electronic rock. Very percussive and plenty of breaks. The Finale is a bit mellow at first, but quickly turns into quite an impressieve piece. Of course the theme recurs here. The closer is For What Is To Come... Piano on this one at first, but later on the biting guitarwork of Hillman rounds this melodic piece off. A rather peaceful piece.

Conclusion

A more varied and maybe also more proggy album by Hillman. Largely the music is quite good (like the epic Wheels Within Wheels) with some great melodies. Too bad about the automated percussion, I think live drumming would lend more life to the music. On the other hand, some pieces I did not like, like the insistent passages Pulsator and Convergence or the merry, synthetic sounding Upon Reflection. A mixed bag hence where lovers of the old Hillman will probably not be disappointed, but also fans of Fonya and similar artists could lend an ear.
