Acuity - Skyward

Artist: Acuity
Title: Skyward
Label: Retrograde Recordings
Length(s): 68 minutes
Year(s) of release: 2004
Month of review: [02/2005]

Tracks

Summary

The music

The meat of opener Vector is happy sounding guitar melody. Around this some histrionics of the wide-legged-stance-hardrock-guitarist type and some choppy drumming are included.

State Of The Art Love Song is not that, it is a declaration of not being an android and other sorts of non-human human beings, shaped in sort of a Rush like song. Having said that: the track is sung in a nagging tone of voice sounding like the smartest kid in class (you know, the one you'd want to whack after five seconds) and each declaration is followed by an annoying guitar riff.

Transcendence starts off with acoustic guitars, moving into the vocal section. After a couple of minutes the track picks up a bit with some electronic stuff, but moving off into a morass melodically. This morass does contain some nice sound bites, including mellotron and some nice guitar stuff. It also contains a lot of doodling. Anyway, even though the length might suggest this be an epic, not so. In the final vocal section Styes makes an attempt at coming to the sort of philosophical finale we know from Discipline, and comes close enough to make it sound good, without being very original. Things are looking up.

Milwaukee is an essay written from an Hannibal Lecter type personality. Taken on by the story this track has more structure than its predecessor, although it does tend to skirt around during instrumental sections. The guitar is harder now, but does work to stay within the song, not drawing all attention. Ample room for rest is made. However, in trying to bring the much needed dramatics to the lyrics, Styes falls short. Stronger than with Transcendence the composition echoes Discipline but can't help fall short, as does Styes in his rendition.

Cul-de-sacs Of Venus is an instrumental bit that seems more intended for demonstrating all the instruments, than that it makes a point. It sort of floats by, giving each of the instruments their time.

Voyager starts with an electronic brass blare, leading into the track's beef with a couple of sentences diverting from that and returning again. In short: with the lack of melodic focus we have heard before. On the other end the build up of the strange guitar intermezzos is quite beautiful. And then we move into more electronic brass blaring. The longish instrumental section build from this is less jumpy and not bad at all, built around the alternation of guitar and synths. With the bridge to the next section the guitar starts overstretching immediately as does Styes' voice not much later. We do hear the return of the strong guitar riff of the start even with added mellotron, making for a great finale. As beautiful as that may be, it is not enough to carry the weight of a track this length.

Conclusion

The thing about shooting high is that you have to aim low (or am I messing up my references now?) On a number of occasions I get the strong feeling Styes is trying to make another Unfolded Like Staircases. He is not a good enough composer, instrumentalist or vocalist to do so. So, Styles failed at making a brilliant album, but there definitely is something about this man's talents that makes me feel he could have pulled off making a good, maybe even a great album. By aiming as high as he did, though, he did fall short of doing that. Milwaukee has great moments, but lacks in dramatics, State Of The Art Love Song is even pretty bloody irritating. The other material falls in between, mostly lacking direction, even though there are good sections. The verdict would have to be: sorry, but no cigar. But please, do try again, cause I do have hopes that another time around we can say: Have a Cigar (blimey, there go my references again).
