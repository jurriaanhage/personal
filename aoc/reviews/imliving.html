Ego Eimi - I'm Living

Artist: Ego Eimi
Title: I'm Living
Label: self produced
Length(s): 59 minutes
Year(s) of release: 2001
Month of review: [03/2005]

Line up

Alexander Skorobogatov - guitars, vocals, bayan, programming

Tracks

1) Transfiguration 5.57
2) I'm Living 13.45
3) My Heart Waketh 5.30
4) Light Of The Unearthly Day 5.03
5) Night Of Ukranian Steppes 8.00
6) Elixirs 7.03
7) Underground Earl, P. III 4.29
8) The Great Friday 8.30

Summary

Another Russian self production, by a musician who does everything himself.

The music

It is hard to believe that Transfiguration is not played by a band. It certainly rocks, although live drums could have taken it a bit further. The vocals are surprisingly maybe in Russian, while the music is a combination of indie rock, psychedelic rock with varied vocals: some of the vocals have elements of Bocelli, in others the vocals are rather dreamy. An excellent song with plenty of sound spectrum filling guitars and keyboards, and some excellent turns of melody.

I'm Living is by far the longest track on this album, almost fourteen minutes. It opens quietly enough with dramatic, low vocals, in Russian, subdued, and accompanied by soft synths. Then the rock sets in with psychedelic tinged, repetitive guitars. There is a slight Arabic tinge to it all. Too bad the drums are programmed again, although it could certainly have been worse. After four minutes, the music takes a turn, leaving us with vibrating synths and piano. A guitar strums lonely in the back. Later passages can be quite hard with rthyhm guitar and synths turning out quite a volume. The vocal material is okay, but not much variation in the vocal melody throughout. On the other hand, the instrumental sections are way more prominent, and here at least we have plenty of variation and dynamics. The final minutes are very Ozrics.

My Heart Waketh has more of a folk feel, and a very good vocal melody too. Sung with a passion (notwithstanding the natural flatsoundingness of the Russian language). The folky music is accented by rhtyhm guitar drones now. It all does sound very seventies for some reason, although the overall bareness also reminds of eighties New Wave outfits. This song however is a bit too bombastic for that (the main instrument sounds like an accordion, but it could be the bayan mentioned in the line-up). The concluding guitar solo rings out clearly. Excellent song.

The folkiness is even stronger present on Light Of The Unearthly Day. Plenty of drama again, here, and a bit of aggressiveness too, in the vocals. Similar in a sense to Aragon's vocalist. Mainly strumming acoustic guitar and bass on this rather straightforward, bouncy tune. However, at the end we move in heavy metal territory, after a short transition on organ. A strange combination, but not so strange when you hear it.

Night Of Ukranian Steppes is a rather lengthy piece, which continues where the previous track left off. Metallica combined with Peer Gynt is what we get here. The second part sounds more like a different tune. The slightly operatic vocals are back, and the vocal melody is fine.

Elixirs opens with strumming acoustic guitar, with some strange vocalizations. Slowly the song builds up, for some reason I am often reminded of Aphrodite's Child's Demis Roussos. There is a certain similar passion in the voice, although Demis' voice goes much higher. The guitar is in psyche mode again, finding place for a nicely melodic solo.

Underground Earl, P. III has fast played acoustic guitar, obtaining a rather nice effect. For the rest this is a rather noisy folksong, with a bit of electronics towards the end.

The Great Friday is the aggressively opening closer of this album. We are back in metal, back in business with plodding rhythm guitars, and plenty of breaks and tempo changes. After three minutes or so we break into something akin soundtrack music, after which a lone melodic guitar sets in. The next passage is for a lone bass, kind of awkward sounding in a way. Then the metal comes back in. There is a certain Peer Gyntness to the music again.

Conclusion

Well, quite a surprise this one. Not progressive, more eclectic in the way styles are combined into a single whole. The exoticness is enhanced by the low Russian vocals, and it has to be said: the vocal melodies are generally good. Elements of blues, folk, psyche and metal can be found here as well as a dose of modern electronics. I wonder whether the relative isolation helps in any way to obtain a unique sound, which in this case is sometimes gloomy, sometimes dramatic sound. The production is on the lo-fi side, and one could have wished for real drums. However, it did not bother me, and if this is your type of music it will not bother you. Since our copy was plainly a cdr copy without actual artwork, it does leave me to wonder whether this record can be bought anywhere in any way.
