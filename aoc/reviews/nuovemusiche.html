Summary

This is a songbook of music from days long passed, very long, mostly sixteenth and seventeenth century. They are modern jazzy renditions, on which Pillow is accompanied by the members of avant prog band Tunnels. It was named after one of the earliest songbooks known, written by Giulio Caccini. The accompanyment is down by the members of Tunnels.

The music

As already mentioned, this is a collection of very old songs, most of 'em rearranged by Pillow and with assorted help. This results in technically modern sounding stuff, but rather unlike what you would normally expect from an outfit such as Tunnels: it's Pillow leading the way on this disc. There's no hiding Percy Jones's bass sound, though. The music has happy sides (Come Away, Come Sweet Love, Can He Excuse My Wrongs?), deical stuff (Exulta, Filia Sion has Pillow singing 'hallelujah' half the track), jazzy stuff (Il Mio Cocente Ardore, Usurpator Tiranno), and just plain good stuff (Amarilli Mia Bella, Dido's Lament From Dido And Aeneas, The Fatal Hour).

Conclusion

Without claiming knowledge of how the originals sound, Pillow's voice sounds convincing on this material, in no less than four languages: Spanish, Italian, English and French. Still, it took me an extra listen to get used to these vocals that aren't simply musical, but proclaiming as well. The addition of the band Tunnels has given an interesting and, if I may say so, rather appealing angle to this material. The overall result is a mostly strong album that has a lot of Tunnels peeking through. Be warned, though, that vocally this album is something of an acquired taste.
