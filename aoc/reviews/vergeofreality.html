Summary

The music

Roppongi Night opens the disc with a rather traditional type of jazzrock. The composition seems mostly intended to create room for improvisations (or what sounds like it) for the various instruments. Drums and bass are mostly used for creating the rhythm back drop, only guitar and keys (electric piano) are used for the solos. The tone is light, sort of Shakatak without the vocals or Koinonia.

Edge Trigger opens a lot heavier, introducing both progressive and powertrio influences. Even though the carpet-like synths do return through the track, it is dominated by flashy (almost blaring) keys and both freaky and tranquil guitar sections. Beyond The Verge shows a more active bass, heard in both a solo and more aggressive dueling. Evergreen is more piano oriented, less jazzy, more muzak with a distinct Richard Clayderman quality.

The music remains accessible, often friendly sounding. The solos do not diverge onto the road to nevermore, but remain mostly melodic or are to short to get very far. The upside is that lengthy meandering is prevented, but it also results in compositions that lack identity. This non-committal and vacant approach more often than not irritates more than a bit. Apart from that: the solos do pick up a bit on length, and with that on freak and meander factor (or does it only seem so?)

Conclusion

This disc is non offensive at first glance. It's vancant approach, however, is such that I do feel offended by it. If you add those blaring synths to that a picture is painted that can not easily be considered pretty. Those into solo-oriented jazz or jazzrock might still find something of interest. Those into progressive better steer away from it.
