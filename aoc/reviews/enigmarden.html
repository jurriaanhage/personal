Summary

The music

Previous releases from the Russian EM outfit Roz Vitalis -or at least the ones I heard- were stylistically highly similar to the Tangerine Dream sound of the early to mid seventies. This album features mainly shorter tracks that have an experimental feel similar to that in some Cuneiform avant prog releases, although their roots are still clearly audible. The sonorous sax sounds used, on the other hand, remind of Urban Sax.

Breath Of Leaves and Ah Ty are some weird tracks with both folky influences and a church organ. A combination which works well enough to be repeated in later tracks. Stress Of Tenderness has a high singing lady creating a somewhat Japanese sound. And there are more tracks that have Asian influences. Not necessarily all that markedly

Charistas features some harmonium-like keys with a lady who sings amazingly badly off key. Not the best track of the album, this is. Enigmarden is a somewhat abstract track, sticking together seemingly random bits of organ and electronics. The result is rather jumpy, to the point of being neurotic. Not quite sure what this one is about. Looking For The Stars uses a lot of tribal flute sounds. Pretty tranquil, pretty nice, but also pretty vacant. The flute is carried on into the next two tracks, although at times under attack of synths, sometimes blaring ones. Be Aware Of Strangers combines organ with flute and xylophone. Like a number of the tracks before this has the sort of experimentalism found with a number of Cuneiform bands.

Conclusion

Even though older releases sounded good enough musically, I always found the derivative nature of the music something of a problem. Roz Vitalis's music can not be called derivative anymore now, having shifted from pure EM towards experimental avant prog. An unexpected shift it is. The price for this newfound originality, however, is a a high one. One that's too high for me. This has resulted in an album that is more interesting than it is good.
