Summary

Hailing from Atlanta, GA this band is actually a duo, Hodges and Sands, both hailing from a band called Section 8. They are assisted by many on their first, selftitled, effort.

The music

Internal Combustion features two kinds of vocals: low ones and ones that are higher and certainly refer to Geddy Lee (in his later days) and in fact the vocal melody also reminds one of Rush. The song is a bit of a plodding one in which the chorus (the Lee part) stands out as very melodic. The bass work is very pronounced on this track. Just Out Of Reach, with help of drummer Jimmy Mouton, is different altogether: Martyn Bates-like vocals (the singer of Eyeless From Gaza), a great vocal melody, lots of piano and the humming of the fretless bass. At first I found the chorus a little too catchy, but especially at the end it works well. A very appealing track. Another thoroughly melodic track is The Rain And The Rainbow. The singing is a bit overdone here, I'm afraid, but again the melodies are fine and the song is built well and features a great guitar solo at the end. Like A Star refers a lot to Rush again. It's quite slow moving and again contains some good melodies. The ending is quite percussive (by Warren Z) and again this is a very nice track. Hanglider is the only instrumental of the album with the guitars being the main attraction. Still, the piano and keyboards add their two cents. The guitar soars in effect and this corresponds quite nicely with hangliding (at least my impression of this as a non-hanglider). The basswork is wonderfully moody again and the song features a very spaceous (airy?) guitarsolo. The ending is almost entirely basswork, with a slight Arabic tendency. Great track. I often think when the singing starts that they also use a few guest vocalists but everytime the credits say otherwise. This implies that Hodges can use his voice quite well and in many different ways. In Motion is a song on which the other two members of Section 8, Joey Fabian and RJ Vealey, feature (they also do on High, One To Live One To Die and The One). With a complete band, the sound is somewhat heavier. The bass is as prominent as before, but on the whole I like this song less than the previous ones. The music is more like plain rock. Time Has Frozen is also not that melodic, mostly guitar and not so interesting vocal melodies, but the percussion is quite nice. High seems to continue this line of music, but fortunately the music turns out to be a lot more melodic and succesfully so. Not Just For America starts with a strange blurp on keyboards. The song is rather bouncy and one might say a little commercial. Lots of things are happening though on this track, which has a somewhat worldlike atmosphere, but the song is not very fluent, more a bit plodding. The song moves right into One To Live, One To Die, which opens with quite a lot of percussion (very Japanlike). This song is quite rhythmical, with lots of percussion and basswork, and groovy with rather high vocals. After this not so melodic track, No Surprise has an especially good vocal melody and a ripping guitar solo to boot. The music is still quite percussive though. The lyrics are about mans relation to the environment. Closer The One opens moodily with soft vocals and eerie sounds. Then follows a rather soft part with meandering vocals, but that is rather pop like. After a short silence, the music returns. Not The One, but a slow, sparsely instrumented, version of Internal Combustion.

Conclusion

A surprisingly good album, with generally very appealing and well composed songs mostly in the Rush vein. The boys show more variety on their album though than Rush has lately been doing. Not all tracks are great and especially in the middle of the album, the music is not melodic enough for my tastes. The recording and production are certainly well done and it shows the band has recoreded it in a "state-of-the-art" home studio. The music is not overly complex or technical, seemingly being more interested in composition. Do not let them go unheard. This band should have a wide appeal.
