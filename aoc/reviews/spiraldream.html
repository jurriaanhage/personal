Summary

Kalo is a Japanese threesome, assisted by a vocalist on three tracks.

The music

Kalo has a dreamy style, reflected in gentle pianos, synth carpets, harp sounds, gently lingering guitars. At times when there's no percussion almost floating somewhere between Kitaro and new age.

The three vocal tracks -despite their English titles- are sung in Japanese. We get the more typical Japanese vocals: somewhat insecure and off key, at times painfully so, with a dreamy feel about them. Rerakamuy does have a nice guitar solo though (with artifical drums), cranking its appeal up a bit.

Aside from the apparently live drumming of Koro Uemura, there are also some drum computer sounds. The harshness of these sounds stands in sharp contrast with the gentleness this album breathes. And that doesn't work out very well. Even though these drum effects do not last very long in general, they attract the attention in the most horrible way when they come along. The final vocal track vaguely reminds me of More Than A Woman (yes, I do mean that one). As if my mood needed a plummet.

Into Existence and Sensitive Air are sudden flashy tracks, reminiscent of Tangerine Dream round 1986, both in musical style and use of keyboardsounds. With even more crappy drum sounds, irritating background loops pushing to the fore and a lot of neuroticism this forces itself on one. Even while it's pretty, well, bad, really.

Conclusion

This album holds a combination of a couple of more symphonically oriented tracks and a larger number of tracks that are really pretty much electronic music. This makes for a bit of a varied album, which is needed to keep the attention alive with this sort of music. At times the band perform well, coming up with a pretty good mix, but just too often they spoil it. The harsh drum sounds play a strong role in that, but also some of the vocal sections and synth sounds that are just a bit on the tacky or overly flashy side. The end result is a pretty decent album for those leaning towards EM that has a lot of the qualities as well as problems of eighties Tangerine Dream material, without being a rip off. This is probably partially caused by the fact that there's a bit of Kitaro around too. Still, this album serves okay as background music. Having said that, when it moves near the centre of my intention, my irritation levels start to rise handsomely. If you're not into second half eighties TD, best stay away from this one.
