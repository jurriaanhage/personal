Summary

I hope they will not make the releases of this band and its subsidiaries more complicated then it already is. This band is almost Von Daniken and almost World Turtle. There's a compilation out on Kinesis containing remixed versions of their best, most progressive, tracks. This CD contains both the album C'est la Vie and the EP The Ember. The booklet promises to release also some of their demo tapes and also that other album, Stoat and Bottle, that they made. So, Haze goes digital at last.

The music

Pure progressive on this album with intricate rhythms, lots of keyboard and guitar. It already shows in the keyboard dominated intro to Mirage. Note however that the soundquality (also because the quality of the tapes is necessarily amateuristic) leaves to be desired.

To my mind Haze is a typical neo-prog hippy outfit of the early eighties doing a lot touring and having a lot of problems on the way. They got where they are the hard way and that has its merits. The music is nice though not overly compositionally succesful, but more a nostalgic look back. A number of good tracks, most notably the longer ones.

Mirage has indeed a Middle Eastern feel to it, For Whom is a forceful track with vocals that sound a little frustrated, The Hum starts out typically progressive with a keyboardsolo, Gabadon is named after label (or the other way around) is build on a repetitive guitarriff and is not so good. Roger's Revenge is a sort of jazzy instrumental and not really good. Don't Leave me Here is rather poppy with forceful drumming, but a nice one. Fallen Leaves would according to Haze have been a good single and it is a indeed not very progressive in the strong sense, but rather relaxed and pastoral. The vocals are a bit hazy (Moody Blues) also. On better listening not a good single, because of the instrumental intermezzo, which is rather free form and not pointed enough for a single I'd say. The Load is one of the better tracks on the album, being more involved than the others with a slight of Peter Hammill reminding phrasing of music (also refers to Cliffhanger). The singing is rather manic and there are many signature changes.

Of the "bonus tracks" The Ember is the first. Of the songs on this CD, this is the newest and compositionally its somewhat better than most. The melody is good and the song is build on a repetitive, high-pitched guitar riff. The jazz-rockish The Ceiling's Coming Down is more or less a joke and should not be taken too seriously. The bass is very prominent on this one. According to Haze this track should be a mix between Iron Maiden and Duran Duran. The heaviness of the guitar indeed is comparable to Iron Maiden, but the Duran Duran reference I could not really hear. The closer of the album is the longish Mountain. Starting out with a guitar riff this is a rockish track with double vocals. The track is a litte frantic and borders in the vocal parts on the neurotic. In this track the band comes close to old progressive rock bands with the accent on rock and not on progressive and it has a jamming feel to it.

Conclusion

For the completist and I'd advise everyone to try the Kinesis compilation first; better sound and a good overview of the band. Then you can then decide to delve deeper into the enthusiastic, nostalgic, neo-progressive that Haze has to offer.
