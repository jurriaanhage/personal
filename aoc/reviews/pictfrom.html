Cyan - Pictures From The Other Side

Artist: Cyan
Title: Pictures From The Other Side
Label: SI Music Simply 54
Length(s): 60 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1994
Month of review: 07/1994

Most music and lyrics by Robert Reed with occaisional help from Nigel Voyle, A. Steele, C. Lawless and Andy Edwards. The artwork is a bit trite, very Hobbit and not really well-drawn. I like the backside though where they have projected the front on a sphere.

Summary

The most notable change on Cyan (or equivalently Mr. Robert Reed) is that Mr. Reed does not sing (except some backing) but Nigel Voyle has taken over from him.

The music

The voice of Voyle is a bit high and sharp and a real change from Reeds' Nick Barrett-like vocals. Still you get used to Voyles higher voice quite easily and I guess it's an improvement, because Cyan doesn't sound that much like Pendragon anymore.

The Hobbitist neo-prog of Cyan is still influenced a bit by Pendragon and I hear some Oldfield echoes in the guitarwork. Then again it's the keys that are most prominent on this album and what results is an album that is certainly on the softer side of neo-prog (Pendragon, Iq, Jadis well you know), with some ballads along the side making the album quite accessible, though very much in the progressive/melodic vein.

The album starts out with the long The Guardians. Very melodic of course and a lot of keys. The song is quite up-tempo and varied (as it's supposed to be when of this length). It contains very up-tempo keys, some cheesy parts, electric and acoustic guitars, march-rhythm and a few not obvious rhythms as well. The singing is quite harsh and a bit biting. The song has a an Oldfield feel at times in the guitarwork. The melodies are intricate and vary quite a lot enough to keep anyone satisfied though it makes the song a bit forced at times. The song is so full of melodies that it tends to get stuffy with it. On the whole I must admit that, save the cheesy parts just after the beginning, I like it. The next song starts out quite fairylike. The stanza is a bit to frisky for my taste. The vocals are a lot sweeter on this song though and the lyrics are very optimistic. There's an ELP organ intermezzo somewhere in this song as well. Solitary Angel is a supersweet ballad, with Reed's quavering voice inserted. Broken Man is not very interesting. A song, not very progressive, very melodic with some harmonies and bagpipes (not real ones). All Around The World is a song for a better world and a bit of a sing-a-long, the kind Paul Menel would write. The end of the song is quite good, with the piano (a bit like Supertramp or the Buggles) and some opera-like singing. Tomorrow's Here Today starts out quite like a pop song, but has an overtly jazzy intermezzo. Follow The Flow is a ballad, with only singing and piano and 'strings'. The masterpiece should have been Nosferatu starting out with an opera voice and piano. It continues menacingly as it relates to us of monsters, killers and all that. Even this song can't escape the cheesy parts though. After some five minutes we get into a softer part, which might be described as a love song within a song. This song is as expected very varied, but can't convince me entirely.

Conclusion

My idea is that some of the songs on this album are not interesting enough and some songs are just a compilation of interesting ideas. I do like the first and last song though, but mostly for the ideas not for the song. The melodies are usually alright, but sometimes a little obvious. The same goes for the execution. It might be good if Mr. Reed would try to write songs that I like in their entirety, because my CD doesn't do subskipping.
