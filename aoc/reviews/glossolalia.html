Summary

The name Steve Walsh should be familiar to all of you. The lead singer of Kansas, currently back to its old line-up including Kerry Livgren (well not really part of the band), teamed up with Trent Gardner for a second solo album on Magna Carta. The previous one Schemer Dreamer from 1980 seems from a different era.

The music

Behind some of the ugliest artwork in music today the title track opens the shiny disc. Riff dominated rock dominates this first track, but for variety includes some modern rhythms, various samples and of course the soulful vocals of Walsh. A rather shortish rock track with a heavy, catchy chorus. Heavy lyrics are a part of Serious Wreckage in which the protagonist kills a child with his car. A moving track that is musically very bare. The music resembles a ballad in a musical, also because a story is told. The lightly classical chorus is tense. After the second chorus a slow moving dark instrumental interlude sets in, to be replaced by classically styled vocals. The bridge that takes place at the end is an empassioned one. A really good song. Heart Attack is back to rock, claustrophobic rock this time with some a rather cold sound and spoken vocals. Not a strong track. Kansas might seem a tribute to, but the music is not really similar. This is more a bluesrockish piece with a fluent melodic part. Compared to the previous track an oasis of melody with a bombastic chorus including some classical interjections on violin. Again a very good track, which ends surprisingly in a very chaotic, not but unpleasing way. Slide and acoustic guitar open on Nothing. A short lullabye with a good vocal melody. The heavy rock of Haunted Man seems like an updated version of Kansas' Power album. The verses are quiet enough, the raging chorus functions as a counterweight. The longest track on the album is Smackin' The Clowns about a fire in the circus in the hometown of Walsh in his youth. A driving opening dominated by breaks gives way to the story sung in a distinctive way. Like in Serious Wreckage Walsh takes on his actor's guise, telling the story. The chorus is in my opinion not very good, too little melody. Plenty of vocal variation in this track, it seems that Walsh is telling a number of intertwined stories. A track with some very good moments, but maybe a little disjointed. That's What Love's All About is a bit of a screamy rock track. The verses are peaceful enough, a bit bouncy and the acoustic guitar melody sounds familiar. The strings are very Kansas like. Mascara Tears is like Nothing a solo track of Walsh. Opening with some thoughtful piano we come to a part where Walsh croons his lyrics in the style of seventies James Bond music. The music is more of a waltz now. Then the sun almost breaks through into the music when the vocal melody changes. If they happen to call the next Bond movie Mascara Tears, I guess Walsh has a chance of winning. The album closes with Rebecca an organ driven love song with female backing vocals.

Conclusion

Quite a nice album from Walsh. Not typically progressive I would say and certainly not much in the Kansas style at all (not necessary either). The album is a mixed bag of tracks including rock tracks (that I tend to like less than the others), but also includes some ballads, some mixed songs (like Kansas and Mascara Tears) and some moving highpoints such as Serious Wreckage and the greater part of Smackin' The Clowns.
