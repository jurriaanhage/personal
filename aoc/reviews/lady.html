Jane - Lady

Artist: Jane
Title: Lady
Label: Repertoire PMS 7046-WP
Length(s): 44 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1975/1997
Month of review: 06/1997

Line up

Gottfriend Janko - organ, synth, piano, vocals

Summary

See Here We Are Maucher was only present on one album and is now replaced by Gottfried Janko, and we might suspect that the sound of Jane has become more organic again.

The music

Well the first track shows that this is really the case: an up-tempo, driven, at times organ drenched track with unfortunately rather simple structure and the drumming is a little too monotonous and maybe also a little too up front in the mix. The vocals are mostly by the newcomer and his voice I like less than for instance Hess's vocals.

Scratches On Your Back reveals rather sad sounding vocals and the organ sound reminds me of Van der Graaf Generators Aerosol Grey Machine. Again the drumming is a little tame, but the song is varied enough to be called progressive.

Music Machine is more or less like the first track but a bit longer. The vocal melody is quite alright and the song even contains a synth solo and some rather experimental percussion at the end.

Make Me Feel Better has a very definite Procol Harum influence in the rather prominent organ part of the track. For the rest it's bluesy.

(Wishdream) Lady is sung by Panka. Although it is up-tempo it reminds me in melody of Morning Dew (but that song is MUCH better), although it reminds me more of something I can't remember the name off. A rather attractive song with again experiments on the synths.

The best track on the album is Lord Love in which the singer whines quite a lot but it is befitting the subject that is about how Lord Love can stand all these bad things happening in the world. It starts with a low organ sound, then some singing and a nice guitar solo using the same melody as the vocals. The drumming might have been a little better, and in fact it just doesn't groove as it could have. Anyway, a very good and moving track.

Midnight Mover is a long spacey jam with quite a lot of organ while the jazzy intro to Silver Knickers still yields a loose bluesy track that reminds a little of Led Zeppelin. The closing track is a slow one and shows that harmonies are not the strong point of Jane. Never were.

Conclusion

Again, organic progressive blues rock with accented vocals that sees the return of the organ and thus enjoys a more varied sound than III. Still, the power seems to have left the band in a way. This is because I find the drumming a bit tame. The music is more melodic than on III and therefore this is an album that is suited for seventies progheads that do not mind a dated sounding organ presence. Influences that I heard were Procol Harum in one song. Again, the album stays below the quality of Here We Are (and Together if you must now), but is still among the more better Jane albums.
