Summary

Well, the Eggies are back with a new album with some stunning artwork (can this be sold in the US?), which should appeal to all you spotty youngsters out there. On a more serious note, this is their fourth album, the last one of which was not a great success, but was there most mature release to date IMO. Now they've come back with heavy sonic sculpturing and a lot of attention has been devoted to production and such, so let's see what we can make of it. Also the first album with the same line-up as the previous.

The music

The first tracks out with Yeah-ie-yeah-ie-yeah, making me fear the worst. Fortunately, the track is typical in a sense of Egdon Heath, although the sound has far more rock to it, extrpolated from the previous album, which also contained a fair number of heavier tracks. The track can be divided into three moods as it were: the heavy part (mostly at the beginning), the Darling, Honey part which is more melodic and at some time is sung by two vocalists one of which sings in a desperate way. I like that. The third part as it were is yet more melodic (Time after Time...) The melodies are good and to be honest I'm not too satisfied by the rough parts, because I do not like the riff that much, but with the instrumental intermezzo and the good melodic parts, one might say that it only helps to bring these out better.

The next track also starts out heavy and it seems that this is a new aspect to the music of EH. Again, I'm not entirely satisfied with this less melodic approach. As with the previous track however they make amends with some very nice melodies and it also helps of course for variation. Again, this track contains every aspect of progressive rock as we have come to love it: good singing melodies, non-standard drumming, lots of variation, lots of different melodies, several solo on different instruments. The end of track is characterized by a more chaotic approach with many vocals intertwined with a bombastic ending to it. Very good.

The third track is more of the new style but for those reminiscent of the EH of Him the Snake and I, there's a lot of eyewinks to that earlier album as well. This track is less heavy than the previous ones, making it more melodic. The guitar riff is good on this one.

Telepathic is rather the ballads from Him, the Snake and I. The melody is good and the track also reminds me of both Kayak and Queen. There's not much variation (I mean, it is a ballad), although the penultimate part is very percussive and the last part is a plushy carpet of synths. A good song.

The start of the next track is hectic and reminds me a bit of the instrumental part of Supertramp's Fool's Overture. The subdued vocals after this go into the up-tempo melodic rock chorus. A good melody by the way and it works out well. Again, enough tricks and variation in this track to keep even the most easily tiring listener occupied. The middle part is rather like Alan Parsons Project (Fall of the House of Usher) or Morricone, but that's simply because an orchestra is employed there. Some threat in that orchestra. After this the song as it were returns to its rock format and continues where the orchestra broke in.

The next track starts acoustic and is a little too sweet. After a few verses it gets a little louder and a good melody sets in, saving the song from boredom and making it flower. Especially the guitar is well done on this track.

The longest track is usually the best on a progressive album and this also holds for this album. This track is really very good and rivals the great Secret Fence from the SI Compilation album and Thousand Stories of Him, the Snake and I as well as the title track of the Killing Silence.

The song starts out with some Morse code and some hardly audible singing. It's about the crash of a UFO and is told by someone not present. The first verse is sung with a sort of desperation and somewhat subdued, while the chorus is more loud and up-tempo and very good. The next verse is rather bouncy with much percussion. Then it moves into what you might call a haunting extra chorus. Then we get into the intermezzo: a haunting one with lots of sounds, but little melody.

Around eight minutes we get a thoughful, delicate piano intermezzo, after which a build-up starts with bass, acoustic guitar and percussion. The end is majestic in a way and keeps on building up with organ and electric guitar at the end. The build sounds like the music is plodding along against a strong head wind.

The last track starts out very loud and bombastic. The vocals parts is more in the background a little dreary. The song is about renewing parts of people to extend their life and beauty. After the dreary sounding verses, the tables are turned, and the song becomes very heavy. A lot of variation in this heavy part with all kinds of vocals and different rhythms and haunting melodies on the synths.

The last track has some typical EH piano as a ghost-part. Possible because this would otherwise be the first EH album without an instrumental.

Conclusion

A rather heavy album, but cohesive and no uninteresting tracks. They've certainly worked hard on this one with all the vocals and tricks to make it more interesting. Fortunately the melodies are still good and it's not just playing in the studio for them. Heavily recommended for neo-proggers and a sure buy for EH fans. On the whole probably somewhat more "progressive" than the Snake.
