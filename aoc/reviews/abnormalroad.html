Summary

A do-it-mostly-yourself musician from the States who recently got to promoting and rereleasing his music.

The music

Opening with Divided By Three this is rather typical for Ray's work: dominated by guitar (both melodic and more meandering), featuring some flat sounding drum programming and of mediocre production, it is the composition not the rendition that seems to count. Also as a song this one does little for me, being a bit too thematic and repetitious. There is some variation in speed, but it is all pretty straightforward. Your Short Life is the longest track on the album and opens with darker guitar work. Also repetitious, but now I like it. This track also features the hoarse vocals of Ray that sound pretty badly recorded. This song is a bit more complex and I like it more. With American Sheets we move into guitar pyrotechnics including the background soloing by clarinettist Rick Schultz. Actually having reviewed two others discs of Ray, the songs here do not offer much difference with respect to the ones on those discs. Ed (Part 2) is like the opener a bit of a repetitive tune with plenty of parts intertwined. A Find Predicament is a driven piece with some tension building in there. Still for the most part this is guitar hero type of music. Ideas is a vocal piece, not very appealling to me, but There's A Riot Outside is nicely experimental. A plodding piece with plenty of the(matic) guitarwork, but also some fiddly stuff on the keyboard. Something a Little More Original is the next one up and basically a rather nice guitar instrumental. No Air To Breathe sounds rather live with the clarinet far in the back. Basic rock this, repetitious, but not necessarily monotonous. For the rest of the album I found little to tell about, having said it already. Oh wait, there's this longish one Bomb Day that combines some Hendrixian playing with the typical poppy riffs of Ray. It also includes a fairly Science Fictiony opening. Incendiary Larry has some soundscapes tones at the end and some effects. The bonus track includes some vocal warblings.

Conclusion

Like the previous two discs of Ray reviewed by yours truly, this is a guitar album with rather bad production and soundquality and a drum computer instead of a live one. The vocals of Ray are a bit hoarse and not well produced. The odd combination of clarinet and guitar is still there and for the most part the songs here feature quick fingered guitar playing (notwithstanding the tempo as played by the drums; I mean the music is not really fast itself). The music is definitely structured there being little jamming around.
