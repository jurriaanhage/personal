Summary

Xang is a new band from the North of France, Cambrai in fact. The band is on a new label called Galileo from Switzerland.

The music

The album opens with The Revelation (Gaia). Darkly toned keyboards, lots of cymbals moving into an up-tempo keyboard riff followed quickly by driving rhythm guitar work. The keyboard work is somewhat in Mark Kelly style, while the guitar is very varied (but plays a supporting role for the most part) and the pace of the song slackens and quickens quite a few times. Very symphonic and energetic, and with a lot of swing. In Misgivings the guitar also gets a part in the melodic whole. The intro is reminiscent of Pink Floyd, but that comparison fades as the music becomes very energetic again with long meandering keyboard solo's. At the end the pace goes up even further with some powerful drumming. Comparisons are a bit hard to make. Browsing through their list of influences I see nothing that is similar to what these guys make. The best comparison I can now think of is Gerard an energetic version of Odyssice or a more symphonic version of Gordian Knot. My Own Truth is the third track and it has a dark edge to it. Again, lots of keyboards and plenty of signature changes. What bothers me a bit is that some of the music is lacking a bit in memorable melodies and sometimes (but rarely for this type of music) I get the impression that the music is not really going anywhere. The guitar sound is quite heavy here and the music moves into the direction of progressive metal. The Prediction features piano, washes of drums and is, because of the guitar work, close to jazzrock. Shortly however, the music takes a turn: good tension building leads to dazzling keyboards. It can now be compared to Faith No More's Woodpecker From Space. Then the music abruptly dies down to feature only soft hammered piano and a softly crying guitar. The rock returns with a screaming guitar solo. The best track so far. On The dream we hear the music replayed from a...tape? It is followed by some peaceful piano playing. This is in fact the right time for it and shows also the melodic side of the band. Halfway through the album we come to he ten minute Bitterness. After an intro on guitar making me think of bands like Threshold, the keyboard sets in again with its meandering solo's. The music then winds down very slowly to something akin to Camel with very sensitive guitar playing. The Choice opens in Floyd style with relaxed drumming (for a change). However, this is not for long as the band erupts again into driven, up-tempo prog. Just after the middle we break into a passage on church organ, but soon the guitar comes along for the ride. Then the guitar takes over with a jazzrock. The closing track is The Light (And two out of four band members profess a liking of Spock's Beard?). Well actually the band goes on the same footing as it did mixing jazzrocking guitar parts with atmospheric interludes and an orchestral break to round the song off. This song is not as long as it seems. After 6.46 or so, the sound is cut off to return at eleven and a half with droning sounds of planes coming down and an occasional acoustic guitar.

Sometimes I really have the impression that I'm not listening to a record but really hear them play live. I'm not sure what that means. Either the sound is very alive, or the band tends to draw out the songs a bit longer than I would expect. It has certainly nothing to do with the recording quality which is good. I can very well believe that this band comes over live terrifically.

A small note about the lyrics. The album has lyrics, but there's no vocalist, so the thing to do is to read the lyrics to this concept album while it plays. The lyrics are in fact quite good and the story is quite original as well.

Conclusion

Typical instrumental progressive. On the one hand the band takes its time to let the music evolve and the musicians meander. On the other hand the band can stop short and take a turn into a very different direction. What I liked about the album is the high energy level and drive, while the melodic part of the music is for me somewhat below par. What I like less is that not everything sounds as "meaningful" as it could be and also that sometimes I feel the melodic side to be lacking. In retrospect this is a bit nitpicking and I'm sure that everybody who likes this type of music will love this pure symphonic record. The band does have its outings into jazzrock and progressive metal, but the sound is definitely prog. An album choking with ideas and lots of enthusiasm and I have the feeling that many proggers will love this one.
