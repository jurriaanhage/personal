Arcansiel - Normality Of Perversion

Artist: Arcansiel
Title: Normality Of Perversion
Label: Mellow Records MMP 203
Length(s): 40 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1994
Month of review: 02/1996

Line up

Gianni Lavagno - drums
Gianni Opezzo - guitars, piano
Paolo Carroni - lead vocals
Paolo Baltaro - keyboards, bass, some vocals

Tracks

1) The Girl From Heaven And The Wolf Don't Care
If You Are Glad To Live 6.36
2) Holy Wolf Suite 14.12
3) The End 7.05
4) What Does "The Night Becomes True" Mean? 6.14
5) I'm Crashing Videotapes Behind The Mirrors
Against Your T.v. Set 5.52

Summary

A sudden change of personnel in this band. Both Clerici, Galletti and Marinoni have left and have been replaced and especially the leaving of Galletti is felt, because he wrote all the songs. These days, the tracks are written by newcomer Baltaro. The other new member is Carroni. It is probable that a departure from the 'old' Arcansiel will become apparent.

The music

The band is now quite a hard rocking unit, with a vocalist having a voice that can be both low and high (like the guy singing Whiteout on Don Airey's K2), a hardrock singer. Unfortunately he uses quite a lot of Yeahs etcetera and he also screams a lot. Well, can't win 'em all. The first track is then what we might expect a past paced melodic track, neighbouring to hard rock. He ends rather abruptly with a quick fade-out of the female backing vocals. Still, it sounds a little amateuristic to me.

The next track starts out with fast paced piano, grinding to a halt. The vocalist tries to introduce rap into progressive rock in this track. The vocalist also has quite an accent, making it hard to understand him, but fortunately lyrics have been provided for. Personally I think the melody lines, especially for the vocals, are too weak for such a long track.

The third track is something entirely different, being the kind of jazz that is played in dark and obscure bars, with a large role for the saxophone.

The fourth track is kind of a ballad. Very melodic and the melodies are well enough, but the presentation is a little, well, not too subtle and elegant mostly because of the vocals.

The fifth one isn't much of anything. Some accordeon, some bar sounds, some female backing vocals and a little bluesy in atmosphere.

Conclusion

An album with less progressive content than their previous album. I wouldn't call this band Arcansiel anyway and a new name should have been chosen. The result is ambiguous, introducing 'rap' on the one hand and some late night bar jazz on the one hand combined with rather average progressive rock, not enriched by the hard rock voice (and not to forget the excessive amount of yeahs and whatever) of Carroni. The album has its moments, but they are too few by far. Should I have to, I'd choose 1 and 4 as the best tracks. All in all, they have managed to leave the trodden path of progressive rock unto other paths combining a little metal, a little rap, a little jazz and some blues into a surprising coherent album, but unfortunately the music has become too simple and mainstream and the presentation too amateuristic to be of notice.
