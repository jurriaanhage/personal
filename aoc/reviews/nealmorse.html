Summary

The first album out on Ear Candy, a division of Inside Out, I was surprised to receive this first solo album of Neal Morse. This guy just can't sit still (without recording an album). but like he said himself: he's not getting any younger.

The music

The albums opens with Living Out Loud. The vocals are of course recognizably Morse, but maybe slightly more nasal. A difference with Spocks Beard is also that the singing is more relaxed and less over the top. The music itself is also notably less bombastic. This first track has a Bruce Hornsby kind of piano riff and shows that this album might very well be an attempt at singer/songwriter music, while retaining some of the elements of Socks Beard. There's quite a large organ presence on this album, but always underneath. Acoustic guitar and a folky tune pave the way for an optimistic sounding track with depressing "loser" lyrics. A strange contrast. The folky tune is recognizably Spocks Beard (but since Morse writes the music there, one might also say recognizably Morse). After a slow dark interlude we end up in a distorted solo and the vocal part returns. Landslide is a rathr sparse, but very melodic track. It might be likened to June from Kindness Of Strangers in style, but without the harmonies. Goosebumps during the chorus. A very moody and relaxed track. That Which Doesn't Kill Me is one of those against all odds-keep your chin up-tracks. This song reminds me musically of Francis Dunnery's solo records being quite a straightforward sounding rock track. Everything Is Wrong is another sad, moody track lots of beautiful piano. Nowhere Fast is a melodic ditty with funny lyrics about a one sided infatuation. Emma is a sad track with strings, a bit folky in the middle. One might think of the solo album of Damian Wilson here. A Whole Nother Trip is the progressive piece of the album, taking some 24 minutes, divided into four parts. The first of these is the Bomb That Can't Explode. After a rather dissonant opening we get into Latin style with a cello no less. A good long track with some memorable melodies and some direct pointers to the Spocks Beard oeuvre. The next part, Mr. Upside Down is a strongly rocking and also quite funky. Heavy guitars, Yeah-Yeahs, well you know, the works. The Man Who Would Be King might remind some of Senor Velasco from The Light, while It's Alright is a soothing closer for the album.

I can't really see why the Whole Nother Trip should be regarded as a single track as well, since the songs stand on their own.

Conclusion

Not for nothing has a new subdivision of Inside/Ou been called into existence. The music here is not prog, far from it, but the music does retain some of the characteristics of Spocks Beard: trying to sound larger than life, somewhat similar lyrical content. For comparison well it may help to think of people like Francis Dunnery. I like the quieter songs best. Stylistically the album is quite broad, especially the closing epic with excursions into Latin, reggae, funk and of course rock.
