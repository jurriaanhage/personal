Summary

Hal Darling send me this album for reviewing so that is just what I'll do.

The music

It should be prohibited. Releasing a CD under the name Darling with just a drumkit, keyboards and sequencing (almost) as instrumentation and then being able to construct such an attack of neurotic percussion that not just the neighbors but virtually everyone in my street decides that they will spend the night in a neighboring village. The inaccessibility of the track is mostly due to the high speed and contrariness of the track and the large number of tempo changes that are spilled all over unsuspecting listeners. All in all Hal makes a kind of music comparable in mood to Art Zoyd and Univers Zero but usually quite neurotic and using mostly keyboards and very importantly his drum kit, because that is his main instrument. This almost implies that the melody is not so important on this disc and that is indeed the case. Because of that rarity, these melodic restpoint are are chloride free swimming pools in a desert. I have to say however that the music is certainly not whimsical and the tracks can be listened to as songs, although very neurotic and frantic ones and I like them (mostly). An expection to the neuroticness is Country Wedding which is a hommage to his wife and is melodic and delicate and can be compared to Yanni. Forever Again is also a rather quiet piece with a slow middle part while 48 Ahornstrasse has such an accessible melody (according to Hal) that he had to destroy its sweetness by putting in some offbeat drumsolo (brain-cell incinerating, Hal?). You might have noticed by now, that some of my comments have had to be culled from the booklet accompanying the CD and this is true. The meaning and coming into existence of all tracks have been meticulously noted down for you to read and we find there that Hal's approach to music has a lot to do with humour and also with putting himself down. Whether this is to do away with criticism or just a healthy look upon himself might be hard to tell, but there's not much difference in quality between the songs he puts down and the ones he does not. To conclude with Hal: "if "progressive" mean "moving forward", then take the next step. Progressive redefined"

Conclusion

A nice album, but very inaccessible and specifically interesting for drummers and advanced neurotics (like myself). People fond of RIO, Univers Zero, Art Zoyd and you name it might want to check this out.
