Summary

When I played this CD for the second time, my mother passed by and asked me if this might be Queen. Now, my mother doesn't know anything really about that kind of music, but I think she was referring to the new singer of Egdon Heath, Maurits Kalsbeek, because in the sheet that was accompanying this CD, it was mentioned that the guy sounds like Mercury.

The music

Stubborn as I am, I do not really agree, though at times (be they seldom) he does sound a bit like him. Another thing is that he sounds a bit like a he has sung with some hardrock band (the way he uses his voice, not high but a bit controlled, like Dio and Tate do in the lower regions of their voice). Well, never mind, fact is that Kalsbeek has a good voice. Does this change of singer have any effect on their music (I mean their last one The Killing Silence was a very good album, though I think In the City might have been better if it was produced, played and sung better)? Not really at first listen, but they have grown more diversi- fied; there's more than ordinary prog going around, they have become more accessible. Still the keyboards are still very pronounced, and for the good listener there are a good many hidden keyboardmelodies to find. The first song on the album is not a song, it's an instrumental. It's really Egdon old style and I wouldn't be surprised if this song was written after Stempel left (or maybe before) and that that's why they haven't made a lyric to it. Also it might be seen as a sequel to the closing instrumental on TKS. I think the instrumental is a strange way to start and it doesn't really make any sense putting it on as a first track. Second on is Gringo. This song is symphonic in the sense that it contains a lot of keyboards, but for the rest it's mostly a song, not terribly complicated but a terrific melody. The subject is also very recent, being the problems with fugitives in mostly Germany (I'm afraid though that they might have misunderstood the word Gringo here, because they think it means Stranger, whereas I believe it to be specific to foreign white people, but I am not really sure (I'm only Dutch myself)). On a Bench is very subdued with only piano and the lyrics are about people without homes, that sleep on benches. A moving song. Witness has the spirit of the One for the Vine Genesis but also of the later Genesis (Horn-sounds). It has some different moods, but in the first part the most of it is a bit haunted on the up-tempo side, but having heard half the song we have a soft piano intermezzo, continued by a softly sung part with which the song ends. Lyrically the protagonist has been trialed for murder. Mother's Dream starts with a melody that might sound a bit Easternish with some Ravel thrown in. The singer has many voices we learn, some is sung in a agressive way (What! Egdon aggressive?) and some parts are sung in a soothing way. The sbuject is again interesting: it's about a girl who's a mannequin and who is asked if this is what she wanted or what her mother wanted. After a while the intro returns, while Maurits sings along. Then the keyboard comes up and it's time for some reflecti- on... Now we come to what I think is the best song of all, Slightly in Des- pair, and though I'm not all for ballads, it is one, but the melody is just right. It's moody while the singer accuses himself of being to selfish in his relationships and the refrain has a very good melody to it. Satellite is not like the former at all. Again a varying song with soft and aggressive parts, with the latter being more noteworthy in this song. A very worthwhile song about not being alone anymore (the satelli- te's are watching us). The song sounds a bit like Technocracy by Winter (if that's any help), but better. The last song is again very varied (am I repeat-repeat-repeating my- self?) and it's probably the most progressive of them all. A bit up- tempo like the former, it puts all in perspective by stating that it's talking about things in stories is easy when you don't have to live them. After a heavy part, we have a very quiet intermezzo and the singing starts again accompanied by the driving of the drums and then finally the guitar gets it's chance (and takes it) accompanying the singer until again we reach a quiet part and then all is peace. Curtains close.

Conclusion

I think because it sounds warmer than TKS, that it is easier to like this album. Still, it certainly is less progressive. Strangely enough it is also more varied, but no varied in tempo, but in musical style, with songs that border on symphonic hardrock and songs that contain only a piano. The lyrics are also very good and thought about and as far as I'm concerned the Egdons have never written a song as good as Slightly in Despair (not real prog, but a song, a composition in the real sense. Something you might hear on the radio sometime (and what a relief that would be)). If I should note what I think most noteworthy of this CD, I think it would be vocal melodies which are varying from very good to great throughout the album. Very worthwhile.
