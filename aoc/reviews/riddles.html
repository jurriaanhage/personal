Summary

Busy with progressive hard rock since 1989, this is the first album by this band from Colorado, that has come to my ears. In comparison with earlier releases Leviathan (EP) and Deppest Secrets Beneath, this is the first release of the band in this line up, only Skeen and Lutzow were present in the older line-up.

The music

Either an album like this begins with a sweet and soft keyboardintro or as in ths case the band starts of loudly with banging drums, a wall of guitar and some keyboards more or less in the background. On the other hand some fastpaced acoustic guitar is involved and the drumming is a little offbeat, making the album a possible candidate for the label "progressive". In this track that is mostly built on a vacuumcleaner guitarsound and menacing keyboardriffs repetitively thrown in, the (rather high) vocals and the vocal melody are the weak point. First off the vocals of the singer are not to my liking and I'm not sure whether I should be glad or not that the vocals are in the back of the mix and he doesn't articulate well either. A far cry from well-known progressive metalsingers like La Brie, Tate and less well-known Damian Wilson. For the rest plenty of variation and tempo changes, and I like the way the keyboard is used, although one has to listen well; the guitar is the important instrument here.

The next track starts in mid-tempo and is a bit Saga like. Again the vocal melody is not an attractive one. After a minute or so, the drums become more heavy and the keyboards prepare the way for the guitar. After the guitar has had its way, the song takes a turn for the threatening, with what sounds to be a quick bass in the back and a frantic keyboardriff.

The next one up is a bit of a tame track in the beginning with not so much guitar (lead or otherwise), and only after four minutes or so do they really enter into the song. The often used vocal melody in this song is better than earlier.

On Pages of Time, we are advised not to become poets but tv repairman and the band wants us to turn back the pages of time. Well, I'm not all for that, especially going back to the days of the big band. As might be expected this song is a lot heavier than the previous one and starts of well, but again the vocal melody is not very interesting and the singing has more or less become yelling. A nervous obtrusive track.

Are First Loves Forgotten? is the longest track on the album (yea, we know, we can read). This is a ballad with mostly acoustic guitar and the singing is more agreeable here. The percussion is nicely dosed on the background and after more than 5.5 minutes the electric guitar sets in slowly, and I lost the song among the scales that are played quickly and with little feeling. At the end the sad vocals of the beginning return.

So Where Is God? is a complex track starting out with heavy guitar and is interrupted at times by a part full of breaks and

The fastpaced energetic Confusion contains modified vocals which works in a positive way and Don't Look At Me contains the first good vocal melody (in the chorus) and good use of keyboards (also a little against the grain at the end).

The last track Passion Above All Else is a slow starter with sadly sung vocals. The vocals are doubled here and that works well. This first part is comparable to Are First Loves Forgotten? Probably the best track on the album, although in view of the length I would have expected a bombastic climax to the track and now the song is a bit long for so little variation.

Conclusion

Complex enough and enough keyboards to be named progressive, but on the whole not a very satisfying album. This probably has to do with the fact that there is not much melody and especially the vocal melodies deserve more attention to make it interesting for me. The vocal is a bit high and thin and I'm not too fond of that either. Comparable to Dream Theater but less melodic and a stronger liking for playing scales.
