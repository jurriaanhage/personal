Summary

During the late eighties and early nineties, this Dutch poprock group with some very definite progressive tastes did quite well for themselves, with two moderately succesfull albums, and a fair amount of airplay for their singles. More often than not, those singles had B-sides, not released on the albums. Recently, there was a discussion on this band in the Dutch music newsgroup. My co-writer, alongside some positive words on the band, expressed his regret at still missing some of the B-sides. To his surprise, this enticed one of the original band members to respond to him, offering to close this lacuna in his (and my) collection. As a means of retribution we offered to review the two I Spy albums, thereby re-introducing these two underrated discs.

The music

The International Feel is a rather odd opener, with its Tom Waits like vocals, and choir towards the end. Despite its oddity, this was I Spy biggest succes, making it into the lower regions of the Dutch charts. This track rolls over...

... into Coney Island, carried by soundeffects. This track is based on Steegstra's strong vocals, aside keys and effects. Slowly gaining momentum, the mount towards a climax is ascended. Very strong track, the album's highpoint, through trickling water moving into...

...Picnic At Hanging Rock, which has a more laid back atmosphere and has more guitar and rock. This song is based on the movie of the same title (or is there a book as well?), about a couple of school girls on a picnic disappearing, never to be seen again. Towards the end the instrumental section is closed off by a melancholic 'time to go...'. Also a strong one.

Guide-line has a strong and pushy intro, making clear why this was chosen as a single, despite its length. This track is carried by a sonorous throbbing bass line, the way we heard more often during the era.

Private Room is a tranquil track, with trickling guitars and keys. It has the feel of an intermezzo, especially since it's wedged between two high profile tracks, but it can't quite justify its length.

Last Dance is started by a very strong theme, and features a bridge that has to be heard. The build up towards the climax on this one is quite wonderful. Unfortunately the drumming on this single is a tad on the choppy side. This can't hide its strength, though.

So-long is a slower track, about 'Tarzan' musing over things not working out with 'Jane'. This one leans very strongly to the melancholic side.

Elisabeth Dane rises out of the flames of So-long with an odd, somewhat experimental intro. The oddness remains, alongside moodiness and melancholy.

The title track is more open and melodic, almost romantic even, at times.

Solitary Confinement gives the album a fitting finale, feeling as solitary as its title might suggest.

Conclusion

Not only does this album contain quite a number of very strong songs, it also has a certain feel of solitarity and melancholy that had me fall for it completely. Its uniqueness is still quite apparent fourteen years later.
