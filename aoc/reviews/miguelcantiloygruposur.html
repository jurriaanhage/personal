Summary

Viajero Inmovil from Argentia continues its strings of Southamerican (re)releases. This time an album from 1975, as always packaged in a slim digipack. Short album this time, without bonus material.

The music

Sur Alabanzaz is the up-beat opener. The wailing vocal choir makes it sound very seventies, or maybe even end of the sixties. Although it ends on a hectic note, with a screaming Cantilo showing the back of his tongue, I am reminded of Jesus Christ Superstar.

Naturangel is the natural follow-up in which the band takes it a bit easier. Melodic vocals over acoustic guitar is pretty standard. Although it starts a bit later here, the pace does set in, accompanied by varied vocals. The guitar play has an excellent drive, but the vocals sound a bit messy. We continue with melodic electric guitar, after which we return to the soft vocal parts. An eruptive track with both rough and clean vocals.

Musi and its follow up Demian are mid-tempo track, both with good vocal melodies and the captivating voice of Cantilo. His voice is rather high, and not always steady, but he can hold my attention and has emotion.

Una Manera De Llegar is a bit more up-beat, but again has some excellent vocal melodies, although if you prefer your meticulously planned and timed, then you might be less enthused. The music is rather percussive on this one, while the violin gives it a special feel. The vocal may remind one of Yes, although I find it hard to categorize the music as progressive at all, at least not in the sense that it is derivative of the bands we have come to know as the initiators of the style. The music is varied, richly arranged and has plenty of dynamics, which gives it a sophistication which in my viewpoint should be enough. The fact that it was recorded ages ago, might be a problem for some: the productional quality is not that high. It also gives the music a dated feel, although the songs stand up.

La Leyenda Del Retorno is a psychelike guitar rocker. There is a sense of mystery to this one, with a Cantilo searching for the top of his lungs. He finds it. This comes close to the Early British Progressive Rock movement. The violin adds a hectic dose at the end.

Algo Esta Por Suceder has a bit of a Focus feel. Vocally I reminded of the early Rush years, and the song also has the type of dynamics that Rush has. The guitar is maybe a bit more Santana styled, with a hint of blues.

Las Imagenes Qu Ves Entre Las Nubes closes down this rather short album. On this one, the vocals are very much in the back, behind the violin. A bit of a chaos, although the driven rock that burst loose halfway is more than okay.

Conclusion

If you are into the rocky side of the early wave of British progressive rock, then this might be something for you. The spread is large: from soothingly acoustic to meandering sax with screaming vocals and some psyche guitar thrown in various places, this comes out as a dynamic affair with some really good vocal melodies. The vocal harmonies give the album much of its dated feel. Cantilo can both whisper and screech, and delivers an emotional piece of work, on which electric and acoustic go hand in hand, and which has plenty of drive.
