Summary

In the booklet Louie explains how he came to like and love progressive rock and also about his audition for Dream Theater and also some other things, like having played with Daevid Allen, Steve Hackett and Richard Sinclair and a host of others. Promising I must agree. The timings as you can see, are even a bigger mess than IQ had on their The Wake CD.

The music

The first track reminds me quite a lot of Vangelis I must say and I do really like it. It doesn't sound a bit trite, although it's typically and opener. And the good reader will of course ask: where's part 2? On the next CD, maybe?

The next track is more percussive, but still very much on the synth. The sound is a little rough with a lot of varying. Not so melodic as the previous, and he seems to have been inspired by a classical composer. Bombastic stuff, with lots of rhythm. Who is Nico B.?

The next track bring a little quiet into my room, after the almost over the top predecessor. Rather moody beginning, here, while the continuation is more thematic and the melody is has an Arabian feel. Again I'm reminded of Steve Hillman here. Some TD influences, but more on a rock basis, making it sound more in the direction of Hawkwind. Also the lesser known Cybotron can be heard here. This song also shows that it's not just keys here, because it also contains a very nice guitar solo. After a lot of bombasm, a some more relaxed part, but the bombastic sound absolutely returns.

Colleen has a Chris Isaac ring to it. The sound of the acoustic guitar is very spaceous.

Mermaid in the Mist has a beautiful and delicate pianic melody and also the percussion is by piano (oh, how I love that). The fact however why lyrics have been included in the booklet stays a puzzle to me. There's really no singing here.

The plodding Sultan's Waltz has a Vangelis ring to it. Not one of the most earstriking tracks on the CD.

The first vocal track is Blinded Eyes on which Louie himself sings. The melody is rather simple, but the vocal melody is good and the singing is a little over the top (compare Roger Waters, but maybe a little less so). Spacey stuff and good. The picking up of the tempo make this a very powerful combination of synth, prog and space rock.

Circles is a bombastic ditty, with a lot of sounds and intricate melodies, but not very memorable (like most other electronic stuff). The break into acoustic guitar is very abrupt and one might get the idea that this is an entirely different track. Then the tempo picks up again, and the song becomes a rather happy one. The fade out is very short.

Braindance is a more relaxed and more typical EM. Kind of repetitive a la TD, but not full sequencers like them. There are some vocodals here, with the rhythmbox now fully awakened and even a bass solo.

Aurora Borealis is dreamy and cosmic, while Beauty in Her Eyes is a romantic track on piano. Then comes the watery/bubbly Will I Dream. A little percussive also and not very melodic, but nice.

The closer is the vocal, medieval The Alchemist and the Magician. The lyrics are more or less along the lines of Lucassen's The Final Experiment and musically one might say the same. Lots of variation in this track. I'm not too fond of the very melodic singalong vocal melody and also the lyrics are a bit trite, but hey, can't have it all.

Conclusion

That Louie is a professional guy, is easily recognized on this CD. The sound is very loud and clear and the music is usually quite bombastic. The music has influences from space rock (Hillman), electronic music (Vangelis ao), but it can be subtle and simply beautiful as well. Best tracks are (arguably) 1, 3, 5, 7 and 13. Well worth a listen, especially for the EM oriented progger.
