Summary

See also their earlier tape Volar&eacute;. This tape might not be available, but I thought I'd give you my thoughts on them anyway.

The music

In contrast with the line-up of the Volar&eacute; tape, the cello and ocarina are not present on this tape. The music has not changed overly much, still a bit jazzy and mostly in the Happy the Man vein. The guitar and the keyboards are now even more important, with again a big role for the Fender. There's a little more fire in Blitz then on songs found on Volar&eacute;, but also because of the soundquality, do not expected wonders here. Most of the music is still a little subdued, but it can be noticed that at least the guitar wants to get out. Certainly a song to keep in mind by the band for their CD (when will that be then?). The guitarsound on the first track can even be called distorted and gives rise to a King Crimson (THRaKish) sound.

The next track is a litte bit more jazzy with prominent sax. This song is centered on the playful melody on guitar and the estranging, claustrophobic distorted, well, yes, what? All in all the bands tends to move between recent KC and old Happy the Man, and although they lack the real intensity of the first, they at least try to come close. They almost succeed. I have to admit though that in some way, the compositions of a band as KC have more direction and sound more natural. I do think that this is personal, but so much has been put into these three compositions, that although it might be fun listening to live, the attention does wander.

Conclusion

Nice tape. A little heavier and some KC influences can be heard in addition to the jazzy Happy the Man influences. The compositions themselves (especially the last one) are sometimes a little too disjointed, making me wonder at times what they're at.
