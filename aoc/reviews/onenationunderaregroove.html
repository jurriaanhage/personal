Summary

Life is like a box of hardcore jollies, you never know what you're gonna get... Magna Carta's sub label Magnatude releases this, an album containing 'explorations of the music of George Clinton's Parliament and Funkadelic years', played by a newly thrown together group of jazz/funk musicians.

The music

Opener One Nation Under A Groove gets the groove in right away. (Not Just) Knee Deep has a bit of salsa, a boppy bass and a clear piano. Cosmic Slop is somewhat psychedelic, has a nice bit of organ, and is somewhat reminiscent of King Crimson soundscaping and Ozric Tentacles bubbling. Flash Light is a bit on the saxy side, despite the jangly guitars. Not all that interesting. Bop Gun is more balanced, also containing organ and turntable. Mothership Connection is pretty jazzy, based on guitars and sax, with only the bobbing bass giving away its funk origin. The sax towards the end is almost fit for a smoky bar. Give Up The Funk maintains all the groove its title would belie, even taking it Up For The Downstroke. Chocolate City has a strong bass with sax & scratch. The climax is nicely eclectic. Closer Hit It And Quit also features some saucy sax and a persistent organ line.

Conclusion

In general you can say that the music on this album has more of a jazzy, and less of a funky feel than the original Clinton material. Which is not a bad thing for me, considering I'm more into jazz than funk. Despite this modern interpretation, always a dangerous process, having led to disasterous renditions in the past, the music hasn't lost its sassiness and soul. At the end of the day, though, there's a still a little more funk in this one than would be best to pull in prog lovers. If you like your odd bit of funk, though, you're probably gonna like this. Best tracks are (not Just) Knee Deep, Cosmic Slop and the two closers.
