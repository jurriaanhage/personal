Summary

Recorded only very shortly (May/June 2000) this is the twelfth or so release of Rick Ray.

The music

The opener of this album is, when you discount the rather noisy overall sound, quite a nice track. Notwithstanding the rather straightforward riffing in some parts, there's more to this song than it at first seems, especially the intertwined guitar work, the more highpitched playing (to be heard as a kind of chorus in this instrumental) and the reversed percussion add a nice touch to this track. Robot Assassins opens in slow tempo, in the typical lo-fi production of Rick Ray's other records. The vocals of Rick Ray have been vocoded in this track, a wise decision I think. First of all his voice is not beautiful and secondly it fits well in with the claustrophobic sounding music and the lyrical subjects that are often about estrangement and such within society, now or in the future. Notwithstanding the basis of the music is the blues, and this song is not as proggy as the previous one in which more was occurring. The music does have its melodic, ascending chords, and I think I also hear Rick Schultz's clarinet in the back. As always the drums are programmed, in my opinion not a good choice, because of lack of groove. This is in many cases at least partially compensated for by the flashy guitar playing, but the music does not have the drive I feel it should have. Somewhere around the fifth minute there's a slight turn in the song, the drums have a different sound to them and the guitar playing becomes more experimental and sound oriented (instead of riff oriented). After a simple rock track such as Little Zombies (with lyrics that do not seem to fit the music quite well) we return to with a mid tempo instrumental with meandering guitarsolo's by Ray and underneath a rather strong bass presence. Quite a succesful track with some nice melodies to contrast with the more free form already mentioned solo's. Hypnotic Neurotic has mars rhythms, the clarinet of Schultz almost inaudible and the fast soloing guitar of Ray up front, both in melodic and in meandering fashion. On the whole a melodic and in a way optimistic track. The "title track" Mind Control Incorporated opens a bit percussively and this time the clarinet is a bit more audible. Still, not a very interesting track, being built on a not so distinctive vocal melody, in fact the lyrics are more spoken than sung. The keyboard effects seem to be the other dominating factor in this track. The sound is a bit ehm...as blurry is to pictures. Napoleon Brainapart is a very slow instrumental, although as always the fingers of Ray are moving fast, hence small notes. Melodic, straightforward and rather clear sounding. The opening of This Is The Place moves into the direction of space rock. The song has a nice eerie intermezzo with high ethereal sounding guitar in the middle, after which the song gets underway again ending in an extensive guitar solo. Attack Of The Mindless sounds a bit different after the previous, as if the production is different. Prescription Of Ignorance is quite repetitive in structure, and because of the keyboards effects sounds quite spacey. The melodic keys opening of Looking All The Time are a good idea, but the sound doesn't come out well. Dark guitars fill The Delusion that has a bas signal to noise ratio, possibly because of the samples. The Stranger is the not very striking vocal closer of the album.

Conclusion

Most records of Rick Ray sound alike. Some records have more vocals than others. Some even have different vocalists participating, but for the most part we have (many) guitars, a drum machine, clarinet, alternation of instrumental and vocal tracks, but this with alternating success. This album has its share of nice track such as the opener, but a few rather boring track such as Little Zombies. Underground rock with progressive touches, but like I've said the production, sound quality are lo-fi and the singing is not strong.
