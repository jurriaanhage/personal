Spock's Beard - Kindness Of Strangers

Artist: Spock's Beard
Title: Kindness Of Strangers
Label: Giant Electric Pea GEPCD 1022
Length(s): 56 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1998
Month of review: 01/1998

Line up

Neal Morse - vocals, piano, synths, acoustic and some electric guitar
Alan Morse - electric guitar, cello, mellotron, vocals
Dave Meros - bass, vocals
Ryo Okumoto - hammond, mellotron
Nick D'Virgilio - drums, percussion, vocals

Tracks

1) The Good Don't Last 10.05
2) In The Mouth Of Madness 4.45
3) Cakewalk On Easy Street 5.02
4) June 5.29
5) Strange World 4.21
6) Harm's Way 11.06
7) Flow 15.49

Summary

After their great Beware Of Darkness they are back.

The music

The opening to The Good Don't Last consists of organ and low cello. The melody is a bit sad. Then we come to a hectic part with an instrument that sounds a bit like a violin evoking Kansas. Then the organ is brimming again and we have a typical bombastic passage. We break then into the vocal part with easy going acoustic guitar. Slowly the song builds up as instruments are added, and there's even time for a little jig like ditty and a subsequent part with very heavy guitar. As you might have gathered by now there is plenty of variation, with among others slow cello, quick acoustic guitar, folky intermezzo's, heavy guitars and a full organ sound. The song is about the way that some bands are just not meant to be, but the end of the song The Radiant Is shows that Neal has full confidence that Spock's Beard will continue to exist. Musically this last part is quite powerful and compelling, but also a little melodramatic. The song ends with a string quartet playing, ehm, well something.

In The Mouth Of Madness is a rather frantic track with carnival influences. You have indeed to enjoy the madness of the riff, but the chorus is quite friendly, although it ends quite darkly. The guitar figures quite prominently on this song and the song contains a real freak out on the keys (remember Lucky Man) and duels with the guitar. The vocals on this track are very loud and the band tends to play around a little at the end.

Cakewalk on Easy Street was written under the motto: as long as you have your arms. After a pianic tune and a heavy guitar part we come to the vocal part. The chorus is escpecially compelling and is sang along with easily. This chorus sounds very hopeful, like it's really a cakewalk although the song is in some sense meant ironically. The pianic theme recurs while the guitar and organ make sure your speakers have other things to worry about.

June is an acoustic ballad where Nick sings up front wit the others and shows that the band can sing harmony very well (as if Thoughts had ever left any doubt). For the rest, the melody is very nice and friendly (although about the demise of Spock's Beard), but is also meant as a breather after all the violence of the previous tracks, although the song ends quite loudly.

Strange Worlds is the last of the short tracks and like many of the other tracks contains a very catchy melody with plodding drums. The verses are a bit bumpy and the chorus is quite heavy and once in a while the theme of the intro recurs. Like on Cakewalk there's a kind of hope radiating from this song, although some of the lyrics point in a opposite direction. The band has something typically American, like Crack The Sky and Kansas also sound so very American. Hard to define, but that's how I feel it. There are also a few pointers to the Beatles (Day in the Life).

After the bombastic, ELPish intro the second epic Harms Way. The lyrics are sad and the vocal parts are rather relaxed with a bluesy guitar solo and free form additions on keys. The music is now quite jazzy. Then we come back to the verses of the beginning after which we get the intro again. During this bombastic part we also get some vocals and the song ends anthemically with mellotron and again that hopeful note in the music, of not giving up.

We end with the multiparted Flow with some lyrics of a guy called Tony Ray. The beginning is about as freaky as In The Mouh Of Madness. This is alternated with a more flowing part. The vocal part really shines being rather soft with a nice melodic guitar solo to round it off. After this we come to the hasty piano leading us into the next part A Constant Flow Of Sound. This part contains some of the aspects of the first part is funkier and bouncier. The chorus is as compelling as the first part. The last part is a very flowing part (how appropriate) that ends in a long guitarsolo.

I like the artwork on this cd definitely less than on the previous one.

Conclusion

Strange though it may sound, at first I was rather unimpressed with this album. It sounded much rockier and the songs seemed a lot more accessible and as it came to me later, the vocal parts are for the most part easy going while the freaking is in the non-vocal part. The melodies do seem to be more catchy. This of course should make it easier to like this album, but still it took me a while to notice that this is still very much progressive. Strange in retrospect. References are a lot less clear on this album, and that usually means that the music is bad or that the band matured. I'm sure it's the latter. An optimisic and I'm not afraid to say, a very good album. Hard to say which of the last two I like best, but to be on the safe side: make sure you have both. I'm hooked.
