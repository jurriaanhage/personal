Summary

My review of their previous album is only a few months old and then their new album falls into my lap. A nice touch is that they thank my boss at iO Pages, Rob Hanemaayer.

The music

What can I say? First of all the production and sound are better and the songs are on average much longer as well. It seems the band has tried to be a little more adventurous and in fact it works out well. The lyrics are usually stories and strange ones at that. Not really about dragons or fantasy things in general, but more anecdote like, without an actual indication of what the song is about. Vague is the word.

On the previous album of this band I heard influences of Kayak, It Bites and Genesis, but on this album for the first time I hear definite references to IQ, although the Genesis influence is still the most prominent. This IQ reference is largely due to Johans vocals that are somewhere between Nichols and Dunnery formerly of It Bites. They sound a bit wavery, but passionate and desperate, which is rather close to the way Nichols sings. The vocal melodies are good, like on the previous album.

Twelve Feet Tall starts with synths, but the first part of the track is in fact up-tempo with busy drumming and numerous fill-ins and some nice guitar work. The mellotron is also present somewhere in the back for the afficionados. The middle part is rather quiet with a repetitive melody played on synth and a flute-like melody I assume also comes from a keyboard. Then the vocal starts again and in the second verse, the drumming returns as the rolling of the sea and then suddenly the storm begins and the drumming becomes heavier. At the end a rather familiar melody can be heard reminding me of IQ.

Blinded tells a rather dark story, while musically it starts out rather mellow with soft synth strings in the back and some piano in the fore. Then we hear guitar work so typical of Steve Hackett of Genesis of course and in fact this is the one feature that reminds me so much of Genesis. The atmosphere of this track is rather dark and although it does not become faster it does become louder and this really works well.

The longest track on this album is The Pelican Lie which has nothing to do with the Pelican Brief in case you wondered. It's about an old and wise man who only listens to himself. The beginning of the song is rather mellow with an important role for the keyboards that sound very much like Banks and in fact makes the track sound like Genesis. After another keyboard solo, we get an almost a-capella part with whispered vocals. Then the bass falls in and we get some typically Genesis like acoustic guitar accompanied by flute (well, synth). Then we return more or less to the first part of the track, although a little more involved. This song has a lot of keyboards, compared to the previous ones.

A quick piano riff opens Famous Last Words, after which the guitar takes over. This song shares its dreary atmosphere with IQ first two albums and in fact the guitar also comes close to Holmes. At this point on the album the band itself brings little new, although the melodies are very good throughout, but the sound of the band is rather similar throughout.

A Sign of My Decline is typically Banks playing, especially in the beginning. Still, the end of this track is very memorable and is the fitting climax of the album.

The track that follows it, The Final Decision is more like a postlude. There are no vocals on it, and the guitar and synths both fight for dominance with the keys slightly on the upper hand.

Conclusion

Very typical melodic progressive in fact, but of the better kind. The atmospheres evoked are moody, dark and dreary comparable (but not as dark as) the ones evoked on The Wake by IQ. The melodies are good throughout and I do like the playing that is rather close to both Genesis and IQ at times. More notably, the guitar can sound as Hackett and Holmes, while the keys usually sound like Banks playing (a little too for my tastes). The drummer is a very busy man and he makes the music more dynamic, but also a little unruly at times. The vocals are rather desperate and a little unsteady, but I like them. Still, after an album full they sound a little too much of the same. Still, this is an appealing album, that should appeal to everyone who likes melodic progressive rock, especially if you are very much into keyboards.
