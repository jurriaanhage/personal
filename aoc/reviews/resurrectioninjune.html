Summary

The music

Opener Resurrection In June I sets the tone. Eerie sounds with somewhat spacy guitars and dito synths. It's highly reminiscent of earlier Tangerine Dream recordings, only less filled and eclectic, although the guitars at times have Geoff Mann's wobbly sound.

Burning Rome/Fatum I starts off as pretty much where its predecessor stopped, but as it continues the synths take dominance, with this referring to a later TD period, or some Klaus Schulze solo stuff from the mid seventies.

Going Through The Night brings us something quite new: it reminds me a lot of Edgar Froese's solo efforts, especially Aqua.

Conclusion

Tangerine Dream pretty much sums up what this album is about: all sorts of early to mid seventies TD music, with some influence from solo efforts of (former) members, with just a bit of wobbly guitars thrown in at times. As far as I'm concerned this was about the best EM ever made, so I quite enjoyed listening to this album. And strangely enough, it didn't really irritate me how much it lacked in originality.
