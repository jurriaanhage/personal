Summary

This specially made (for me that is) cd sampler will introduce you to the music of Notturno Concertante and Luz Escondida. I have indicated the sources above. This is just something that came up. Hopefully next time I will have their complete new album Rewriting The Past for review.

The music

The album opens with the piano of La Danza. A soft melodic track with some choral sounds and a low bass. Per/versi is from the forthcoming album. It is a playful tune with the acoustic guitar taking the lead. The melody is quite nice and ehm mellow. In that respect the band fits very well on the Mellow Records label. The rhythm part is not so straightforward though. After the filmic Becalmed we get again a more playful tune: Giga, which is followed by the quiet acoustics of Nocturne. Dreams' Hunters is maybe a bit too mellow for my tastes, but the long The Ghost Of The Sun At Sunset opens promising with long lonely tones again very much in the style of a good soundtrack. The percussive part however sounds too mechanic. Surprisingly the track turns out to be a song. I think this can be compared in atmosphere to Pendragon's And We'll Go Hunting Deer, the jazzy piano notwithstanding. Female vocals are on Good At The Art Of Surviving. She has an Italian accent, especially when she talks, but her performance is still good. This is also the first track in which the electric guitar has something to say and we come close progressive rock. The main theme reminds me of Morten Harkets Tell Me What You See. Half Human Town is a short vocal track, melodic and acoustic. Quite traditional. The title track of The Hiding Place has male vocals and directly reminds me fleetingly of later Pendragon. The song is a bit uneventful though. Hopebringer is a pleasant pianic ditty, but in L'Aria Che Cammina the drum computer comes round the corner again. I would suggest leaving out the drum track entirely. Ice you should all know from Camel. In fact Notturno has something of later Camel (Harbour of Tears) in their softspoken approach. Some may feel this side of Camel to be boring and sometimes it is, but if the melodies are good I'm not against this type of music. This version of Ice is quite different from the original. This version is from Luz Escondida and I'm not sure - and did not verify - that it is the same as on the Harbour Of Joy Tribute. Lots of piano on this one (instead of acoustic guitar) and quite jazzy at times. The acoustic guitar is used for the more bouncy parts while the electric guitar does not really shine as on the original. The original is for me one of the most emotional instrumentals ever made. In fact the tracks by Camel that move me most are instrumentals (Ice and Stationary Traveller). In fact, I have to remember to play it right after reviewing this disc. After the uneventful My Spectre Around Me Night And Day, Not For Me is a romantic song with a good vocal melody and somewhat operatic singing. The singer still has that accent, but her voice is good. The guitar solo is quite sharp (and good). The high pictched vocal style is continued in Vision, a cover of Hammills song (featured in a shorter version on Eyewitness). The song fits in well with the rest of the songs. The drums are real ones here, it seems. After the peaceful piano and later acoustic guitar of Il Mare Della Tranquilita we end with a hazy keys piece. Another reference that might help here are bands like Edith and Blue, also operating in the mellow, melodic areas of progressive. I have the impression though that the melodies of Notturno are, though maybe a bit too much on the mellow side, quite memorable.

Conclusion

Something for the mellower among you. I am not very satisfied with the tracks that featured the terrible drum computer, but the melodies are good, the music soothing and especially the instrumental tracks often have a strong soundtrack like quality. We can hardly call this progressive rock, since the rock is largely absent and also the word progressive goes a bit too far, but the music is (generally) pleasant to listen to.
