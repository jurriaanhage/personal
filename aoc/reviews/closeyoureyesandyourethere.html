Summary

A self produced album from Moline, Illinois. I wonder if the band also released this on vinyl or cassette since it contains a CD only bonus track. Maybe a joke. I also wonder about the fact that Prometheus has only the third and fourth part on this CD and from the subparts of Lifecycles we may conclude that some of the musicians have read some Heinlein.

The music

The album opens with the bagpipes of Methuselah's Children. With the death vocals of the singer of Angelkill (under the poetic name of Blakk) this is a rhythm guitar dominated track with plenty of bass and heavy drums. Prog metal is maybe not the correct word, complex hard rock is I think a better description. Take the music of a band such as Iron Maiden or such and extend the format to include acoustic interludes, introspective solo guitar and plenty of rhythm and mood changes and we're getting there. Nothing wrong yet with the music here. Okay, it sounds a bit "cheap" productionwise, but with a budget like this, this is hardly surprising. What I do not like however are the vocals. All band members sing at least a piece and it is Benner who sings here. His vocals are high, but not very likable I'm afraid. The same holds for the third bridge where other members join in. More care should be taken here. Plenty of variation though. Some prts sound better than others, I like it for instance when the piano sets in although it is only in the background. The drums sound a bit on the muffled side. The next track is Broken Glass. Again a long track, 14 minutes, opening with some sensitive piano playing and a downpour lends a depressive air to the whole of it. Again very much guitar dominated with a catchy chorus and a driven rhythm section. Again the vocals are not very good. The singer is now Harker, and I like his less prominent vocals a bit better. Prometheus is a tro parted track, two short parts in fact. Benner sings here again, and now he sings in the lower regions, sounding a lot better than in the previous tracks. The track opens somewhat classically with stringlike synths, but stil the music reminds me a lot of seventies and eighties hardrock, but then with an epic approach. For the second (actually fourth) part we come again to the stringlike synths and quick drum rolls but also featuring some meandering guitar work.

The major track of the album is the instrumental Lifecycles. Opening with fast paced drumming, a heavy bass, quick guitar playing and quite a lot of keyboards, this is a rather repetitive first part. The overall sound is dark and quick lending an air of haste and also of menace to the music. However, the music is and stays quite melodic. The keyboards sound a bit thin though. After this overture, we come to Time Enough For Love?, the next phase in life according to the band. We move right into this piece, no slacking the pace. Sail Beyond The Sunset is much slower and has some "clavecimbel" like playing as it accompanies and complements the guitar. Maybe a little too much repetition here. Halfway, the music is transformed slightly, but melodically there's little variation. Rebirth? is back to the energetic guitar work of the previous tracks. The band calls this track a symphony and because of the strong classical leanings (but without losing their identity) they can very well do this.

Fear Of The Dark is metal again, but rather complex. The sound quality leaves to be desired and the vocals are not great again. Although this is often the case, I would not advice the band to just leave out the vocals, but try to improve them. One track like Lifecycles is okay, but filling an entire album with this kind of music would be very hard. Melodically the track is not so interesting and the many breaks are rather tiring. The ending is quite surprising though and ending with the bagpipes of the first track.

The closer on my CD is the short Cursing Infinity. Keyboards mostly on this track. A nice thematic approach, and playful as well.

Conclusion

Something has to be done about the vocals. The way Benner sung in Prometheus was quite alright, but for the rest, the vocals diminish the value of the music greatly. Also, some more attention should be given to the way things are recorded. There is an overall muddy sound to things. Of course, these probably have to do with budget limitations, but still it would be good to keep an eye our for improvement. Musically it can be quite complex and epic hardrock might be the best way to describe the music. Sometimes I'm reminded of one or the other hardrock band, but this is mostly because of the hardrockish vocals. The music sounds quite classical at times, especially when the keyboards are playing. The guitar work is melodic, but can also rage and meander at times.
