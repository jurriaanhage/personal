Summary

The sixth cd already of this German, Floyd-oriented band.

The music

Days Of Wrath is the opener of this album and with over six minutes, the shortest one. It also reveals my largest gripe with this band: the nasal vocals of Bettina Wirtz. The bio labels her as expressive, and I guess she is that. But her accent and nasality makes her me to very inpleasant to listen to. There is a drummer in the band, but the drums here seem programmed (maybe he is using an electronic kit). The drums are rather percussively played, giving the song a rather tinny, modern feel. There is a sinister feeling hanging over this track, which is nice, but the execution is not as good I would have liked.

Next up is half an hour of Thunderstorm, divided into seven parts of average length. Here the to me known Floyd influence rises strongly, with laid back guitar work and soft organ in the back. Camel might also be used as a reference point here, or Jane on their first album. The band builds a nice atmosphere here. The guitar builds up strongly here bursting with vitality, but also time for some tender playing. The second part has elements of Deep Purple's Child In Time (and some other song that I cannot name; the opening keys part), but is mostly acoustic. This soothing part has vocals again. The second verse replaces acoustic guitar with a bass. The voice goes up here, becoming more passionate, but Bettina's voice is still a problem for me. We break rather surprisingly into the third part which has organ brimming, and sustained lines on guitar. Rather vintage sounding, with some 'Arabic' sounding melodies. Then it starts to rain, and the moody sax sets in. We have now entered Pendragon's 2AM, it seems. Okay, not really, but the sadness is about the same. This part however is actually more of an acoustic affair, rather soft and under the skin, with a bit of jazz, plenty of acoustic guitar and a bit of nostalgia thrown in. The Floyd influences comes back in in the next part as well as parts of Part 2. The vocal melody returns to the plaintive chorus, which is in fact quite nice. We close down with brimming organ, and strings on synth, building a dark atmosphere that works well.

Force Majeure is the second epic, a mere twenty minutes. Again broken up into part, we have four of them this time. This song is a plodder, opening with piano and grinding rhythm guitar, and a bit of mellotron thrown in for good measure. The lyrics have a certain naivety to them, that gives the songs a sheen of amateurism. Mainly the turns of phrases that turn me off. Okay, back to the music. The music is quite a bit heavier here, but the band stays focussed on evoking suspense in their music, which is of course all to the positive. The second part contains quotes from well-known politicians opening with a quote from 1918 stopping at second world war for a minute to continue via Vietnam to the WTC destruction. The relatively short third part again has that combination of piano and rhythm guitar, with a short vocal interlude, and ending with a chorus and soft piano. The final part is also the longest. It has a cosmic feel opening with keyboards and organ. For the rest plenty of atmospherics here.

War is the final track, in which the sax crops up again. Organ and piano make for a somewhat occasional type of music. Then we come to the song sec. Mid-tempo Floyd oriented material with some more outspoken sax and of course some vocals. Organ dominates the musical map this time.

Conclusion

Well, you must have noticed by now. Solar Project still makes Floyd oriented rock, with a good feel for tension and tenseness. There are some memorable melodies here, and they often reoccur in the epics. The lyrics can be a bit shaky, but my main problem lies with the vocals. The nasal, accented voice of Bettina is hard for me to stomach, even if she does try to use her voice in different ways. Fortunately, the vocals are not overly much present, so that helps, and my problems could well be a personal thing (although generally I do not have problems with accents).
