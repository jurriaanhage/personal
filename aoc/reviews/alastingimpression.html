Summary

It does not happen often that you get a cd that rereleases something released after this website started. It is the case now. Leger De Main (French for Sleight Of Hand), is mainly the band of the Rodler brothers, with sister? Melissa chipping in for the vocals. Their first two albums are on this double set together with two acoustic bonus tracks.

The music

To Live The Truth opens slowly and rather friendly, but turns into the complex, dense progrock I have over the years grown to expect from the Rodler brothers. The usual reference is Rush, although Melissa's voice does not immediately remind of Geddy (thank God for her), so the reference is not that strong even. The fireworks are on, and it has to be said: there are some great vocal melodies on this song, especially towards the high paced end.

Crystal Fortune opens with bombastic keyboards, but the vocal parts are in a more relaxed style. At these moments, the music of Leger De Main reminds of a band such as For Absent Friends: melodic rock. Immobile Time is also such a compact song of the type that you often heard coming from the SI Music bands in the nineties. This holds only for the vocal parts, because the instrumental parts sound more complex, and more meandering too.

Enter Quietly is not one of those compact songs, it is more than twenty minutes long. It opens with minimalistic acoustic work, of the repetitive kind. Then the pace goes up a bit, and we surprisingly enter some kind of Latin territory with a playful flute and percussives. The next phase we enter combines the flute with electronic percussion which does give the song a totally different feel. Only after six minutes or so we come a vocal passage. The melody reminds me of Rush from the eighties, although somewhat lower. Just when I though to remark that Melissa does not show emotion, she immediately becomes more forceful and the vocals' pitch rises. Past halfway we arrive at the next instrumental section, which is as dynamic and flashy as ever. It is short though and soon gives way to rather sparse vocal parts. The vocal melody does not have a special appeal, unfortunately. After that we are back in meanderville with Chris playing the guitar hero part. I am not that fond of this type of material. The song ends on an electronic, dreamy note.

Distorted Pictures closes down the original album. It starts off with some more complex time signatures and plenty of variation. Again, the vocal melody lacks that extra spark to make the song work. Now it is too much something I heard before on the same album.

The two bonus tracks are acoustic versions of Crystal Fortune and Immobile Time. Melissa sounds more intimate here, for some reason.

Disc two opens with Some Shall Search. The style of the music is now more geared towards a combination of the scary side of King Crimson and jazzrock. There is more of an edge to it, and that makes it interesting. The vocal passages are rather calm again, but not for very long: soon we are among the washes of cymbals, and thus we alternated until we arrive at the rather hectic middle part. The link between the vocals and the instrumentation is at first not apparent, but soon they come together. Then the music is gone, completely, and it can start to build up again. Acoustic guitar, electric guitar (but quietly) and busy drums go to work. In fact, this part is quite a lengthy one in which the percussively played drums dominate, and the keyboards follow. Again, we enter quieter waters with the acoustic guitar taking over fully, after which the melodic guitars and washes of cymbals take over again. There is a nice flow to the music at this point. In fact, the final few minutes are all quite dynamic.

Changes With The Day opens with very synthetic sounding keyboards, followed by the balladic vocal parts of Melissa. Her voice is a bit plaintive in places. Surprising is the strong, driving keyboard solo that follows right up. They have the pomp of ELP to them. The drums give the song a nice flowing pace during the chorus. In between such passages, the acoustic parts take a lower pace, but soon we are back in a more active phase in which Melissa shows a bit more bite. Again, an easily remembered and appealing piece of work.

The next one, Silent Monsters also opens with keyboards, dancing almost. The guitars seem to have given way to the keyboards, and the melodic material on this second disc is also quite a bit better than on the debut. The band can still rock out very dynamically, listen for instance to the second minute of this song. Having more keyboards around does not mean that there are no guitars around. In part, this song reminds me of the cacaphonic side of Hackett's excellent Voyage Of The Acolyte.

With Do Whispers Die we are back to the more neo-progressive oriented melodic rock ballads (think Saga, Rush and their followers). The Story is a worthy closer of this album, with a telling theme, and well, the chorus is really great, as are the instrumental fireworks afterwards.

Conclusion

With Rush as the main reference point, it is not surprising that Leger De Main are rhythmically challenging, compact and have quite a modern sound. The female vocals are different from Geddy's so you never get the idea to be really listening to Rush at all, which is a good point. At other moments, the busy instrumental side of the band may remind some of Liquid Tension Experiment of Gordian Knot, but compared to the former, Leger De Main actually writes songs. But the reference to Gordian Knot's debut (especially on the more Crimson oriented second disc), is one to remember. The second disc is, for me, much to be preferred over the first one, although the latter is certainly not bad.
