Summary

Hailing from Athens, Georgia, this band contacted me to listen to a few of their tapes. One of them is a live tape (only 20 exist so I wonder whether people will be able to get them), but here's a tape for everyone to listen. In their thank you's Mike Sary of French TV is noted, maybe this is an indication.

The music

Because there's not really a lot of space between the songs (which is a little harder for me on tape than on CD, you might imagine), I will try to explain in plain words what the music is all about. First of all, the band is more or less in the Happy the Man style and some echoes of VDGG (cello, sax) can be heard as well. Seemingly the most important instrument however are the keyboards and mostly notably the Fender Rhodes (not a keyboard that is very popular these days). Most of the music is rather relaxed and like Happy the Man rather in the Cantebury style. Still, at times the music gets a litte more excited and that is just what this band needs. It needn't blister (other bands can do that), but the problem with a band like Happy the Man is that it might get a little static on the long run and maybe even a little distant. What I do like about this band is that aside the usual progressive instruments and the necessary ingredients, like signature changes and breaks, the instrumentation helps out where diversity in music should otherwise have been present. In this way, the cello gives something extra. The next track however, opens with a repetitive guitar pattern, which gives rise to a more menacing heavier sound. Slow moving, but getting there. Again the cello gives a little extra flavour to it all, but in this case, the effect isn't that great. There is some energy there, but unfortunately the potential is not entirely realised and some of the breaks are a little farfetched, but the melody line is very nice. Fortunately, the song has some rough edges.

The next track is again rather quiet with a repetitive acoustic guitar and nice drumming. then we move into a pianic part and the keyboards following it sound a little dated to be honest, but this band does live more or less in the seventies and as such I se no real problem here.

The bad points of this tape then. First of all I would like to mention the sound of the tape. Of course this is no major production (it's a tape not a CD) and so we might expect them to be short on soundquality and production. Especially the drums suffer in this respect and I find the sound kind of dry. This might be something to keep an eye on. If music these days was just finding the right tunes and even moods, it would be a lot easier than it is. It is a good decision to release a few demo's before getting to the next step.

The other side of the tape containing two songs is also rather quiet and contains at the beginning a lot of sax and acoustic guitar and also an important role for the Fender. Then we get some cello and the afterwards the music becomes more powerful and enjoys a good melody.

Conclusion

Very nice tape with entertaining material in the jazzy Happy the Man format. The instrumentation and the good melodies (when they're not freewheeling). Still, although the music is indeed nice, something more could have been done with it, as I feel it. There are some rough edges to the music, but for the most part it is more or less laid back. Better production and a clear, sharp sound might help a little, but on the other hand it might be that the band wants it sound this way. In a sense this band can also be likened to Land's End: the same laid-back atmosphere with an occasional outburst. Land's End however has the advantage of having a singer fitting in with the music. Also, where the keyword with Land's End is "desolate", with this band it is "friendly" (modulo a few outbursts).
