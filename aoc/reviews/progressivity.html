Summary

The third Tunnels album with John Goodsall of Brand X guesting.

The music

Syzygy Incident opens with rather fast drumming but slow moody bass and keyboards that accompany it. In addiion one can hear some slightly deformed acoustic guitar (which can also be a bass). It sounds almost like a harpsichord. The music is like its two predecessors typical fusion/jazzrock virtuously played, complex, many things propping up. Although the first part is Spanish tinged, a later part is very gamelan like.

On Wall To Wall Sunshine we get violin in Ponty style and some blistering guitar work with the drummer hammering away and the keyboardist filling up the background. With Frank's Beard we move into the keyboard dominated area of jazzrock. Lots of Fender like tones here, you know the subdued ones. The violin is again present to insert of hectic phrases of its own. In this case however the duel is with the keyboards, not the guitar. The last part is more panicky and features a lot of violin. Actually I am not too fond of that. The music is also quite laidback here, although the violin itself can be harrowwing.

Diabollocks opens in moody style but turns into a funky piece of work soon enough. The bass rumbles here, the tunings make for a more adventurous sound and the mood of the song seems more important. There is a certain playfulness here, but also mystery. A very disjointed affair at times.

After Progressivity we come to the mile long 7,584,333,440 Miles Away. The opening is quiet enough although it has its disquieting moments, under the skin as it were. The music on this track is a bit vaguer than usual. The first part is mainly jazzrock, leaning towards the jazz a lot, a later passage still has the rumbling bass and fast drums, but is also airy and hazy. The instruments sound more loose from eachother than connected in the sense that they are playing the same composition. For its length, its does not pack enough material for my tastes.

Some Things Must Last is a moody one with a violin dominated opening. Then the keyboards take over to meander a bit. This more of a ballad type thing, than a rock song. With Fusionauts we move into more experimental areas, something they should do more often, and which I tend to miss a bit compared to their debut album. The guitar figures again on this one, Orfeo's Dream is quite different from the rest, mainly because of its being bass dominated and the presence of the voice of Sarah Pillow which sounds almost like a low violin. A moody but fresh sounding track with a strong bass undercurrent.

The final track is High Tea At 49th and 10th. A rather bouncy affair with a strong laid back jazz feel.

Conclusion

I think I like this one less than the first Tunnels. The air of experimentation seems to be largely gone, and the addition of violin does not help in my opinion. Where the first one sounds very vital this is much less the case here. This album does have a broader range of sounds available. As the first one, this album tends to be jazzrock fans only.
