Summary

The music

Taneshina is a rather modern sounding jazzy album. Most tracks have a certain playfulness about them. Light sax or piano melodies, but at times a minimalistic key (Tri-band-boom). The drums use a fair amount of breaky rhythms. The sound of the soprano sax is pretty often present, adding its usual snappy squeak, and pulling towards endless drivel. The drums and bass are mostly background, although the drum rhythms have a certain alt feel at times as well, adding some newness. Opener Til_na_n0g seems to have a hint of Russian folk music interspersed. Nagoya No Itoko features a duel between two saxes, which comes across pretty strong and fresh, easily compensating for the pip. The piano solo following it is a bit more traditional, and with that boring.

Conclusion

Where a lot of this type of jazz albums tend to get lost in borderless soloing, this album seems to be just saved from that by the lightness of piano, whilst the sop sax is trying to pull it into meanderville. In its genre the album is quite nice, but still not intended for those not liking the soloing side of jazz.
