Summary

This is a re-release of a 1989 album.

The music

The opener starts with some clear sounding piano. Pretty nice. From this we move into what reminds me off mid eighties Tangerine Dream, with some of those elongated weeews we know from Kitaro. Towards the end we even get a visit from the lady from The Great Gig In The Sky.

De Agul... is another instrumental track with Kitaro and TD influences. Not bad in itself, but rather uninteresting.

Brillaras is a vocal track in which aformentioned lady shows she can also do words. Apart from her heartfelt delivery, this track sounds a bit flat.

Cita Clandestina reminds me of the Mission Impossible theme. This track is well driven, but the drums once again sound less than lively.

Al Reverso Del Tejido is led in by guitar, later augmented by organ and boxed drums. The large vocal section, in which Hamelink's vocals are further augmented by an opera-like ha-ing the back, now and then approach the irritation line, but manage to stay on the right side. Well crafted as this track is, it clearly leaves its predecessors behind.

Tributo A La Eternidad sounds like a love song, or a fado even, in which Hamelink's dramatic voice starts to sound overly so.

Ciudad Sin Tiempo starts pleasantly experimental. Unfortunately this is soon taken over by Richard Clayderman quality piano. With this track the original album is concluded.

Heroes Sin Medalla has a nice delivery, although Hamelink does pull The Great Gig voice out of the bag again. One of the better tracks.

Rigel has a certain My First Casio quality. The sound quality is terrible and the composition is brow raising, if composition is what you want to call this.

Palabras has a man huskily repeating the title, with The Great Gig Lady going near berserk at times, over gentle keys. Quite a reprieve after the previous track, and not all that bad in itself.

Conclusion

With a couple of electronic tracks, some more or less symphonic stuff and a bit of almost fado-like music, this has become something of a melting pot. Outside that, some of the music, especially the electronic music, seems to lack in originality. The recording quality isn't very good, notably so on the bonus tracks. The overall result is an album that has its moments, mostly in the symphonic stuff. Having said that, Anima seem unable to put together a string long enough to make this into a decent album.
