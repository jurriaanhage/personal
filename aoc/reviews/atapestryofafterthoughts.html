Artist: Chaos Code
Title: A Tapestry Of Afterthoughts
Label: self produced
Length(s): 55 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1999
Month of review: 04/2000

Line up

Marty Aletta - keyboards, rhodes piano, chapman stick
Dan Squillaro - drums, percussion

Summary

Coming from around Baltimore/D.C. this is a new prog band, the leader of which has recently moved to England. I'm not sure about the current status of the band, but with their paradoxical name and the bio referring to such notable bands as the new Scandinavian ones, they got me interested.

The music

The album opens rather quietly with The Cave, soft keyboards, slow percussion lead up to a guitar solo. Then the pace goes down again, and acoustic guitar takes over. The song is definitely dreamy now, a bit folky even. The music reminds me at times a bit of the acoustic side of Genesis. The vocal melodies are well taken care of, the vocals themselves do not sound flashy, but they do have that something. After a few words from the Stock Exchange accompanied by something akin to a funeral march, we come to a subtle piano part that reminded me a bit of the darkish, slowish side of Aragon. The music now starts to build up again and in fact here I get the same feeling of longing, the same capacity to captivate, that this band shares with the Scandinavian bands, topped off by an emotional guitar solo. Heights Of Time sounds a bit old. Especially the vocals sound a bit flat on this one (I think this has to do with the recording/production). A recognizable track that stops and starts, with some tense moments. Past halfway, the song takes a turn, starting to sound a bit like Van Der Graaf Generator (except for the vocals). Then the pace picks up again and some ascending guitar lines, build up the tension again. Antidote To Entropy opens with flute. Then military drums set in and some strong guitar work. Again, the band takes its time to develop the themes, but they do so in a meaningful way, giving a clear nod to King Crimson along the way. The flute again comes to the fore a bit later with a mesmerizing melody, recalling In The Court... The guitar then starts to do something, but instead the sound dies down again and then the song starts somewhere else. I would have liked to hear what they were thinking of doing in the first place. Days Of Reflection brings the ingredients of the previous track with plenty of variation, both melodically as well as rhythmically and even mood wise. The dominant part is the one of the flute, the dreaminess might remind some of the Italian bands. The vocal part is rather quiet and a bit faraway sounding. In this case there are even backing vocals. Those are not that great, some better production is necessary here. The vocal melody sounds a bit familiar. The instrumental intermezzo is quite similar to Sinkadus, with the flute and the rather careful, understated arrangements. The song may seem a bit too tame, with the slow bluesy guitar solo right there in the middle. Fortunately, the guitar takes revenge towards the end with a great melodic solo. I do however feel that even more could have been made out of it. There's nothing wrong with the playing, or the emotion that is in the music, but it doesn't come over as clearly as it might. This is probably due to a lack of good recording facilities and money and hence understandable. A Silent Scream opens with acoustics. The vocal part sounds a bit depressed and the guitar solo is again a good one. In this case the backing vocals work and the high ethereal keys also lend a nice touch to the music. The continuation of this track is a bit more modern than the previous ones. Some of the vocal parts do sound a bit doubtful on this track. Gravy Fries is an instrumental and it is also the most jam like piece on the album. Sax and meandering guitar make up this rather short piece. The Devil's Trombone is again in the style of the big KC. Estranging horn sections on this piece, punctuated by repetitive guitar runs. Then a nice theme sets in on the piano keyboards, accompanied by the flute. The vocals are again a bit flat here, but fortunately the singer makes up in part by sometimes singing a bit more powerfully.

Conclusion

A good album that still lacks a bit in the production. This only shows that possibly everything could even have been more beautiful. As it is, the melodies composition, their all perfectly in order. Sometimes I have my doubts about the singing, the band maybe needing a singer who treads more on the foreground in a way, but on other hand, the singer does fit in with the oftentimes somewhat depressing music. Nods to King Crimson (In The Court) and (the acoustic) Genesis and bands such as Anekdoten and Sinkadus are quite plentiful, but I wouldn't call the band derivative. Vintage prog and a new band to look out for.
