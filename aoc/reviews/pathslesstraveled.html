Summary

The music

Opener Pure is a long track, starting with rather technical instrumental stuff, of the King Crimson denomination, although there's a ghost of Cream's Sunshine Of Your Love, slowly moving into less complex waters. After a short bridge halfway through the track, it turns out to be not instrumental. Summerlin's vocals have improved to fitting ones, duelling with the Sunshine theme. Towards the end a guitar solo comes sort of loose from the track, some kind of afterbirth, which is okay in itself, but poorly attached to the rest of the track.

Man On Fire is clearly presented by a singer with a somewhat nasal voice, dominating other instruments. Instrumentally the track is laid back, lazy almost, with throbbing basses, resulting in something mildly mid eighties Tony Carey. As the vocalist exerts himself a bit more the nasality becomes less obvious, but starts sounding piped. Halfway through the track fades out, to be replaced by what's probably part two, an instrumental bit, sounding like payed on giant glasses, as an intro to something starting far more rocky, with loads of guitar, to continue into an eighties poprock track. Not very progressive, in no sense whatsoever, put pretty okay to the ear still.

Farpoint enfolds with a lengthy and nicely guitar driven, driven by any means, intro. This gives way to a very flat vocal section, uninspired female singing, crappy rhythm and uninteresting piano. Fortunately this is pre-empted by a vocal duel, which does sound inspired, with the return of the guitar. The interchanging of interesting sections and the flat vocals continue. A track with some very strong sections, but the parts with the sole female vocals are pretty poor.

Out Of The Clouds manages to come up with an intro that creates tension, exploding into a climax, before the Kansas like vocal section takes off. The vocals have the strength needed to bring the concept home and rhythms are well constructed and played. Despite the Kansas echoes this track comes off very well, showing enough originality. Best on the album.

Consequences is a pretty alt/rocky track, without any enticing instrumental or melodic interest, although the instrumental section stands out compared to the flatly sung vocal part. Not bad, but not interesting either.

Emperor's Poison starts with mesmerizing keys and hypnotic rhythm. As the guitar sets we slowly but deliberately move in the direction of King Crimson project like material, although not as relentless, and with the strength dying down before coming to full bloom, mingling in some psychedelic influences, Ozric type, as well. At the end of the day this turns out an instrumental which is too long for the amount of compositional ground covered.

The Disciple is opened by some nasty sounding vocals, but the Strong guitar takes off in a nice, if rocky, way. The track seems to pick up strength in steps, with a couple of lulls taking it further every time, towards a rather heavy, rhythm driven feel, making for a good climax.

Trouble With Troubadours is a rather straigthforward track, not all that interesting.

Oceans is mainly and poorly vocal, with some acoustic instruments as cello (or viola) and flute.

The Datura Blues comes up with an instrumental track that isn't always interesting, but finishes off with a lengthy section that balances on the edge of melody and experiment (slowly moving towards the latter). Definitely interesting, and nice too, if you're so inclined.

Conclusion

As far as I'm concerned Cobweb Strange is the best known band on this label, which made me fear for the quality of this sampler. I was pleasantly surprised, the Cobweb Strange was as might be expected but Karma Lingo put in a full thumbs up entry, and some other stuff has its moments too.
