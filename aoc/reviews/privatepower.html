Summary

The third Paranoise album to be released after Constant Fear in 1988 and Start A New Race in 1992. The band takes its time it seems. Now to the music and maybe as importantly for this band: the lyrics.

The music

The album opens with Arabic singing but against a rock background with plenty of bass and also violin. The music can hardly be compared to anything, but there is definitely a strong world music influence in here. After this short and percussive track we come to Instability, Containment, Rollback. It opens with a sample of one Michael Parenti and again features violin against a backdrop of low noisy guitars/bass. The vocal melody is quite accessible and recognizable, while the percussion is again quite prominent. On the next track the Arabic chants of the first track are continued (sampled it seems), but with a very Westernish vocal melody. Together with the fiddle we arrive at a marriage of Arabic music and something akin to square dance kept afloat by a good vocal melody. The didgeridoo strikes in the middle of the song when a percussive interlude features as a backing for the Musicians Of The Nile over which an electric guitar freely solos. Towards the end we get some Frippian guitar playing. On Mechanical World the rock vocals return. The music can be likened to Parallel Or 90 Degrees possibly becomes of the up and down movement in the music, but Paranoise definitely sounds more American. Of course, PO90 dopes not include world music into their spectrum. It is striking that both bands are very interested in what happens in the world around them. Because of the violin one is tempted to think of Kansas. The samples of International Monetary Fun (IMF for short) are punctuated by rocking riffs. Again the percussion and bass playing is quite audible and lends a nice groove to the music. Melting pot is probably a good term to describe the music. I'm also struck by the fact that the samples are often well intregrated into the music and sometimes come to lead a life of their own. The intermezzo is quite a-melodious with lots of things intertwining. Constant Fear bring a return to melodious rock music with violin and a not-so-melodious guitar solo to keep it "interesting". Again, the vocal melody is good. Structural Adjustment brings us back to the equator somewhat. The first samples remind me of the monks in Tibet, but most of the song is rather groovy/funky and because of the modern rhythms quite danceable. At the end of this long track the violin and electric guitar are also allowed to contribute their piece resulting in a something akin to a jam and moving into the general direction of psychedelic music. Private Power opens with a repetitive collection of samples of Noam Chomsky trying to bring a message home. Lots of percussions here and playing around with samples. The music and samples are repetitive, but the vocals start only start after three minutes bring some melodic relief. In Tarana we dive into Indian music with a large didgeridoo presence. The vocals are also quite Indian sounding. Power is added by some strong riffs in the back and plodding drums. Not There features some good loud vocals in the great chorus, but in the middle the band tends to fiddle around a bit too much. Centerless Grinding brings again repetitive samples put to a groovy backbeat and enlightened by some violin. After this rather short straightforward track we close with Monuments, which is somewhat longer than indicated on disc. The music opens quite complex a bit like King Crimson but with violin. Then the song becomes quite melodious with some enthusiastic singing by Thorne Palmer. The song does contain some breaks, so there is some variation. After the 5.20 minutes the song was supposed to take we quickly start again with wailing samples.

On the backside, but probably from the titles of the songs as well, you may have gathered that there is more to the music and lyrics than plain amusement. In fact, Jim Mathus sees this record as a statement. He hopes that the samples introduces the third world culture into our own creating a new culture instead of swallowing it whole as he finds is now happening.

Conclusion

The groove never lets off on this record. On it you can find samples of various people integrated with potent rock, but never letting the actual message get out of sight. This might be a bit bothersome for some, but on the face of it, I think it's okay. There are plenty of world music influences on this album, but melody is present throughout most of the album and also the band does not avoid plain rocking riffs. If you want references, it becomes a bit difficult. I can only give you a flavour of the "intent" of the band. Sometimes I'm reminded of Kansas, sometimes of King Crimson, sometimes of Parallel Or 90 Degrees, but the band is also widely different from all of them in at least a few respects, so do not tend to think of them as derivative. The good thing is that basis of the music is still the rock we all know, but music Africa and Asia are added to rather good effect. An eye opening, sympathetic release. And progressive as well.
