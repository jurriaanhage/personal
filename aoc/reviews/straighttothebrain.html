Summary

Heartscore is mainly the classically trained Dirk Radloff, here with his second self produced offering. He calls the music art rock...

The music

...and he has got a point there. The music on God Lay Dead, sounds quite complex with plenty of varied riffing, and some Gentle Giantlike vocal elements (on the other hand, the vocals also remind of Peter Hammill). The lyrics are all borrowed from (well-known) poets, also the next one, the pacey progmetal song Long John Brown And Little Mary Bell. Lots of strands of music intertwine, made more restless by the use of more than one vocal strand (in that sense the music can be compared to Peter Hammill). The vocals are not that strong though, they sound a bit amateurish, sometimes it can sound even quite punkish. The music is very riff oriented giving a bit of an classic rock feel. The guitar is by far the most prominent instrument. Fortunately, Radloff did hire a live drummer. Anyone is a plodding rock song, with Radloff shouting out his lyrics somewhere under the heavy rhythm guitars. The main problem with these songs is that lack appealling melodies. The instruments are mainly percussively oriented, and the vocal melodies are not distinctive enough. The vocal lines are a bit better on the blues rock of Love Is Like Whiskey.

The title A Dream Within A Dream will sound familiar to many, and indeed the lyrics are the poem by Edgar Allan Poe. Again, complex/fickle guitar dominated rock harbouring a large amount of restlessness. It certainly does not make the song easy to follow, but my guess is that this is partly the idea. This is one of the more likable songs so far. Again there is a certain punkishness to the affair (both the low-budgetness and the energy), thus reminding me in places of a wayward band such as Victims Family. The chorus is very melodic this time around.

The Schoolboy is a weird song, almost a pastiche of some kind. The guitars are punkish, the vocal lines have a humoristic tinge, and the song has some nice trumpet, piano and violin work for added melodicity. Interesting, and very prog sounding too. The final part is almost string orchestra like, but there is still something unserious about it. Or is that me?

The Tides Rises, The Tide Falls opens like a pure rock song, a bit with the riff drone of a song like Paranoid. Of course, Radloff injects his doubled and tripled vocals here and there. Ballad Of The Gypsy is a plodding piece of rock again, while The Arrow And The Song has vocal wailings of various kinds. There is a certain choir like quality to the vocals, but the vocals are not what I would call beautiful.

Her Eyes opens and continues like a seventies hard rock song. Good-Bye is even more furious, especially in the opening, with the drummer really going for it. The remainder is lighter, but still pacey. It seems if they took a normal song and tried to play it at double speed.

The Day Is Done is the longish closer. It features flute to begin with and yes a string orchestra of a kind as well. Where the flute comes from I can't really tell. This is by far the most classical tune on this album, and also the one with the most melodious vocal lines. Maybe Dirk should do this more often, and not just at the end. Like the other long track, this one contains a more varied instrumentation, also including the violin again, while the guitar also sound a bit more melodic than usual. There is something of Queen in there.

Conclusion

This album has quite a bit of originality. They call it art rock and I cannot see any other name that fits. The guitar is the dominant instrument, while the (somewhat accented) vocals are often doubled or tripled (in Hammill style, but not as well sung and not as well recorded). The music is often quite fast, hasty, and restless, and strongly riff oriented, with the lo-fi production giving a bit of a punkish feel overall, but a LOT less straightforward. The end result is a not so melodious as I want to have it, but that does make the more melodious parts stand out more. Especially on a song like The Schoolboy when trumpet, piano and violin come to introduce variation on an instrumental instead of compositional level, do I start to like the music. A bit less vocals and a broader instrumentation like on the closer and Schoolboy would help me appreciate the music more.
