Summary

The View From My Window is Manning's fifth album.

The music

Undoutedbly I won't be the first to note that Manning's voice is quite akin to Roy Harper's. Opener Phase makes that very apparent, but the poppy feel, featured by flat rhythms and pretty hackneyed guitar and sax solos remains far from Harper on other aspects. The flatness of this opening track belies the rest of the album.

The View From My Window has a somewhat eastern feel, making it almost the opposite of its predecessor. The rhythms sound different, and we even find a dissonant sounding flute in the intro. The sparse coldness of Phase is completely washed away by this warm sound. As the vocals set in the track has a slightly change, but the tribal and ethnic feel remains.

The Rut returns to that strict guitar and drum sound of the opener, with a nice sounding eastern motif across. The motif sounds good, but the flat drumming once again pushed to the fore in an irritating way. The intermittant instrumental section focusses on the motif and variations on it. Interesting section.

After The Rain starts pretty gently and soft, to get moving halfway through, with Manning's vocals taking off, and his guitar becoming a bit stronger. Especially during this section he fully sounds like aforementioned Harper. This strength is shortlived, before too long we return to the gentleness. Nice track, but nothing too extreme.

Blue Girl is a pretty relaxed track, with gentle percussion and melancholic vocals, sort of smokey, almost John Martyn. Not a very pronounced track this. The sharpish guitar solo during the bridge and the Glenn Frey type sax solo towards the end make this a track that would have fit perfectly on an album from the second half of the eighties.

The start of the closing Suite is sweet (no pun intended), gentle piano vocal, with the feel of a lovesong or lullaby. After a short jazzy bridge, the instrumental section fleshes out, mingling different influences as well, more progressive, at times with the romantic feel you find in some Quidam material. This longish section leads into another vocal section, which no longer has the full feel. The second lenghty instrumental section is more flute based, and rather gentle. It lacks the fluency and beauty of the first. As it progresses sax, guitar and synth get their bite at the cherry too, but most has been eaten already. This second section really is a bit long for the material presented. After a final closing vocal section we slide instrumentally towards the end. The track has good moments, but stretches too long to keep the attention.

Conclusion

in my previous review of a Manning disc (Cascade) I stated that the disc sounded a bit too much like a one man project. Two years further down the line I feel the same about this one. The use of guests of course helps, but the drums often sound to me as too flat and unoriginal to fully support, and the guitars (although a seperate player was employed) sometimes sound to sharp. Especially the drums on Phase and The Rut are a real problem for me, being mixed so far to the fore. Apart from that Manning's sharpish voice remains an acquired taste. So, the verdict. Well, not really one, actually. After having heard several Manning albums, I'm still undecided in a way. The Suite is pretty good, as are other tracks, but there are always such tracks and sections which are pretty weak, yet present. On the other hand, there's some other material that almost floats by unnoticed. These fluctuations are what prevents Manning's discs from becoming albums. So, I'd say that as always there is some good stuff and some less good stuff.
