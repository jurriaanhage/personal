Summary

This is Polish Ankh's fourth album, showing a shift from hard progressive towards more alt directed material.

The music

Opener Expect#1 is a bleepy computer intermezzo from a Star Trek type computer, introducing the rather electronic start of The Trick. This track has a rather wavey feel, with straight drums, a lot of electronics and halted vocals, reminding somewhat of the computer-era Kraftwerk. As it progresses The Trick gains momentum and fluency.

Love Supreme has a rather guitarry opening. As the initial violence dies down a sharp track with King Crimson like looping guitar rises. The guitar melody played across at times strengthens the mesmerizing effect. The rhythm keep a modern feel.

The title Moonlight Drive sounds a lot more romantic than this intricate track is. Having said that, the combination of electronics, rhythm and guitar loop makes for a pretty decent combination, and a rather full sound spectrum, reminiscent of Hungarian band After@all, but due to the combination of rhythm, guitar and synth also of Chemical Brothers, especially their Block Rockin' Beats. Any vocals are more vague vocalisations in the back, the track is lead by keys and multiple guitar layers. Nice stuff.

Loop Slow Vocal Mix starts with percussion and guitar looping, later replaced by flute and synth, with the scream in fear of someone plunging down from the 21st. Intermezzo like track, but nice.

On The Way is once again by a Fripp style guitar riff, with lingering vocals, brewing into a swirling total.

Never opens with dueling clear guitar riffs and sampled looping vocals in the back, beforing really starting vocally, although the vocals never go beyond vocalisation. This is yet another mesmerising track.

I Want You is formed around a Three Of A Perfect Pair riff, later bursting loose into a rather stronger variant of that, with some vocal doodling across.

You And Me is rather alt, modern type rhythm and distorted jangly guitar. The progressive influences have gone, but some of the electronics remain. The end result doesn't entice me much.

Hiding-place is an instrumentally directed intermezzo, jangly guitar and bleepy synth. Not an addition.

Escape has something of electronic pop. Not the bleepy form of the early eighties, but it doesn't have the rocky feel of before. The guitar loop no longer has the KC, and with that, it's lost the enticing feel of its predecessors.

Blue is another of those Kraftwerk feel tracks. Despite the presence of acoustic guitar, the track has a rather cold, experimental and electronic feel.

Loop Fast Violin Mix has somewhat whiny guitar, sounding like an electronic violin. As the track moves on, a real violin moves in, but only momentarily so. This track seems to link the later tracks to the previous ones, with loopy Fripp guitars and the narrative vocals. Pretty decent after the couple of tracks gone before.

Unexpected is exactly what you'd expect to find at the end of an album with the three Expects that've gone before.

Conclusion

This album is a bit more electronic and less hard edged than previous albums. Having said that, a clear King Crimson influence remains in the guitar parts. Whereas previous albums could be best categorized heavy progressive or prog metal, this has more experimental rock influences as can be had with recent Galahad and After@All (without comparing the bands any further). At the end of the day, though, Ankh don't sound as much KC as KC does nowadays, making it a bit too much KC at times, and with the extra influences this one comes out a real nice album, that I would probably prefer over more recent King Crimson releases. Best tracks are Love Supreme and On The Way. But we're not done yet...

And then we move past the last Expect track. And we no longer seem to be listening to progressive music, but something altogether more alty, synthy. The tracks are a tad non committal and less tempting and enticing.

Had this album consisted of just the first ten tracks, mine would have been a positive review, possible complaining about the short length only. As it is, I can't help but find that the later tracks spoil a lot of the fun had in the first part of the album. Still a tentative okay overall.
