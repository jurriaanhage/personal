Summary

A live album from the Far Off Grace tour with a little help from Don Dokken and Patrick Rondat.

The music

The album opens with plenty of drive, not because of the chanting audience but because of the terrific melodic lines, aggressive keyboards and the driven performance of first-off I Can See. Next up is Into The Sun which opens with heavy rhythm guitar and the keyboard section owes to the menacing side of Faith No More. Again plenty of speed and drive in this track. A memorable song with a straightforward chorus, the fact that we can still here some errors in the sound of the instruments imply that no overdubs have been used. The vocals on this track are not always strong, maybe due to a bit of an accent, but the band does rock. The Soul Survives is from the Colour Temple and opens quietly with pianic tones. Again, the vocals sound a bit accented, but one can get used to this. The music continues to be accessible on the whole with some harder edged and against-the-grain passages but the focus lies always on the composition and melody and there's hardly any fiddling about, and the music doesn't sound overly complex either, in my opinion quite a relief sometimes. The Soul Survives is built on a playful and sticks-in-your-head theme with some repetitive guitar solo's in the middle, but the keyboardist also has decided that tonight is his night: no snowing under him. Past halfway we slip into something more balladic and the piano plays along a while until the majestic, but playful theme sets in again. With respect to that the vocal melody is rather meagre. How Many Tears is the only track to pass the ten minute mark. The singer tells me it is a brand new version, but I have no idea, since I do not happen to know the song. The song opens in a relaxed mood, and only becomes loud and full during the repetitive chorus. Plenty of breaks right after the chorus, and the music then winds down to piano and the singer invites the audience to sing with him, in a translation French this time. I wonder if he does something similar in every country. Then the guitar sets in with a vengeance and the song reaches a climax. Don't Miss You is a typical ballad, but one where the band wanted to do it a bit differently lyrically. The chorus is just a little bit too sweet for my tastes. Still, quite a few beautiful, tragic moments in here. Journey To Paris is an instrumental especially written it seems for this concert. Wild meandering keyboards by Werno on this one and later some piano playing by contrast. Spirit Of Life is a shortish instrumental with some powerful guitar playing going from long notes to groovy breakful, progmetal. I have no idea if this track happens to be a new one. Iodic Rain and, less surprisingly, follow-up Far Off Grace are both from the Far Off Grace album. The first of these opens forcefully with Faith No More audible in the keyboards again. The music of the band of course has more of the instigators of the genre Dream Theater and Queensryche. The song has some sinister overtones. These sinister overtones are also somewhat present in Far Off Grace that has an epic sound and especially nice keyboard parts and surprisingly a rendition of Ne Me Quitte Pas worked right into the track. Nobody touches the tragedy of the original version by Jacques Brel, but by its unexpectedness this is done quite nicely (with classic piano to boot). Then the band works itself back into the track itself and closes down with something very familiar sounding. In Dokken's own Kiss Of Death it is Don Dokken himself to do the singing. The vocals of Dokken are much rougher and have a bit more experience in them. Also he has less trouble with his accent, he has none. Quite a good composition, maybe a bit too much like plain metal, but like I said quite good. On the final track Patrick Rondat responsible for some above average instrumental guitar albums. Rainmaker opens bombastically. Good melody lines on the keyboards and plenty of drive and energy make this a typical Vanden Plas track. The singer does go a bit too much towards the higher regions where I do not always like his voice.

Conclusion

A good attempt to a compilation of this progmetal band that gets extra value because of the new tracks, but mostly I think because of the energetic performance (and for some of the fans the participation by Dokken and Rondat). Most live albums to me seem a way of getting easy money out of fans, especially if inspiration fails. This, I feel, does not hold for Vanden Plas, the band gives extra value to their compositions.
