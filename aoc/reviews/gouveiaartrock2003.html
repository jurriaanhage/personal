Summary

The music

Nil is a band making a very laidback form of prog, with Eastern influences. Although the music is vocal, lengthy instrumental sections are abound, indicating the band's history as an instrumental act. The band's female vocalist Roselyne Berthet has a rather wavering voice on the opener, which tends to swerve off key at times too, especially on word vocals. This might have been compensated for by a strong instrumental approach, but alas. Exactly the opening track takes some ten minutes to take a definite form, thus failing to give the support needed. 198 is an instrumental bit which is stronger than the opener, but still does not fully convince on the melodic side of things. After this break the vocals have seriously improved, more secure, no longer off key. Must have been a good break, there. This starts in the short Derives, but is most apparent in the closing of the band's efforts on this DVD, Abandon. This track has a clearer build up, and manages to create some momentum with some nice soloing on guitar and heavy moog-type synths. A rather strong closing.

La Maschera Di Cera was brought to life by Fabio Zuffanti in order to bring back the vintage sound of Italian seventies prog. The start of their offering on this DVD does not advocate that starting point. The flutey and freaky sound has more of a jazz feel. Not before long, though, the heavy synths roll out and we dó get that vintage sound. Still, the band mostly present material from their second disc, Il Grande Labirinto, which is far less heavy on the symphonic side as their debut was. The band's closer on this disc is a rather experimental, jazzy bit featuring Manuel Cardoso of Tantra.

As a bonus we get short interviews with both bands, but these are mostly on the level of "happy to be here", along with some soundcheck views. Then there's a six minute impressionary documentary on the festival itself. Nothing earth shattering here.

Conclusion

This disc has 68 minutes of concert footage, thus leaving out a lot of the material played by the bands on it (both played a full two hours), and omitting one band (Saturnia) fully (except for a minor bit in the extras). I'm not sure I understand this, especially since this leaves a DVD which is on the short side.

Of course it's hard to judge a band's performance at a festival based on just part of it. I would say, however, that what's offered on this disc does not convince me, not to the point I would have expected it too. The Nil offering contained some parts that were not fully there technically, whilst the Maschera footage falls short on the symphonic side. So, I'm left on the fence on this one: we get some nice offerings, but I can't feel satisfied.
