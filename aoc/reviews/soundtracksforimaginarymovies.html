Summary

The music

I would almost get the impression that there's some sort of Tangerine Dream revival going on. That is, if they ever lost much popularity with the EM-community anyway. Once again I find myself reviewing a disc that stylistically lies rather close to the sort of music TD made in the mid eighties, blending in older atmospheres. We have the Spanish guitar, the rhythmic synths with your average electronic carpet weaved into a nice bit of tapestry. And further on the heavy mellotron and some sequencer. These are the style elements we hear on the majority of tracks, starting clearly enough with opener Green Miata Baja Sound.

The Cool Vibe Of Asia C indeed has some Asian sounds, albeit more middle and north eastern than what one would normally associate with Asian sounds. As Four Piece Suite progresses more dance influences are added in. As did TD later on. Silent Service builds up slowly towards a majestic mellotron climax, winding down into some more experimental sounds. This heavy mellotron sound returns on closer Last Letters From Stalingrad, displaying that ST also uses the older TD vocabulary.

Water Through Fingers features odd sounding huffy synths and some eastern influences, thus steering away from the TD lingo. Once again the build up towards a climax pans out rather well. One Step To Freefall takes the TD vocabulary out for a walk on the wild side, it being rather experimentally odd and less melodic than the other material.

Conclusion

There's not so much original about this album. Systems Theory mostly express themselves in a vocabulary that was created over twenty years ago. In that vocabulary, however, they are quite proficient. The fact that ST isn't afraid of some experiment with the TD vocabulary helps in creating an album that despite its length remains interesting. The result of which is an experimental hackneyed album that sounds pretty good.
