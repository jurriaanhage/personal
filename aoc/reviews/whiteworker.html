Summary

Quite a bunch of musicians for a relatively short record. But read on...

The music

The album opens with the playful clarinets of Belgiques, maybe a bit too melodic, but the music becomes more likable when a cello sets in. Violin and percussively played keyboards we find on Out-and-Outer. this is a bit of a jazzy piece with some Arabian melodies on sax and rather up-beat.

After the horn section on Peppermint Lounge we proceed to the longish Watchman. The music is a bit contrary here, some Reich influences, then some flamenco, the next part is music for Hobbits. Quirky and bouncy. The final part is melodious and waltzy.

Soft, short and angelic is Fragile, Broken Blossoms is sleepy, languid. Explosion By Sympathy is much better with its strong melody. Lots of tuba on this record by the way. It is used as a bass would be used by a rock band.

The most varied track is Soylent Red (Not Green. Weirdly enough when I looked at the tracklisting above during the writing of this review, having typed it in much earlier, I noticed that I HAD written GREEN up there. Interesting mistyping.) Food for thought this track with its rock guitar, lalala vocals and somewhat Bernstein like feel. The ending is a bit nervous.

Echoes of After Crying (I have the idea that Mertens never heard of this band) can be found on Oom Pah, which also has some Emersonian organ playing (but not over the top).

For some reason ME reminds me of Gershwins Summertime. The song has some swirly harpsichords, too words that I thought would not mix. The song has both contrary and melodic parts, which makes it nice to listen to. Compare it with After Crying, but a bit lacking in tension.

The closer is funeral music.

Conclusion

This is not really prog, but more like minimal music. There are influences of Steve Reich, Michael Nyman and proggers might here some After Cyring in there. The music does sound a bit more sterile and playful. Nice to listen to, but not something I'd play often (but what is?)
