Summary

Proto-kaw is the first release of music made by one the predecessors of Southern proggers Kansas. The first three tracks were recorded late in 1971, during what's now known as the Cavern Recordings. Tracks 4 to 7 were recorded late 72 in a studio. The two final tracks were recorded live in 1972. Although hissy, the first seven tracks sound fine. The final two have a sound quality that could be called poor, more hiss and far from sparkling. However, there's no sign of drop outs, or any such things.

The music

Strong opener Hegemonium shows some early Van Der Graaf Generator influences, with the same soaring sax sound (SSS), although the vocal parts seem to refer to the first to King Crimson discs.

The start of Reunion... is a bit more toned down than the opener, and closer to what you could call prototypical Kansas music. The tone is grave, with slow flute and organ. Remarkably, though Meredith's voice is clearly different from Steve Walsh's, the way of singing is similar. Another good one.

Nactolos opens with Livgren's grave piano play, to be killed off rudely by shrieking sax. As the smoke resides, the strong vocals, organs, and SSS rise again. The lengthy instrumental section introduces guitars and military rhythms. This track seems to integrate the VDGG directed sound of the first track with the musical vein Kansas made famous.

With Belexes we move away from the experimentalism of the first threesome, having some pomp rock influences in this case, especially because of the rhythms and the pointed guitar riffs. It does, however, remain far from any kind of seedyness. Good stuff.

Totus Nemesis takes the tempo down a bit, having more of a blues rock feel, enhanced by lenghty jazzy intermezzos, which aren't always unexperimental. Despite still being good, this track seems to have less structure than those before.

Greek Structure Sunbeam is a somewhat jammy tranquil track carried by flute and guitar, moving slowly towards a climax.

Incomudro is a track later recorded by the 'regular' Kansas on their Song For America album. Hearing this earlier, somewhat more raw version is rather worthwhile.

Cyclopy is part of a longer jazzy improvised piece, although the SSS is present too, giving this a ring that's more than just jazzy. Pretty okay.

Skont is a vocal track, once again with longish jammy sections, with dominant SSS and strong organ.

Conclusion

In the liner notes in the CD's booklet Livgren expresses his regret at not recording more material during the 71-73 period. I have to fully agree with him on that. Especially on the first three tracks the band perform at a level they hardly reached again after that. And as far as overall quality goes: none of the Kansas albums manages to maintain the standard this collection from three sessions does. Despite the fact that these tracks have different origins, they fit together well enough to hide this fact. Great stuff.
