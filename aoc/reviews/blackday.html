Summary

The end of the cold war. A band from Russia on an American label. With love I presume.

The music

The Beginning Of The End opens this album. This is gloomy stuff, quite atmospheric with slow moving melodies. But then we move into a quirky passage with hollow sounding percussion. A negative point are the vocals that sound rather bad in the higher regions. Quite a lot of variation in this track riff and rhythm wise. However, the music is not of the type I generally appreciate, being nervously jumpy and quite little structure. Also some the passages are a bit too frolic for my tastes. When done well, it can be enjoyable to listen to, but I'm afraid this doesn't quite make it. The recording quality is not bad, but the vocals and percussion could sound livelier. I understand, as always, that this may be a budget problem, but it should be noted nonetheless. The metal continues on the second track, which happens to be the title track. A rather simple melody played repetitively but in first instance rather well and fast on guitar. This track shows that the band can do more than just string things together, because for some reason the weird amalgam of metal and psyche guitar, somber keyboards and all kinds of interjected interludes (preferably a few at the time) make this an interesting experience. The opening sequence is a bit like Hendrix played really fast, but the music even has some funky influences and strangely enough some totally unfitting melodic piano playing. Quite a nerve. I Love That World (as with all titles I give you the English translation which happens to be on the cover as well) which opens with blues based guitar playing. For Azazello this quite a straightforward piece with unfortunately those terrible high vocals again. Musically it sounds quite good, although the keyboard sounds come over a bit dated. In the middle the band starts to fiddle around again while later the song turns into circus metal. Free Flight is a catchy track with a good chorus. The rest of the music has a bit of a jazz tinge with plenty of things happening and some nice rolling drums. Sometimes the vocals sound a bit doubtful, but for the most part the singer takes it easy on this track. Demon Of Love sounds promising opening again with something like circus metal. Then the song takes a turn for the aggressive, but not for long, because through a funky passage we come back to some circuslike stuff. The song is quite "progressive" in the original sense if I may say so. There are some ups and downs pace wise, quite a lot of breaks and on the whole a step up from the previous tracks. Take Your Choice! is first heavy romantic stuff, but then continues with maybe a break too many. Lots of passage strung together and although they may seem nice by themselves I'm not that fond of this way of composing. I do see a likeness here with Moonchild boss Brian Hirsch, the music sounding quite ehm technocratic. The major track is the almost 11 minutes long Night Before The Christmas. This is in fact the first track where the music can develop for a while and the band takes a rest from all the breaks and alternation. The song is not overly metallic although it has its shares of heavy/fast drums for instance. Quite a lot of keyboards on this very melodic track which is by far the most likable on this album. I prefer the way they sing in a more subdued way. The song features some plenty of different passages, but these do get the time to evolve a bit and you don't have to first train your short term memory to arrive at something slightly continuous. This very melodic song does have quite a lot of classical influences especially in the keyboard/piano. The final track is a short one, Christmas. A bit of a folky acoustic tune, sad and yes, beautiful also.

Conclusion

On the whole I'm not that impressed by Azazello. Some of the singing is quite bad (in the higher regions), but the quality of the music (and also the singing by the way) rises towards the end of the album ending with too good tracks. I think the band would do wise to let off a bit on the variation side and try to be less frantic. Some attention should be given to the recording process (mostly the drumms, although it gets better along with the music). I hope the songs were put on this disc in more or less chronological order of coming into existence. Azazello might then very well surprise us on their next record.
