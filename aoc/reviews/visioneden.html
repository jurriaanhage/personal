Summary

I've never had the pleasure of listening to any of their discs, but there's a first time for everything. On their own record label, Omega Records and with a new singer, the Sri-Lankan "Chity".

The music

If you had any illusion about the style of this band, they are gone at the outset: heavy plodding progmetal is featured in opener They Are In Between Us that lyrically is a some kind of X-Files song. The vocals of Chity are somewhere in between the vocalist of Egdon Heath, but with a German accent, going in the direction of Scorpions Klaus Meine (I wonder how long this Sri Lankan guy already lives in Germany). Strangely enough also the melody of this song reminds me of some Egdon Heath song, maybe Gringo from Him, the Snake and I. Still this is typical progmetal so do not let this think this is really like said band. Children Of War continues the line of the previous song, plenty of breaks here, but the music is and stays melodic. In the middle there's some breathing space with some low bass after which there's a wailing guitar solo. It turns out now that this a concept album where each of the songs is preceded by a small piece of this SF story. Lord Of Dignity seems to be a more balladlike track with piano and such, but the chorus is quite heavy. Like the previous tracks quite catchy and tight. Fate Of Centuries is the fourth track, prominently featuring Petra's bass in the opening. As the first track some slight Arabic tendencies in this track and like all previous tracks actually: very bombastic, plenty of harmony vocals and plenty, though not all very fluent, of tempo changes. The vocal melody is quite striking in this track. Distortion is the main feature of Age Of Salvation. In the previous track water was lost from the Earth, a problem that is still present in this track. Mid-tempo verses are alternated with the heavy guitars of the intro until we come to the instrumental intermezzo: a nice bass solo followed by some pyrotechnics on guitar. Dancing With The Devil opens classically with something like flamenco guitar and rhythm. Then the music turns to plodding, the vocals alternate between the dark low vocals of somebody reciting names of the "darker" metal bands and the vocals of Chity (with harmonies of course) that seem to tell us that these bands are on the wrong road, inviting evil and such. The Road To Eden is the first, but short, piece of quiet we get. Not great and not that slow either. Loosing the thread of the story now (was there really any?) we come to Far Away. It opens bombastically with plenty of pianic keyboards, after which the guitars, of course, take over. Gene Genius (Jean Genie?) is a machine that was built in 42 years (not that 42 again!) and instead of protecting destroys mankind. The vocoded, watery sounding, vocals are a nice touch. I'm not that satisfied with some the guitar solo's here. The short Solitune closes shop. A peaceful instrumental with synths, strings, acoustic guitars and soft percussion.

Conclusion

Energetic and bombastic progressive metal. Not many places where you can actually relax and the fact that they use harmonies quite a lot also makes for a filled up soundspectrum. Melodically there's little wrong with this album and this melodicity stays intact notwithstanding the fact that the band likes to insert plenty of breaks and tempo changes into their music. One of the major problems I have with this album is that there's little variety really and the songs are hard to tell apart. The singer is good, though slightly accented and the lyrics are not always totally grammatical or sensible. Summarizing however, this is a nice album of progressive metal with a high energy level with tight compositions and little fiddling about, but as you might have guessed little originality. Still, I liked it.
