Summary

After the Platypus album (time for a follow up, guys?) Derek now comes with a solo album (yet playing in a band, his own).

The music

The album opens with the three parted Atlantis, totalling almost 17 minutes. The opening is in the ELP style, but is quickly transformed into a dark instrumental intro. The music is played foremostly on keyboards here and in a for progmetal, very varied way. The rhythm guitar is used only to put some dark accents on the music. Then the solo guitar sets in after which we return to the not so straightforward keyboards. The music here may be likened to the Liquid Tension Experiment. When we sail into the Seas Of Antiquity there's only a slight breeze and the mist is heavy. A piano sets in, accompanied by the soft sad whine of bass. Then the guitar takes the fore and slowly the mist dissolves and the sky starts to shine through. After some reversed cymbals we come to the Lost Island which is again slightly in the LTE style. The music doesn't blister as LTE does however. Notwithstanding the fact that the music is quite jazzy at times, it sounds bit darker and ehm under-the-skin. The style of this track is continued in Crab Nebulae: lots of keyboards, plenty of variation both in rhythm as well as melody and only a smattering of guitarwork (compared to other Magna Carta releases). The next one up is Box and this one has a very good bombastic melody on which this urgent track rides. Reading a review (by Freek Wolff) of this band puts me on the track of an interesting reference: UK. Although I wouldn't say this album reaches the level of Danger Money (in my opinion very little does) there's a strong likeness in approach: quick but always melodic. After the extremely fast paced Money Shot and the not so interesting jazzrockprogmetal of Day In The Sun we come to the slow but heavy State Of Delirium featuring plodding drums, organ and pressing guitarwork. A rather short track and afterwrads we jet into the drum ridden Space Martini. Again, the music reminds me a lot of LTE, but the sound seems a bit less clear, less sharp. Could be done on purpose of course. Plenty of variation again, but not so interesting melodically. This song is mostly about power and energy. The closer Brunei Babylon still gives us no relief but it ends on a good melodic note.

Conclusion

An album chokeful of variation: lots of keyboard, significantly less guitar work and plenty of not too obvious melodies solo's and signature changes. All in all something that the lover of LTE or UK should be interested to hear. The music is more composed than LTE and I think there are also better melodies here. Best tracks are the opener Atlantis, Box and the closer Brunei Babylon.
