Summary

I know very little of this band. Their name is like that of the Dutch prog mag I write for based on a moon of Jupiter.

The music

75.8 opens the album with sound effects. It continues into the groovy Satellite. The vocals are by both Foley and Sheri. Her vocals are somewhat echoed, while his are also not untouched. A mid-tempo piece, but not too interesting I'm afraid, although the chorus has something. Like the previous track Life has an Science Fiction subject. Life is more "progressive" and has a bit of a waltzlike melody. The chorus reminds me of Echolyn. The guitar solo is quite melodic this time and there's plenty of variation. Nocturne is a relaxed track with low key guitar and low key vocals. There are some more powerful moments in which the guitar gain a bit in power. The synth strings lay a cosmic carpet for the music to build upon. Fever Pitch consists of percussive piano and I'm often reminded of Die A Tribe. That is also a band that can not be described as "progressive", sounding more like "ordinary bands", but is not entirely unlike it either. The music of iO doesn't sound easy, but because of this the music sometimes doesn't really flow well. Foley sings very loud on Fever Pitch while Sheri does the repetitive backing vocals that remind me of the music of the eighties. Here Now In The Knowing opens quietly with soft piano and the clear high vocals of Sheri. The song has a very memorable vocal melody, not only the chorus but also the verses. The music gains in power with distorted guitars in the back and backing vocals that remind me of Sting. The song fades away gently. Nations returns with the percussive piano and the vocals of Foley. A good beginning and the melodies stay good throughout. The music reminds me a bit of Todd Rundgren (and the male vocals too), although it is a bit less slick than that. Stolen Land is a Bruce Cockburn cover. Heavy guitars, and a rather uninteresting vocal melody and chorus. Five Long Days is back to the moody, melodic pop with the piano on the fore. Bruce Hornsby? Chronomes opens with keyboards and effects which moves right into a groovy guitar section. I'm not really satisfied with the vocal part though, too little melody. Closing track is Reverie. A stately/dreamy track with a nice vocal melody. The repetitive acoustic guitar sounds familiar (is that Sailing I hear?).

The lyrics show maturity.

Conclusion

I'm afraid I can't call this prog (in terms of GEPR). I can't really call it anything else, but if I would have to put a label on it, then it would be indie/alternative. This does not mean that the music is very accessible, because it is not. The female vocals are often quite close to pop, the male vocals are more what you'd expect from a singer songwriter, the guitar is noisy and the piano is used in a percussive way while the percussion itself is quite varied. Most appealing songs are Life, Nocturne, Here Now In The Knowing and Nations.
