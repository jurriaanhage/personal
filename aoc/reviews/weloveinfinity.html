Summary

Little is known about them, at least to me. They are from the UK and this is their debut. There seems to be no website, but you can contact them via vivahead@lycos.co.uk.

The music

Seduction is a noisy affair. Not that it is loud, but it is full of sound effects and running tapeloops and a weird vocal loop with what seems to be an old guy laughing in some fashion. In between we hear shards of film music and a road drill. Nothing much changes throughout its five minutes. Think of Reich and Bryars here.

Pig Giddy is a long affair, almost 25 minutes. Again minimal in nature these are not loops but synth loops and good ones too building a mysterious atmosphere. There are also some more percussive sounding parts here, the way piano can do and in between we get repetitive lines of cello. Obviously, these people get their inspiration from Glass and consorts. The music is a bit moodier and more subdued than Glass. And I guess also quite a bit richer in tension, so that the music can be listened to as a kind of soundtrack. I also heard ressemblances to Eddie Jobson's Theme Of Secrets, but Vivahead is a bit more minimalistic. Percussion sets in after the four minute mark, here the music starts to depart a bit from the general minimal nature, although the synth loop continues. It is getting better and better. In the back we get more and more sounds and effects, mainly sustained synths and once in a while some percussion comes by moving from one ear to the other. It is not really necessary for me to keep up wtih Vivahead here, although the music is minimal in nature, there are changes, mainly thematic ones, while the repetitiveness stays. The music continues to be tense and mysterious and grows in complexity to cut back on that at short notice once in a while. As such the music really evolves and in a meaningful way. In addition to synths, the song also harbours some piano playing and with all the string like synths, the impression of an orchestra is built. Great stuff.

Halfway through the album we arrive at track three, Cycle. After the seriousness of the previous track, we are back to the nonsense of the first track, but wait it does also have elements of the second track with the minimalistic keyboads. But the chorus of rubber duckies...

In The Making is quite fast with a focus on rhythm. The rhythms remind me of Aphex Twin (but I am not that well-versed in that area of music). In between, we have moody synths playing in a meandering way, and distorting sound effects a bit later on. This comes quite close to something which may be found on Cuneiform records, a label which might fit Vivahead well. Quite RIO.

Peter is a lot friendlier and strongly thematic. A great theme it is too. There are also some classical ingredients here with violin/cello in the back. Wim Mertens comes to mind here.

The Question means a return to effects and samples with the road drill, the cello (or something of that kind) running in a sad depressed fashion. The vocal effects give the music a Jarre feel (Technicolor), but for the rest it is still the same brand of minimal music, although a bit less so here. Plenty of rhythmic piano here as well. Almost danceable and very friendly too, notwithstanding the road drill. The final part brings in the Question by means of a tape loop and a bit of humour. Concern For A Celebrity is the short closing loop.

Conclusion

Minimal music mainly based on keyboards, but also effect loops, samples and maybe even some live cello. In some tracks the band comes out quite experimentally, in others, and then mainly the tracks Peter and the mile long Pig Giddy, the music is thematic. Especially Pig Giddy is a great collection of minimal melodies with quite a bit of tension. The music is richly atsmopheric throughout. No rock, no prog, but by pure melodic appeal and their ability to build the right atmosphere, this duo has won me over.
