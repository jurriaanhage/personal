Summary

A self produced album from Ohio.

The music

The opening track brings some heavy rock. The vocals are more typical of a rock band, rough, and not that appealing to me. I think this may also have to do with the way are recorded. The sound spectrum is quite full on this track, only after the guitar solo, does the music quieten down a bit. The music is certainly not prototypical prog. It is much too rock oriented for that. However, it is also not straightforward rock or AOR. Think of bands like Crack The Sky and Led Zeppelin here. The Castle Dweller is also a riff based track, with varied drumming. This latter is not surprising since the drummer is also the composer of all the music. A few nods to Kashmir as well here. After a rather complex intro, Around The Corner continues with a quick vocal part, and then we get some more atmospheric part in which the guitar may fiddle around a bit. It's good to have such passages in the music, because during the really rocky parts, the sound spectrum continues to be quite full. As always during the music of No Brass there's a leading role also for the drummer, with all kinds of fills and signature changes. A World Within brings more of the same, but with quite of a lot of bass in there and improvised guitar playing. I'm not that fond of Yeahs and Ohs and there are quite a few of them right here. The pace goes up A Star To A Star, a bit of a jumpy track. The guitar playing at first seems to try to replace the missing organ. Strange. An upbeat track with some a very nice "ascending" guitar solo. The Love Song brings a slow down. Not really a ballad though. The vocal melodies are distinctive, but for me they are not melodic enough I'm afraid. The ending sounds quite emotional. As often happens for me with this band, in Roll On People, the melodic guitar parts and the drumming are the most notable features, although the bass may solo a bit too. The final guitar solo is really good. The Death Of A Drone is the driving penultimate track. It has some King Crimsonesque influences in the guitar playing. Close is True Passion with its bouncy rhythm and prominent bass playing.

The lyrics I read with interest. They have that something -- when compared with for instance non-English bands -- that makes them sound real.

Conclusion

Not really a prog album, but featuring some the elements, especially in the rhtyhm section. The music is strongly rock oriented and the vocals are not great, but the music has energy and drive, the main ingredients of rock 'n' roll. However, for the people that I think are the main type of visitors of this page, probably not their cup of tea. This is not to say that the music has no variation, because it does. A bit like Crack the Sky but without the irony and also a bit rougher.
