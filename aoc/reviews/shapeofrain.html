Summary

Mickey Simmonds is well-known for his work with Fish on Vigil and has also cooperated at some time with Camel and Mike Oldfield. This is his first solo album on what might be called the follow up label to the defunkt SI Music.

The music

The album is a concept album about a girl who leaves home because she feels that her parents don't care for her, but only regard as a toy to play with. Unfortunately on leaving she encounters a man who agrees to put her up, but as you finds out later, not for nothing. She's not killed outright it seems, but becomes wretched enough to kill herself. In between there In A Country having a more general lyric about the bad things in England (I guess). The lyrics to this song at btw not very good. Half the tracks is instrumental.

What about the music then. Well, it's a concept album and that means a number of recurring themes and on the whole the album is rather soothing and relaxed with a lot of synth and piano, but fortunately there are some more heavy moments on the album, for instance the bombastic climax in Twilight or the already mentioned In A Country. These passages and others, make sure that this is more a progressive than a synth release.

The first track is a rather quiet and melancholic intro. The story then starts in Terminus and this song evokes the quiet atmosphere of the latest Camel release Harbour of Tears. The vocals only help to strengthen this belief. At the end however the tempo goes up and the song becomes very loud. Good melodies, somewhat dramatic.

The next track starts out melancholy. The lyrics are also rather depressing. The last verse however is sung in a different mode, with a higher, more angry voice. The synth solo after it is again very loud like in Terminus and fits wonderfully well. After this we get a build up to a more up-tempo, and comparably inaccessible, percussive part.

In a Country is a little to accessible for my tastes and there's a separation between the harshness of the words and the rather easy going mid-tempo music. Also I find the music rather superficial.

The next track is more firing track and can be compared to Landmarq's hectic instrumentals. Drumming is a little weak, but the keyboardplaying is very fast and well-done.

This even goes into the next kind of jazzy track with ELPish keyboardwork. The vocals are rather playful although the contents are certainly not. A little melodramatic as well, so if you can't stomach this.

The next track reminds me of Yanni in this better period.

As should be in a symphony, the climax to the album is not at the end, but a little before it. In this case it is Twilight that has the honour. It starts out very subdued with the planned suicide of the girl. The build up is very slow, as melodies are added to the palette and the vocals gain volume building up to a climax.

The last track is a definite closer with its percussive piano and thundering drum. In the end it becomes sad, with a beautiful melody on the piano and after a majestic build up it faes into the distance.

Conclusion

Comparable to for instance Harbour of Tears and closer to that than for instance to Vigil. It has to be said though that while the lyrics are sometimes a little well, naive and obvious (In a Country) and it might have been better if they had concentrated on the interaction between people which is desribed a lot better.

The music is very melodic and although the synths are very pronounced it's not plain synthesizer music. A problem you might have though is with the drummachine that tends to annoy in places.

Maybe people will find this release a little too accessible, and compare it easily with the softer side of Camel or even with someone like Gandalf. This is not absolutely true, because the album has quite a number of heavier, bombastic and climactic passages and thus makes it more varied than for instance Harobur of Tears of Camel. It is true however that you should not be afraid of a little melodrama and some of the singing lines can be a little too melodic for my tastes.
