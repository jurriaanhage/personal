Summary

Originally a performance entitled Reality Check by Gnosis, the soundtrack of that performance is now released under the name of the ensemble Z-Axis.

The music

The album opens with Name You Poison that functions like an overture. The main theme is very good and memorable and later on some heavy guitars set in after which the theme returns with more force. Quite a bit of percussion on this track. Slow Glass is slow in developing (not too slow) with eerie keyboards and softly pounding percussion. The music here is very ambient sounding, but with some notable rock influences in the guitar work. People might be tempted to think of neo-psych bands like Ozric Tentacles here, but in a way the music seems more minimal and avant-garde. I think a reference to King Crimson is also in order. Emperor's Cascade is much quieter, more electronic, but with live percussion (and I mean percussion, not drums). The music is a bit tame here, a bit world music influenced, although the theme is nice. Later on some high pitched keyboards are added and the drums move into the military style. Nice build-up. On the whole the music so far has a strong live sound, as if the band is really performing this live. Synthetic flutes open the first part of Entropy And Alchemy called Holy Ground. This is a very quiet piece with some nice subtle melodies. Again the band takes its time to develop its themes. The next part is more Tangerine Dream like with both cosmic and more sequencer like passages intertwined. The music is also more definitely melodic here. Then we get a rather weird break into something much more poppy. I'm not fond of this part, which is a more danceable version with something reminding me of Bossa-Nova sounds. People more fond of eighties TD will like this I guess. Later on a sharp, noisy sounding guitar sets in. The percussion is lame. Silhouettes also has this aspect of a two-split, but this time I like both parts, where the percussion is live and strongly present. The final part of this long track is called Ebb & Flow opening with runs of harp and a rather highpitched keyboard sound. A somewhat noisy sound and I'm not sure if people will like this. Then the music takes a turn for the mellow with something akin to French accordion music, but playing the same bolero like melody, but slowly. The melody seems like a variation on Nova's Aurora (Nova is the more commercial side of the Dutch electronic threesome Peru. Aurora was a big hit here in the Netherlands.), but with acoustic guitar added. Suspension is a more ambient piece, long and dark with plenty of room for percussion. The longest single track is also the one with the longest title: In The Country Of The Blind, The One-Eyed Man Is King. It opens friendly with piano and a good melody, but soon thereafter we move into scarier regions and we end up in a loop like piece with some nice bombastic overtones that set in and fade away. Sounds really good. Then the guiatr starts to dominate with long noisy chords. The percussion spins right along. The music is now quite moody, but this changes soon, because the music becomes much more melodic and catchy towards the end. In a good way though. Homecoming has a majestic sounding opening with bells ringing. Later on it becomes a bit mellow. Slat Dance is the up-tempo closer of the album with a good drive, a bit in the style of Art Of Noise. With a short jazzy interruption (in the bonus part) the track ends.

Conclusion

A hard album to review. Most of the stuff is electronic, but the percussion is very prominent and most of it live which gives more dynamics to the music. A few times we get some very accessible electronic music, but on the whole a sheen of avant-gare lies over the music. The band likes to play subtle mosttimes, the guitar is sometimes a bit in the style of Fripp, but what stays with me most is not the separate melodies and such, but the overall darkish feel of the music, the tendency to wait and see what happens on this album. For people who like a mix of Gabriels Passion, modern ambient in the darker style of Roach and some minimalist influences to boot and some Crimsonesque guitars thrown in for good measure.
