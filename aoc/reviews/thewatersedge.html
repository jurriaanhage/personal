Summary

Starting out as Season's End in 1995, Time And Tide have moved into a new era with this new album.

The music

Opener Wasteland is carried by a sparkling keyboard basis. The drums sound a bit muffled, whilst Bedard sounds insecure in his vocals, at times making odd sounds, resembling James LaBrie on live vocals. Not pretty. The track despite the sparkling keys has a laid back feel, disrupted into the bridge, in which the complexity goes up for reasons I can't quite fathom, since it is not necessarily in the track's interest. From this we move into an instrumental section echoing the track's theme. The Mark Kelly keys in the final vocal section fit in with the band's previous name Season's End, while Bedard is trying to sound like Magnum.

Time Away starts as a ballad with lingering and atmospheric guitars. This atmosphere is sweetly disturbed by Bedard's initially slow vocals, untill he starts yelling halfway through. AAARGH! What did you have to go and do that for?

All is more pumpy, with Bedard's hysteria kicking off right away. There really is no way of listening around this man's hollering, apparently devoid of technique.

Media as previous tracks has a nice keyboard twinkle, but the rhythm is uninspired, the guitar unoriginal and the vocals off into neverland. And the media voice over has been done a little too often to add much.

Gemini starts off with acoustic guitars and adds some tacky synths halfway through (hadn't had those yet). Fortress doesn't bring anything new.

Frankly, after what's gone before the thought of a 31 minute epic really scares me. On Part 1 we get a four minute intro, before Bedard starts actually singing. Now there's a surprise. Anyway, it doesn't last long unfortunately, but it's good to know he can sing. After this the track just continues, the only difference being that it's, well, long, really.

Conclusion

Even though there are some echoes of Rush, Magnum mixed with Chandelier type stuff is what we hear most on this album. In this vein the music isn't all that bad, not my style, but fitting in. However, no matter whether Bedard attempts to sing a ballad or an up tempo track, his vocals sound poor at best. Since this sort of music tends to be pretty vocal, things don't work out so well. The only thing nice sounding are the twinkly keys, but they do tend to sound pretty much the same across the tracks. Maybe interesting for big hair AOR afficionados completionists. You never know.
