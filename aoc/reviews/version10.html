Summary

Never heard of these guys before. They are from Brazil.

The music

Leafing through the accompanying booklet one might get the impresssion that this band is not prog at all. Why? Well, the large smiles on the guys for instance. Aren't proggers supposed to look serious all the time, and have the features chiseled into their faces. These guys look young and fresh. Well, the first two tracks will however convince most that the music of this band falls into the progressive category: the music is rather in the Jadis vein, which means not that complex, but mostly melodic with some nice keyboard work. The vocals sound a little far away, so some work needs to be done there. The next track The Witch also has the prog feeling, still commercial, but certainly not bad. The same holds for the next one Lights, which reminds me in some passages of For Absent Friends. The production of the vocals seem a little better here. Endless War is more groovy than the previous songs with a predominance of riffs and plenty of tempo changes. Circus Of Illusions is something else entirely: although a lot slower, the idiocy of the Cardiacs is present in this song. The Link opens with fast paced keyboards and acoustic guitar. Promising. the rest of the track is not as good. After the short The Long Trail we get bagpipes in the percussion rich Steam Train To Mallaig. This is a nice and frolic instrumental. The song Everybody Dance Wih T.S.B. is in fact what you might expect: funky music with trite lyrics. I'm not sure whether this is a joke, but I don't think it's funny. Fortunately it is a short track and thoroughly compensated by the long Turtles, Sharks and Beavers. The music on this track ranges from progmetal with treated vocals to Spanish folk (ayayayayaaaaa) and some nice harmonies. My friend the elephant would like to have some jabuticabas is the penultimate track and features some great guitar work with tasteful additions on bass. A good track this, with an emotional ending. The Cat's Tale is a mysterious track reminding me at times of Fruitcake with its mysterious vocals. Fruitcake however does not insert jolly choruses, like this band does.

Conclusion

Certainly not the new concept in rock music like they promised, but this is, though commcerial, not really bad. The "dance" track is certainly a stinker, but a short one, and the following tracks thoroughly compensate for them. As a whole a likable album, but like I said quite commercia, but also with a sense of humour.
