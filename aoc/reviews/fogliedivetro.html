The music

The first track works on the clock. The percussive part of the music sounds like a clock ticking away, while the lush keyboards in the back make this a grand start of the CD. Then the rather typically seventies guitarsound takes control, but only for a little while, because then some beautiful piano takes over accompanying the Italian vocals. The guitar solo's remind me a lot of I'Pooh's great Parsifal. The vocals sound okay at first but in a later part of the track they sound a little strange, a bit underproduced.

The drums sound a little flat in the next track, Prendi La Mia Anima. Again also the vocals sound a little too rough, too rugged. This is a pity, because the music on this track is really very good; very lush stuff, with good melodies.

The major track on this CD, begins hesitatingly with piano, and then a keyboard intro follows. The vocal part is very subdued and a bit sad, and is the following guitar solo, that sounds desolate. Again, the old I'Pooh comes to mind here. Then the song starts to build up, with room for both keys and guitar to solo in and some rather unexpected breaks. The piano figures a lot in this piece and interacts a lot with the guitar, but it is the screaming saxophone that brings the song to its summit, although the song is then still 4 minutes from being finished. After some more vocals, the piano is at it solo again, but just before the end the guitar comes in again, and the track ends percussively.

The next track is mostly acoustic with flute, acoustic guitar and vocals. A dreamy track, which isn't strange regarding the title. Foglie unplugged.

Ars Nova however features a lot of heavy guitars. The vocals are a bit amateuristic on this track and also the melodies are not very satisfying. I like them best when they are melodic. Stil, a more rocking track between all these melodic ones, can be a welcome change of plan.

Io...Partiro is a melodic mostly acoustic piece with a little keyboards on the side and later on a little flute. Very lyrical and fairy-like with a powerful percussive and pianic ending.

Mago Merlino (Merlin the Magician in case you didn't know) begins with quiet piano playing. The vocals on this track are a bit more dreamy, but they sound better, a bit softer in a way. After the vocals, the bass is allowed on the fore for a while, but shortly first keys and then a very quick guitar solo takes over (the bass does go on though). A very good ending.

The last piece reiterates in a moody way the first track.

Conclusion

Musically this is much in the Italian seventies style, and as such very melodic and lyrical. A very nice album with some very good music on it, but lacking in production and also in the vocals (although with better production, this might not be that much of a problem). Most tracks are good, although Immagini is a little uneventful, but the bow can't always be bent. On the whole a nice album, but the sound of the album is a little too amateuristic, but lots of promise for the future.
