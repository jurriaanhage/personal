Summary

People familiar with Univers Zero (or with Present) will know that there quite a few people in Present, who used to play in the "cult-band" Univers Zero that gave us some good music some time ago. Present also has been at it for quite some time now and this is their... third? studio album and they have one live album. These other studio albums are already quite old, 1980 and 1985.

The music

The album is quite short, 36 minutes and contains four tracks. This length should be no problem, since if the band plays like they should, this should be enough to listen in one go. The longest track Delusions opens the album. It surprises me that the vocals are in English, especially since the title of one of their previous efforts, Le Poison Qui Rend Fou, was in French. The vocals of whoever sings the beginning of the first slightly Arabic styled track Delusions are very low and remind me of the throaty voice of a Dutch beat band, The Shoes. For the rest it is quite hard to describe the music: seemingly every instrument plays a small part that it repeats at given times. Of course, there's more variation than this, especially for the Crimsonesque guitars, but in a sense this is what comes to mind quickest. Another parallel with KC is the fact that the band knows wonderfully well to build up tension by repeating/ascending patterns. The sound of Present is a more avant-garde (Etron Fou Leloublan) I think. As such a band like Kong, in their earlier days, seems the missing link between these bands. The drumming is very varied, with lots of phrases alternating and alternating. The mood evoked by this first track is dark, as one might expect given the last few references, and involves also quite a bit of jazz, at least that is how I feel the influence of the piano in this piece. The same high level of composition is present in May Day: the first part is sung quite hastily, and the horror movish, brooding ascending part is really great, with a strange repetitive beep in there that one notices only later. The melody sounds familiar, but this might also be from a previous listen. At four minutes the pace quickens and we get the "chorus". Then the pace slows down sickeningly and the drawn out drawled second verse is "sung". Then the soundtrack like part returns again. Underneath this all, Daniel Denis, plays forcefully and as always variedly and the bass cries softly. After the chorus the song ends. The Sense Of Life opens quite heavily with modern sounding guitars. The vocals are slightly psychedelic and this song seems to have less structure to it. The nice thing about is that especially with respect to the drumming there's a sense that something might erupt. This does not really happen at first however. We do get another jazzy part on the piano and here the Magma reference comes in. Funny, how it seems when everything "gets out of hand" that the drummer bands ever aggressively to keep the others down. The more psychedelic music of the beginning returns now. Closer Ex-Tango opens cosmically and is a rather strange ditty with some Arabic styled, playful guitarwork and a piano going against the grain. Dancing to it will certainly be a problem.

The lyrics are rather vague and philosophical. The music is the most important aspect of this band, naturally.

Conclusion

A great, short album in the style of late King Crimson, Art Zoyd, Univers Zero, Etron Fou Leloublan and subsequent projects as released on Musea lately and of course, the standard Magma reference, although to my feeling this is more in mood and atmosphere than actual music (Magma is hysterical (Blasquiz), jazzy and operatic). Good melodies, good tension building, good compositions, lots of changing signatures. Although not for the timid, I must admit that there's a lot less freaking than on many other avant-garde albums and if you like the heavier/darker songs of Thrak, you could also give this a try.
