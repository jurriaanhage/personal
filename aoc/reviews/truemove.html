Summary

I received a CD rom copy of this CD from Slava, the CD is not generally available yet. It seems they wouldn't to hear from companies that might want to release it. Slava already told me that this was not straightforward prog, but pop with proggish arrangements. See if that bears out.

The music

After a short introduction with Russian vocals (as on the rest of the album) and heavy on the keyboards we come to the title track. This is a poppy track with a progressive sounding intermezzo with dual guitars and the song ends on a introspective note. City Of Flowers is also a frolic track with an up-tempo keyobard solo in the middle. The sound of the keys is a bit dated, but the song contains quite a lot of variation. Fairytale is a slower and more introspective track with oboe sounds, a string arrangment (synthetic of course) and now for the first time it strikes me that the drums are also synthetic. I always prefer a real drummer. Goodbye? opens with oriental music and again synthetic wind instruments. The melody of this track is moody and quite nice as is the vocal interpretation. The Waltz Of Hopelessness is not surprisingly quite waltz like with a nice intro and a good melody. Opening with clavecimbel, Pilgrim is a medieval sounding track, and as oftener on this album there's a slight, but pervasive folk influence. Oops... is an almost danceable track while Land Beyond The Clouds enjoys a large guitar presence. The music can be likened to a less bombastic Collage (as on Safe) in a short song format. Everlasting Story is a dreamy track with again a folky influence and nice melodies. A flowing track. Look Around is somewhat funky with treated vocals and reminds me of Morten Harkets solo CD. The "aa-e-yaaa.." parts could have been left out. This song sounds quite familiar though so maybe they are repeating themselves a bit. The closer I Sing The Song is the longest track on the album. It sounds very friendly and again the semi-link with Harket can be made. The bombastic intermezzo is quite nice and the playfulness reminds me a little of Collage.

Conclusion

This is indeed pop music with progressive touches and various folk music influences. Often the music can be quite complex with lots of rhythmic things going on, but more often the music is not that complicated. The voice of Andrew Art is a bit laid back and rather low and maybe a little too polished. In my opinion the syntheticness of many of the instruments is a drawback (I admit they might not have the means to use real drummers and flutists). Compared to for instance the Polish bands that sing in Polish it strikes me that in real progressive rock it is easier to cope with such lyrics than in pop music. Progressive music is maybe a bit more lyrical and musically there is more happening. The music on this album is often quite uplifting, but can be moody as well. The melodies are quite nice and the keyboards are used often and for various purposes given the music a more progressive touch. But I wonder if this music is interesting enough for lovers of progressive rock in that it might be too much pop and too little rock. Maybe it's best to compare Andrew Art to a band like A-ha or even more Morten Harket solo: thoughtful song directed music with lots of keyboards and not so much guitar. Andrew Art however introduces quite a diversity of (folk) influences, is more progressive in that the music changes oftener of character, but is also more synthetic and because of the language used harder to get into. I also admit liking the songs of A-Ha and Harket being stronger compositions.
