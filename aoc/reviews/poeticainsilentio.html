Summary

This is the debut of this Dutch avant-garde rock band.

The music

Opening with prominent bass the sound is similar in a way to King Crimson. The vocal part is slow and depressing, with a willowy, quite happy sounding chorus. The singing is more driven it seems on this track than on the mini album by this band, Who Rolls The Dice. The impression I get however is that the music is more an attempt at Art, than at music, if you know what I mean. If you don't well, at times I am thinking that this music might have been used as part of a play. However the music is and will stay avant-garde rock. In the middle the pace goes up a little with a meandering guitar solo after which we end with some breaks. The next piece sees the advent of the violin in a dark and somber piece and lowly recited vocals. Then the singer starts to put more bite into his vocals. I'm reminded a bit of Hammill here at times. The music is for the most part rather subdued, but there are occasional loud outburst in which the pace is upped. Sailor is a short and playful piece, jumpy and quirky. A kind of fast, folky sailors' song. The Bee is another light track. Silly lyrics in the style of Supersister and the music has something of the Discipline version of King Crimson. Then the psyche guitar sets in and the for a moment the song is quite different. Then we return again to the playful part. Morning Mist opens soothingly with subdued singing and then we come to an off-beat part. The vocals here seem a bit "acted" if you know what I mean, as if the singer does his best a bit too much to sing eloquently. I miss a naturalness here. A sharp guitar solo rounds it all off. The follows the instrumental While You Were Looking with good tension building and some quite wild guitar solo's in psychedelic style. Plenty of variation here in this driven piece that ends rather abruptly. Two Gnomes is a quirky piece with a memorable vocal melody. Playfully played with a prominent role for the "Inspector Gadget" like bass lines. The violin also gets another shot, but as accompaniment only. The drumming is quite varied, but naturally so. Camouflage is with its almost 9 minutes the longest track on the album. The song starts tense with shards of guitar. It then incorporates many of the now known ingredients, including this time a theme that will remind many of the desert. The chorus is rather striking, but I'm not fond of it. The instrumental part at the end of the track following it, I do like. Acoustic guitar opens on L'Organe d'Haine. A very melodic piece and very good too. A bit of troubadour and folk in here as well. Actually the singer has a bit the tendency to vocalize as a troubadour does. De Dood is a weird one for most of us. For non-Dutch speakers, because the lyrics are in Dutch, for Dutch readers, because of the lyrics themselves. Musically the song seems subservient to the lyrics. Some awkward drumming and at times wild guitar playing, between the monotonous verses. Then the churchbells ring until we come to track 18. This is Stop Laughing (at leats it is partly so). A jig like piece the first part of which I don't really like. But then the serious guitar playing comes in and that's quite okay. Then we have to wait a little for flute/acoustic guitar piece starts. This is quite a good bonus piece, a bit folky essentially with a few rough edges. I think it's called The Promised Lie.

Conclusion

A nice debut album, but musically I think I prefer the mini album. This album really has some good tracks, but it seems the focus is more on the story line and that distracts from the music. It's a bit too thought-through one might say. As a consequence references to known progbands are more difficult to find so one might say this album is more "original". It also strikes me that this album seems less of a whole than the mini. Maybe too many different types of things on this album, I'm not sure.
