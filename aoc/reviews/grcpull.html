Summary

An old band, Grace, though they only made one album in 1992 (The poet, the piper and the fool). The band started in the late seven- ties but disbanded after releasing one single (Fire of London).

The music

Looking for references we find that Grace is in the folky corner of symphonic rock (where we also find Jethro Tull (old and good) and Red Jasper (new and good) and also might have some Pallas thrown in (mostly because of the tempo of some the songs). Grace doesn't live up to these bands, though I must admit that this CD is a step forward compared to their previous one. First of all I like the vocals somewhat better (though it's not really special even now). The last album was kind of a bore in my opinion.

What about this album then? The Fool starts up-tempo with keyboards after which the singer starts and the guitar plays the melody. The refrain of the song starts with 'Over the rainbow but under the hill' and reminds me of 'Over the hills and far away' by Gary Moore (I just can't help it, I wish I could). The voice is kind of weak in this song. If it wasn't for the folky keyboard intermezzo, the flute thrown in and the overall folky gladness in the song I'd say this was an up-tempo rocker. The fast part has some Jethro Tullness and also reminds me off Flairck at their speediest.

The Mullions are a very strange breed (also to be found on the first CD). They lust for human beings. The song is like a song sung by some old grey person in the Scotch highlands (Fish in 30 ye- ars?); it's very sober and tells a story about the Mullions, how they hunt and eat. The music is based on acoustic guitar the first four minutes after which the flute starts and the tempo is changed and the drums, kayboards and E-guitar takes over and an occaisonal flute solo is thrown in. The voice sounds somewhat better in this song though. Still, I think that the Mullions are called Mullions only for the last sentence: 'There's millions and millions of Mullions in the lake'.

Lean on me starts very soft and easy, a bit percussive like Nomzamo and the sung is song very softly, with the flute piping the tune. The song reminds me of some song by Peter Gabriel..wait a minu- te...maybe 'In your eyes' from 'So' at a slower pace, I'm not sure, but I guess you can guess the atmosphere a bit now: moody. I wonder how this songs sounds when Gabriel sings it. At the end a slow E-guitar takes over. Lyrically it's the same as Mike and the Mechanics' 'The Living Years'.

The fourth song starts a lot heavier and stays that way until the intermezzo, played by keyboards (still up-tempo). After that the singer gets a bit agitated ('I want it now, I want it now') and Mark Kelly also plays along (well, in spirit anyway).

Hanging Rock Part One starts folky (in the Fishy way) maybe a bit like 'Lucky' and is quite jolly. I kind of like this song. It is well composed and I'm not bothered at all by the fact that the song doesn't change a lot melodically (though it becomes a bit tougher at the end when more instruments join in). At the end the first part of the song comes back a bit.

Architect of War can be understood as an anti-war song. It starts out bombastic and the song is kind of loud overall. After three minutes we enter a groovy part and the singer speaks his lines (insteads of singing them) and the whole song changes. I must say I do find it refreshing. A lot better than those nonsense stories about Mullions (or whatever scary they stand for in the real world, tax collectors, Tories, I don't know). Two minutes later we continue at where the original left off. (backing vocals have been added in this song, to give the singer more power). The song ends with marching across the battlefield (hear the drums?). The song kind of ends abrupt.

Every Clown is definitely up-tempo, yeah it's party-time ('Internal Exile') and the lyrics are very encouraging: 'Christ you're just a zero' (I wonder maybe he just means that Christ is a zero himself and that Christ is not meant as a curse at all?). Not really a lot to say about this song, but it's not bad, though you should like Pallas neo-prog I guess.

The last song of today is: Gift. It starts off with soft percussion and some keyboard melodies. The song is sung in low voice (like Lean on me). The bass becomes very noticable after some time and the sounds becomes a bit more edgy.

Wait, the Mullions have returned. The 'song' starts off with a sampled playing vinyl pressing of some sort (crackedycrack) and then we hear very joyful children and the melody is very glad. The song is just as misplaced as Midnight Cowboy on Faith no More's Angel Dust. After a while people even start singing 'La-la-la-la- la-la' and it goes on and on.

Conclusion

The problem with these 'detailed' descriptions is that you concen- trate on technicalities and forget whether you like it or not. Is this album any good. Well, the album doesn't fire me on (maybe Archictects a bit) but I must say that they have done a decent job this time and I have some hope that later on they might even develop their own face. Maybe the best way to describe this music is: I like it, but that's just it.
