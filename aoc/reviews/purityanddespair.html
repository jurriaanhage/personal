Summary

McCabe is the man behind Elegant Simplicity and Proximity. He send me this to review.

The music

Featuring four long and one semi-long instrumental tracks, I'll do what I usually do: go over the tracks one by one: When Darkness Falls is the almost 14 minute opener. After a melodic opening with piano and synth, slightly classical the music becomes quite poppy: a melodic part with keyboards and guitar playing alternated with a more jazzy bouncy part with some flute and mellotron added. The drumming is rather lame especially during the guitar/keyboard part and I'm also not fond of the rather simple melody as played on the keyboard. After a passage containing mostly bass and the returns of the melodic keyboard/guitar part, we get an underwater scene with a nice melodic on the flute. The next melody is also quite catchy and is linked somewhat with the melodic part. Again there's a certain thinness to the keyboards, but maybe this is on purpose. The alternation with some more heavier drums is what the track needs. What it doesn't need is the organ sound of the next up-tempo part. Having described all these passages in turn and indicating where the one passage pops up and returns, it remains to be seen whether this was a good track. Unfortunately I feel this is not the case. Some of the parts are quite nice: the underwater scene, the shrill, slightly Chinese part just after halfway is also quite nice, but on the whole constructing a song by just concatenating a number of passage is in my opinion not the way to go about it. Also I don't see quite well the link between the title of this track and the music. The music varies from moody to structureless to heavy to poppy/jolly and as such one encounters a lot in this track. The just over 14 minutes long Quantum Of Solace opens slowly with piano and organ (again that organ that reminds me well of old men playing organ, not the aggressive Hammond). The theme reminds me quite a lot of Camel's Harbour of Tears as does the guitar sound, although the addition of rhythm guitar is thorougly their own. Again, the song is quite poppy with catchy melodies, but also there's some hammond organ here. The picture for this song yields a church in some place where the sun always shines. I would expect some more stately music then. The same problem here as with the previous track: not only is the music rather poppy (in itself not that much of a problem, but it also sounds well kind of frivolous), but it also seems rather simple and the main problem is that the music consists of a lot of passage strung together. The main theme of The Quantum of Solace returns in Tranquility And Drift, which is just short of twenty minutes. A good melody it is and the music gets a little more powerful here once in a while. The guitar sound can be quite noisy, but the drumming is again a bit lame and in the beginning at least the songs plods along a bit. The next melody is also quite nice by the way, but the alternation between the two seems uncalled for and this rather memorable melody is made more memorable by being repeated very often. The (tranquil) Spanish guitar in this song is new. The soft synthy part reminds me of Kitaro and nicely illustrates Tranquility. The drumming is electronic here. The last part is also very..tranquil.. reminding me of Gandalf's beautiful More Than Just A Seagull. Aching Desire is a more classically styled track with some nice melodies, a sensitive piano and stringlike passages. In the middle there's quite an active guitarsolo with in fact quite an appealling solo. The drumming is still not very groovy, but this is certainly the best track so far. Some of the melodies on this album are encountered again the comparatively short title track. The music is rather up-tempo here with stringlike synths and some moody organ in the back, while the guitar rocks quite heavily. Quite an energetic ending to this album.

The artwork is computer made and it shows. The next time a higher resolution will yield far better results. Aside this technicality it is quite nice.

Conclusion

As it often happens: quite some nice melodies in the pen, not exactly derivative of anything (maybe a little Camel there), but the compositions plainly fall short to captivate. Also some of the sounds are not chosen well and sound a bit amateuristic. This may be subjective and maybe I'm just a fan of heavy keyboardsounds and not much of groovy organ. Also the music is often quite simple. I recommend the band to shorten the songs and concentrate on the tightness of the songs.
