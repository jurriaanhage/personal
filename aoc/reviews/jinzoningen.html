Summary

This is a rerelease of the album under the same name.

The music

Round House produces an instrumental guitar based jazzrock-close-to-progressive. Opener Jin.zo-ni.n.gen is close to quite a bit of King Crimson material, musically. Especially the frequently used double guitars do well at implementing this approach. Rhyhtmically, though, the band does not succeed in coming close to KC: rhythms tend to be flat and uneventful. This may not nullify the melodic candor, but does have negative influences on the apparent quality of the music. Although the ghost of KC is present more often, a lot of the tracks have some lighter sections. For instance, track as Tour Of The Deep Ocean sometimes hints at Focus, including the hackneyed and somewhat sedate feel. It's rather interesting to hear how much life the band can pump into such a long track. I have to say I feared for those long jazzrocky tracks, but they do remarkably well. The sound quality is a bit dull though. Out Of 3-dimension seems to overstay its welcome just a tad: after a tranquil almost esotheric start the more melodic and jazzrocklike main section moves into what at times is just a bit on the freaky side. Even though Suisha Goya No Asa has drums sections that are varied enough, the flat sound of the drums combined with the at times uninteresting and straight chopping do the track damage. This augmented by some freaky and otherwise lengthy guitar sections makes for a more regular and less interesting track. The tracks does contain a section that can also be found in a Renaissance track (possibly Can You Understand), but they might have knicked it too. Romantic Rally has some strong moments,

Conclusion

Whereas Japan only has a small basis in progressive music, there is a rather strong one in jazzrock music. Having said that, a lot of the bands moving in that vein go off into lengthy meandering or freaky sections too often. Not so Round House, or rather, they manage to avert the tendency on most tracks, only tampering off at times in Suisha Goya No Asa. They often strike a balance between technique and melody, doing well in especially the guitar melodies. The drum sound tends to be flat, but not irritatingly so. One of the better albums, musically, in the jazzrock genre. Pity about the flat sound.
