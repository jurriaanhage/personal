Line up

The music

Some people seem to live in the studio. Take Steve Smith, or as is the case on this album, take Dennis Chambers: this guy is present on so many Tone Center records, does he have time to live? Most Tone Center releases are not centered on tones as such but on a trio of musicians, all quite well-known and often being musician-musicians. The kind of musicians other musicians look to. Although these guys are good players, the music that comes out of this kind of releases often has either a staleness or an artificiality that makes the music hard to really appreciate.

On the first few tracks, CAB does succeed in constructing some worthy tunes, though: opener Night Splash in a Latin vein, jubilant almost with a low bass sound, some fast moody bass solo's, keyboardsolo's, while the guitar is foremost in the melody. The drumming is strong, as usual with Chambers, who knows how to set a good drive. The title track has a heavy groove, but gets to be more melodic later on. In this way we alternate between the heavy groove parts and softer moodier parts where the bass dominates. We find some flamenco on So There Is Love. The grooves on guitar are hard again, notwithstanding the music is quite dancably, yet rhythmically off-beat.

Starting Just Perfect, the band starts to meander too much in my opinion. The same holds for the straightforward One For Stern. The Watcher is better with its playfulness and American sound. Rhythmically this sound quite complex. The slowdown into the moody part is quite strong. Mellowness pervades on the club jazz of Atamanashi, although the opening is quite funky. The tuneful Boogie Me has a melody I do not like, while the merry Elastic Man is in Latin style, like the opener. However, the composition easily wears thin here.

The closer Bernard ups the ante for a last spurt. This is pure jazz rock, with some more understated and atmospheric aspects as well.

Conclusion

Yet another trio jazzrock album in the typical American Tone Center style, but with some nice soloing and variation in that soloing as well. After a few songs, however, the level of the compositions seems to go down (or maybe I simply got bored).
