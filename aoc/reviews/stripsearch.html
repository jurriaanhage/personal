Summary

Stripsearch is a project by drummer Freese (Perfect Circle, Vandals, Devo), joined by hip hop bass man Elizondo (Dre, Eminem, Xzibit, Mary J Blige), alt guitar player Ward (Wallflowers, School of Fish) and his saxist brother Jason (Vandals, Los Zalzones, NOFX).

The music

The opener focusses on the Assassin bit, rather than the Baby Face, judging from the experimentalism, led on by the sax, that washes over us, affecting all instruments into a turmoil one might find it hard to raise from. Luckily the gents just manage to keep the track from sounding harsh, shrill or abrasive, but that's about as much as can be said on the subject. There is no real reason to presume the presence of melody (although slight tendencies might sometimes slip in) somewhere within this abundance of experiment. Hardly without stopping we move on to El Terrible (what do you expect with titles like these). The sax has piped down, and the bass has taken dominance in this short track.

Sharp as The Razor, the sax returns the next track, soon enough to lie down and see the introduction of Frisell like guitar playing, later joining very subdued. This track is amazingly mellow, considering its predecessors and title. Orange Fragile is almost as fragile as its title would suggest, pretty atmospheric and esotheric, close to Terje Rypdal, fitting with ECM. And as we have let our guard down, we are hit full in the face with the Hendryx-type-feedback kind of guitar which The Real Deal is. Toomp! Hands Of Stone still has a bit of those Hendryx guitars, but not as badly overpowering anymore, giving room to drums and bass, so that we still end up with a full spectrum. The guitar at moments seems to just try and do some freakish jazzy bits, but remains far enough clear by the smallest of margins.

And then with The Mongoose the dreaded finally happens: freaky, aimless jazzy stuff. The guys just sticking enough together to not sound dissonant, but any melodic tendencies are amply killed off. Goody. Having had that, we (well, they, that is) created room for some tranquility. The Motor City Cobra is rather innocent, really, with some gentle saxed, brushed drums, and laid back guitar. More atmosphere than anything else. As the Baby Faced Assassin Returns we get another bunch of experimentalism, this time mainly guitar oriented, though.

Conclusion

When looking at the players this album looks like another of those powertrios (never mind the fact that they have a very present guest sax player, as far as you can have guests with what might be a one off). And there is that bit of powerplaying every once in a while. Most of the time though they experiment, without going over the edge (the far edge, that is) or create a nice atmosphere. Aside from the jazz squeezer The Moongoose, I find this album overtakes most of the albums made in this vein. Possibly the fact that this is an album instigated by a drummer keeps it from going over the top in any specific category. Good one.
