Summary

The music

Don't you just hate it when a band go and name themselves something that sounds like a guy? Turns out this bloke actually is an eight piece symphonic rock band from Colombia. Most of the time I find references in the press release either off the mark or a case of wishful thinking. This time around I agreed with it: the lush style of fellow South American symphonic rock band Sagrado echoes through this band's material, even though there's no violin present. The album was released in 2003 originally, and now has a well deserved re-release with Musea.

Despite the fact that the band sport two vocalists, the music has a great many long instrumental sections. The combination of the modern instruments, synths at the fore, with traditional latin instruments such as flute and acoustic guitar create the lush style. The female vocalist (I presume this is Beatriz Jatamillo, but pardon my Spanish if I'm wrong) mainly chants, even though she does do some worded vocals. The greatest spark is brought in by the electric guitar, which at times really fires things up. The drums play a pleasant role too, abstaining from being the mere placeholder for the tempo by bringing in some nice fills, without overstepping the approriate modesty.

Despite the fact that there are two vocalists, the band's vulnerable side lies with the vocals, which at times sound just a little too shaky to live up to the expectations created. Still, the vocalists perform well enough most of the time.

On the closing track Brujo Negro steps outside the symphonic rock idiom and brings in some jazz jammy influences. Not the best way in which to finish an album, if only for the difference of style. Best stuff on the album is the three track Duerme Bien song, which remarkably starts out with a Hackett type fast riff.

Conclusion

I'm not a lover of the South American symphonic rock style. Sagrado was pretty much the only band so far in this vein I liked, until Jaen Kief came along. The combination of gusto, compositional strength and craftmanship make for an end result that has to be among the top in its field. A must have for those into South American, and a good consideration for those who aren't.
