Summary

From the bio: after their previous albums The Beach (1999) and The Riddle (1997), which featured complex multi-instrumental arrangements with a progressive feel to them, Blue Flux wanted Sugarbeat to be different. All synths and samplers were confined to their boxes, and only stringed instuments (plus a little percussion) were used for the making of the entire album. Some progrock elements are in evidence, but not as plentiful as could be heard on the previous two albums.

I am not familiar as yet with their previous two albums, so I can not comment on the differences with the album currently under review. The instruments listed for the players are approximate. I could find a listing for this specific album.

The music

5 By 5 opens the album playfully, bouncily and acoustically and in fact sets the tone for the album. The music is not proggy, but it is often quite intricate and in this case acoustic. Electric guitar is featured on Breathless where the guitar owes a bit to the vibrant Oldfield sound. When I listen to the music itself however I am thinking more of a cross between Steve Howe and Ant Phillips. Again the music sounds simple, but if you listen close quite a bit is happening. Later it exhibits quite a bit of swing. After the easy-going Heatwave, we come to the title track which has a more ethereal sound and is a rather strange track compared to the others. The music is again layered and with many melodies intertwining, rings out clearly and tunefully.

Fom the short Resolution we run into Maurice Ravel's Valley Of The Bells which has a bit of dissonance. I was tempted to think of early Hackett here. The music proceeds carefully, as if on foot. Descent is a somber piece with plenty of bass. Kind of mysterious New Age. After the bluesy Drive-By Dessert we come to the rather heavy Bed Of Nails which nonetheless continues to a rather slow track. The guitar is sharp though.

Stay Frosty is in Western style, A Dozen Reasons strumming and jumpy. Tendermouth then is not only romantic, but also fresh and breathes just the right atmosphere. The closer is the traditional Scarborough Fair, well known to many of you because of the Simon and Garfinkel and also the only vocal track on this album.

Conclusion

I would have liked to be able to compare the music of this "band" to their older, more progressive work. I like what I heard, but have to say that the music is admittedly rather acoustic and not prog at all. The music is and compositionally and melodically strong. If you happen to like Anthony Phillips, you might like this as well. Some references to Hackett, Howe and Oldfield are also present. On the whole, a nice listening experience, but one that is both spacy and airy and does not really stick in your mind.
