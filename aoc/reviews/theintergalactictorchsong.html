Summary

The music

The combination of the artwork and band name for this one very clearly point in the direction of psychedelic music. And sure, the guitars, and the overall musical feel are pretty much psychedelic. However, the semi spoken vocals of Myshel Prasad, vaguely reminiscent of Laurie Anderson, on Mars radiate an urgency that belies any psychedelicity(nessdom). A rather convincing opening. On the second track this urgency is passed on onto the guitarist, making him sound like he would have fit in with Rage Against The Machine. This shift from psychedelic to alternative is continued onto Ourobros Omphalos, which is more of an alt track with a serious jangly guitar line, something you would expect from a guitar band like Loop. Absinthe is a rather dreamy track, which in atmosphere seems to refer to Cocteau Twins, although the fact that Prasad uses words diminishes that feel. In Ribbons continues the lingering feel, although it along the way changes into another jangly guitar track. Original Sin is more a song, but due to the flat vocals never breaks through. The end once again has the lingering guitar, as is the main theme of Disolution Of The Order Of The Star. Prasad's singing has a slightly eastern feel in its use in Ice Age, with a bit of an alty lick to the guitar whine. It's no longer lingering but does have a break that is spacy in guitar and distant in vocalisations. The drumming is on the slow deliberate. RS2HB has a little more vocal melody and the same melancholy as Absinthe. Utopia is a bit stronger with more guitar and less jingle. This extra strength enhances the overall feel. Pelican is a bit whiny initially, partly caused by Prasad sounding like Cranberries singer O'Riordan.

Conclusion

Despite the first impression of the artwork and name of psychedelica, I'd find this band leans more to the lingering guitar direction on a number of tracks. Granted, this does have its psychedelic sides, but at the end of the day it's just not that. Then there's a couple of melancholic tracks. Most stuff stands up by itself pretty well, and the somewhat flat vocals fits quite well most of the time. Space Team Electra come up with an album that's decent competition for the Loop's of this world.
