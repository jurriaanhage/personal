Summary

Tale Music Group is the continuation of the Tale that years ago release Riverman Vol. 1. Interesting to note that this album won the South African award for best recorded album. I heard some very good things about this album and I did rather like Riverman Vol. 1, although the references to Roger Waters were maybe a little too obvious.

The music

Joe Boliero lies in his hospital bed, dying. Then he starts to fantasize about a paradise. The first part of Rape Of The Catacomb is a majestic overture to the album. With every song it is described what happens to Joe in his other world, so it is easy to follow the story. The start of the song is very somber with slaves singing in the catacombs. Musically I am reminded of the Alan Parsons Project in his earlier days, the music also being somewhat musical-like. A very good track with a terrific guitar solo at the end and vyer nice pianic additions. Holy Man is a thoughtful piece with double vocals of Stodart and Granville, that becomes a little too easy and mellow for my tastes twoards the end. Compared to the previous album the reference to Waters is lost. Spirit Train is another example of the musical style chosen here: the music is quite accessible and although there is some variation, the music is certainly not complex, though well arranged and recorded. So the music sounds very professional with good singers, but for me the music could have been a little more adventurous and less slick. During Baron House, like during the second part of Rape Of The Catacomb an orchestra is used and becausde of the nice atmospheres evoked, this is also the second best track yet. After this we come to the Lost Concerto, a pice played by Stodart on piano. It recaptures some of the majesty of the overture. Memorial Hall also features the orchestra, but it is quite a bouncy track with lots of lyrics pressed into them. The devil comes up in the next track, aptly titled Beelzebub. The vocals are rather rap-like on the side of the devil, while Joe sings in between. Card Game is very different again, with a slight blues feeling because of the Hammond and a good vocal melody. A nostalgic sounding track thta works up to a climax. As more often on this album the female backing vocals lend a certainly polishedness to the music. Life Of Ryelly is another bouncy track, that like parts of the previous track remind me a little of Randy Newman, probably because of the vocals. The music has something of lounge jazz and the last lyrics remind me in a strange way of Jackson's The Girl Is Mine. Rose Garden is a summery ballad (and I'm looking at the rain pouring down while I write this) sung by Karine Jerg in a lovely, clear voice. The closer is the title track which is a resolution as Joe ascends into Elysium Fields. An orchestral track with good harmony vocals, a pianic interlude with prominent bass, and good tension build up ands as might be expected a great majestic ending to this album.

Conclusion

The concept of the album is quite nice with good lyrics, but it seems that on this album the lyrics and storyline are a little more important than the music, which is quite accessible and not always captivating. There are some good tracks on this album, most notably those with the orchestra, but for the rest it is a little too musical-like for my tastes. Still a professional sounding album that can be compared to the more accessible bands in "progressive", Alan Parsons Project (including the classical side of this band) and Supertramp. Not that I really hear direct references, but people with a liking for these bands or who are into musicals, could go and take a listen. An album that sounds quite okay, but that also tends to lack something in rough edges. Hopefully they will be around next time.
