Summary

I remember well the first time I saw these guys in my neighborhood. They were a heavy bunch, the three of them (with Roland Kok instead of Orlando van Swaay). I liked them a lot although having a singer behind a drumkit doesn't do a lot to improve performance. Still, the music was good and so I bought their demo, which I also liked a lot. Their first album was a bit of a disappointment, because the best songs on it were also on the demo. Still, it went up and up for this band having good reviews and supporting Saga. After that Kok decided to quit and van Swaay from the band Light skipped from that band to this one. I saw them once in this new line-up and they tried to overcome the problem of having a singer behind a drumkit, by moving him up front while playing songs that needn't any drums. I wasn't too impressed.

The music

I will not go over it song by song but just try to give a general impression. What concerns you most I think is the progressive content of this album and I have to admit that it's not very high. The album is neo to say the least and borders on ordinary rock. The structures are very much song like with refrains, bridges and intros (not necessarily in that order :)), although the arrangements are still a bit in the progressive line. The singing is like always, acceptable, but the drumming seems to have lost a bit. The albums consists mainly of energetic songs, with few sensitive ballads to make your girlfriend like the band as well.

Still, to make a CD justified has of course nothing to do with the progressiveness of the release. The thing is: is it any good. Well, if you look at its merits I think it's still quite good, because the melodies (however they may sound alike) are good and give rise to singalong reflexes. Still, they are not songs that keep with you easily, like Everons songs, that you can sing along almost at the second listening (hopefully they will not improve on this :)), but the songs are okay.

Most songs are still evidently Wings of Steel, thanks to the easily recognizable vocals of their drummer. The first track is rather straight, but has the advance of a darling little organ playing along, nostalgic almost. If I'd Say almost sounds like Nirvana unplugged in New York. Heaven is a ballad, but a bit too sweet for my tastes (Extreme ballad). The ballad Still Together however sounds very much like For Absent Friends. The King's Blues is quite up-tempo and well...happy! While, It's So Cold is more in the line of Homesick and quite good as well, with a good guitar solo. The last song is the longest, and might be called the most 'progressive' of the lot, but the difference isn't that noticable. It's a good track though.

There is progression on this album, but it's not in the music. It is in the production and in the arrangements of the songs. The overal sound is more professional and I guess they will be as good live as they always were. Sadly the music interests me less now than it used to (their demo). It has become too well,

Conclusion

More commercial than in the ol' days, when they were very Rush like. Their own sound? Hmm maybe, but also more in the direction of For Absent Friends, which is not an improvement, I know, in the eyes of most progressive rock lovers. On the other hand there are a lot of people too, that get tired of AOR hoping for something a bit more involved, but not too pretentious. This might be it: varied, well played and sung and energetic. I think it's best to give track 1, 6, 7 and 9 your attention to get an idea of what this band is up to.
