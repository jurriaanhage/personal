Summary

Having had no Cyclops discs to review since CYCL 064 (except The Time Capsule which I got from the band itself) it is good to finally have some new things from this label. In fact it turns out I missed out on the previous disc of Lynne which is a precursor to this one in the so called Safar trilogy. This is Bjorns seventh album to date.

The music

Opening the album is the Proglogue To Dream Of Wolves. Typical film music, quite bombastic and dramatic with lots of things going on at the same time. The atmosphere evoked has something of Arabia and the desert. Demon Moon is a more restive piece with lush synths and acoustic guitar. A gently flowing piece. In style, Lynne may remind people of Gandalf, although Gandalf is maybe a bit more elaborate and less rock. People like Jeremy and Fonya are also comparable, but in some way I think Lynne is more of the melodic, electronic type. Shapechanger is a peice in which the menace of the wolves is depicted (or should I say desounded?). Again, some great melodies here especially the one on the flute in the opening. Quite some extensive guitar playing of the slightly distorted kind. In Leiria - Lips Or Sword the guitar rages again. The Ghost Warriors Of Caluz again has that Arabic feel, maybe even something of Ravels bolero. It is quite rhythmic with its military drums. The Spirit Rider is a psychedelic track with some wah-wah like sounds. It is quite funky in places with some nice bass playing and on the whole goes into the Cybotron direction. Palimak's Revenge continues the album with a more bombastic approach with some hard edged guitar in there as well. Again, the melody is very strong and after a short quiet intermezzo the song comes back with a great melodic passage on guitar. In Kyrania the acoustic guitar is the prominent instrument giving a breather after the powerful previous track. Queen Hantilla is ponderous piece, slow moving. The Beckoning Sea is the longest track with slightly over ten minutes. In this track we encounter the many faces of the album once again. The closer is the somewhat sad Epilogue - Safar's Song.

The booklet does contain texts by Allan Cole, but the lyrics are not part of the music. You can just follow the story as it progresses. In a sense one might say the music is a soundtrack to the story.

A reviewer pointed Amin Bhatia out for comparison with this work and although the sound of Bhatia (on Interstellar suite) is a bit colder and uses more effects, I think he does have a point, since both can be understood as soundtracks for 'something' and in the bombastic parts there are certainly some parallels to be drawn.

Conclusion

I did already like Lynne's The Void. This is something else altogether, but it is also something better: the melodies are good throughout and the music ranges from thoughtful all the way up to bombastic. The sound is good, the programmed drums "palatable", and overall this album is much more a whole than The Void in which Lynne still experimented with a number of styles. Fans of the style of Gandalf, Jeremy, Fonya and the like are invited to go and have a listen. The music has overall a somewhat mellow/melodic sound, but there are plenty of rough edges left.
