Summary

Memory & Imagination is an album that collects material that was previously released in some form. It contains material recorded as early as 1981, but most was recorded in the late eighties and early nineties. All of the material on disc two except for the final two cuts has never before been released, and the two cuts at the end only on a cassette by a small Canadian label.

The music

Cleveland is an instrumentalist. He mainly uses keyboards and other electronic support, but he also uses guitars (later Barry Cleveland wrote that almost all music is guitar, especially on disc two which has only percussion besides and a bit of lyricon. In his opinion that is the most striking of the whole album: it is all guitar). His style is somewhat remeniscent of Gandalf, although he uses far less guitars. Most tracks have an open clear sound and a somewhat lazy feel with a friendly approach, almost to the point of being hypnotic. The use of rhythm (albeit electronic (later Barry Cleveland wrote that except for some samples on Hawk Dreaming all percussion is real)) clearly prevents the music from moving towards the new age realm, which is only emphasized by the sections that have no noticeable rhythm. It definitely does blend in with the background nicely. There is a Steve Roach feel as well, but Cleveland doesn't involve one as much as Roach does. There are some minor exceptions to this rule. The start of Abrasax is slightly Frippy and Mythos has a tenor clarinet sort of sound in it that is less dreamy. Echoes On Echoes is a Fripp guitar loop track, as heard in the late seventies and early eighties (the track was recorded in 2003 for the Echoes radio program).

Conclusion

This collection displays Cleveland music from different eras. Due to the fact that the tracks aren't presented in chronological order, the album more or less hides the fact that he started out with some Fripp influenced stuff (this is the older material), and then moved to more atmospheric stuff. Cleveland's music never pushes to the fore, it remains friendly, lulling almost. Do not look for excitement or experiment in it. If you like the gently floating electronic stuff as made by some , this would be a good addition to it, since Cleveland never bores (well, hardly ever).
