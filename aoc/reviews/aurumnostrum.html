Sinkadus - Aurum Nostrum

Artist: Sinkadus
Title: Aurum Nostrum
Label: Cyclops, CYCL 048
Length(s): 58 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1997
Month of review: 03/1997

Line up

Rickard Bistrom - bass, vocals, guitar
Fredrik Karlsson - keyboards
Mats Svensson - drums
Lena Petterson - cello
Robert Sjoback - guitars
Linda Johansson - flute, vocals

Tracks

1) Snalblast 18.28
2) Manuel 11.06
3) Agren 16.51
4) Attestupan 12.04

Summary

With these song lengths, what can go wrong? Also, not so strange that they used Manuel for the 3rd Cyclops Samples: it's the shortest song on the album.

The music

I know a guy who says an album should contain at most six songs, but he also wanted to imply with that that an album should not be longer than 30 minutes. This guy is NOT a fan of progressive rock.

The new "sensation" from Scandinavia one can say. A few months ago on the Day of Dreams festival someone involved with Mr So & So started to converse with me and told me of a new sensation from Sweden and although I couldn't really understand him, in retrospect I think he meant Sinkadus.

Needless to say Sinkadus introduces a lot of variation in their songs and it is also very nice to have some cello on this album especially to introduce some sad notes.

Musically the band sounds rather like King Crimson to me, with loads of Mellotron and the friendliness of Court of the Crimson King (i.e. minus Schizoid Man). Still, it's not all just plain beautiful here and in addition to King Crimson some more lighthearted influences from Genesis can also be heard and also the use of the Mellotron is sometimes more in the vein of Banks than of KC's MacDonald.

So going over the songs one by one (there ARE only four of them) we start with Snalblast: in the beginning lots of keyboard, but the guitar also has its niche and in fact it plays some rather nice melodies. There are some vocals in the middle, but the vocals themselves are not very important and in fact hardly anybody will understand them as they are in Swedish. In view of the length of the tracks it is only natural that Sinkadus deals mostly in atmospheres in connecting themes to themes. The song has a number of faces, one of which reminds me a little of Robbery Assault and Battery in that sounds rather happy and more like a tune than "serious" music and also at times the flute makes the music sound rather merry and reminiscent of say Flairck. On the other hand, the vocals are rather depressive and also the more involved and aggressive parts of the track (usually involving the guitar) are rather dark sounding. The Mellotron then acts as emotive wallpaper especially in the quieter introspective parts.

Manuel I was already familiar with from the 3rd Cyclops sampler. This is still a very good track wit nice melodies and a very sad atmosphere. Especially the melody on the flute is very emotive and the interplay with bass guitar is very nice. This song is very much like early era King Crimson and also in this respect similar to say Landberk.

Agren starts desolately with again a sad melody played by the flute. Then we get a more heavy part with menacing guitars and military sounding drums. The nwe get a thematic part in which first the flute and then both guitar and flute repeat a theme for some time. Very melodic and reminding me of Camel. The flute also helps of course in this respect. The singing is now done in harmony and also sometimes in a "question answer" way. Nicely done. In this song rather up tempo parts with good melodies are interleaved with atmospheric parts.

Attestupan (what DO these names mean?) is up last. Contrary to the previous songs, this song starts out in a high tempo with busy drumming, organ in the background and overall a sort of jamming feel. The vocal parts are rather dark and angry, while the music now has a stalking pace. The music then continues with the same ingredients already spoken of in this review.

It is strange that the album is so balanced in the sense that no song really stands out as being their best and also the difference in lengths between the tracks is hardly noticeable.

Conclusion

A good album, but it will take a few listens to really get a hang of it. On first listen it all sounds "nice" but nothing more, but after a few listens this improves and the sameness to the music dissolves, but not entirely. Seventies progressive in the vein of King Crimson (first album), Genesis (around Foxtrot) and Camel in the flute parts, but it doesn't sound dated. The playing and sound are okay, while the singers aren't very good or expressive, but they do their job well. I do think that some people might think that there is a sameness to the music but personally I think the strong melodies make up for that. Not a classic, but very promising debut to say the least.
