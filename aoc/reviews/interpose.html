Summary

The music

Interpose is a jazzrocky outfit with a symphonic rock approach. The basis of their tracks is improvisational guitar and keyboard playing. Rhythms are clear, but fail to draw much attention for themselves. The sound is pretty open and accessible, far more than with other likewise Poseidon artists. The guitar style at times reminds a bit of Allan Holdsworth. And then there is vocalist Sayuri Aruga, who doesn't arrive on the scene until five minutes into the first track. Her powerful Japanese vocals introduce a slight shift in the music and intensity, creating a shift towards the sound of Shingetsu, especially since the compositions also makes a shift over yonder.

Dayflower is another progressive track. This one lacks the initial instrumental section, but opens as a vocal track right away. Now we find that the latter part of the track has the instrumental section, but this continues the symphonic feel of the track's opening section, featuring a sparkling section with keys and flute, that reminds me off Symphonic Slam. Zitensya starts off with what might be called avant swing piano but slowly develops in a more experimental and somewhat neurotic affair in which initially guitar, but later bass and piano take important roles. This track lacks the sort of warmth found in the first two. Despite this there are some interesting bits in it.

Koibumi immediately returns to the warm sound, with synths and light flute. This lightness is carried further into the track, even as the guitar starts carrying the melody, but disappears once Aruga comes in, three and a half minutes into the track, developing more of a romantic sound. The mid section has some nice melodic bits, but the track doesn't manage to draw as much attention to it as others do. This can also be said about Last Sign, which has some keyboards and guitar, but those fail to build the sort of melody we heard in previous tracks.

Conclusion

Interpose are different from other Poseidon acts. Their sound has some heavy symphonic influences, especially in the vocal sections. The instrumental sections on the other hand are more in the type of vein we would expect from Poseidon. The way these styles are blended by the band is quite worthwhile, even though the album does seem to lose some momentum towards the end. Those who can appreciate the odd bit of Holdsworth-type jazzrock along with their symphonic rock should like this disc.
