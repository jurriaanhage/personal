Summary

The music

Les Fradkin was new to me, I must admit. A look at his site, though, told me he is "Mr. Midi Guitar". This album is advertised as a rock opera, but rock can be found only osccasionally, pop would be a lot closer. Les's preference for electronic instruments is quite apparent, coming across in all his use of them (although there's a Mellotron MK II rumoured to be present on the album). The drums are mostly used to keep time. Synths fill in a lot of the silence, and the use of guitar synth makes this album almost sound like a synth album, while it was made by a guitar player (I fell for that trick before, but not this time). Les's vocals are functional at best. Insecure when he goes for higher reaches, often more spoken than sung. But mostly: Les can't sing. Having said all that, I'm still not done... I can't help but feel that the compositions are all pretty much standard pop songs. The pasting closed with electronics can't help in hiding that. Never does it become stylish, often it is a tad on the seedy side, sometimes terribly so. Rehearsals For Retirement has the mellotron and some nice sounding Hammond, as well as a real sounding guitar. Les's flat vocals and the tin drumming make this an iffy track, still sadly far above average.

Conclusion

What can I say about this one? It must be pretty clear I thought it bad. And I must say that I can't really find any selling points which are just outside my tastes. Even though Les undoubtedly is pretty good with his equipment, he's not so good with music. Some of the stuff might have been okay in the mid eighties when this sort of electronic sound was at times used in pop rock, but now it sounds dated at best. Rehearsals For Retirement sounds markedly better, but still is not more than a drop of water in the desert. The overall conclusion can only be: steer away from this one.
