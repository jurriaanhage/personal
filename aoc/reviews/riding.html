Summary

This is a compilation of some Hillman's early material only before released on tape. I've heard people say that they like this stuff better than his first CD for Cyclops, Matrix. Well, let's see.

The music

Most of the stuff on this album is indeed typcially Hillman: sequencers put us back into the seventies and begin eighties reminding me of Tangerine Dream, mostly, but as a whole Hillman is more melodic than TD. Also, there's plenty of rock as well and this is of course not something you'd easily find with TD. The strongest reference I can think of to this type of music is Cybotron from Australia. The problem is however that probably not very many people know them (they should however).

Well let's take a closer look at some of the tracks: Stormbringer starts out easy and works its way up to the speed of metal. Pillars of Sand sounds a little Middle Eastern which by its title should not be surprising. The main instrument here is the flute. The next track is rather menacing, a bit like Jacula with some church organ on the side. Mysterium is majestic and rather sad, while Point of Contact is rather soundtrackish and thus more atmospheric. Earthpulse is rather like TD in a standard way, but Chamber of the Moon is more dreamlike and reminds me of Gandalf. Accumulator sounds ethereal at the start, but becomes more up-tempo. Sashida is rather mysterious and is a flowing track with a good melody. Both Altair and Journey Back are rather soft tracks. A large part of the album is consists of Riding the Storm parts 1 - 6 and is indeed the most varied and interesting, combining many of the elements that are part of Hillman's music. The track has a good main melody, is rhythmic, dark and sounds again rather like Cybotron. Quite a lot of guitar in this track as well. It's far less prominent on most of the other tracks.

Conclusion

A good collection of tracks, but mostly for lovers of synthesizer music I think or maybe people interested in more rockish forms of this kind of music, like Hawkwind for instance. Also, you get a lot of music for your money and the tapes are probably hard to obtain anyway.
