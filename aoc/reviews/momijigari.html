The music

Momijigari takes up more than half of this mini album. It opens in energetic symphonic style reminiscent of the best of ELP and Yes. This is super symphonic. The bass player is very much in Squire style, while keyboards and organ vie for the most prominent position. The flute is likely enough synthetic, while the following organ part is a bit more sinister than the great examples. Here the name King Crimson comes more to mind, with mellotron and the like. Indeed, we might have the Japanese take on Anekdoten right here. Not original at all, but great to listen to, and melodically it is very strong too. The band has its feet in just about anything, although old school sympho dominates. Then we come to the vocals. The female vocals are in Japanese and although the music stays powerful and strong, the vocalist is not so strong. She sings high, and a bit wavery. Maybe, it is simply a question of not being used to it, and I am indeed not a big fan of the higher registers. The vocals make the music sound more like Renaissance, but compared to that band, these people are way more powerful. Strange is that the guitarwork can sound very psyche at times. You know, I can actualy forgive them for the vocals. I mean they may not be on key the whole time, but the music itself is so powerful and strong, yes, one gets used to it. Halfway, the KC influence comes in strongly in the part where the Fripp of the Eighties comes in. I wonder what Momijigari means? Something like potpourri maybe? Some lovely, wavery flute comes in next. This is acoustic territory, mainly and rather melancholic. The final few minutes are full bombast in the vein of Gerard and Mastermind.

The big question when we enter Higansugimade is: can they keep this up? Strangely enough, this seems to be a totally different bands. The vocals are now Japanese, the music is really heavy and seems as if recorded in a basement cellar. The guitar are full of reverb, and the music is fast and thunderous. This is very much like Hawkwind, but more punkish even. Halfway, the symphonic element sets in with some really nice warm mellotron, but I thinik that first half of the song is gonna cost them some fans.

Ryoukiranman is similarly a combination of psyche of punk with crazy Japanese vocals. We are now moving into Ruins territory. Wacky. The second quarter brings in more typical progrock elements, again similar as on the first track. The King Crimson influences are quite strong. Halfway we come to mellotron country again with some really nice bass work. This is very neo-prog like, with Mark Kelly style keyboards. Towards the end they freak out again.

Conclusion

Difficult to categorize. The first and extremely long track is a prime example of seventies symphonic rock, notwithstanding the vocals. The second and third tracks are psyche/punkish with washes of mellotron, but especially the last song is very wacky and out there. I do not think many symphonic rock lovers will like what they do there, although they go for full symphonic rock in the second half. But it has to be said: besides the somewhat boring first half the second track: what they do has its merit, and the opening epic takes its influences from just about any symphonic act from the seventies. Recording quality varies a bit.
