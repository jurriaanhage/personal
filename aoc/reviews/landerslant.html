Summary

Vivahead continue to release their home produced sombertronics.

The music

DSQ opens like a string orchestra playing tone poems. He notes stretch out, and have a violin like feel. Probably everything on this album is constructed by synths and keyboards, and in fact, the violinic sound is harsher than usual, like a mellotron for dark ambient/industrial music. On the other hand, the music strongly resembles Fripp's latest solo outings, the soundscapes, and with it shares the often present feeling of foreboding.

DB Wilderness is drum 'n bass like (or whatever I take that for). Plenty of abrasive music here, with a sing-song melody running through. I am thinking Controlled Bleeding here. Slartholemew's Elation is quite a bit friendlier, largely repetitive, piano oriented, with raw sounds setting in in the back halfway or so. Notwithstanding its simplicity this one really works well.

SK is another short one opening with some vague sounds running into a humoristic kind of flutish music. What are we ridiculing here. All those things off-key... Sonic Treasure is the second of the long tracks. Again those chamber music instruments come in, including something like a bassoon. This makes the music more RIOish than ever, while the shorter ones so far are more electronic, these pieces have very much a suite like feel. There is not much development however, everything moves slowly, similar to Hammill's The Light Continent for instance. I do wonder what the instrumentation of this album is actually, because I do happen to spot a clarinet here, and a oboe most likely.

Lake Trip is a friendly one, a bit languid at first, but later when the mellotron comes in, mystery is added. At times we do have something of a beat. Clever Fish is an example in point. Overall the style is similar to Serge Blenner, although a bit more lo-fi. A merry melody dominates, although Vivahead goes a bit further in what they mix into it. Paper gets crumbled, weird bird calls, we got it all.

Allsides is music that dissonates from all sides, attacking your ears. It is short, but in the chamber orchestra style of track 1 and 5. Furthermore is the closer of the album, although we get a short bonus. Again, the melodic play is very repetitive giving the tune its signature. For the rest we have a drum box doing its work and spoken vocals. The track does have a nice tenseness to it.

The bonus track is a short, somber excursion into sound.

Conclusion

A combination of RIOish material and electronic music, intermixed with plenty of effects and samples is what Vivahead offers. The RIOish pieces are nice to hear (and are they played with real instruments?), while the electronic pieces can be tuneful, but also have something of a novelty quality, making them nice listens, but not something to repeatedly listen to. Clever Fish and Furthermore lie between the two, being more electronic and melodic, but also have something that entices.
