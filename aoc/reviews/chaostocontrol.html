The music

From thick wood one saws planks, is a direct translation of a proverb from my country. With this proverb we mean that something or someone is not doing seomthing very subtly. This certainly applies to the metallic opening of this album. After a minute or so though the clam vocals start. Here we also hear that the keyboards also play a role on this album usually a must for people from the progressive corner to like the music. The guitarplaying is quite varied ranging from pyrotechnics in the vein of Marty Friedman to sawing rhythm guitars. As always the strength of the music is a direct result of the tightness of the compositions and the quality of vocal and vocal melody. In the first track at least these all are quite okay, although the chorus is not entirely to my liking. Notwithstanding the fact that the music is thoroughly melodic a liking for speedy metal is certainly necessary as exemplified by Voices, which also contains a fair amount of scale playing. The band concentrates on playing tight compositions very fast, but does include in its compositions quite a lot of variation. Into The Sun is striking because of its especially appealing chorus. For some reason or other I'm constantly reminded of Enchant, possibly because of a similar vocal style, although Magnitude 9 is a lot more metal. The next one Keeper Of Your Soul opens quite bombastic and is quite varied throughout with quite strong rhythm guitar work and some more classical (a la Malmsteen I mean) lead guitar parts. As often is the case, this metal band likes to refer to classical music. Don't Stay is a rather a accessible track with an oft repeated title. Not bad though. With After Tomorrow I become just a little tired with the energetic music and although there is something to be said for not including YARB (yet another rock ballad), it might not have been out of place here. Fortunately this song does include an atmospheric interlude. The penultimate track Y2K (short for the year 2000, although technically it is the year 2048) is enriched with some dark sounding spoken vocals, while the closer Writings brings us more of the same but with nice plucking on the bass and double vocals.

Conclusion

As often is the case, plenty of info with this album are meant to show that this is really a worthwhile band. In this case they are at leats partly right, since I enojyed the music on this album quite a lot. not being too fond of those heavy powered progmetalbands with their quickfingers, this band sems to have something more to give than just quick fingers and double bassdrums. The extra lies in the melodicity of the music and the vocal lines and having quite a lot of variation without killing the song eniterly. Sometimes the band tends to forget itself a bit and the fingers are rushing up and down the scales, but there's just plenty of breathing time left. Lots of bombast and plenty of variation on this progressive (speed)metalalbum. A pitfall for this band might be that the vocals are so prominent that they tend to dominate the album and will make songs sound alike. A bit more varied use of the voice is advised.
