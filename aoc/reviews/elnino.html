Summary

Headquake of Eldritch was an album I was quite satisfied with. They have returned now with El Nino, after that influential wind. Fortunately I now got some more info on this band so I could even derive who plays what in this band.

The music

The album opens terrifically with the short Fall From Grace: a memorable theme and some nice effects on the keyboards. The follow up Direction Home is a hard driving track with plenty of variation and tempo changes. The melodies are not as striking, but if you listen close you can hear some interesting additions on keyboard, unfortunately though they are somewhat in the back of the mix. During the interlude, the piano and rhythm guitar stand afore and contrary to many prog bands the piano is not most accessibly played. After the mandatory guitar solo there's even room for a keyboard solo and especially the keyboards at the end of this track is very good. Heretic Beholder is an up-tempo track. The musical part of this track is quite good, a bit plodding, but with a great guitar solo and keyboard play (slightly classical in the solo, but weirder during the rest of the song). The vocal melodu is not especially great however. The keyboards in the intro of Scar are almost house-like, but the riffing is of course prog metal. The vocals are two sided here: the industrial-like transformed ones and the metal-like ones. Bleed Mask Bleed is a more melodic and rather accessible track, especially during the chorus. I'm not that fond of the acoustic intermezzo, this part needed a better vocal melody. Fortunately this part is only short. The Last Days Of The Year is the ballad opens with cello and something between acoustic guitar and clavecimbel. This waltzlike continues with some nice piano and of course we encounter that unavoidable guitar solo. The vocal melody is certainly good enough to carry the song. From Dusk Till Dawn reintroduces the rhythm guitar and a rather fast pace. Again, a very accomplished composition with nice melodic additions of the keyboard and quite a number of breaks. The long To Be Or Not To Be (God) continues. Opening with a bass line, this is a driving track. The melody of the verses are not great, but the melody of the up-tempo chorus is very nice and also the way that the guitar takes this melody over. The all-out organ solo past the middle of the track is certainly surprising and also its length. After this we return to the intro and the song reboots and ends in an eruption of noise. The title track closes the regular album. It opens as mysterisouly with shards of rhythm guitar and again the use of organ. The acoustic guitar playing is percolating on this rather accessible song. The bonus track available on a limited edition is Nebula Surface. On my disc it was connected to the last track, making this last track last over eleven minutes. Certainly not a filler this track, with again the chorus standing out. The song is a slower down, moody and

A small note on the singer: of course he sings in the metal/prog metal way, but he usually stays down in the lower regions and only goes up once in a while. If you now my tastes, you will understand this is how I prefer to hear it. Also, he uses his voice in a variety of ways, but generally he sounds quite muscled.

Conclusion

On first listening I didn't think much of this one, but it is one of these growers and using headphones to hear all the details (especially of the keyboards) may be a good thing to do. On the other hand one might say that maybe too much of the music occurs in the back of the mix. The vocals are okay on this done, muscled and at times somewhat high, but Terence is not a real screamer. I especially like the non-obvious sometimes estranging keyboards on this disc, giving an extra flavour to the music. Many of the choruses are great and memorable. The band may pay some more attention to the melodies in the verses, but still most songs can stand on themselves. If you're out for this one, it may be good to try and get the version with the bonus track; it's worth having, although it is one of the more accessible tracks. A succesful follow up to Headquake.
