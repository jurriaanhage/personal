Summary

Another Rick Ray disc. His changed address reads 20301 Ball Ave., Euclid, Ohio 44123, U.S.A.

The music

Always In Your View is the first track. It is a vocal track and one of the longer on the album. Featuring the clarinet of Schultz in a question answer game between the vocals and said clarinet, this is a rather pacey piece and enjoys a few changes of pace. The middle part is filled with a typical Ray solo, fast fingered and high pitched, but laced with some spacey guitar playing. If this is your first review of a Rick Ray album I'll go over the essentials right now: the music is guitar rock with a few progressive touches. There are both rather simple and also some quite complex tracks. Most notable point of attention is the guitar work, which is quite typical, like I just said: fast, high pitched and often quite meandering, but in a good way, because Ray can keep people's attention with his playing. His singing is below standard, on the hoarse side. One might argue that it fits with the music, which has a lo-fi production and a kind of underground feel. Many of the lyrics have references to state and government (who don't come off too well) and also quite a few of biblical references (although the lyrics are religious or anything). At times the lyrics are moralistic. A noteworthy aspect of the music is the use of clarinet played by Rick Schultz. It is playd more or less as a guitar, mostly soloing away in a meandering way. A drawback of the music I think is the fact that live drummers are not present. Because of this the music hardly every rocks really well: the intensity of the guitar playing does not jibe with the grooves laid down by the drum computer. To continue then... Utopian Order is a very short track and not impressive. Guitarm And Hammer has two sides: the vocal part is bouncy and not particularly attractive with its rather straight forward riffing, but the guitar solo is good and the effects and bassplaying the middle make up for it somewhat. Another Dimension is quite a nice track, a bit apart from the rest. This time the vocals are coded and melodically the music is more interesting. There's a bit of menace in the music now. Time Seems To Fly is the longest track on the album: the opening has a Beatles influence and there are some harmony vocals on this one. The music is quite relaxed in the beginning, a bit ballad like. The music is also more melodic than usual. Of course the track has its typical guitar solo, and towards the end quite a bit of sound experiments on guitar. Freedom No Longer is a sampled vocal with some dark sounds over them. The title track then is a rather active piece and consists of layers of noisy guitar, some clear percussion and rather accessible vocal parts. The Monolith continues with an alternation of mid-tempo vocal parts and up-tempo passages in between. The guitar playing is a bit repetitive, but here it doesn't hurt. Again some against-the-grain sound fx and it ends in lush keyboards, something that one almost never hears on Ray's records. In This Sphere continues with jumpy rock music, with some sinister vocal parts and some playing around with the fx. Usually Ray's records have quite a few instrumental, but on this record not yet. Also Ray usually does not play around very much with fx, but that is also different on this record. Of course. Right after my remark on instrumentals we get not one but three. Deadman Boogie is a melodic, extended guitar solo, rather pacey at times, but lacking in groove (yep those drums again). A Wheel Within A Wheel is a rather complicated thingy on guitar. Good pace and varied, melodic and clear playing on the guitar here. That's what I like to hear. The Voices Of Tartarus is a spooky piece and after that we come to The Writer. The hoarse, gravelly vocals are back and the clarinet as well. Not much difference in style with the closer Corruption Had Taken Over.

Conclusion

Lots of music and not dissimilar from the other records except for a few things: the part where there are no vocals I liked best by far and also the Beatles influenced long track was nice. For the rest the typical ingredient to a Rick Ray record as explained above.
