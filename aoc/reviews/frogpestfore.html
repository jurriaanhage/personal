Summary

Well all of these titles have been reviewed by us, except for the cd's who were distributed by Roadrunner in the Benelux (Magellan and Shadow Gallery). Hence if you want to know more about these releases chances are you can find more about them here.

The music

Vapourspace - Osmosis (LTE Remix) starts off with a Jansen/Barbieri like reworking of a Liquid Tension Experiment track. Personally, there is not much that I like here. Trent Gardner - Aria For Italy/With Father (Demos) then with a soft spoken and melodic track leading into Reins Of Tuscan from the Leonardo musical. This features LaBrie in musical style. A good introduction to the album this with jazzy piano and rowdy guitar.

Terry Bozzio & Billy Sheehan join in with one their dark tunes The Last Page (Off The Wall Mix). Zoomy, gloomy, with a bit of guitar. Think of the Jansen/Barbieri and Karn crowd here, but also nods in the direction of the Tone Center releases, David Torn and Attention Deficit. Like the album, the tracks features a lot of spoken word, added mostly for atmosphere.

Shadow Gallery - Society Of The Mind is of course a very different animal. Arena type bombast, catchy vocal harmonies making this catchy rock track sound like Threshold, but with higher vocals. You might also be tempted to think of Star One.

Robert Berry's first contribution is the short instrumental and filmic Spears And Buckler. James La Brie's Mullmuzzler's Afterlife is not bad, but notwithstanding the melodic vocals and a tendency NOT to go over the top his voice, it has its weak parts. The album itself does include one very good song (the last one, Falling).

Jordan Rudess freaks out on Crack The Meter. Jammy guitar and synthrock with plenty of verve. As long as it is not an album full, I am all for it, Robert Berry returns with The Wheel Of Time, which is folky in outset. One might be tempted to think of Mostly Autumn here, but not too much. Lots of melody on this one.

Andy West With Rama rages on the mad jazzrock of Mad March. Distinctive runs and heavy drums make it one of the louder pieces. Family Jewels is by Magellan and has a Tullian opening, but at one third we move into the area of Vangelis. The keyboard sound is dark, also a bit experimental, but it has some more accessible melodies as well. Rather filmic stuff.

Steve Morse Band's Majorly Up is a good instrumental, from a good instrumental album. Usually these albums are for fans only, but this one is really worth a listen. Explorers Club's Passage To Paralysis is again a Trent Gardner thing, Magna Carta's house musician. For him this is a rather accessible slow song with Steve Walsh doing the vocals.

Niacin is Hammond, Hammond and Hammond and one those bands who can still improve on what I think was their third album. Good instrumental rock with a focus on the groove. Closing down is Steve Walsh again on The December People's We Three Kings Of Orient Are. A Robert Berry project which some people seem to hate, while others love it. I am one of the latter crowd.

Conclusion

A good introduction to some titles who you might otherwise never try your hear on.
