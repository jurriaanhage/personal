Flight 09 - Forbidden Lullabies

Artist: Flight 09
Title: Forbidden Lullabies
Label: Neurosis Records
Length(s): 48 minutes
Year(s) of release: 2003
Month of review: [10/2003]

Line up

Igor Savich - compositions, guitars, keyboards, lead and backing vocals
Constantine Savich - bass
Art Piyanzin - drums, backing vocals

Tracks

1) You Got My Love 5.44
2) Look Around 6.30
3) Something's Wrong 5.12
4) Both To Be Alone 7.02
5) The Absolution 5.52
6) Shade 5.45
7) In The Darkness 6.08
8) My Wheels 5.36

Summary

Their second album so far, the first also having been released on the American label Neurosis Records, the label of Rick Ray. The band however seems to originate from Uzbekistan and this album was mixed by Vitaly Menshikov of Progessor.

The music

Many people would not refer to the music of Rick Ray as progressive rock and this will very likely be the impression that the opener You Got My Love leaves behind. This is repetitive bouncy blues rock with a strong focus on the guitar work and the gravelly vocals of Savich. He does not have a great voice, but it does fit the music.

On Look Around, the outlook is different. It opens with keyboards and rustic guitar. The vocals sound quite a bit less 'dirty', in fact this is a somewhat up-beat pop track with echoey vocals. There is a nice bounce in the guitar riff here, with an Arabic tinged melody. A more distinctive composition than the previous one. The guitar work towards the end is also quite nice, the vocal work is a maybe a bit too long the same.

With Something's Wrong the blues rock returns. The synths bring over the effect of a horn section. The rhythm guitars are dominant here and the gait is again bouncy, an impression increased by the violin choirs (synthetic). It is hard to compare the music to anything specific, but the blues influence continues to be the most apparent one. In addition the use of synths does give the music a slightly different feel. This for instance is especially true for the fluting bridge. The rhythm is a bit too monotonic for progressive rock.

Both To Be Alone opens quietly and peacefully, fingerpicking guitar, flute like keyboards, the works. The vocals are somewhat accented, a bit wavery, in a sad fashion. Bit by bit the music becomes more forceful, with the guitar setting in and the keys becoming more audible. The instrumental intermezzo brings us back to the acoustic guitar and flute like synths of the opening after which this song really gets underway. The sparse vocals, the electrifying guitar, the repetitive tension building keyboards. An excellent song.

Past halfway we encounter the pacey The Absolution. Again we have electric guitar backed by synths. The drums are a bit more proggy here than on other tracks. The rhtyhm guitar dominates, together with the pounding drums and the gravelly vocals. The chorus is strongly melodic, getting quite poppy during the guitar solo. Like earlier on I hear elements of Ageness, although Flight 09 is more of a rock band.

Shade is a ballad, with repetitive synth strings in the back. Again, the composition is a strong one, although the rendition is like all of the music somewhat underproduced and I can imagine people having problems with the vocals. In my book however, the former is more important than the latter.

In The Darkness refers to Dire Straits in the opening, the guitar work that is. The song itself is a tragic one. Slowly the song gets under way, with the vocalist grinding out/growling his vocals. The pace goes up a bit more halfway with the guitar leading and the drums pounding away. Deep Purple may come to mind here at times. The guitar work is quite melodic and likable with a nice climax at the end.

Final track is My Wheels, which opens with acoustic tinkering. Again, the vocal melody is well taken care of, while the slow bluesy electric guitar solo continues the lazy feel of the song.

Conclusion

A blues rock band with elements of prog, similar in some sense to for instance the Rick Ray Band and the like, but less focused on the guitar and also a more melodic approach. Compositionwise this is quite a strong album, especially songs such as Both To Be Alone (should that not be Born To Be Alone by the way?) and Shade. The versions here are somewhat underproduced, of course a funds problem of some kind, while the vocals are distinctive, accented and gravelly, something which may be a threshold for a more general appeal. However, I still find the end result to be quite charming, and not without its merits.
