The music

This album is close to what you might call synth rock. It's not really just synthesizer, but they are certainly prominent. Also there are no vocals and Steve uses Drum programming (and I don't like programmed drums). Still, the idea of this album is I think to construct songs from a number of melody lines and play those songs using a guitar and synthesizer making the rhythm section unimportant and thus better left to machines.

I will not describe the songs one by one, but more the general idea. The music on this album is quite modern and up-tempo, but can be cosmic as well. Mark Shreeve might be a good reference here (and I'm talking about that man's later releases, not the Schulze imitations from the seventies). A longer song like Matrix also contains some TD/Schulze parts. When it gets a little faster Hawkwind influences are obvious, with all the beeps and squeaks. To try the various kinds of styles, try Dawning Light as a somewhat relaxed song Matrix part 2 as more up-tempo in the Hawkwind style and Tritone as more menacing kind of song.

Conclusion

Basically an appreciable, varied and enjoyable synth album, containing all kinds of synth stuff, being just a little bit cheesy at times, but usually quite distant from New Age, more close to rock and like I said quite enjoyable.
