Summary

The band Egdon Heath will stop existing/has stopped existing at the end of 1999. This is a rarities disc meant for the die-hard fan and only available from the band itself. E-mail them for more info.

The music

The album opens with The Other Side. Like Mulder says in the tongue-in-cheek writing in the booklet, plenty of space for the bass player. Acoustic guitar, a whale like bass, lush keyboards and clear tingling on this track from 1985. Like on the first album, the vocals are the weak part of this very quiet and strangely moody track. The guitar solo is quite nice as is the closing keyboard section. The sound quality is a bit wavery and noisy in the quieter parts with a few dips here and there. The next track is from the same sessions. This is Wild Dogs, which is a much poppier and weaker song than The Other Side. The sound quality varies during the track which is not a good sign. The band does work with moods here when the bass/drum take over in the middle and then the guitar sets in quietly against a backdrop of tinkling keyboards. But the chorus is quite bad. Home & Dry is played live in the Frysian town of Lemmer in 1985. The vocals are almost ununderstandable, the pronounciation is quite bad, the kind of vocals I expect in punk songs. Plenty of repetitive keyboards with some high pitched guitar work, but unfortunately too far in the back). There's something akin to wailing sirens stuffed really deep back into the mix. Strange. The Wyvern was recorded a month later for local radio. We are still pre-In The City. The sound is a bit better here, but the works are still a weak point. Musically this is a pumping piece. It shows that already at the outset, the melodies were present. What was lacking was a good singer, experience and a good studio. Dodona is something else entirely with the winds blowing and a moody keyboard (Fender Rhodes like). The whispered lyrics are somewhat strange. Like the band I can spot the word Pindakaas (Dutch for peanutbutter). Maybe a bit too many ideas in this track which is filled with various melodies on keyboards and some "choir" at the end. Don't Deny Yourself is a preproduction of No Second Faust. They are right in saying that the chorus doesn't coming off well. The recording techniques are a bit better here. Although the vocals are much better they still lack the loud declamatory character of Van Der Stempel. Weekend is actually the same song as Message on The Killing Silence. The vocals are not very well articulated. This is a rather pathetic sounding piece. Romantic piano playing and a feel guitar solo round it off while Kayak raises its ugly head in the keyboard section and the ground is laid by the moody bass sound. Then the song takes a turn for the poppy. Halfway through the disc we come an instrumental In The City medley as played during the presentation of The Killing Silence in Paradiso including the mysterious Secret Fence. Weirdly enough I also recognize Wettons Don't Cry in here. Coming back for the encore with Stand Straight, I think the least favourite of the songs from The Killing Silence. The singer obviously has fun on stage. His vocals are quite strong and can be heard clearly. This is one of the more poppy tracks from TKS with an optimistic chorus. Lyrically it is close to In The City. A track that is very much alive, with a speed up at the moment. Jinc is the instrumental from Him, The Snake And I. Personally I would have preferred On A Bench from this tour since it was quite different from the album version. This song was recorded in Het Kasteel in Alphen aan den Rijn. This is important, because I happen to live there. Enter nostalgia. This somewhat jazzy track is followed by an acoustic live version of Gringo. A very strong track with a high emotional content. The song stands up easily also in this rather naked form. Head In The Sand from Nebula closes the album in the same naked form. The song does sound quite heavy though with violence on the acoustic guitar and some tasteful piano playing. Very good.

Conclusion

Well, the sound quality and all that leave to be desired in the first half of the disc. It gradually gets better though and the music of Egdon Heath deserves to be heard, even the ones left out of the albums altogether. The live tracks are quite nice with an enthusiastic Stand Straight, the In The City medley and the acoustic Gringo and Head In The Sand. The band owed to Genesis when they started but never very much. They've always their own typical sound. All in all a must for the fan. The other people are invited to first try out The Killing Silence, Him, The Snake And I or Nebula. Whatever you can lay your hands on first.
