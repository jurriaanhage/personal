Summary

They've been releasing music since 1991, with four CDs (two of which are compilations) to their name. This is their fifth.

The music

From the psychedelic artwork one might suspect that the music on this album is psychedelic. The first song, As Time Goes By, does nothing to let you think otherwise: spacey sounds, spaceous acoustic guitar work, quite repetitive, low singing, squeaky sounds. The music is somewhere between rock and ballad. At times the rhythm takes over a bit and the music becomes more driving with quite some Hammond added. A negative aspect of this track is the rather high noise ratio (they can't have done this on purpose, can they!), of course mostly during the quiet parts, of which there are still quite a few. In short, Hawkwind and Pink Floyd with a large Hammond presence, and I like it, but like I said the sound quality is not up to par. The latter remark also holds for the title track that opens quietly with slightly Indian acoustic guitar. The composition is quite nice, typically Hawkwindian spacerock, but the vocal melodies especially are quite nice. This rather long track has quite a long cosmic intermezzo with what seem to be astronauts talking. At the end of this track the noise largely disappears but the keyboards sound distorted and I'm not sure whether this should be the case. In the next track the noise returns. This track is quite short and is pastoral with mostly acoustics and a female voice wailing softly in the background. Swimming Through The Misty Days is a moody lament with the slighty wavery vocals of Stroem, but I have to admit liking his vocals and in fact, the vocals of spacerock bands are generally the part where the least care is taken. The atmosphere in this track is very well evoked. After the more up-tempo Changes we come to one of the best tracks, melodically, of the album: What Have They Done? Really a terrific, impressive track. Very majestic and stately. The ending is quite "unspacerockish" with percussive piano and quite menacing. The Hammond returns in Guilt. Again, the melodies are very good and the driving music can really fascinate. Actually, this continues to be more or less the case for the rest of the album: great vocal melodies and all thoroughly in the spacerock vein. The noise level seems to be less towards the end of the album, but unfortunately A Storyteller's Night is the extremely noisy closer. Here Kayak also seems to be an influence (in the vocals), but also with a slightly medieval touch. The high "ahohohoh-haaa" vocals of Mia Lorentzon return.

Conclusion

Very decent early-Floydian, Hawkwindian spacerock, that lacks in the sound quality area. Quite a lot of noise in this recording and one has to kill 16kHz and higher to get rid of it. Too bad, since the music on this album certainly deserves better. I might even be tempted to just forget about the noise and enjoy songs like What Have They Done? A last point of criticism is the fact that the lyrics do not always fit well to the music and the vocals are not great techincally. These points of criticism are however compensated by the fact that the singers voice fits well with the music.
