Thieves' Kitchen - Head

Artist: Thieves' Kitchen
Title: Head
Label: self produced
Length(s): 63 minutes
Year(s) of release: 2000
Month of review: 04/2000

Line up

Paul Beecham - bass, backing vocals
Simon Boys - vocals
Wolfgang Kindl - keyboards, backing vocals
Phil Mercy - guitars, backing vocals
Mark Robotham - electric drums

Tracks

1) Mute 16.01
2) Time 7.15
3) The Return Of The Ultragravy 11.00
4) Integrity 9.10
5) T.a.n.u.s 19.30

Summary

The familiar ones of Thieves' Kitchen are Mark Robotham of Grey Lady Down, and Phil Mercy who has a solo album out on Mellow Records. When they started they told me they wanted something more "progressive" on the English prog scene (in other words: not neo). Here is Head.

The music

Opening with high pitched keyboards, Mute quickly goes into a busy meandering instrumental intro in which also electric guitar figures with its heavy chords. Bombastic. However, then we go into a pianic part where the opening theme is recaptured. The vocal part sounds well, not really special. The music is now a bit tamer, and the music reminds me of Jadis. Yes, really. The vocals of Boys are bit different from those of Chandler, but the vocal part is certainly quite accessible with a nice recognizable chorus. The instrumental parts however are much more wayward then those of Jadis, so this is where the similarities end. The instrumental music here, especially on guitar, is in a sense repetitive, meandering, and the feeling I get is that the music stops and goes, stops and goes. Often, the music comes a bit too a stand still, not flowing, because of the many signature changes, and then at some point, the melody takes control a bit and the music flows again. I'm not altogether sure I like this, also because some of the instrumental parts hardly seem to go anywhere. Towards the end of the song the vocalist shows more of the back of his tongue, making the song sound more dynamic and the very good theme returns. The end the of track also makes good a little bit, being a bit more driving. After this 16 minute track we come to the melodic Time. Again the chorus is good with some nice melodic keyboards, but the "Don't let me hide" I could do without. The guitar work is rather chaotic in this track and the drummer tends to thunder on. The intermezzo is quiet with a prominent bass, acoustic guitar and some slowed down vocals. Then the guitar sets in again, lots of notes there. After the guitar part, the keyboards take the fore again. Quick fingered Wolfgang shows his abilities. The Return Of The Ultragravy shows the same variety as the previous track, with plenty of signature changes, sawing rhythm guitars (hey, this even sounds like slow progmetal). The vocal parts meander, especially in the slow and bouncy part. This is compensated for by again a good melodic chorus. Again plenty of variation in the guitaristic middle with some nice licks and melodies, but also a dose of gettingnowhereness. The piano riff builds up very nicely here with some mellotron in the back. Very nice. Integrity is rather Gentle Giant-like. Even the fastpaced vocal part (although not the sound of the voice) reminds me of them. The organ, the "industrial" sounding interlude and the intense No More Integrity... are the best parts of this track. There are even some mellow backing vocals. The final track, after the wake up call, is T.A.N.U.S. Again, the some of the vocal parts remind me of Gentle Giant. No harmonies though. A loud track with again lots of variation, but also like on the previous track, there seems to be a bit too much going on. Some nice jazzy piano playing with a backdrop of mellotron and nice percussion. Modern day life is a bitch as we can hear on this track. After a dreamy interlude in which the vocalist tries to calm down a bit the guitar starts to meander again. The song winds a bit down towards the end, with some melodic guitar playing, washes of drums and subtle piano playing.

Conclusion

Original, but still typically progressive music from the U.K. I couldn't really make any significant comparisons here. Do I like it? Well, that depends a bit. The problem is that the music is not just complex, it also has a tendency to wander and meander. A consequence is that I like parts in every track, but also every track has parts I do not like. It seems that much attention was paid to the choruses, that are melodic and recognizable, but the verses often go unnoticed. The keyboards play a fairly big role, but the guitar is still the main instrument right here. The guitar sound is a bit on the discordant type, but occasionally gets nicely melodic. I admit liking it more in the latter case.
