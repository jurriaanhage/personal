Summary

The swan song of this Dutch band. In the booklet it is stated that most of this material was recorded live in 1994, quite some time ago that is.

The music

I was told that this should be Cliffhanger's most accessible album to date. After their very good To Be Or Not To Be, I was disappointed by their follow-up. This album I think cannot be compared with any of the two since it seems only to be an album that ties up loose ends. A nice thing about such a live album is however that relatively many of the tracks presented here will be unknown to you (as they were to me). The first track is Escape. A rather poppy sounding track with a very present bass. Notwithstanding the poppiness it is quite an enthusing track, brimming with energy. Oh Cloudy, Cloudy Sky/Here Comes The Utopian opens thoughtfully with dark overtones on the keyboards, this of course is the Cloudy part. Here Comes The Utopian is like the first track an up-tempo song with some Kelly-like keyboards, again a prominent bass sound and quite a lot of organ. The ending is typical prog: meandering keyboards and lots of breaks and intermezzo's. The singing of Huigen is not great, I guess he sings a bit too low, not all out, but for a live performance not bad either. The title track opens almost happily. One of the more striking tracks I think since the vocal melody is quite accessible and memorable. Halfway we come to a more quiet intermezzo, also quite nice, but the jumpy beginning returns later on. A recognizable riff here, but I still wonder where I heard it before. Genesis is evoked on the longish Good Things (Last Forever) which opens with acoustic guitar and somewhat Genesis-like keyboards. A good melodic opening and the vocal part is also quite nice. The bass solo later on definitely evokes Twelfth Night and the emotional guitar solo at the end make this a highpoint so far. Feels Like Flying is a newer song, recorded in 1997. It fits well within the others. All registers open on this one especially at the end with the strong choral sounds, trying to convery a feeling of flying. A nice track all in all. Hopeless is the longest track on the album. Opening with soft dark acoustic guitar the song continues with a jumpy part that sounds certainly less interesting. All a bit too easy going. The singing is sometimes almost painful to hear, so cutting. The instrumental middle part is very powerful and energetic conveying a sense of urgency to plastic that the band failed to do on most of their songs. This song really rocks in the way that the Flower Kings do live. Certainly one of their best songs. The albums closes with three tracks that also ended up on the debut. The songs all sound quite fresh, with Colossus being the first (reminding me in a way of Procol Harum's A Salty Dog). 4 Vessels is a dreamy track because of the strong presence of the keyboards. On the other hand, the drumming is quite fickle and brings a sense of unquiet, expectation. The synths on this track are very good. The closer Kill You Darlings I'm not that fond of. The instrumental parts are okay, especially if the keyboards and guitars really get rolling, but the vocal parts are not that good. Again a few deja-entendu's in here and as always I'm not able to tell where they are from, just that I know them.

Conclusion

After a so-so start this album turns out to brim with energy. The spirit of Genesis is quite present at times, but not overly much. This effort therefore deviates from their studio albums, but the band is still very much Cliffhanger. The song Hopeless is a great one and also to hear some of those old tracks in a more fuller sounding energetic way is a bonus. Also since this album contains quite a lot of "new" material, there is no reason not to add this album to your collection of Cliffhanger albums. If that collection is as yet empty, you might be tempted to try them on this on, since it is certainly their more accessible one and a very pleasing one at that. A thing to check out, is whether you like the rather harsh, nasal vocals.
