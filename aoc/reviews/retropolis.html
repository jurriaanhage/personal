Summary

Roine Stolt has been known to die-hard prog fans as being a leading member of Kaipa. This is third album of his new band The Flower Kings, which started as a solo project, but is now well-known within the realms of progressive rock under this name. After less than a year they are back with an album with a nice title: Retropolis. It wasn't my first encounter with them, because I did once see them play live. Although the percussionist on the one hand made sure that the performance wasn't too static he did manage to divert my attention from the music too many times. Still, the concert was alright and at times even good. Let's see how they are on record.

The music

The album starts out with ping-ponging and someone reciting The Rhythm of Life. Just an intro for the following Rhythm of the Sea that starts remarkably Genesis like (I like it too). The track continues on a more heavy note and soon enters a jazzrockish guitar jam. An imposing piece of music, in which the listener is pulled form left to right and back to front. The melodies are good and there's a lot of variation without meaninglessness. Five minutes into the second track, we ease into quieter waters, literally, with all kind of bubbling sounds. Soft keyboard sounds, mellotron, and percussion move into a beautiful played on a flute (or a digital representative thereof), after which the freeflying jazzrock returns to the fore. The guitar is then taken over by repetitive over the top organ playing in accompaniment of some impressive percussion. The closing section of the track features easy going acoustic guitar, making for a breather after this hectic stuff.

Track three starts out playful and quiet and is the first track with vocals. About the vocals, I've never read this, but they remind me very much of the vocals of one John Wetton. The song is rather balladlike and flowing, and is very agreeable. Again, the synths remind me of Genesis, while the guitarsound is reminiscent of Uwe D'Rose's (Landmarq). After the well-done chorus, the track picks up a little speed and becomes more percussive. The guitarplaying in the chorus is very emotional, while at the end there's some room for a gentle piano, playing the waves of the sea, the rollers and coasters on the beach.

The intro the next long track already sounds familiar and also reminds me a lot of synth playing by Banks in the time of One for the Vine. Still, after this intro the track turns out to be organ loaded up-tempo, more rocking track. Personally I'm not so fond of this track, although there's nothing really wrong with it and it certainly has its moments, although the main riff is a bit on the boring side. In the middle the track takes a turn for the better, where the music becomes anthemic with good vocalizing and harmonies (somewhat Yes like I have to admit). In this way the track really blossoms into the a flower. Still the ending is VERY Yes inspired.

The next track is a small pianic interlude and is indeed romantic.

The Melting Pot is a good name for the next track, because it features both delicate piano, Arabic tunes played on soprano sax, bombastic church organ and what have you. A nice way to get rid of a number of good ideas in just over 5 minutes.

The start of Silent Sorrow is rather accessible and based on the plain verse-chorus principle. The song is mid-tempo and although it is not complex, it stays interesting because of the arrangement and the changing vocals. After this first plain part, we get into more varied water with a lot of percussion (all kinds) and a score of melodies with some real beauties in there. At the end the rocking chorus is repeated and works up to a crescendo.

The Judas Kiss starts out brilliantly with a very good melody with a rough edge. The song is very driven and had played though my head for at least a day. Again, the song is very anthemic and at times approaches the melodic version of hard rock. Especially the end of the track of a rare beauty. By far the best one on the album.

Retropolis by Night is rather quiet and should be seen as Retropolis revisited.

The next track is also instrumental and reminds me a lot of those improvisation tracks by KC on the Great Deceiverbox. As a whole not very satisfying, because some parts are a little too playful for my tastes. A lot variation though as with The Melting Pot. Still it also contains some very nice parts.

A peaceful beginning to The Road Back Home. The song reminds me of a lonely open road with the sun in the sky with an occaisonal cumulus cloud blocking the light. The track is rather friendly and optimistic and for some maybe a bit cheesy.

Conclusion

Well, an album that is easy to like for everyone I guess, or perhaps Henry Cow is more your cup of tea. Still, this album is a worthwhile find for anyone interested in progressive rock and due to its melodic content even beyond that. From piano to a grinding organ, from soft playful percussion to heavy drums, from easy going acoustic guitar to searing and soaring electric guitar, counting among its influences Yes and Genesis without sounding much like them, this album is a certain recommended. Very pleasant and at times captivating (The Judas Kiss), and in this way certainly comparable to Pendragon although the Flower Kings tend to have more cards up their sleeve.
