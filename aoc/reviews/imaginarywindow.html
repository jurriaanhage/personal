Summary

Cast is back, but strangely enough with a self produced disc, which has been correspondingly hard to find in Europe. Later I heard the album was out on Musea after all. This might be the place to check.

The music

The album opens with a perfectly symphonic instrumental. Not really an intro since over eight minutes is a bit long for that. On the one hand I'm reminded of Collage, because of the guitar and the melodicity, but partly at least the music has a strong Latin American feel (the flute part). The guitar parts are quite soaring. Alter Ego (On Hands Of White Neck Bandits) is very Genesis like with quickly sung lyrics. I'm not too fond of this track however. A Blossom In The Spring is also quite Genesis like, but the song is more melodic and the singing is more fragile and I like this better. Dawn is a rather typical symphonic instrumental, with a strong theme and some great drumming. As earlier I'm reminded of Collage here, again because of the guitar sound. The keyboards are quite different however. Simple Things is again a rather short, but melodic piece. If you like the softer pieces of the Genesis, this is one for you. The piano is playful, bu also quite beautiful. After the slightly classical Snail we come to Around And Around. The singing reminds me in a way of The Flower Kings (not the harmonies though). I'm not particularly fond of the vocal parts. They sound a bit messy. Personally I like the fragile vocals of the guitarist. The instrumental intermezzo is quite nice however, with some good drumming. After some flute playing the song has a bouncy free-form ending. Dessert Rainbow is a slightly classical interlude and the shortest track on the album. It sounds quite merry. Cotton Dreams features Hernandez back on vocals. His vocals are rather wavery and dreamy. I tend to like these tracks more than the shorter vocals ones. Imaginary Window is a long excursion into symphonic progressive with a prominent place for Vidales: lots of different (and interesting) melodies, lots of mood changes and lots of pace changes in this tour-de-force. The closer is Where's The Light? (Switch On The Wall). This song is sung by Brassea (I guess, since he also wrote the lyrics.) and here I like his singing better. A majestic ending to this CD.

Conclusion

The shorter, more direct vocal songs lack a bit in melody and I'm also generally not fond of the vocals there. I do like to hear Hernandez singing with his fragile wavery voice, although Brassea is quite at home in the closer of the album. There's plenty of room for energetic and melodic symphonic progressive with worthwhile melodies in the other tracks and the instrumentals are good as well. It seems everybody is looking for this album here in the Netherlands, but at the time of writing there's still no distribution for it. As Vidales already pointed out to me the drummer, Brassea, is a very good one and I comply with his judgment. The music generally lies somewhere between Genesis and Collage, but not forgetting classical influences (in Vidales' play) and Amerindian folk influences. A mixed bag, very symphonic and hence something that most of you will like. Best songs are the last two.
