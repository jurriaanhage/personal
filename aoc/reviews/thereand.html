Summary

Well, they had some albums out, they did two or SI and now they have landed themselves with Cyclops. The vocals take getting used to and they do tend to become annoying after a while; it's all so distant and, well, indifferently sung. The material on those albums has an air of sameness to it, but nobody can deny that Lives and Times has their own sound. The most worthwhile tracks were the filmic, atmospheric instrumentals.

The music

And more of the same on this album I guess. The tracks are not very memorable, but distinctly Lives and Times. Their are some weak tracks on it, like 4, but also some good ones like the scary, dissonant Darker, the slow moving Lizard Baby and the long This Year's Drift. The other tracks are like I said not memorable enough to warrant mention.

This Year's Drift is another of those long instrumentals. The previous album has one and it as the best track on the album. It turns out that this might be a trend. A lot of sinister piano and keyboard stuff, slowly evolving.

About the artwork: it's very, very ugly.

Conclusion

I'm getting to wonder if anyone can differentiate between the many tracks Lives and Times have unloosed upon this world. I'm afraid that this album will get little attention from the public, any public. They have their own style, but not something that is good in the market. To compete with the likes of Cocteau Twins and Dead Can Dance they still have an awful long way to go, with their progression from album to album in mind. Also, some people might spot a few of the dark undertones of a band like Univers Zero.
