Summary

It takes a little computation and in fact I'm still not sure whether Fearless is as exactly that long, but it should good enough. These Danish guys sounded promising on the Moonchild compilation disc, so let's hear if they have anything else to say.

The music

The album opens with gunshots like a western. The first song Paddling as it is called is an extremely fast paced track, but with slow doubled vocals. The drumming is quite furious. The instrumental part features acoustic guitar. This Song is also quite up-tempo, but not as much as the opener, and features sax. The guitar work is somewhat reminiscent of later King Crimson and the opening sounds rather chaotic. This contrasts with the rahter quiet vocal part, but very quickly we come back to the more involved instrumental part. Cycle Of Events opens by building tension. After a tension breaking interlude the music continues. If I should have to characterize the music up to now, then it would be one of rolling drums and overall a strong percussion presence, while the keyboards are usually in the form of droning organs. The guitar sound is varied, but generally on the heavy side. The vocals are a bit on the rough side and I certainly like them. Dinner At Uncle Cool's is a two parted track. It opens with a heavy part called Music, in which again I taste some kind of American Western influence. The second part is mostly atmospheric with a recipe rather softly sung and followed by some rather quiet guitar work. The noise returns in Kings And Court Jesters. Again I'm reminded of King Crimson, because of the guitar sound partly. The music is a lot noisier though. Half of this disc is taken up by the title track divided in eight parts. Again, here, that powerful organ driven sounds, combined with the abrupt changes, which generally indicate a vocal part. The second part takes us to an even higher level of energy with the guitar dominating. The singer shows in this song that he can sing really well. In the keyboard ridden part V expectation is build up and the song comes to a climax quite soon already in the track. The song then takes a long step back and we are left with what seems a softly playing musical box. The vocals start the tension building in part VI, while the closing part VIII is rather sad yet ends on the noisy note. The closer of the album is Santa Cruz and not the best way to end this album, I would think since it is by far the weakest on it. On the other hand it is probably meant to be funny. I see them now, sitting on the back of a yacht, playing their acoustic guitars and singing along to the tune. All very relaxed.

Strange how I am also reminded of Pink Floyd: Fearless is a track of this band, although this Fearless has more the length of Echoes. Santa Cruz the closer does not really fit in well with the album and as such might remind some of St. Tropez. However, this probably is coincidence.

Conclusion

If you are not afraid of raunchy guitars and love the sound of the organ, this is the place to be, or more precisely, the CD to buy. Generally on the noisy side, but never fiddling around, these guys from Scandinavia deliver an album full of seventies oriented progressive, but with its feet also firmly in the nineties. The only reference I could make was to King Crimson, but actually the music should sooner be compared to Bigelf and Spocks Beard, and there is some kind of family ressemblance to Floyd, but generally Fig Leaf is noisier and more chaotic. A band I really would like to see live sometimes. Sooner the better.
