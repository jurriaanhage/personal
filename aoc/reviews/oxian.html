Summary

Barrock is a band from Italy and I once heard they were a theatrical group, that made music along the lines. After releasing a demo, they had a song (The Witch) on the Exposure 88 compilation (the third in the series) and lately they released L'Alchimista on a Japanese label.

The music

They have now released their first one for SI and again no neo-prog, because Barrock honours its name. They have written and played a conceptalbum (at least I think it is) containing a variety of compositions: a fugue, some spinet, but mostly somber and sinister keyboards like TDs Sorcerer or Goblin with some very classical interludes. There are vocals, but not much.

Instrumentally the album is well-played, although I think the drums are a bit thin, sounds like a drum computer. The vocals are good, though maybe not always to everybody's liking.

From the very start of the album it's evident that the approach is classical. It starts of with a fugue and strikes me as a Bach ripoff (mind you I'm no expert on Bach). The third track is quite melodic and accessible (meaning less progressive). The fourth song uses spinet sounds and I'm not too fond of it. The other shorter songs are low key and somber, which means that they have my approval if that's worth anything. From Il Vampiro on they start out to become a bit more bombastic and theatrical (just what you'd expect from Italian progressive).

You might draw parallels between Oxian and a project like Strangers on a Train, but Oxian is more classical and knows a larger variety in instrumentation. I also like the vocals a better. Also you might see a new Renaissance in them, though I must admit to grade Renaissance (the first album I mean) a little higher, but the style is comparable. Renaissance IMO has succeeded better in combining the classical with the progressive, and with Baroque, it's usually one or the other.

The Witch was just the one song and this is an entire album. At times they are a bit more polished now, but they can still have those intrigueing interludes. Still, you might say that they've only now revealed the baroque in Barrock. The vocals aren't heavy in use by the way, although there are two of them. Lyrics in both Italian and English have been included.

Conclusion

This is a worthwhile CD, even though I'm not overly fond of the fugue, Oltre i monti, Rosalidia and parts of Guerra!, the remainder leaves enough to be joyful about.

Afterthought: I wonder what went wrong with the backside of this album. It almost seems like they put the wrong colours behind the picture (like you can on a Commodore 64).
