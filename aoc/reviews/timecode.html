Summary

The last album I heard by them was Pure (so I missed out on Facing Gravity). I didn't like their performance at Planet Pul in 1997 very much, and I now I have to judge their newest brainchild.

The music

After I listened to it for the first time I didn't really pay attention to what song was playing and how long they were, and I was in fact very surprised when I found the album was this long.

As the CD starts playing, the record starts playing. After this small diversion we get quite a long intro with various melodies and riffs. Edens voice is the same as on previous albums, very typically accented. The verses are rather quiet with soft guitars, soft drumming and the vocals in the focus. The chorus is more up-tempo. The last part of the vocals are done by the soprano Birgit Gotzes. The drumming might have been a little more active. The subject of the lyric seems to be reconnaissance on other planets.

The title track starts out with quite a few breaks, but when we come to a more melodic part with electric guitar. The vocal parts are of course melodic as well. The melodies are nice, but in some strange way the song doesn't really get going and some of the tempo changes feel unnatural to me, leading nowhere. Again, I'm not too fond of the drum sound.

Half Empty Half Fool is quite a nice track with attractibve guitarwork. In fact the guitarwork on this album is the most enjoyable part, besides the melodies, which are also quite nice. The song contains a nice introspective part with pleasant singing and the following guitar solo is also quite okay.

Child of Hope reminds me a little of the somewhat unadventurous songs of Peter Gabriel and it can also be compared to the band November that released their only album on SI Music.

Living in the Human Race is a varied track with interesting lyrics "As she knees upon my face" and later "And started eating chicken wings". Again, some of the parts do not fit together very naturally.

I'm not too fond of Ferengi Lover, most notably the chorus. The guitarwork can be compared to that of Saga. The next one Have a Break starts out with quite a lot of violent and active playing. The vocal parts are slow and dreamy with flowing bass work. Later the track becomes more hectic with guitar solo's and an "Arabic" melody. After a more quiet intermezzo, the song starts to build up again, hauntingly and restlessly, but unfortunately the guitar takes over here crushing all good intentions.

The closer Mountain High is the longest track. Eden has a rougher voice here (but not all the time). The song is a little dreamy and slow at first, and in fact nothing much happens in the track.

I do think I have more or less broken the code of the tracks. They stand for units of time (Timecode, see?). The codes more or less approximate time, the square being approx. 3.10 minutes, the circle about 1 minute and the triangle about half a minute, but Expedition is a little off then (but it is also quite strange it has the same code as When the Night Begins).

Conclusion

Typical guitar-dominated neo-progressive (Saga,..) with quite long tracks and accented vocals. The melodies are okay, but on the whole the music isn't what you might call captivating or anything, just nice to listen to. There's room for rather complicated intermezzo, intro's and quite a number of breaks, but on the whole they sound artificial to me. Also the music is not very inspiring or energetic.
