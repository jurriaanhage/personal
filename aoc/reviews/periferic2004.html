Summary

The yearly Periferic sampler, this one from 2004. Some of these songs have already been reviewed on these pages, so I will try not to spend too much time on these.

The music

Erdész Róbert opens with Welcome To My Brain. After a vocal introduction we are in Electronic Music territory. This is rather up-beat, dance-like material with some vocoded singing as well. The melodies are fine, and the music is played versatile enough. The ending is organ mainly and the overall feel is quite groovy. The album for this song is still to be released.

We continue the Solaris influence with Kollar's Musical Witchcraft. The song Utazás Az Andokba has quite a bit of flute, violin and percussion building a song with a mellow Latin American feel. A bit too mellow for my tastes. This one was previously unreleased by the way.

Solaris' Noab comes from the forthcoming second archive disc. It is a relatively short and symphonic live track with guitar that reminds of Camel and brassy keyboards.

Fédra - Vudu is again a track from a forthcoming album. I am not familiar with the band, which seems to operate in Ozrics like territory, but thus far in a rather relaxed mode. The organ is quite dominant in places. Halfway the guitar starts to crank up the power a bit. The music is quite tuneful, but the drumming is a bit too monotonous for my tastes.

D Sound's Feny is not a very strong track. A bit too catchy with both female and male vocals. This is not how I remember the 'band' from their previous album on Periferic. That was at least more proggy.

Korai Öröm is the Hungarian answer to the Ozrics (OT are quite a popular band in Hungary). Their effort is called Balkan Beach. Washing waters, didgeridoo like elements, whispy keys and snare drum fill this rather atmospheric opening. The percussion is low and slow. Very relaxed material, no blister.

Mindflowers' Ego is one from the forthcoming album as well. It features plenty of all-out organ playing and some rowdy guitar work. The tone is very much jazz rock with an improvised feel, and like I said plenty of organ.

Blues For Gabor is indeed very much a blues track by Kada. It is also one of their most safe tracks, and I get the impression thus far that safe is the keyword on this collection.

Vedres Csaba's Mes&eacuite;k I is a nice short vignette of piano playing. The tone is slightly jazzy, and melodically this is excellently done. Is Riz Levente really somebody else? Oh boldog Az reminds me of a mix of Vedres and After Crying. The music is light and melodic, especially melodically. The song does have some vocals, there are not many of those on this album. The vocals are in Hungarian and fit well with the music. A mellow track, but beautiful as well. Halfway, the violin sets in and the pace goes up a bit. We never reviewed this one, but this is really promising. They really know how to build up a song.

Fugato's Etnoid has the light classical feel of the band, so it shows off the band as they are. Fugato is known for their excellent melodic work, and classical sound. They may be compared to Rumblin Orchestra, but the latter is more proggy, owing to a Triumvirat likeness. Fugato is more in the vein of the classical side of Alan Parsons Project, with the melodicity of the folk. Some really fast piano here too.

Secret Service is the closing epic by After Crying, also the oldest song on the album. Not my favourite track of the album (that's the opener New World Order). The vocals remind a bit of John Wetton and overall the sound is quite melancholy and modern, and has a few citations of King Crimson and Ravel's Bolero worked into it. Only at the very end does the music get a bit louder, with a big band like outburst. A grand finale, almost in the vein of UK's Carrying No Cross. Very good.

Conclusion

Many of the new songs here do not do much for me, it all sounds so safe, and only little has any staying power. Notable exceptions are the up-beat EM opener, and the older tracks 9 to 12. Riz Levente was to me a very positive surprise, I had never heard them/him before. In view of the fact that this album is likely to come cheap, consider trying it out to get a view of what's happening in Hungary these days, especially if you are not (even) familiar with After Crying for instance.
