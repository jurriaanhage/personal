Twin Age - Month Of The Year

Artist: Twin Age
Title: Month Of The Year
Label: Altair TA9601
Length(s): 40 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1996
Month of review: 01/1997

Line up

Johan Hansson - vocals
Carl Johan Kilborn - keyboards
Joergen Hanson - drums
John Loewenadler - guitars
Petter Petterson - bass

Tracks

1) In The Well 8.00
2) The Majority 4.51
3) Newgate 8.25
4) Showpiece In Her Showroom 5.16
5) Emily Dawn 5.34
6) Marble Island 8.14

Summary

Well, not much to tell, except that someone not into progressive asked me about them sometime ago. The Scandanavian bands are doing good the last few years and now it's up to Twin Age from Sweden to make their debut here.

The music

In The Well starts out very much in the Genesis vein, with percolative acoustic guitar and a good vocal melody, that contains a lot of subtleties. In the next part the vocal melody is somewhat different and has a definite Kayak ring to it, but the break into a Genesis seventies keyboard solo and some IQ guitar make the song take a different turn. The vocals remind me a lot of the singer of Ark (Jon Jowitt's old band) and maybe a little of It Bites. I like the voice, very sympathetic.

The Majority is sung with a high voice mostly starting out with piano and again has a Kayak note to it. The composition is without a doubt a great one, has been put together with great care and everything fits, It is also a little strange one, especially the beginning which has some kind of Britpop ring to it. Tantalizing.

Newgate starts out with lots of guitar. After a slow vocal part with guitar the mellotron/acoustic guitar atmosphere is typically Genesis. The music is rather moody and progresses nicely through various melodies.

Showpiece in her Showrooms begins like an It Bites/Ark song and on the whole is more or less in that vein. Signature changes throughout and the occaisonal mellotron keep the interest lively and especially the change into the pianic part is very welcome and also the IQ guitar.

Emily Dawn contains quick keyboards, that remind me of something, building up a tension. It is somewhat more energetic than the previous songs.

The beginning of Marble Island sounds like Genesis, Lovers Leap of Suppers Ready to be precise. It has a slow build-up and has double vocals, while the guitar take over in the middle. It has some weird signatures right there and I wonder everytime whether everythings ends up right. It ends like it started.

Conclusion

With their feet very much in the seventies, mostly Genesis and Kayak are evoked but closer to home I can mention the vocals of the singer in the Ark/It Bites style and the guitar sound reminds me a little of IQ as does the atmosphere of the music. The music is moody and the tempo of the songs is mostly low. The singer does his job well and the music has feeling, because of the way the melodies are stringed together. This is an album that a lot of people can enjoy and God knows what this band might amount to when they shake their roots of a little and keep the sameness from creeping into the music. Best song is The Majority.
