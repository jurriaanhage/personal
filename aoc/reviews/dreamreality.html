Summary

Gaze by Cross on Cyclops was his/their fourth album in a very new line-up. The first were called Uncovered Heart, Second Movement and III - Poison into Medicine respectively and each song on this CD comes from one of these albums although it might be remastered, remixed or even rerecorded with the current line-up of Gaze.

The music

After the nice intro Changing, we get the first epic, Fire. This song is in fact a rather straightforward song with a rather bad chorus. The vocals of Gaze are still husky and nasal and I'm afraid it's not a voice everybody will like (compare with Kravitz but less balls). Within Fire we get a rather noticeable break and it seems a new song has started. It's kind of slow and as always the guitar figures prominently. Later we get a more ecstatic part with busy drumming, rough guitar work and keyboard solo's. Some of the melodies are not bad, but as a whole this track is more like a sequence of parts than a whole.

The Armoury Show is a melodic mid-tempo rockballad with soaring guitars and a delicate piano melody and a nice keyboard melody as well. Still the composition lacks coherence and the vocal parts are boring and even tend to irritate after a while.

Later songs can be ballads, up-tempo or whatever, but I still don't like the vocals. It sounds like Cross overdoes it or something, but it CAN sound good in either the more mysterious passages like Courage or when he changes the tone such as in the beginning of Run For Rescue. Courage by the way starts off quite well, but due to its length and the poverty of ideas sinks away in a morass. It turns out that for the most part, the melodies are nice at times, but the compositions lack strength.

Exceptions to the general rule are the nice instrumental Fanfare Song (and Changing is also quite nice) and the closer Yearning, which are both rather short tracks. The first of these is built on a few good melodies and approaches the orchestral Enid, while the second is, although not as good, quite similar to Pendragon's Please and consists of one guitar solo. Nicely done.

Conclusion

Neo-progressive that is very melodic, some heavy guitarwork and rather dreary vocals. The compositions are not to write home about although the melodies can be nice. For me the vocals and the way the songs are built stand in the way of me liking this album. Too few ideas.
