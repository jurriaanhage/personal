Summary

Matt Guillory and Matthew Bradley you might know from Age Of Impact, (by far) the worst of the Magna Carta projects IMO. Still, I remember the vocalist was responsible for one of the two good songs on that album, Fading Fast.

The music

Within A Stare is the opening track. Rather typical power-prog-metal one might say, but not bad. Plenty of variation and, typical for American bands, lots of harmonies. The chorus is especially nice with a good vocal melody and washes of cymbals. Very bombastic and in the Dream Theater style, but also with some darker Arabic stylings and some nice additions such as the quick acoustic guitar (or nylon string?). The vocals are of the muscled kind, not a striking voice. Miracles In Yesteryear opens loudly with guitar, but the band takes gas back for the verses. The chorus involves multiple voices and as such is certainly a lot quieter. In the instrumental intermezzo the band tries to showcase their ability one by one. Despite The Waves opens a lot more accessible and melodic (almost neo-proggish), and although they tend to fiddle around also on this track it is certainly the most tight of the compositions so far. One might be reminded of Enchant here (and at other times as well), although the vocals of course sound different. A nice track, up-tempo track. Whispers is a short instrumental (keyboards only) introduced for variation. It is a rather moody and flowing track with a very nice melody. Ashen Days brings us more of the first two tracks. The vocal melody is not very memorable and I find this song rather boring. Andromeda Sunrise is like like Whispers, but now its the turn of the guitar. I like the keyboards better. This Time Around is a pressing track with a rather melodramatic chorus. Hills Of Memory reminds me at first a bit of Landmarq's Forever Young, but this mid-tempo track turns out to be rather different (I wouldn't have expected otherwise). This is certainly the quietest vocal track. It is also a bit disjointed especially with this Forever Young reminder setting in every once in a while. Can't You See is the penultimate track in which the powerful opening belies the following hazy vocal part. The rhythm guitar sets in during the vocals parts and the vocals are partly vocoded and it includes some ELPish excursions on the keyboards as well. The closer Living In Fear is also the longest track on the album, hence the song can take its time getting in gear. The keyboards function (mostly) as strings (as they often do on this album) and I would have hoped they could have expanded more on the solo keyboard work (like the short piano passages) hereby introducing more melody and variation into the music. There are some colourless passages, but towards the end some emotion creeps into the vocal melodies.

Conclusion

Not bad, but not good either. I find this album mostly lacking in the melodic department. There is a balance and not too much fiddling, but the compositions often sound a bit too artificial and well they lack identity. Comparisons can be made to Enchant (a band where oddly enough the compositions are the strongest point), but mostly Dream Theater.
