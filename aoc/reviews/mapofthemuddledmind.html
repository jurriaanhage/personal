Summary

B.C. is just Brian Calhoun, who performs all the duties.

The music

Conception as the intro, and includes some interesting sounds. If I ever thought that single man bands may lack in groove or power, then I stand corrected here. The Grim Road Of Banality is a hard hitting tune with electrifying guitar and a somewhat Enchant like chorus. Like Enchant's the music of B.C. is strongly song oriented. Calhoun does have a more varied vocal approach, showing some balls at the end of the song.

Imagination continues the relatively accessible style of progrock. This style is what I would call typically American. I can imagine Calhoun being a fan himself of bands such as King's X and Rush, with elements of AOR. The variety sits again between the rather roughly rocking verses and the softer spoken chorus, in which harmony vocals are applied.

After the mid tempo The Dead Show, we came to the bass dominated opening of Chaos Before The Storm. The bass rumbles nicely, and are accompanied by some ethereal guitar play and Calhoun's vocals. The drums sound a bit automatic here. It may just be me, but the music is also a bit more ordinary here, as in straightforward rock music.

The Worm is an angry track in which Calhoun combines rather soft voiced verses with an aggressively sung chorus, speaking for the Worm I guess. This is a nice track again after a few less interesting ones. The riffing is not straightforward and the drums roll along lending a bit of extra power and flow.

We are approximately halfway when we encounter Manic Tranquility. It opens with atmospheric strummings and effects. The vocals lie somewhere in the back, a bit whisperingly. This is indeed a good beginning. Then the vocals turn more muscled and emotional. Past halfwya, the music is more atmospheric again with some melodic vocals in the back, and some vibrant guitar work. Very good. Then the stop-and-start riffing returns.

One Way Out opens percussively, and with the rhythm guitar grinding 'em out. The songs hold a bit more challenge now, and it also seems Calhoun is getting angrier and angrier. This track is close to if not smack in the middle of progmetal. Surprisingly heavy this one.

In Time is in stark contrast. Easy going, warm acoustic guitar jangles, and Calhoun sings in minor mode. Then we step the power up a bit, bringing in a somewhat psychedelic feel. The music is very slow by the way, as are the sad vocals. Although there are some powerful passages, the music stays slow throughout.

I Can Live Again has a strong bass presence again, and is rather groovy. The drums seem programmed again, similar to Chaos Before The Storm. The middle part of this song has strong bass play and reminds strongly of the Discipline era King Crimson. Surprising. Choices is a straightforward, plodding rock track, with a memorable chorus. The song also has some vocoding for variety, and a nice strumming acoustic passage in the middle. The Voices on the other hand, is an a-cappela song which works very well with two or three vocals going at once.

Conclusion

This one man band is situated somewhere on the crossing lines of progmetal, progrock (Rush, Enchant) and alternative rock (of the type liked by progrockers, such as King's X). I cannot tell from the playing that this is indeed a single man band: it sounds full and visits the entire spectrum from subtle to aggressive and back. The music is songoriented, and the main instrument is the guitar in all its forms. The vocals are varied too, which is a good thing. An admirable effort, even if a few of the songs failed to interest me.
