Tsukinoumi - Sivle Redyc Chowder

Artist: Tsukinoumi
Title: Sivle Redyc Chowder
Label: V!tal/Poseidon VR-012
Length(s): 31 minutes
Year(s) of release: 2004
Month of review: [04/2005]

Line up

Ando Masahide - guitar, bass, vocal
Takahashi Susumi - drums
Tagawa Kaoru - percussion
Yamamoto Takao - 12 string guitar, bass, vocal
Wakita Yamao - sax

Tracks

1) Black In Purple 7.29
2) Sounds Strange 14.31
3) Black Of Night 9.01

Summary

This is my first encounter, being a live mini cd. The band is from Japan.

The music

Black In Purple is a relaxed opener which shows the bands penchant for jazz. The music is not pure jazz certainly. There is plenty of attention to atmospherics, and most of the sounds are drawn out long and lazily. In that sense, there are links to Soft Machine in their earlier years, but also, with the addition of saxophone, to more current bands led by people like Hugh Hopper. There is plenty of flow and groove with a smattering of psychedelica, making this is a succesful opener. The sound quality is not great though, at least not what we have come to expect. Sounds Strange is twice as long, opening with tinkling percussives. Not something that can satisfy my ears for long, since there seems to be rather little structure in there. After three minutes of this some kind of tribal percussion plus vocals set in. This is not a good idea. Only halfway does any of the melodic instruments set in, building a long extended jam based on the tribal percussion, electric guitar and harmonica. Seems we are back in the seventies here with a free form blues band trying their luck at an open air festival. After the ten minute mark the music drops out entirely and we are in for some atmospherics: low key hi-hats, occasional notes on the guitar. The saxophone meanders till we move into the next one, Black Of Night, which is the conclusion. The percussiveness of the previous track stays, but this time we have all participants seemingly playing against instead of with each other. Okay, the drummer sets the basis alright, but the rest... This song gets to be rather loud and hectic along the way, building for a crescendo finale. The finale does not come at the end though. We get plaintive sax, followed by a new build-up.

Conclusion

Too bad about the second track on this one. The sound quality is not that great, but acceptable for a live obscurity. At least the music comes out plainly audible. The first track is a good one, but the second one does not have much to offer in listening pleasure, and it is about half the total length. I hope a studio offering from them offers more of their psyche/jazz in which the flow and groove of the first song are preserved.
