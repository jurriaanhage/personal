Summary

Essentially a duo from the States, Edwards is an experienced singer/performer and Earls a producer who played in a gothic, a synthi pop and a psychedelic band, hope to make an impression with their first effort.

The music

The first opens with cheesy pop with Yanni like keyboard sounds. The vocal part especially does not promise much for what is too come. Too melodramatic and slow for me. Black And Blue makes a much better impression. It is a darker track with the male vocals having the lead and female vocals in the back. All in all it has an early gothic/New Wave feel (Sad, Lovers & Giants and such). All in all a bit on the slow side and maybe a bit too "programmed", but a step forward from the opener.

Tasteful keys, slow percussion and guitar feature on The Way Inside which like the previous track is a dark one. At some point we move into an easy going part with nice atmospheric pasages, but on the whole a bit thin sounding. In this respect there are parallels with New Wave.

Edwards dominates on the ballad Esther. Compared to the opener a much more likable track in which she ressembles Edie Brickell. Then we move back into gothic with a somewhat more rougher approach. On the whole this one, the title track sounds a bit amateurish, and the somewhat harsh vocals are not that strong either.

After a calm atmospheric intro, Down To The Bone becomes more poppy because of the A-I-A-I-A-IJ-A chant by Edwards. In addition there are also darker vocals on this melodious track.

Another rather poppy tune is Dream Love, but with dark effects on guitar. The female vocals are doubled here: both spoken and sung. She's In Parties is a Bauhaus cover, a band with which This Midnight Stream can be compared for many of their songs. One may think of Depeche Mode here as well with somber low vocals and overall a percussive sound. The bonus track is a remix of the title track now sounding very much like a house track.

Conclusion

All in all, this is not an album I can recommend. First of all, the style is not progressive at all, although some proggers might like it. The music is a combination of gothic and New Wave with a few rather ordinary songs thrown in. These are mostly the songs on which Carole Edwards sings, while Earls tends to keep himself to the darker tunes. In the bio the band says their style has something of Dark Wave and that sounds about right. However, the music does fall a bit short production wise, in clarity, and in fullness of sound. The songs themselves are not bad (although I really didn't like the first track) but lack a definite face and memorableness.
