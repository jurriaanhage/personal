Artist: Wolverine
Title: Fervent Dream
Label: Zizania ZEGO799

Summary

This is a newly recorded minialbum by the Swedish band Wolverine. They made a very favourable impression at the ProgPower 99 festival in Tilburg. It remains to be seen whether this favourable impression extends to the recorded material. Although it says so on the CD, the CD does not contain a webpage for the band. This is according to the booklet a production error on the part of the cd presser. They hope the next pressing will contain it.

The music

The album opens with the didgeridoo (by Kevin Sumner) of Whispers On The Wind. This is a rather slow track in which both the grunt of the drummer as well as the progmetal vocals of Zell and Landegren. Zell had a good voice. Clear, not too high. Of course, some people might be put off by the death vocals, but they are more the punctuation to the lyrics than anything else. The vocal lines are very accessible. Plenty of variation in this song, but not variation for variations sake. Dream Theater is the first name that comes to mind, although Wolverine is a bit heavier. Halfway the song seem to win in pace, but the band still doesn't push right on through choosing to take a bit of gas back into an atmospheric part with a very nice guitar solo. Majestic progmetal. The ending is a bit too artificial. Echoes opens with a string quartet and acoustic guitar with some nice low drumming. The electric guitar bridges the distance to the vocal part. The vocal part is a quiet, naked one and therefore the strength of it is both determined by the quality of both the voice and the vocal melody. The former is good, the latter does not distinguish itself well enough. Then the music takes a turn for the majestic with powerful chords, and the grunt vocals enter the stage again. During the next vocal part the music starts from scratch again winning in strength until we arrive at the instrumental intermezzo with hammered percussion and fast rhythm guitars. The drummer grunts in accompaniment of his own double bass drum. The song ends on melodic note. More Than Grief starts off at a fair pace. Plenty of signature changes before we finally arrive at the vocal part. The phrasing of the singer is quite strange. Again, the music has a very full sound, maybe even overfull: noisy guitars in the back, a piano loop, the vocalist, and of course the standard things: bass, guitar and such. This makes the album a rather tiring, but also eventful experience. During some nice flowing drum work, the tension builds up by virtue of the two guitars. The guitar works itself into a high pitched frenzy and keyboards lend urgency to the music. Again seems at first a ballad, but one realizes quite soon that this is not the case when the numerous guitar set in yielding a complex and full sound spectrum. A very melodic song even in the faster parts with goosebumps during one of the guitar solo's (I remembered that part of the live version where they extended it a bit). The album ends with the short Last Words, an acoustic peaceful piece.

The biography of the band asks for a judgement of the artwork which was made by Mattias Noren from Sweden. I like it a lot. However, because of its darkness the jewel cases tends to reflect too much and you have to really look to see what band this is. At a glance this is not obvious. So for the next time it may be wise to make a digipack or something or maybe a less reflective case.

Conclusion

Great songs on this mini album make for an interesting experience. The contrast between the grunt and the ordinary vocals, the strong guitar work, the use of keyboards and strings, but mostly the energetic and varied compositions and good playing make this a good addition to any progmetal lovers' collection. The presence of a grunt vocalist may be a point not in the bands favour to some, but the vocals are dominated by the progmetal vocals of Zell. The atmospheres evoked are often quite epic and majestic with powerful chords, varied and flowing drum work and occasional melodic additions on keyboard. The only negative point I could find is that the band might use the sound spectrum a bit more sparingly and leave things out once in a while. To me now they seem like young dogs trying to do do everything at once.
