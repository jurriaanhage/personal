The music

The opening Prelude is a cinematic piece, melodic and light. It doesn't stay this way, because soon the rhythm guitars break loose. The arpeggio's of the electric guitar go in the direction of Malmsteen. Amulet Of The Sun continues the music in rather the same way: choral keyobard sound, quick keyboards passages and hammering drums. The rhythm guitar lay down a heavy layer. The melodic sounds a bit Arabic. The vocals are a bit on the rough side and sound a bit faraway. The vocal melodies remind me a bit of Malmsteen again (something akin to You Don't Remember I'll Never Forget). The music is quite driven with plenty of melody because of the keyboards. The Malmsteen reference is strengthened by the medieval sounding keyboards that sometimes prop up. This also holds for the classical sounding intro to the follow-up Wyrm. The music is however more complex and interesting than that of Malmsteen even in his earlier days. However, the likeness stays. The song does have a great chorus. After this terrific track we come to a ballad, Endless Cosmos. The music in this track is definitely going in the Dream Theater direction. The music is melodic, but also has a varied intermezzo and has a mysterious air to it. Shadows Fall is an organ ridden piece, not so fast and not so loud, but featuring the necessary rhythm guitars. Rather typical progmetal with an epic chorus and a great keyboard intermezzo. Powerful with resounding vocals, that have a kind of sadness in them. Lost In The Void is again a good composition, with an outstanding and memorable vocal melody. Essentially this band does little to extend the genre of progmetal, but they certainly know how to write a song and think up melodies. Again the clavecimbel like keyboards remind of Malmsteen. The song is not very heavy or fast. With Sad Clown Of Europe we come back to classically influenced pieces. The guitar melody is quite appealling though and it's not just floods of notes here. The Slow Poisoners has a rather surprising opening: angelic vocals on a backdrop of classical clavecmbel like keyboards. Then of course hell has to break loose and it does: the pace is quite high and the music is driven with rhythm guitar, vocals and keyboards as the prominent parts. The complex, somewhat pompously sung, chorus is attended by prominent hammering percussion. Afterwards, the guitar is allowed its solo. On Nemesis, the music becomes quite accessible, melody wise. The music here reminds a bit of Royal Hunt on their best days. In the same style we continue with Darkness Planet Earth which is followed by, the for me unpronouncable Guenhwyvar. This track opens with a groovy rock riff with a prominent position for the bass player. A plodding piece. Emire Dolls is another of those track that comes to live because of the strong vocal melody in the chorus. It is quite a heavy piece with rough vocals and a somewhat Arabic sounding melody. The Finale closes the album with its slow epic melodic guitar work.

Conclusion

A strong progmetal album. Good compositions, heavy but still melodic pieces and a singer who does not go out of his way to outshine the musicians making for a coherent whole. The style is rather typical for progmetal, but in the melodies, keyboards and guitar playing there's a strong nod to Yngwie Malmsteen and baroque music, and because of this also a bit to newer bands such as Rhapsody. However, the music of Eniac Requiem is definitely less speedy and over the top than the latter. All in all it is quite accessible, but not too.
