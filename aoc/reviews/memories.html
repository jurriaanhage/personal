Summary

Despite the fact that the bio is in French, I'm pretty sure it only tells this is a combination of electrical and acoustic guitars, and that they really like it. I presume Loo is French, but don't even know that for sure.

The music

Over The Hill is a friendly opener. A keyboard melody with guitar melody breaking out across it, somewhat remeniscent of some Yanni material. Pretty nice, but somewhat glib.

Spanish Highway starts with a slow build up in volume. Strong rhythm, with a repeated twangy guitar lick, a keyboard tapestry in the back and some lone pianos or Spanish guitars put in the light. During the break the rhythm disappears and the tempo goes down a bit, to return to the original form.

I haven't the vaguest how Old Man got its name. This is a melodic guitar and keys melody that sounds more sunny and young than it does old, well accept for the fact that it's not all that original.

The title track Memories starts a bit more serious than the previous tracks, a bit slower, less frivolous, building up to a drum band climax. Kind of okay, but still polished well enough, to be gobbled down easily.

Napoleon Way starts with choppy keyboards, sort of electronic pizzicato, with ditto violins. This all leads us to a somewhat childish melody with the usual instrumental heroics. Not at all to my liking, this. Its length tests my endurance.

Last Dream is a keyboard melody with some drums that sound even more out of a box than they are.

The sweetening continues, with Alone With You going for the teeth. As the guitar comes up, the sweetness relinquishes somewhat, but still.

Midnight Sun starts with some perky guitaring, to deteriorate into some outright happy whatevering.

Paradise Song is based on sampled voices. This makes for a nice change, especially after all the sweetness presented in the previous tracks. Besides, the melody is nice enough to hold up on its own, without being a reprieve.

Memories Part 2 is tranquil piano track. Briefer than one might have hoped. Nice one.

Angela features acoustic guitars, and lots of electriconics. This track isn't too offence, but it doesn't exactly enrich the overall structure either. As the track progresses, the guitaristics seem to electrify.

Blue Light I guess is a closer that gives a nice average of the album: some guitar of various kinds, flat sounding drumming, and keyboards.

Conclusion

The bio gives Jean Pascal Boffo as a reference for this album, with which I'd have to agree. Unfortunately, I don't like Boffo's music at all (I do like the artwork of one of his albums, though). Loo's album doesn't offend me the way Boffo's do, but still, there is a certain gayness, sweetness and simplicity about too much of Alan's music I can't particularly appreciate. Nice tracks (Memories, Memories part 2, Paradise Song) are exceptions.

Alan's memories turn out pretty consistent. Maybe his was a peaceful (to the point of boring) life, filled with sunny beaches and tanned people, so far. At least, that is the atmosphere his album breathes. But you never know, this album might propel him into sudden stardom. I have my doubts about that, though.
