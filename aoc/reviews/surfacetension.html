Summary

Started in 1994 from the ashes a Philadelphia they recorded two demo's and an EP earning them a contract with Sensory. Sean Malone helps out on their first full album.

The music

The opening track starts out with a slightly classical opening. Some nice low sounds in this opening as well. Then the band leaves the signature of its style: heavy guitars and quite a complex structure. In the vocal parts the piano returns, the vocals are somewhat on the high side, but not of the shrill kind. The guitar solo is a nice melodic one. Plenty of variation in just one track with some nice features such as church bells. If you are in need of a reference I would say Enchant (I'm saying that often lately), because of the doubled vocals maybe. This band is less composition directed as Enchant though. One Wing is rather different. The vocals are almost standalone here and are articulated in a rather special, and I have to admit, interesting way. A rather disjointed, or should I say many-faced, sounding track in a way, and I do not mean this in a bad way right now. On East Of Knowing, an instrumental, Sean Malone is present to give us a Grand Stick solo. The East if definitely present in this Arabic sounding track, with some hints of Flamenco, including a Spanish guitar. I probably would have enjoyed a little more speed or fire, but on the other hand it might as well function as a resting point. This latter remark fits well in with the beginning of If These Walls Could Talk. This is a rather melodic track after its rather heavy opening. Quite some folk influences and as often some rather surprising breaks and interjections (Zappaesque rock, some Malmsteenish guitarwork). One can admire them for putting such diverse things into their songs, but the songs themselves are less compact for it, so if you are after this, this might not be for you. I always like hammered piano playing, using it as percussion and this is how The Guardin' OF Eden opens. Although the music is melodic there is something quircky about it especially in some of the vocal parts. The chorus is a quite striking. I get the impression that the lyrics can be read in at least more than one way. The Convolution Box is an instrumental and with eight minutes of quite respectable length. In this track Latin/Spanish influences are combined with a strong rhythm guitar presence. Reminds me a bit of that the way Spocks Beard did the "I drink milk with Tabasco" in The Light. Smile Under Sad Eyes is one of the more "usual" songs on this album. Vocal harmonies form the focal point of this love song. I'm not that happy with this rather commercial song. The closer is the almost thirteen minute track Design Of Enlightenment. After a strong, slightly military sounding intro, the guitar burst loose. The vocal part however brings us back to quieter waters. The lyrics are not really sung at first, more like recited. The song becomes a rather melodic one, not as full of breaks as one might expect.

Conclusion

A rather diverse piece of work. Not everything is to my liking and since I'm the kind of guy who prefers his songs with a definite "this is what we are and that is where we are going, doesn't it feel natural?" (I tend to like it in a band like Spocks Beard however) I sometimes have trouble following these guys. I'm still in doubt about the vocals: sometimes I like them, sometimes they sound a thin. Potentially however I like this type of vocals and I think the problem will disappear as of itself. On the whole definitely an interesting new face and maybe even a breath of fresh air on the progmetal scene. The band has taken the liberty to put as many influences into their songs as possible, but because of this the songs can sound rather fragmented (which naturally makes them harder to get into, less catchy if you please) and lack a definite groove.
