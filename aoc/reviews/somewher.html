Summary

Formerly released on SI was Serpents in Camouflage (I don't have it myself).

The music

A critics dream this band, because well...you might have guessed, they do sound a bit like Genesis. Funny thing is that I've screened the entire biography and the only time I was able to spot the name GENESIS, there were always at least two letters not in the same word. (Hmmm, trying to hid something are we?) Fact is that the old Genesis wanted to play together for some years, but due to problems between the managements of the various old members, they would never be able to pull it all off under their own names, so they invented a few people (and NO PICTURES in the booklet). Funny thing is that they do play live and I should have heard about the old Genesis playing live again. Maybe it's Yes' Union taken another step further.

The booklet starts with the lyrics to a song called Heretic (which I don't know, but maybe it was on the former album and forgot the lyrics).

The first song takes us back to the lawnmower song (Tom-ti-dam-ta-dam). Most of the album is musically and lyrically the Nursery Crime/Foxtrot era, with tons of lyrics and consequently, loads of Cyrus' voice, which some people might say approaches the voice of Peter Gabriel in that era quite good. They celebrate their heroes in five multi-parted songs, never underreaching the nine-minute mark. I might joke now that after playing covers for some years they gave the songs different names and put them on an album of their own, but that's not right, not even here. The thing is just that the style is so evidently Genesis that you seem to hear parts of the real Genesis songs, because it sounds so familiar, but I am not too sure that they have actually been stealing. The music has all the quirkiness of the Genesis in that time, both mucially and lyrically and as such it's quite charming to hear. Of course (why of course?) they can't approach the original, but I must admit that they do a good job, knowing how limited their playfield actually is. Every song has it's moments although the totality becomes a bit too much of the same. Best songs are the last two and I also like the short placentas a lot; they give a little flavour to the whole.

Conclusion

If you can't stomach the Genesis of today, either take a trip on their older albums or try Citizen Cain if you want to here something new, again. An album low originality but high on nostalgia.
