Mary Newsletter - Nuove Lettere

Artist: Mary Newsletter
Title: Nuove Lettere
Label: Spaziomusica
Length(s): 41 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1996
Month of review: 02/1997

Line up

Mario Bramè - drums, vocals
Massimiliano Galbani - vocals, guitars

Massimo Necchi - bass, flute
Davide Pisi - solo electric guitar

Tracks

Summary

Got this CD from the bass/flute player to review, and well here it is. Unfortunately the info in the booklet is all Italian, and although I was able to make something of it, I'm not sure how much that actually is, but in this way I hope to keep improving on my Italian. Because the lyrics are given all at once and not separately for each number it seems this is a concept album. Strange is however that the band does sing parts in English while almost all lyrics are in Italian.

The music

How many will there be by now that start with Introduzione? This track begins very quietly with piano, while later on acoustic guitar (and something sounding almost like banjo) and a flute involve themselves. The pressure is build up by repeatedly sounding a few heavy drums, but it does not yet break loose. The next time this is done, a fuzz guitar comes to the fore and now I notice that the mix of this album is not perfect. The drums sound rather flat, especially the higher frequencies and overall it still sounds a little rough. The melody on the piano is very nice and the bass is allowed its solo also in this track and on the whole it is rather pronounced. This continues on into Le Mie Mani, which sounds very much like the seventies, with a more or less psychedelic guitar sound. Part of the guitarwork is repetive while another is allowed free reign. Because of the repetition, tension builds up gradually which is released in a percussive part that also has singing. The singing is a bit high and not very steadfast. The music is now rather bouncy and not very interesting, but in the end the piano comes back to make up for that a little. The song ends rather abruptly.

La Danza incorporates has a bluesy atmosphere with a wailing guitar, but also some more folky influences can be heard. The next track starts a little chaotically (which is also nice for a change) and after a more structured, keyboard based part the singer recites the lyrics in the back of the mix (which is better), while the bass and the guitar are now in the fore. Something that comes to mind right here are the Alan Parsons Project from their first album. This does not go for the rest by the way. Again, I can't shake loose the (blues)rock feeling I get listening to this band although instruments like the violin give something more and of course, the tempo change is also something not often encountered in plain rock. The track has a nice, friendly and melodic folky ending.

Penultimate track Thanks Daddy!! brings yet another early seventies progressive rock sound, comparable with bands like Clear Blue Sky and other obscure rock outfits. The song is a little slow at the beginning, but picks up speed into mid-tempo later on. The track moves into the long track Thoughts, which is more or less based on the same ideas. A lot of change of tempo, but for the most part soaring and weeping psychedelic sounding guitars, played with feeling. The melodies are nice on this track, while the music on it sounds a little distorted. The vocal arrangements are rather involved, but they sound a little too chaotic. The rest of the track is really very nice with good melodies and on the whole the build up is quite good.

Conclusion

Very much in the seventies style this is an album by a band that can improve on a number of points: first of all the vocals are weak; they sound a little thin and too uncontrolled. The production could have been better, which is most easily heard in various parts of drumming. The sound is not just seventies, but also rather in the psychedelic style, especially with regards to the guitar. The piano fortunately also has an important role to play, because they give the music the flavour. Personally I would enjoy a more updated sound.
