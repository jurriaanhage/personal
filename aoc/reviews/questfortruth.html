Summary

Now one of the heads of the Moonchild label, Brian Hirsch recorded a solo album in 1994. Very little info in the "booklet" so we can pay more attention to the music. From the tracktitles and such I tend to conclude this is a concept album.

The music

The album opens with the title track. A rather peaceful opening which reminds me somewhat of Vangelis. Still, there are some passages that would be rather untypical for the Greek: the military drumming, the electric guitar and sometimes the keyboards are not so melodic. The central melody of this piece is quite good and strong enough to uphold it. Quite orchestral in parts, the only thing I don't feel good about is the percussion at times, which is a bit too tinny. Journey For The Highest Realm is, like the opening track, based on a strong melody, but is more direct and focused on tht melody. The Pearl Of Great Price is a pianic piece and a good one with the piano also playing the percussive part. Later on the synths become more pronounced lending the whole a more romantic feel. Attack Of The Sea Dragon has quite some (electronic) percussion in the opening part Sea Of Mines. The music is quite orchestral with kettledrums. After a more silent interlude the closing of the track features a strongly melodic electric guitar. After this Inevitable Declaration we come to The Impassable Tempest. Quick piano at first but later on we come to more quieter waters. This is not strange since this part is titled Peace!!...be...still. A beautiful melody reminding me as happens often on this disc of the serenity of Vangelis. Love In Training continues with piano and as the title might already indicate is a very accessible and sweet piece. Hirsch comes close to the edge of easy listening here, but he doesn't cross it. Compared to earlier tracks it is the least varied. Land Of Light is a mellow track opening with something akin to flute, but later on the music becomes more electronic. The peaceful mood stays throughout the piece. Climb The Mountain opens with its theme. The music on the second half of this disc seems more one-tracked. Climb The Mountain fortunately has a strong ending which is much louder and involved than the preparation. The theme is still there, but the electric guitar gives the music an edge. Man's Last Stand reminds of ELP at first, but then we get a break into the next part, which is also organ riddled. The optimistic Temple Of Knowledge is the somewhat bombastic closer of the album.

Conclusion

A disc of electronic music. Not all just synth, but some electric guitars, some more rocking parts with (unfortunately) electronic percussion. However the use of percussion is rather restricted making this a warmer album than for instance Indeed. Because of its high melodicity and strong melodies, this album should appeal to lovers of Gandalf and more so Vangelis.
