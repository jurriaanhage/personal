Summary

The title of the album should I guess refer to Pink (Anderson) Floyd (there was something with Council there as I seem to recall a Pink Floyd cover band called Anderson Council). This their second album.

The music

This album opens strongly with Phoebe's Diamond. After some introductory samples, we come a strong driving part with powerful keyboards. The sound is great and the melodies appealing. The drums plods through the composition, and although the keyboards are very prominent, it goes too far to call this plain electronic music. There's more to it than that. Queequeg is rather similar to the opening track at first glance: powerful plodding instrumental progressive. In the middle we come to the peaceful beach with an electronic seagull (or some other birdy) and a lone saxophone takes the fore. Afterwards the synth rock returns. Absence is a short and somewhat strange one. At first the music really decalibrates you when you listen with headphones on. After this short ditty with heavy guitars, we come to the funnily meant Happy Pat. Islands, sun, sea and beach dictate this marimba ridden tune. Knightwinds is the longest track on the album. The intro is one of foreboding, radio whispers and distant thunder. Then the flute sets in in its wavery way and the keyboards lay a soft carpet on which we can rest our ears. Then a sweeping saxophone sets in and the percussion picks up. A compelling composition. This part might remind people of the sometimes powerful passages in Pink Floyds music to which the music of this albeit instrumental band can be likened very much. I would say however, that Mansion Maze is both more electronic as well as more forceful and are generally quite concerned with atmosphere, but do not ignore melody. A strange thing however is the singing of Amazing Grace during this track while aborigines sing further on in the back. The music of that same traditional can also be heard slightly later on. Stingingman continues the line of the album, with again high pitched solo's on the keyboard. Redival II is the successor to the first one on the previous disc. A bit of a bouncy track this, with sax and some more piano like playing. The track I think least of up to now, it is a bit lame. This is more in the line of the previous album it seems, a bit too repetitive and not really catching ones attention. Somebody is working away on something in Memnon's Wisdom. Hacking, sawing and drilling while all the while gurgling synths are at work. Now we come to the Rollins College INTENDO bonus tracks. I'm not sure what the idea of these tracks really is and whether the music is really part of any game, but it seems that samples from all kinds of programs (like Mortal Kombat) were used for synth music. Lots of people dying and screaming in their synthetic way during this track. Still, the music is not bad and will remind some people of middle period TD. Sonic Linoleum is a bit more dribbling and really sounds like music for computer games. A bit too much in fact. The closing of the album we find in Tetricity which opens darkly but heavily computerised. This music is 'real' electronic music, but although not really for computer games, it is not up to the level of the non-bonus part. It sounds a bit too tinny for that.

Conclusion

Think of TD with rock and no sequencers, think of Pink Floyd, but a little louder and without the dreary vocals, think also of Cybotron or Chris Hillman if you can but with sax and flute instead of guitar and you might have some idea to what this is about. Except for Redival II with which I was not that satisfied, this is an album full of great tracks containing strong melodies and powerful playing. The bonus in the form of the Intendo tracks form a funny addition and the Immortal Kombat track is even quite nice. At the end of my review of their debut I remarked that I hoped they had a future and this shows that they have one, and it should be bright indeed. The element lacking in their music as I found then, I did not miss it now so improvements have certainly been made.
