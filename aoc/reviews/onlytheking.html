Chrass - Only The King

Artist: Chrass
Title: Only The King
Label: Chrass Records CHR CD 2
Length(s): 20 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1997
Month of review: 01/1998

Line up

Rudolf Koenen - lead and backing vocals, guitars
Olaf Matthias - drums
Thomas Knapp - keyboards, synths, backing vocals
Bernd Blecha - bass and bass pedals

Tracks

1) Only The King And The True Man 11.17
2) Caught At The Borderline 4.55
3) What A Brilliant Head 3.37

Summary

I've reviewed a CD of this band before and I didn't like it then. They've now released a cheapish (price is 12 DM+P&P, 400 pressed) mini cd with just one track in three parts. According to them they've improved.

The music

The song is a short story about a king and his not so friendly doings, so one might call this a mini conceptalbum. After a slow opening with intertwined vocals (they sing this very cautiously) we come to a poppy part with a dito verse. The harmony singing is not very well done here, but the following thematic part on guitar is very nice. In the next vocal part again it shows that the band should pay some attention to them. The vocals sound a bit wavery. Then we come to a heavier part with a nice melody and better vocals. The music is still very accessible, but the changes between the passages make this prog nonetheless. The next part is very well done with clear hammered percussion and echoed vocals. I'm talking about the off-beat part (The man kept on running) and it sounds well...new. The first part ends with the nice guitar theme and we move right into the next part with soft electronics. The next two minutes or so are quite poppy and up-tempo again with some prominent basswork, after which we get a spooky electronic intermezzo after which the music builds up again, yielding a kind of space rock. The ending of the second part is a bit trite. The last part sounds quite happy and isn't very complicated, but lyrically it's quite different, since the true man dies in this part.

Conclusion

On this mini album, Chrass plays only one song of Marillion type progressive. Although the band is connected to Asgard the sound of Chrass is a lot poppier and less bombastic. The drumming might be a little more exciting on the disc I think but the other instruments are okay. Compositionally there has been quite an advancement made by this band and the melodies on this album are often quite nice, although sometimes a little simple. The composition shows that the band by now knows how to evoke certain emotions within the song. I'm not that fond of poppy progressive myself, but that's very personal. The question is whether they do it right. Of course, the band still has quite some room to grow and especially the vocals need some attention: when they sing, they sound like they're singing live and maybe they are. I don't mind singing like this live, but a recording should sound better in my opinion with less muddy harmonies and some parts (mostly in the beginning of the album) the vocals sound a bit wavery.
