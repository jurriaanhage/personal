Summary

The music

Upon seeing the artwork for a moment there I thought this would be a tiring prog metal release. The album's title, however, turned out to be more indicative of the music presented, which takes a Celtic musical style and gives it a rocky twist. This in itself is not so unusual, after all, we know the likes of The Pogues, The Levellers and Dropkick Murphys. These bands are all from the British isles, though. Fathom isn't. They're from Long Island.

The band combine folk classics (such as The Wild Rover, but also Whiskey In The Jar, sung in a way that seems an ode to Lynnott's inebriations) with self written material. The guys emanate a nice bit of enthusiasm, but both the recording quality and the certainty (or lack thereof) of their delivery spells that these guys could take some steps towards more professionality. Their performance sounds somewhat loose (as opposed to tight) on both guitar and vocals. Difficult to pin down, but it's there.

The Two Towers steps outside the borders laid down for the rest of the tracks. This track has a feel close to prog metal, although not fully there. Possibly because of the genre's novelty to the band, a vein that feels unnatural to them, helping in creating a song that does not really belong on this album. DiBartolo's vocals already stay clear of being an asset on the other tracks, but pulling of this track is clearly outside his grip. Mystery is less striking as different, still its origin is more of a progressive nature than the majority of the material.

Conclusion

There is nothing specifically original about what these lads do. Some of the ditties they use have been around since before sliced bread, and their own material fits in nicely with it (save The Two Towers). Still, the style in which they deliver the music makes the album pretty worthwhile right till the end. That, I would guess, is this band's greatest achievement on this album. Sure, I feel the band could be more professional, but their enthusiasm, and the fact that they found a blend of traditional and contemporary that I feel hits a lot closer to home than that of similar artists, makes this album nice, without being important.
