Sfumato - Demo 1999

Artist: Sfumato
Title: Demo 1999
Label: self produced
Length(s): 45 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1999
Month of review: 01/2000

Line up

Marcus Dorr - vocals, keyboards
Roger Klambauer - bass
Bernd Reinbold - keyboards, vocals
Jorg Schmider - guitar
Sven Schmidt - drums, percussion, vocals

Tracks

1) Wrong! 5.40
2) Jake The Grain 11.50
3) Spleen 6.12
4) Take2 6.41
5) Paranon 14.21

Summary

Demo cd of this new German band.

The music

Wrong! opens the album with shards of lyrics and a heavy pumping rhythm. The vocals are a bit accented, but this is not disturbing. The vocals are quite expressive, not sticking to reciting the words. There's quite a lot of variation here with the main style being progmetal, but with some interesting making ingredients: guitar played with a feeling, a quiet bass intermezzo, hammered piano and a driving ending. The sound quality is okay, but the production is typically one of a demo, although I wouldn't say it is bad. Jake The Grain opens totally different. A bit in the line of Inquire with dark soundrackish synths. In the mid tempo vocal part the band reminds a bit of Enchant (the voice for instance). However the vocal melody is not as interesting and the vocals come over a bit wavery. The music may also remind some of Cliffhanger, being on the darkish side. For its length a bit weak in ideas/melodies. I did like the intro, but after that it just did not seem to go anywhere. Spleen is a much calmer song. There are some background vocals here that the band should pay a bit more attention to when they will record their real debut album. The song itself is a bit ballad like, and reminds me a bit of Galahad (don't ask me why). The guitar meanders a bi, while the vocals take the lead. However, the vocal melody does not appeal as much as it should making this a somewhat boring track. The ending is somewhat better with a guitar "solo" accompanied by some audible bass playing and nice melodic synths. In Take2 the band can let go instrumentally. Again the strong presence of the bass player is felt. No snowing under of this guy. Plenty of variation in here with introspective piano, driving passages and some nice tension building. On the whole still quite a neurotic and involved track with the focus on groove and drive, but like I said some more quieter parts as well. With better production the song could be a bit more blistering even. The closer is the long almost fifteen minute Paranon. After an intyro on acoustic guitar we come to the rather mellow vocal passages. The vocals sound a bit flat here. The keyboards add a nice flavour to the music and I think they should do this more. The overall atmosphere of the song is soothing and relaxed except for the poppy chorus. Halfway we get a break into a quiet vocal part, the vocal melody being quite good. Then the rhythm guitar sets in in a heavy way and the music gains in pace.

Conclusion

Not bad for a beginning, but there is also plenty of room for improvement. The backing vocals are not used often, but if they are more attention should be paid to them. The band does not sound derivative, although I've dropped a few names here and there. At first I thought this would be a progmetal band, but this turned out to be false. There are some heavy guitars in there, but the approach to the music is much more relaxed. The vocals are okay, but also here care should be taken in recording, the vocals have a tendency to sound less than steadfast. Not all of the tracks were good. I liked the first track and the instrumental, while most others tracks I liked sporadically. Sometimes, as in Spleen, the vocal melody holds too little appeal and I think the band could give the music a little more thought. Most of these things are things that will resolve themselves in time. What counts more is a distinctive approach to music.
