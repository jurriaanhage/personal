Thomas Metcalf - One

Artist: Thomas Metcalf
Title: One
Label: Clockwork Records CWCD-1
Length(s): 43 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1989/??

Summary

This album is quite old it seems, but I have the impression that only recently has the music become entrusted to the CD-format, which is one of the reasons why I'm only now reviewing it.

The music

I know nothing of music in any technical sense, so I wouldn't even know whether the titles Thomas has given to his songs, D, C, A etc. mean anything at all. The first track 'D' takes up six minutes. It all starts out rather quietly, but then we get a rather dissonant sounding part a bit in the Chinese way. This is again followed by a rather dark, slow and dreamy part with lots of noise in the back (I'm not sure whether they are part of the music or are supposed to to represent the sizzling of the rain along with the thunder.) Then again a more up-beat part with really lots of things happening, in both the percussive as well as the keyboards side. The music sounds rather repetitive and if you want me to name any reference than I guess it should be Art Of Noise, although that band sounds a lot more accessible and I think also does not work that much with musical contrasts such as exhibited on this first track. The repetitiveness of the music contiunues with 'C', the second track. The music again reminds me of something like a gamelan or Chinese music although it even contains shards of cosmic electronic music, rather like soundscapes travelling from one ear to the other. But then the beat picks up and really a terrific sequence starts that builds up the tension of keyboards accompanied by drums. 'A' is a rather short track mostly built upon percussion and the dark keyboards in a supporting role. In this way the music comes close to industrial music, but not of course of the NIN sort. 'B' is the follow up opening with high keyboard sounds. Again I feel the presence of Asian music. This is quite a rolling track. Then, the doldrums. The music sounds a bit like a sampled cello here and slowly takes on a church like quality, but not quite yet. The fifth track 'E' is quite a percussive track, heavy drums, the sounds of machines at work, blowing off steam. This might be nice music to play for the robots putting cars together. Then the music becomes a little bit more involved and louder. All kinds of themes are now scurrying across the soundspectrum. The track ends with The last two tracks take up almost half the album. First of these is 'G'. The first part of this track is quite 'ordinary' with quick keyboards, quite some percussion, but rather fluent. The first or so minutes of this track reminds me of the Zeuhlish fusion released by Musea. Then we get a darker part and strangely enough does the music sound organic and natural although the sounds typically are not. The last track is 'F'. This is the only track to contains some vocals, but only of the lalalala-kind. As more often happened to my surprise some of the music here reminds me a bit of Jobsons Theme Of Secrets, especially in that albums more experimental, percussive parts. Thunderous sounds throughout this stormy track as well as plenty of both dark and playful tunes. Halfway the music seems to stop, only to let a dark basssound rear up its ugly head. Music from the swamps it seems.

I wonder that maybe the song were originally meant to be in the alphabetical order, but he changed the order somewhat.

For those who are interested: the album contains a small comment on the music by Larry Fast (aka Synergy). Although he does not say explicitly that he likes the music he did find it music to be listened to and not to be treated as wallpaper. A challenge.

Conclusion

Already the second auditioning of this disc revealed that it is far more accessible and melodic than I had first thought. Listening with headphones also revealed many more details. The music on this disc is not for people into progressive rock as such and like our friend Fast said: this music is to be listened to, experienced and not to be used as background music. Electronic music far from New Age and actually quite far from most of what I've heard in electronic music. To my ears the sound of the gamelan and Chinese music were often evoked and the sounds will at times remind you of the Art Of Noise, but much more varied. If you liked Jobsons Theme Of Secrets and don't mind a little more experimentation and especially the "Chinese" influences, this album is certainly worth a listen. It will take some getting used to, but if you take the time there's really a lot to experience here. Be my guest.
