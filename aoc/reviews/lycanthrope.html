Summary

Mangala Vallis have made quite a name, especially due to their many good performances throughout Europe. Since their debut they have enlisted former PFM and Acqua Fragile singer Bernardo Lanzetti who has added even more spice to their seventies inspired progrock. The touring has made a second album overdue, but here it is.

The music

Echo Absolute consist of weird sound effects only. We arrive in Watcher Of The Skies territory with the pure symphonic progressive of Cosmotraffic Jam. The music also often sounds like Spock's Beard, the way of playing the organ but also the optimism in the music. Fortunately, the band does not forget to provide some excellent melodies. The fat mellotron sound reminds of the Scandinavian bands, but there is one big difference: the level of optimism and up-beatness. Lanzetti has a great voice (one that he can easily reproduce live by the way) with as a trademark his somewhat goat like vibrato. The song ends in slow piano style, nicely melodic, and a welcome bit of piece.

Call Me Alias recaptures the main theme of the previous song, and an excellent one it is. This time it is given the Scandinavian treatment. The singing is wavery this time, bringing in quite a dose of sadness and tragedy.

With Lycanthroparty we return the up-beat symphonic rock of the second track, rather like a modernized Foxtrot-era Genesis with a bit more pace. The guitar actually has a guitar solo this time, with a bit of cabaret thrown in at the end. Hum/Animal Song is the final short song, after this we have only lengthy ones. The song is very much a continuation of the previous song. Again plenty of mellotron.

The Boy That Howls At The Moon is the first of these long tracks, and the longest on the album. This time the opening mellotron take their time giving way to acoustic guitar. After five minutes we run into progmetal territory with very crunchy guitars and mellotron going at it. The music then swirls away with Hammond organ and synth solo's. Still, the song can't move me as did the earlier songs. It seems to lack something in the themes and vocal lines, or maybe it is just a question of them being a bit too similar to the earlier songs. At this point, the band is mainly going through the motions, notwithstanding the presence of fire in their playing.

The Mask is just some two minutes less of music. It opens in the strongly optimistic Beard style, with some excellent symphonic firework. The thematic work is strong, as is the drive. Is this one going to be an instrumental one wonders as the music proceeds? No, but the singing only starts after the two minute mark. This song is one of the most memorable ones with some excellent vocal work. The bell like sounds remind a bit of Oldfield's Tubular Bells. The middle part has some weird bird like sounds, and dark organ presence. Then the music is off again. This is just what the album needed.

On The Transparent And The Obscure we open with strings (I did not see a credit, so they might just be fake). The organ is more typical for VDGG, the mood is similarly dark. For the remainder, this song has a rather easy-going gait with acoustic guitar doing the pacing. The song has a bluesy guitar solo, and closes down with harmony vocals. I am reminded a bit of Zucchero Fornaciari here.

Conclusion

Foxtrot-era Genesis seems the focus of inspiration with Spock's Beard being a good second and the Scandinavian bands third with a nod to VDGG in the last track. The music is often very up-beat and optimistic and the band has some great assets, especially the powerful lungs of Lanzetti. Compositional I would prefer a bit more finesse, a bit less going throwing the motions and a bit more adventure, especially in a song like The Boy That Howls At The Moon, that comes over as rather anonymous. But most times the melodies are good, the themes captivating and all musicians are fully capable (of course).
