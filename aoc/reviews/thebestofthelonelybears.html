Summary

I feel in some way ashamed I had never heard of this band. It features Hymas (of Jeff Beck) and Bozzio, both people I know, but I had never heard of this band and they made three albums of which this is a compilation. The song Mozambique was taken from Bozzio's solo album Solo Drum Music.

The music

Kill King Rat opens the album and seems a rather poppyish track at first: up-tempo, a rather easy melody, but at the end the saxophone enters upon the stage and during its wringing the drums become a little more busy and the song turns into a jamlike track. Ma Grande Ourse (my big (she-)bear) is a rather short and spooky track with much percussion going on and a screaming in the back. From The Nacfa Mountains is like the previous a group composition with some quick piano work and on the whole a rather festive, slighty Latin festive atmosphere. Oka is a traditional with rumbling percussion, a sad saxophone and garnished by keyboards. The music is quite moody and thoughtful at first after which the drumming picks up and the music becomes more rhythmic with lots of jungle birdsounds and more of that kind. Quite a driving track this, but the fancy drumming takes some getting used to. March Past 29 145 749 B is a rather chaotic, repetitive track (but not very) with a good melody played by the saxophone and a menacing role for the keyboards. As always Bozzio is quite busy filling all the gaps. Si Tu Vois Ma Mere (a song by somebody called Bechet) is a rather moody tune, that one would expect from a lone saxophone player in a smoky club. Sartre is a Bozzio composition, and like many of the other tracks offers fusion/jazzrock, not very energetic, but more subtle and technical. The breaks and so on are rather common in this hectic track. Our Red Sea is another traditional, rather slow moving with piano and thumping percussion. After Los Ultimos Dias, in the style of the other group compositions, we come to an extended drum solo from Bozzio's solo album Solo Drum Music. The rhythms are quite world-like. The most memorable track on this album Quanah Parker. The melody is really great with loose drumming, playful samples and fluttering sax. The album closes with Nana, slow moving, but thoughtful jazzrock.

Conclusion

Although one might be tended to call this fusion, the bio is right in saying that this band incorporates more: the rhythms are varied, the melodies are both selfpenned and traditional and some songs really have that traditional air to them, and the music often places itself right in the world-music realm. The playing is impeccable as one might expect and the drumming of Bozzio is as always a pleasure to my ear. Compositionally the album is not especially outstanding, but there are certainly no extensive egotripping solo's here and the music is largely thoughtful, not energetic. Fortunately as well the melody is not forgotten, but the music tends to be quite repetitive. A liking for jazzrock/fusion is recommended for this album, but aside this also lovers of the more adventurous kind (transgressing into world-music) are also heartily invited.
