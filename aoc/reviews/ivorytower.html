Summary

The debut of this German prog metal band. As seems usual no lyrics with the promo copy but it does include some biographical data: having called themselves Ax 'n Sex earlier on, they found out that they were categorized wrongly (surprise) so they changed their name to Ivory Tower.

The music

One Life In Asia features many of the typical ingredients of prog metal: highpitched vocals (a la Bruce Dickinson), plenty of breaks, heavy guitars and some keyboards for melody. The song does contain a, not so melodic, keyboard solo, but on the whole the song cannot impress: in some parts it sounds downright awkward. A Distant Light tries to open in a more melodic and majestic way. Although it does feature double bass drum this is not a fast song, more plodding, but it does contain some nice and memorable vocal melodies. Although there are also some breaks here the music is tighter and seems to flow more. The song is punctuated by the everpresent guitarsolo which is embedded in a blanket of organ. With regards to sound and production, I feel the drumsound could be a little more clearer (not louder!). A very strong composition. The opening of Alive promises a lot: quick, melodic bass, ascending runs on the keyboard amd powerful chords. Dream Theater will come to mind during the vocal parts. The vocalist is more of a screamer though. This song has all the energy, but is also quite accessible, melodically. The chords and riffs are also okay and I even did hear some Art Of Noise like sounds come along. Too bad about the screaming (I mean here when he sings rAlihihiive) In Falling Leaves the tempo goes down again and the guys use some more unusual keyboard sounds. Spring is the ballad of the album: just piano and vocals with just that hint of emotion. Not everything in this song is the way I would have done it, but it certainly a nice shot. Opening with a long keyboard part, She starts out as a nice song. The vocal part does not really live up to the expectations and the composition turns out to not as strong as necessary. And well, now we get it: the cover of John Miles' Music. In my opinion one of those songs that one cannot really expect to cover well, since the song and the rendition seem to be part of everyone's DNA. Still, the song does seem to fit the bombastic progmetal style very well. What does irritate however is the singer: he tries to sing the song differently and I guess the band might have pulled it of if they had asked John Miles to do the singing again, or maybe someone entirely different. But this guy tries to sing it such that it sounds both not the same, but to compensate for this, it sounds unnatural, one of the worst things to do to a song. The closing song is also the epic of the album. Over 14 minutes in length it shows a more epic approach to music, a face they could show some more on the coming discs (not all the time). Halfway, the guys shift into gear with plenty of breaks, but this time actually leading to something. Then the curtain seems to fall and a lone ethereal guitar is left on the stage. All in all quite a likable song.

Conclusion

I'm not particularly fond of the timbre of the singer of this band, which is a bit too high for my liking. Still there are some nice songs on here, but also a few tracks that do not quite make it. Lovers of progmetal could venture a listen. Compared to the rest of Limb stable that I know, the music seems to be more accessible and melodic. To my feeling the drumming could be better and the guys tend to include some nonobvious and again, to my feeling, unnecessary breaks.
