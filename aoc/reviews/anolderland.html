Summary

This album contains a number of older track and improvisations and should thus not be viewed as the follow-up to the appreciable Terra Surranum. BTW, this is a review based on my review for IO pages. As such it is rather short.

The music

Having listened to Terra Surranum as a preparation for this album, I found it a lot louder than I thought it was and I heard more of the Genesis influence. It turned out that my idea of the album was mostly coming from the last few long epics. A large difference with An Older Land is that the latter is a lot more fragmentaric and for instance a track like Jazz Magic Potion is probably fun to record (I think), but not really an interesting track. There is, however, compensation in the form of the classic Wake To Find Me Dead, which is really a great track, laden with emotion. Most of the other tracks are good listening, and although Dross (the improvisation) is a little too fragmented too really captivate, there are some good ideas in there. The closer is a bit strange. Seemingly we have a song in three versions at once (in sequence) and as such it's rather a strange track. Again, as usual with Cyclops releases there's that extra few minutes not noted on the cover or in the timings.

Conclusion

It turns out that although being less wholesome than Terra Surranum, this is an album with some highpoints that are never reached on TS. As such, it is worth seeking out for anyone who liked Terra Surranum, because this is unmistakenly the same band, but also for people that listened to Terra Surranum and found it too unstructured. The tracks on this album (save Dross) are all rather structured and to the point, but not neo.
