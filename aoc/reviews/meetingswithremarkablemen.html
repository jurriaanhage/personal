Summary

Glenn Astarita = Resonant Swamp Theory. He sent me what seems to be a copy of his album for reviewal.

The music

It is obvious quite quickly that the main part of RSTs (or Astarita's) music is percussion. Essentially it is percussion with melodic ingredients generally on keyboards/synthesizers. This makes for somewhat fragmentary tracks that are not to be loved as songs, but that have to be lived and experienced. This does not mean that all you hear is rhythm, in fact a large part of the first track is quite dreamy and atmospheric, but these are also the less striking parts, being rather subdued. The break at the end of the first track is a little awkward. In Allen's Weakness the bass plays the main role while the organ plays the melodic part. One might think of ELP here in one of their freaky intermezzo's. The music does have a tendency to bounce a bit too much here and I'm not really fond of it and the circuslike ending doesn't help either. A name that pops up here, is the name of Makkiwhipdies (also bass prominent) that I reviewed some time earlier. G-frog's Weakness starts out as a collage and is rather unstructured with some weird breaks and parts thrown in. The most steady part of the music is a synth part which is quite groovy. The track is a little too fragmentary for my tastes. The same holds for Miss Pattle. Variety rules also on Around The World 2. I now tend to get the feeling that the music really is too fragmentary for my tastes. Lots of things are going on in sequence and everytime, the "song" has to pick up speed again and seemingly starts all over. Of course, it may be quite nice to put all these things together here as a humble and jumble of tunes, breaks and changing signatures, but a person has to be able to old on to something and I'm really at a loss here in this regard. I'm more at home in the atmospheric spooky parts such as Sightings. It is not unusual for Astarita to put a King Crimson tribute, the famous Talking Drum, unto his album. As he diligently goes about covering the piece (no I did NOT compare them part for part) he concludes with All Rise. The electric guitar and percussion take the fore on this track, but my problem stays that the tempi vary too greatly hampering the flow in the music sometimes. In Around The World 4 we are then passing through India, with added some gamalan like percussion and melody on the flute. Flower Child is a welcome exception to the rule that percussion plays the prominent part it does. With acoustic guitar, synths strings and flute this is a very peaceful, but short track. Seance is also quite calm and soothing, as you might expect from the title and it seems we have, just before the end of the album, come into deeper, calmer waters. The closer Hunter is quite a spooky one making me think of horror soundtracks and Univers Zero.

Conclusion

Although one can always keep an open ear for new things and interesting experiments, one of the demands I make of music is that there is certain flow or structure to it, that appeals. After the first track on this album, this only occurs again in Sightings and later in the last three tracks, while for the rest of the album, it is all a jumble of breaks, changes of pace, short passages, tunes and mostly a lot of percussion. I'm actually a lover of rhythm, but when the changes come so often it is hard for me to feel for the music. Note however that this is a consequence of the line of music RST is in and not necessarily because of any inability on his side. I do feel I have to warn people that in addition to being quite complex, the music on this CD is hard to get used to.
