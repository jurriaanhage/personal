Summary

Everon is a German band, produced by the well-known Eroc (of solo and Grobschnitt). I've seen them live a few times and they are quite good, although the lead singer/guitarist etc. tries to do to much and might be better of delegating some of the things thus improving the performance. Their former CD (Paradoxes) was a very good one, one of the better ones that year, and seeing them live with Now and in Uden only confirmed this. A good band, although I was never fond of the singing; I mean it isn't rapping, but this guy sings so quick hardly gives you time to listen. After releasing their first CD they already had a few new songs quite quickly. Still it took them quite some time to get this new release done (artwork and so on), but here it is and I'd better start reviewing:

The music

The first song starts of very promising with a quick and melodic pianoriff. The haste he used to have has somewhat left the singer, but he's still a quick singer. The first song is actually an orchestral bilogy (a variant of the well- known trilogy), with the first part being the quieter part. The second part is a lot more bombastic. It's griefing that this song is well disfigured by the guitarsolo, which I thought was totally out of place. They have this more often. I rather have them when all instruments are at it together than when one has the leading part. The guitarsolo's are usually not very awe- inspiring, with an exception to song 7, so I wish they'd leave it at that.

Well on to song 3, which is very atmospheric, in the sense of Clutching of Straws, while the 4th is quite screamy and hard pressed. The 5th song is another varied one with it's quicker and slower parts and good melodies. The 6th is a bit unevenetful and the melody doesn't strike a chord. 7 is another good song, with an alternative to their otherwise either very bombastic or very quiet intros: the melodies are good in this song and so is even the guitarsolo.

Song 8 is quite optimistic musically and sounds like an anthem (a bit of a Big Country feel to it in that part). The song has some nice intermezzos, making the whole a bit more tasty. Song nr. 9 doesn't add to the remarks on the previous songs so I'll leave it at that.

The title track is something else, because of the fact that the vocals have been done quite differently, the song isn't very dynamical like the others, but a resting point on the album. The atmosphere is kind of French with a soprano saxophone and a bit of laid back. BTW the lyrics to this song can be found in the middle of the booklet next to the large drawing. This song is quite different from the rest and a worthy conclusion of this album.

Conclusion

Another neo prog release of one of the better bands in the field. It doesn't reach the height of Collage, which is far more varied. This has something to do with the vocals, because they tend at first listens to make all songs sound like each other. A few more listens reveals the nature of all songs, but still it mostly powersymfo enthusiastic and professionaly played. Maybe not the classic people were waiting for, but satisyfing to say the least. Best song on the album are the first two. What struck was the fact the refrains stick easily in your head. Most songs are good or at least alright, the only exception being 6 and 9, which cannot make an impression.

Note: this is the first album to be distributed by Roadrunner throughout the world.
