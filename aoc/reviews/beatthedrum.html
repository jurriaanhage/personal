Summary

Well, you might have read my interview from 1993, in which the band promised to have an album ready quickly. This is it. A new drummer and keyboard player (Ronnie Brown from the old days). I also had a chance to see them play this album live and notwithstanding the illness of Alan Reed (thoroughly compensated by the vocals of Graeme helping out) the band made a good impression playing the right tracks, and playing long and good.

The music

After the problems with the Day of Dreams Festival Pallas decided to compensate the comers with a free tape. This tape already told of great things coming (see my review of that tape on this site). The album opens with Call To Arms, a rock song with a great ending and the high voice. Beat The Drum is one of the best tracks on this album. Long and well-developed, this is the epic side of Pallas at its best, but with the catchiness of The Wedge and some Art Of Noise-like samples. Both these first tracks have the spirit of mobilization in them, starting things up. Hide & Seek was already to be found in a preliminary version on Sketches. The song is quite accessible with the keyboards prettying things up and as a vocal melody that is right on the spot and a nice nod to Yes. Insomniac is tied to Hide & Seek in a lyrical way and in fact the previous one seems to flow right in. Again quite of a lot of keyboards in the back and quite an extensive organ dreched intermezzo. A varied song with plenty of moods and sides to it. Track five, All Or Nothing, is a very percussive track with a large bass presence and very catchy chorus. I'm not too fond of the chorus, though. Spirits is a totally okay one again. A rather mysterious one with some spooky world music influences and again worthwhile additions in the sounds dept. by Ronnie Brown. The music is rather balladlike, but gets majestic towards the end. Man Of Principle is my least favourite of the tracks on this album. Opening without direction, this is a song that is far too catchy with a singalong chorus and a fake horn section. Ghosts is more in the line of Spirits, a moody track that picks up momentum after a while with heavy guitars and quick percussion and a great guitarsolo and old-style keyboardsolo towards the end. The highpoints of the concert I viewed were Crown Of Thorns, Beat the Drum, the second part of the Atlantis Suite, but the next one takes it: Blood And Roses is the perfect ballad, sad and captivating, a great melody, great subtlety, and well you should hear for yourself (you have if you got the tape). Wilderness Years reminds me somewhat of the latest Galahad, a band also known for their sensible compositions and updated sound. The album closes in a slightly Floydian fashion (with an IQ vocal melody), that after a cold start ends powerfully and bombastically.

Conclusion

Pete Nicholls once remarked that Pallas was much further in the aspect of songwriting than Marillion. I'm not about to make any such statement, but if one compares this album to The Sentinel and The Wedge, it turns out that this album comes out most favourably: The Sentinel has quite a lot of tiddly bits that seem to go nowhere (very well exemplified during some parts of the live Atlantis suite), while The Wedge was always a bit too commercial for my tastes. Here, at last the band seems to have found its niche. LEt's hope they stay at it. The newer songs (i.e. the ones not directly descending from Sketches) give me hope. And live it sounds even better.
