Summary

My first encounter with this band.

The music

Stiletto In The Sand (no, not a hammer), opens this album in high tempo. A typical overture for a concept album by a prog metal band. It does show that these guys can play well and quickly. After some dissonance we come to War For Sale. Again the tempo is high here. The chrous is quite a catchy one and the song contains a few other worthwhile vocal melodies. The lyrics are about war and guilt. An appealing track. The music is also good in the following track, Out Of Nowhere. Everything in this track is typical for prog metal (i.e., heavy guitars, solo's on electric guitars, noticeable drumming, keyboards taking care of the melodic side and harmonies making it sound American) and so there are no surprises(besides maybe the flute played by Cadden), but within those limits this is a good composition, with a slight leaning even in the direction of Arena. The chorus is perfect with vocals woven into the fabric of the composition. On track four, Mystery, we hear lots of keys in the intro, reminiscent somewhat of ELP and a vocal melody that refers back to War For Sale. Hope For Us? is the first breather on this album, opening with pieno and followed by at first rather soft vocals and later does typical harmonies and quite a lot of strings as well. The chorus is quite accessible and like the entirety of Broken, quite melodramatic. Act II of this disc opens with I Believe. Quite bombastically in fact. This is the track that features the vocals of James LaBrie. Quite a lot of string like sounds on this one at first, but after a while the guitar disappears into the distance, screaming and the piano takes over. Roads Of Thunder opens quite loudly with organ and a lot of breaks. The chorus sounds familiar (from an earlier track). Again it strikes me that this album contains so much lyrics, but fortunately the singing is good and there plenty of time for instrumental parts as well. During a spoken part the drummer plays some interesting signatures (don't as kme what it was). The phone rings and... we're into Spoken Words, the next ballad. The strong powerful vocals on this song are by Laura Jaeger and this is a successful attempt to a duet. In this ballad we meet again the protagonist and his "love" across the computer cables. in the following track, New World Order, however it turns out that the two have been under surveillance as they plotted subversive actions through viruses. On New World Order, Royal hunt's D.C. Cooper sings along. After the instrumental firework of Chased there's another beautiful ballad that ends on a more powerful note, Ghost Of A Chance. The closer is Christmas Day, opening again with piano. A great melody on this one and a worthy end to this album.

Conclusion

An album in the line of Operation: Mindcrime. This I guess is one of those albums is one of those albums that set the standard for this musical direction (along with Queensryche, DT, Fates Warning and Savatage), one that many a band will find hard to live up to. The musicianship and compositional qualities are strong and although the music can be quite catchy, I have to admit being caught. Also, this is one of the few prog metalband of which I really like the vocals. These vocals are the best ones (not sound, but the way they have been arranged) I've heard lately and the harmonies sound professional. All in all heartily recommended.
