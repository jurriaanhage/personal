Tracks

Summary

A mini album from Switzerland. You might know this band from their Life Will Live On on the second DURP compilation. The album can be obtained for USD 10 righ here. Contact address of the label is Dazed Music, Maja Tinner, Klichbergsteig 2, 8038 Zurich, Switzerland.

The music

Got Used To It opens with soft sounds until the guitar makes its entrance to introduce the song itself. The vocal parts however do almost without music, except a slow hammeringly played piano. The vocals are fluctuate a lot. The way the title of the track is pronounced reminds me of a song by Marillion on Brave I think it was. There Hogarth also sings this line. Plenty of variation in this track where (progressive) metal guitars alternate with acoustic guitar and piano. The guitars are reserved for the intro and the chorus (well, chorus). The dissonant piano makes its entrance, just when I thought it became a little too cliché. An imposing tense closing fragment rounds the track off. Heavy guitars and rolling percussion open 3rd November and then the songs makes a 90 degree turn into something rather spooky. This is only for a little while, because then the music becomes progmetal. The drumming is rather groovy, but maybe a little too varied. The breaks do not really come natural in this track. The vocals can be rather intense, but I can not consider the vocals beautiful, maybe a bit too sharp. The guitar solo in the middle is quite good, starting off in a slightly bluesy way, later accompanied by some organ. Weirdly enough the music then becomes rather jumpy and frenetic. Often the music seems written to fit the lyrics, a bit like a "soundtrack to", if you see what I mean. After a sad piano the song is concluded by a sharp guitarsolo. After a balladic beginning the closer Un-Coming also works up to a sharo guitarsolo. Then also melodically the song takes a turn. At the end we return to the acoustic beginning.

Conclusion

Moondaze lacks in what I call the details: nothing really bad here, but the combination of the not very appealing vocals (but do hold on to the emotional outbursts), an unbalanced sound (too much percussion), a certain lack of coherence in playing and composition, and the sometimes awkward lyrics (and a few errors as well), make the overall impression the band makes one of not quite being there. Nothing wrong with that, but it just doesn't make for something I can recommend. What the band does have going for them is their style which lies nicely between progressive metal and progressive rock (they could do with a little less epigonism in the progmetal parts), but also their way of writing music to fit the story of the lyrics. The ideas and concepts are there, but a more careful working out is needed.
