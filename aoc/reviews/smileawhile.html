Summary

A rerelease from Germany on Musea. This is a band I've never heard of to be honest (yes it happens). The bonus tracks are live ones from 1971 and the band used to call itself Fashion Pink at some time after Pink Floyd, but the music of Brainstorm has little to do with PF to be sure.

The music

Aren't they beautiful? Yes they aren't. In fact they are extremely ugly as they are pictured in women underwear on the cover of the CD. Well, artistic license I guess. The music is more important. What is the music like this time, I hear you ask. Well it starts out rather on the inaccessible side with a lot of horns and is more or less a hectic version of fusion. Some pointers that might help here are Soft Machine especially around the Third and Fourth album. Other influences on the sometimes downright Avant Garde and RIOish music are Caravan, John Coltrane and National Health (but with less attention to keyboards).

As such we can expect and do in fact get an album full of complex music with much experimentation and often hard to follow musical meanderings, mostly ox the saxophone. In Zwick Zwick the music is rhythmically very interesting and the keyboard and saxophone seems to be distorted somewhat yielding a melting pot of sound and melody.

Watch Time Flow By is a short interlude with vocals for the first time and they enhance the idea that this effort is thoroughly in the Cantebury vein. They are also in the booklet compared to Moving Gelatine Plates, but I have nothing by this band, so I really couldn't tell you.

The next track on is one of the longish one and the title shows that humour is an important part of this bands ideas about music, escpecially since the three parts of the track are called: Bosco Biati, WeiB and Alles. The song even contains a drumsolo (of all possible solo's). The vocals on it remind me vaguely of the wordless vocals of Robert Wyatt and the ending of the track is chaotic to say the least (seems like a bunch of musicians falling over their instruments).

In Snakeskin Tango they go even harder at it, when the "singer" opens with a death rattle. Grunt avant la lettre? And that with a tango.

The titletrack (in six parts) starts out softly. Then the pace picks up nicely and the band showcases its ability. This track seems to be a bit more serious than others and although the style is the same as on former tracks there is little difference, but the song itself seems to work out a little better than the earlier tracks. The song features some prominent flute work by the bass player.

You are what's gonna make it last is an up-tempo vocal track played live, while Thesen & Antithesen kicks some freestyle ass with a lot of flute in the quieter parts. The bass is all on my left speaker on that track and it doesn't help the music. The song is rather good though.

Back to the studio album there's some Tullish flute work with some bluesrock influences as well. Not so interesting a track. Also in this track some melodies reoccur and this melody is exactly the one that already sounded so familiar. The last track is more or less in the style of the beginning but with a rock feel to it as has the previous track.

Conclusion

A German Canterbury band to be sure with lots of variation and the saxophones as most prominent instruments. Repetitive, sometimes melodic, but also harmonically complex, this is certainly only for lovers of the genre and within the genre a decent effort. At the end of the album (more specifically the last two tracks) the music falls back a little.
