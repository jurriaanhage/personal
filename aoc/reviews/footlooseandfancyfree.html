The music

Bill Bruford's Earthworks is by now a full blown jazz quartet, with the original base line up of bass, piano and drums (all acoustic, of course) supplemented with a saxophone. And not only in line up is this a jazz outfit: there no longer is any hint present of other musical veins than jazz, so be forewarned. This double CD contains registrations of two concerts at the PizzaExpress Jazz Club in London, held in June 2001, and shows a well integrated band (not a drummer with a couple of other musicians). If it weren't for the applause at the end of tracks, you wouldn't know it were live. Unlike the other three instruments, the saxophone isn't present constantly, which is for the best, since it more often than not has a shrill quality, aside from regularly dragging the tracks from melody towards technical experiment. The tracks tend to be laid back and almost smooth sounding at the one moment (like the title track, for instance), neurotic or seemingly improvised at others, making the album a nice blend of various jazz styles. This in all sums up pretty much the album, showing that its strongest point is a weak point in my eyes just as much: there is simply too much jazz in this concert for me to really like it. But worthwhile jazz all the way.
