Aurora - Lake Of A Thousand Tears

Artist: Aurora
Title: Lake Of A Thousand Tears
Label: self produced promo CD single
Length(s): 21 minutes
Year(s) of release: 2000
Month of review: 04/2000

Line up

Rob Zuljar - keyboards
Eric Bontekoe - drums, backing vocals
Dave Pijpers - bass, vocals

Tracks

1) New Entry 5.05
2) Dreamcatcher 6.00
3) Lake Of A Thousand Tears 10.04

Summary

A new Dutch band with a self produced promotional single. The music is also available as a demo tape and obtainable from the band.

The music

New Entry is the first track. Opening with lush keyboards, far away vocals and with rhythm somewhat on the foreground. The vocals are still a bit "Dutch" and they sound a bit depressed. However, they do seem to fit with the music. The music is strongly melodic and the guitar is not missed. Of course, with such a demo recording as this, the sound quality and production are not yet perfected, but as usual this should clear up in time. As their main influences the band cites UK. Marillion, IQ and Pink Floyd. The latter I don't see at all, but UK is certainly present. However it seems that there is still something lacking, because the music doesn't dazzle yet. There's something lacking the dynamics. It gets better when the singer tries to put some emotion into his vocals, because otherwise they sound rather flat. The theme of the mid-tempo Dreamcatcher sounds very familiar, but it seems to me very improbable that they know Art & Craft by Schwartzeneggar. As in the previous track, a strongly melodic approach. Altogether a good composition, but unfortunately the spark doesn't come over as well as it could have. The epic track of this single is the closing titletrack. After some seagulls, the music is dominated by some nice melody lines on the keyboards, but the music tends to drift a bit. I'm not sure whether speeding things up would help, but it does seem to me that things are played a bit slower than they should have. Fortunately, the pace goes up a little, just before the vocal part. What I like very much here are the melodies that, like those of UK (and I'm thinking Rendez-Vous 6:02 here), have a rather mysterious quality to them. The lyrics are not that strong, but the chorus is good. The drummer succeeds well in keeping the music apace. Then we get a slowing of the pace with keyboards effects and some nice basswork. I'm not altogether happy with the verses, which lack distinction. The driving keyboards right after the chorus are very good. The most appealing of the tracks on this minicd.

Conclusion

Promise they do have, but there are some things that need improvement. Besides production and sound quality I am thinking mostly of the vocals, that miss that extra something to make them stand up to the instrumental parts. On the instrumental side, I would hope for a bit more energy and more drive. Oftentimes it happens that the music is nicely melodious, but nothing is really happening, it just tends to drift on: no tension or expectation is created. This may be what I am missing.
