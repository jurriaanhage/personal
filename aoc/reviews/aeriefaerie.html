Summary

An old album actually, but some parts of it are new. Leafing through some of the press material that I got on The Enid I found that RJG has won a case against EMI and Polydor for not giving him credits for writing he did on some Barclay James Harvest tracks. Unfortunately, he does not get money for this, because according to the judge he waited to long with making his claim. What struck me most was that he co-wrote Mockingbird, an emotional song that I like very much (if you do not know it, try to find it). I also read that in the future there will come an Enid night in The Royal Albert Hall with a hundred piece orchestra.

The music

The first part of this album is quite symphonic and the sound is very loud and good, making it apparent that some remixing was done. Again, this band shows that its style is unique among progressive rockers, in that it uses classic stylings and compositions along with standard progressive rock instruments, like synths and elecric guitar. The problem with this approach that through the use of keyboards for an orchestra, the sound is a little synthetic. On the other hand the use of rock instruments make the music easier to perform and also a bit more intense than the coldness and distance that classical music often creates. As often with The Enid there are some parts that I definitely do not like, for their often cheesy melody. In this case it is in Bridal Dance that for the first time I get this feeling. Also the main work Fand has some turns that make me well, turn the stomach is a bit too harsh, but they make me feel a little mellow. Thing is I'm not romantic and some melodies are just too sweet for me sometimes. Fortunately however, the music is not often like this, and we find on this album the somewhat playful opener, A Heroe's Life (strange spelling) which is a isn't too slow, but a little energetic, especially compared to the rest of the album, the second is more or less acoustic with flute while the fourth track is a bridal dance which is rather trite and happy. The main part of the album consists of the darkish, melodic Fand, which is very thematic, dramatic and playful at times. The track is usually quite slow and on the majestic side.

Conclusion

A thoroughly classic work with as main attraction the long work Fand, but the first track is nice as well. Again the music is both original, because of the approach, but the music is sometimes too sweet for my tooth and thus is not something I'd play often. Also, the synthetic sound of this album, while in the classic style, might put off people ordinarily interested in classic music. These people I'd advice to go see them when the play with an orchestra in the Royal Albert Hall.
