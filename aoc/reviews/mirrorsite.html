Cliffhanger - Mirrorsite

Artist: Cliffhanger
Title: Mirrorsite
Label: Musea FGBG 4242.AR
Length(s): 57 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1998
Month of review: 05/1998

Line up

Rinie Huigen - guitar, vocals
Dick Heijboer - keyboards
Gijs Koopman - bass, pedals, keyboard
Hans Boonk - drums

Tracks

1) Rainforest 7.16
2) The Final Frontier 4.57
3) Mirror Site I 11.35
4) Mirror Site II 9.51
5) Mirror Site III 5.23

7) Truce 4.43
8) The Undiscovered Country 8.51

Summary

The third studio album and seemingly the only Dutch progressive band that is actually liked a lot over the countries border, the other Dutch progressive acts often derided as being too simple. After there very nice Not To Be Or Not To Be. Heijboer has left the band and has been replaced by a new keyboardplayer.

The music

Rainforest opens the album with its synthy dreamy intro. Drops of water and other sounds enrich the atmosphere. After a while percussion, a low bass and melodious on guitar accompanied by hammered keyboardplaying. The melodies are quite nice and there's a lot going on in this track especially towards the end. The guitar work could have been a bit more fierce, especially towards the end. The guitar solo of the next song is a lot better and in fact it's melodies is a really good one, the bass playing is also very nice here and the piano and keyboards form a welcome addition to this palette. The verses are quite alright in this track, but the chorus is a bit too mellow. Quite a Genesis influence in this track with Mellotrons and the guitar work remind me also of Hackett. Mirror Site is what people will call the Ragnarok of this album because of its length. Musically the first part is closer to Innocent Victim with biting vocals (some Ian Anderson comes to mind rather than say Hammill) and a rather dark sound to it. After the first vocal part we get a nice atmospheric part with a prominent role for the bass. After a more rocking part we get a rather quiet part with acoustic guitar. This is a rather sleepy making part, but I'm not sure whether this is something bad or not. The lyrics bu the way are all about computers, something I do know a little about, but like less to be confronted with. The next break in the music reminds me a lot of Gabriel-era Genesis and it comes close to evoke emotion, but not close enough. Something is definitely lacking here. The "right" melody perhaps. The first part closes with bubbly bass and keyboards. Mirror Site II opens quite loudly with experiments on the keyboard (I hear a little static here. Might that be my headphone?) Cold, whining keyboards and acoustic guitar take quite some time and frankly it's quite monotonous. The vocals on this part are rather mysterious, like the singer is trying to tell me something without somebody else listening in. The singer sounds a bit far away here and I'm not sure whether this was done on purpose and the vocal melody is not great either. At the end of this part we get a nice vocal reprise of the first part and the song ends quite quick and rather typically of this band: fastpaced keyboards, guitar playing in the shadows. Mirror Site III opens with a screamy guitar played repetitively getting higher and higher. In between the guitar plays a more melodious part. Mostly guitar on this track and in fact this is a number of guitarsolo's almost, with organ in the back and rather prominent drums, and I have to admit, the drummer could do with some extra groove, it's all a bit dry right now. The song ends with some vague sounds. As a whole Mirror Site is not the most cohesive of tracks, especailly the second part is lacking and the vocal melodies throughout have a certain sameness to them. Probably written on a Sunday Afternoon this is again an instrumental of the hand of Koopman. The song opens a bit...willowy?...rather simple and slow although the ascending guitar is nice. After a bouncy excursion into Camel resembling jazzrock we come back to the slow part. Not an impressive track this. Below par in fact. Truce opens promising and enjoys a surprising vocal melody. A bit bouncy and it sounds rather familiar. Again the prominent bass work is striking, but they do fiddle around a lot in this track. The keyboards/organ are nice though and the break into the majestic part in the middle is great, but then the pace slackens again into an acoustic part. Quite a poppy track this. The Undiscovered Country is the closer and is instrumental. The sound is quite spaceous (so the title can well be said to be a good choice). The (great) main theme sounds remarkable familiar, but everytime I think I have it, the melody takes a turn (Camel maybe?). A melodic track with lots of mellotron. A good conclusion to the album.

The sound and production are okay, but a little more power to the music and also the vocals wouldn't hurt..

Conclusion

In my opinion a step back from Not To Be Or Not To Be. The music is not bad, but some of the (vocal) melodies just do not strike a chord. The music is a little more songdirected and never as harsh as say Innocent Victim or stretched as Ragnarok. Some more Genesis on this album as well than on the previous one and although I wouldn't say they have less a sound of their own as they did earlier, I'm not as overjoyed as I thought I would be.
