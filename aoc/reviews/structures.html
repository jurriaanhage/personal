Summary

Carlos G. Plaza is a solo instrumentalist who with a little help from his friends recorded this album. The music is supposed to be on the fringe of classical and prog music, with for this album the focus on the latter. The album is obtainable from the website.

The music

The album consists simply of seven structures, Structure 1 to Structure 7. Structure 1, not surprisingly, opens the album. The track exists with grand sweeping gestures, long drawn chords and melodies, intertwining and letting go. Not bombastic or anything, but orchestral all the same. The drumming is probably not done by a live drummer and lacks a bit in feel and groove. The music does change pace at times, being rather fast paced most of this track with highpitched keyboards and piano fighting for attention. Then we slip into something more comfortable with piano taking the slow percussive lead and the keyboards being put back to second place, but still responsible for the melody. Track 2 is a more bouncy track in part, but there are alternated with more melodic passages. The music reminds me a bit of the Enid. The music is a bit too easy going and lacks in force here. The melodies are also a bit too mellow for me. On Structure 3 it seems time for lunch at first, but then the flute sets in, giving the music again a Camel tint. The piano is a bit jazzy here. I do feel the music could be a bit livelier, especially when it gets a bit of pace; now it all seems a bit static. The theme is quite nice. When the tracks are slow of themselves, like Structure 4, one can just concentrate on theme and flow and energy and drive are not important. Piano and keyboards are featured on this introspective melancholy track. Structure 5 is by far the longest of the seven pieces. The first part is again very much in the Enid style with a large role for the percussive piano. In the slower parts, the flute plays its part, while in the quicker parts the keyboards take the fore. The music sometimes gets quite aggressive and with the flute some Hispanic influences are felt in the music, temparement. Structure 6 again goes somewhat in the direction of early Camel, but the meandering keyboards are more like the soundtrack of a gangster movie. The use of the electronic drumkit is felt. The last track, Structure 7, is again an up-beat piece with live percussion this time, but the percussion does seem a bit soft. In parts it is quite dreamy.

Some very nice artwork accompanies the CD (maybe the title of the band could have been a bit more legible).

Production and soundwise this is not a bad album, although the music can sound a bit thin at times. With the means at their disposal I think they did quite well.

Conclusion

The music on this instrumental album ranges somewhere between Camel and the Enid with varied success. Much of the music is beautiful in a sense, but that is not enough. There must be energy or drive as well at times, rough edges. Sometimes these are necessary (to me). In some places one can do without (Structure 1 and 4) and in some the drive does come into the music at times (Structure 5), but in some cases the music just doesn't catch on.
