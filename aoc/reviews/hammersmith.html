Summary

The live albums of the Enid were once released in a limited way. I'm not sure whether this still holds, but I'd doubt it. Anyway this is the first one.

The music

It starts out very eloquently with, like it says, the National Anthem (the English one of course). Judgment starts as a military march with prominent percussion. The guitar and synth do play a part but not a large one. The track also has a Middle Eastern feel to it. Threatening track although after a number of minutes the marching rhythm is dropped and the song becomes a whirl of melody, being a bit on the dramatic side.

The next track is more playful and can remind some people of Genesis in their more romantic moods. Then comes a quiet part after which the guitar comes to the fore with some forceful drumming. Again we are lost in a more or less romantic, yet bombastic part reminding as so often of old Hollywood movies/musicals, but with a little more force to be sure and not as sugared (although a little).

Even more playful is Mayday Gaillard with its too accessible tunes. Almost circus music. Not my favourite you might have guessed.

Next one is part one of Charades. Rather bombastic piece. The second part starts out percussive and is rather repetitive. The wind instruments make it sound Easternish again. Interesting track.

Last on is Enid's Magnum Opus Fand. Not such a long version as you might find somewhere else. This song is mysterious and also a bit threatening, but still the underlying ingredients are the same, but this song still stands out as one of the more accomplished Enid tracks.

Conclusion

Naturally, we will not see departure from the classical minded Enid that we have grown used to. The music on this album is live and usually quite forceful and bombastic, but also dynamic on the whole, so it's not just blearing out of your speakers. The level of the music is rather good, with good rendition, but of course you might have guessed that there is also NO orchestra playing here, and more and more I tend not to find this much of a problem, although I would certainly enjoy hearing it played with orchestra once. The weakest track on the album is Mayday Gaillard being a little to sumptuous in its playful melodies. So, if you look for something between the somewhat cold classical music, but more forceful and bombastic than the romantic Hollywood movie soundtrack, with some progressive touches thrown in, then look no further. Very melodic, sometime too.
