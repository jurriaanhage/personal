Summary

Back after their quite succesful debutalbum that I also found to be a very satisfying album with the right mix of King's X and instrumental interludes.

The music

The album opens bombastically with Oh God. After the symphonic beginning, the music takes a downturn with the vocal part that is very much like the Tabor solo album and King's X. The vocal part is a bit too straightforward with a slow verse and up-tempo chorus. My problem with Tabors vocals is that his distinctive makes the vocal melodies sound similar. Positive points are the penetrating low bass and the repetitive Take Care Of It... The Beatlesque Better Left Unsaid is up next. The vocal of Tabor are slightly vocoded here. The song is ballad like with some sweltering organ and a nice bridge. In the guitar work I'm reminded of Spocks Beard. The Tower continues along the same lines, but with more organ and a more up-tempo sound. A catchy track but too my feeling there's too strong a sameness to the previous tracks and the chorus is a bit too straightforward, notwithstanding the driven and varied drums. Cry opens with a crying guitar. Again a strong riff dominates the track. In the instrumental middle section I hear some references to Genesis (and Spocks Beard) with some flashy drumming livening up the song. With I Need You we are back to the basics. Plenty of variation, but less appealing melodies. The track 25 opens flashingly with guitar, drums and then lkeyboards. I guess this is the Dixie Dregs track with all the instrumental pyrotechnics that these people can and do show. An orgasm of keyboards, fast paced drumming and loud guitars. Gone opens quite good with some moody playing, long stretched chords on the guitar and sensitive drumming. That is until the rock breaks out. The lyrics fit in well with the playing here, as Tabor sings of a quiet surf on which his boat floats. The music really has that aspect of darkness, quietness, but something threatening in the air nonetheless. The bass playing is quite prominent here and the choris is appealing. A nice track. The main title on the album it seems is the 7 parted Partial To The Bean (A Tragic American Quintogy). Like 25 it opens as an orgasm of sound with lots of things happening at the same time. Notwithstanding there's an air of jamming to this track. Gently we glide through the tracks such as Yoko Two-No which sound positively symphonic a bit in the style of UK, but less flashy. On Yoko Three-No the guitar takes over for an extended solo to hand it back to the Morgenstein/Sherinian duo for a weird interlude called Platmosis. Fast cymbals and weird sounds until we return to the rock of Yoko Againo ending with Yoko Outro with a good, melodic guitarsolo.

Conclusion

an album that lacks the variety of the previous album I'm sorry to say. The music is too much dominated by the song directed approach of Tabors other work (the same for his vocals), while on the debut the instrumental pieces have the album most of its charm. Now a sameness creeps over the music. If you are into this kind of thing (for instance if you liked Tabors Moonflower Lane), then the album is certainly worth a try, because it does not contain some nice passages that liven it up a bit. For me this is not enough.
