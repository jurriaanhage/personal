Summary

At this moment not for sale, but you can always contact them to see if this can change.

The music

The albums opens with harpsichord, cello and choral music, melodic and appealing. Tammorriata is the follow up, a complex sounding piece in which accessible guitar lines fight for dominance against more dissonant Crimsonesque riffs. The sound is a bit in the background here, also the guitar. The drums are quite up front and we have quite a busy drummer right here so they tend to run off when the other two are fighting. Typical progressive in a complex meandering style, this is certainly not as light hearted as Italian prog ordinarily is, but not as hard-edged as A Piedi Nudi either. Somewhere in between. This track it seems goes under in good intentions, because the tracks sounds very fragmented and disjointed. Pullecenella 'n' Croce is the next track and for some time the lengths of the tracks will increase. It opens with low down bass (contrabass?) and one is tempted to of experimental but moody jazz here. The sound seems a bit better. The drumming is again varied, but the music here is now more of the kind exhibited by RIO bands. Then the song turns into pacey rock in which the guitar dominates. The mix could still be a bit better, because the guitar is still a bit too far in the back. Adventurous music here on which the contrabass player tries to get as much out of his instrument as possible. Things other people usually try to keep in. Plenty of variation in this track maybe a bit too much. Compliment to the drummer, not only for keeping it up, but also for displaying such a groove. While I'm typing this it is hard to keep on typing instead of tapping along with the music. The guitar work toward the end is more melodic and I especially like the pointed, sharp playing in the last minute or so. Menadi Danzanti opens with percussion and a bubbling bass in the back. The music here has a Mediterranean feel to it, being a bit danceable. The guitarwork however is quite heavy (but not loud). The heaviness continues during the track, comparable to King Crimson, but it is also belied by the accessibility of the bouncy melody lines. The driven percussion continues to pervade the whole dragging it seems the rest of the music in its wake. I like the powerful up-tmepo parts, but sometimes it get to be a bit neurotic. 'O Vico then has a playful dissonant opening. Then the music develops into something more melodic, but not for long and not easily either. As such one has to search for some melody. There is no singing on this album, but there are some shouted words on this track, that has some folk influences as well and, and this one hear in every single track, a drivenness. Some of the guitar parts are a bit too meandering and I would hope for some more melody or maybe a little less variety would help. The final part of this track is a bit more easygoing, still quite sharp, but less loud and a bit free wheeling. Trupea'e Calore is with over 11 minutes the longest track on this album. Some classical influences in the up-tempo opening, and again some terrific drumming. This guy must have some influences from Arti & Mestieri's drummer. The bass pops in and out and even plays the main melody line. The rhythm guitar then abruptly takes over, but fades away slowly, wallows around in the back while the acoustic guitar takes over. Then the pace returns in something that sounds a like a chase on horseback. The middle part of this track is rather relaxed with some interesting sounds that I am hard put to recognize. A very free kind of music, but because of that also disjointed. The dark noisy guitar work takes over again with military drums, the return of the acoustic guitar. The final part is quite danceable again, although there are few people really dancing to rhythm guitars. A Resurrezione'e Pulcecenella opens subdued with tinkling keyboards and soft acoustic guitars. This is a totally different side of the band altogether. Halfway the pace goes up a bit, but it doesn't get loud, only bouncy. Then however the rhythm guitar sets in again, but as it is the playing of this guitar is quite melodic. Also quite tense and driven. The effects on the percussion are quite nice. One of the better tracks, mostly because of the good melodic yet driven guitarwork. The title track is the last major track on this album. The volume goes up quite abit which I think is a mistake in the mix. Heavy guitars on this one, this I could even call metal, but the playing is quite rough and noisy. The drummer lifts the pace a bit using a double bass drum. Rather hectic. Slightly before 3 minutes the music takes gas back moving into a moody phase, but the first part returns in full force. The closing instrumental is similar to the first track.

You can now read on in A Fine D'A Streppegna which is the follow up to this one continuing the story of Pulcinella.

Conclusion

This album now 3 years old is certainly not bad. The band shows to have some interesting views on what constitutes a song, they put a hugh amount of variety in their songs, but it doesn't get neurotic quite as often as you might expect. They work well with moods, which are usually of the not so friendly kind and the guitarwork is usually quite heavy which only adds to this. The drummer is great putting live in everything. The production does help him a bit, because he comes out a bit loud in comparison with the melodic instruments. The music also borrows from folk music (Italian I guess) and because of this may remind some people of Arabic influences. Adventurous and although I would like them to take it a bit more easy, pay more attention to atmosphere and giving everything its time to build up, this is a interesting prog album and band to get to know more closely.
