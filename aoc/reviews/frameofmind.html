Aztec Jade - Frame Of Mind

Artist: Aztec Jade
Title: Frame Of Mind
Label: Capital Music Group CMG 9704-2
Length(s): 42 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1997
Month of review: 12/1997

Line up

Timothy Becker - keyboards
Leon Ozug - vocals
Matt Howenstein - guitars
Bryan Kowalski - bass
Rick Miller - drums and percussion

Summary

If the promotional material is as good as the music we ARE in for a treat. Very professional looking.

The music

opens with lots of bombast and has overall a high tempo and a large and rather noisy sounding guitar presence. It enjoys a nice instrumental intermezzo and has a rather poppy chorus. Although the song is quite short there's plenty of variation. I haven't said this already but this band is thoroughly in the progressive metal vein, i.e. Dream Theater, Fates Warning, Queensryche and all the others. Contrary to these bands, the band does not write epics, but concentrates on short and varied tracks, and in that sense is similar to later Rush, but lots harder and quicker.

To Believe is the next track and it has some nice meters and tempo changes with thunderous drums and menacing church organ. I'm not fond of the singer when he sings in the higher regions though. All in all still a nice track.

The slow verses of Mad Not Crazy are quite okay, but I really don't like the up-tempo chorus. It's too farfetched and is strangely frolic. Maybe this is meant to illustrate the madness of the I of the story told in this song.

With Desperate Land the sameness creeps into the music. In fact what seems to be lacking is variety in the vocal melody although the additions made by the keyboards are often quite tasteful, the string like ones in this track, although the solo in the intermezzo has little purpose in my opinion.

Nosferatu is an acoustic balladlike track with dark voices whispering words and a good vocal melody. Maybe this track should have come before the previous one. It's a nice track although the subject of the lyrics, vampirism, is a bit trite. The organ like keyboards of To Believe not surprisingly return. In the middle the song picks up speed for the chorus and I'm not satisfied with that part.

After a nice intro the vocal melody of the first verse of Atlantis is not especially nice, but the melody of the chorus is rather striking and the almost jig-like intermezzo on the keyboards is also well-done. The bridge sounds a little unnatural.

Regatta Fugue II is a very poppy and optimistic track. I don't like it, but maybe the album needs the change. Stained enjoys a nice chorus and again the intermezzo is also quite nice, as often happens on this album. The break back after the intermezzo to the chorus is rather abrupt however.

Gates Of Babylon is not a cover of Rainbow's classic. The music naturally has some arabic influences and is rather repetitive with a poppy chorus. What strikes me in this song is that during the "verses", the music sounds like a wheel that wasn't greased well enough and that when the chorus starts, the wheel rotates easily.

The lyrics of the closer Requiem are impossible to misinterpret. The song is about everything that is wrong in this world. The song opens optimistically however, although very soon we come to an up-tempo driven part with double bass.

Conclusion

Obviously this band is in the prog metal vein, a line of music that is quite popular these days and a line of music that relatively many (relative to the total number of progressive bands) bands have chosen. Contrary to the more well-known progmetal bands such as Threshold, Fates Warning, Queensryche and Dream Theater, this band believes in the short song format with plenty of variation (quite often too much in my opinion and not well-dosed), but unfortunately the songs lack in the melodies of either the verses or the chorus. There are some memorable melodies on this album, but during the album a sameness can creep in. This sameness will probably disappear after a listen, but the band has this very typical metal tendency to make songs that sound the same. On the other hand the keyboards do a nice job in making the music more melodic, while the guitar is used often as a generator of a noisy background instead of a solo/melodic instrument. I would advice the band to take it easy in at least some of the songs, because as it is the music tends to be rather full and most listeners would like a breather once in a while. For me, I would advice the singer to take it easy in the higher regions, but that is a very subjective thing.
