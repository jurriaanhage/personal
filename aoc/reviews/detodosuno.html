Summary

For IO Pages I only recently got this tape to review by famous Mexican band Iconoclasta. I have their first two on one CD and I wonder if that album is still in the Guiness book of records for being the longest CD. You will not find any samples on my page of De Todos Uno, because I got it on tape (and I got it from Io Pages so no samples anyway).

The music

The album opens heavy on the guitar with a loud sound and rather hectic playing. This is powerful progressive/jazz rock. The sound of the guitar is rather biting and there's a lot happening all at the same time. Energetic track.

Invocacion is a bit repetitive and sounds a little too happy for my tastes and is quite heavy on the keyboards. Sounds a bit like a heavy metal version of Spyro Gyra (just kidding). Again, driving, over the top jazzrock, but not as empty as most jazz rock bands. Still, again, here the spectre of nowhereness is present.

The next one up introduces (female) vocals for the first time and although the vocal parts are not that complex, the intermezzo's are again high energy explosions of sound.

A Cada Paso is again instrumental and not one of the best. Some of the breaks in this tracks are just a little too much for me and the tempo changes just a little too often here. Also the melodies aren't really attractive.

La Etica del Verdugo is again a more melodic track with much guitar, both electric and acoustic. The vocal melody is very nice, but the guitar sound is very hard on my ears. At the end the drums are allowed to play a little, which is always nice.

The second side of the tape brings La Maternidad del Alacran. After the acoustic start we move into a frolic percussive part with a rather accessible guitar melody. Folk influences can be found in this song which is mostly played on the electric guitar although there is some acoustic guitar and a little piano playing along. In the middle of the track it seems at first that the songs character is about to change, but the musicians think better of it and although the bass now really starts to intrude, the tempo only picks up. All in all a nice track. A bit laid back in parts, but in others driving and the rather mellow guitar melody that is sometimes used is forgiven.

No Puedo starts as an acoustic classical guitar piece. A very nice piece by the way that along the way changes character to become quicker and more playful. At the end the intro comes back.

Following is the up tempo La Profecia. A neurotic track to say the least and again I'm not really charmed (as with A Cada), of the placement of the breaks but more even the uninteresting menadering guitar playing on this song. Very little attention to nice melodies, but more or less run of the mill jazz rock.

The longest track on the album is La Busqueda de la Verdad en Si Mismo. Again we find vocals on this track and with the acoustic guitar this enhances the (Spanish) folky roots of this track. The more rocking middle part of this track with some percussive, but not very prominent piano, is nice, but nothing to write home about.

Despendimieto is a rather quirky one expecially the keyboard parts, that can be likened to Genesis at their most playful.

Conclusion

Jazz rock with a difference you might call them. They are quite hectic and heavy to be sure, but there's a lot of drive and energy in the music. Still, on the whole some of the guitar solo's tend to go nowhere just a little too often.
