Summary

Recorded in 1984, this is the rerelease of Delirium's Theater of Delirium. In case you wonder what the titles mean, buy the CD, because the translation of the titles is on it.

The music

The titletrack opens with a synthesizerriff that sounds a little dated and is inspired mostly by ELP, but also makes you think they will start directly into Market Square Heroes. The drums sound a bit hollow and the singing is a bit loud. Technicalities like these usually rear their ugly heads, especially for self produced albums (and this one is already quite old). The intermezzo is up-tempo with mostly the keyboards up front. The vocals on this song are rather in the Italian style.

The next track has a high pace and a rather trite melody unfortunately. Typical instrumental prog, with lots of changes, busy drumming, but the keyboard melody is just a little too much for me.

El atrio de las animas breathes a calmer atmoshpere, starting with acoustic guitar and moving into mid-tempo afterwards. The melodies are better here. The guitar sound on this album reminds me a bit of 12th Nights instrumental period, and the track has an Ekseption twist to it. Very nice track.

The next one up is a slow one, with occassional bombastic outburst. The guitar solo is most important here and is high and clear while

Like Al atrio... Asilo De Ancianos also has that 12th Night sounds hidden in there, but this slow track sounds almost like EM. The vocals are rather dreary and desolate.

The next one has an intriguing melody and contains numerous tempo changes. This is no problem because the melodies are good and so we are led from one into the other, dragged along as it were. Again the keyboards are the main dish. At the end of the track it turns into almost circus music with increasing tempo and rather awkward intermezzo's.

Lagrimas starts a little shy. Slowly it builds up with nice melodies and in a way it reminds me of Eloy, also a band that used to build up their songs slowly and methodically with lots of melody and all a bit cosmic sounding. Then the song more or less erupts and we get typical up-tempo seventies prog with ELP keys and thus with the more Italian sound to the band, PFM is not far off.

Santo Officio is a catchy up-tempo track with singing, while the closer Nocturno En Caminata brings back ole ELP (and thus again PFM).

Conclusion

12th Night, Ekseption and ELP or better yet PFM are combined on this album, that has a seventies sound. The quality of the recording, a muddy mix which wreaks itself mostly in the drums and the vocals, leaves a lot to be desired, but what can you expect from a self production from Mexico in 1984. The musical material is nice and in fact here's just one track, Armageddon, I didn't particularly like. For the rest the music is mostly up-tempo or at least is partly so (save Asilo De Ancianos). The melodies are quite nice and themes recur on the album. On the whole a charming album, but like I said the recording could have been better.
