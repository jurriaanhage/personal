Summary

Citizen Cain I have already reviewed once when they were still on Silly Insects This album, Ghost Dance is on Mellow Records and contains songs from a 1984 Citizen Cain. A new album, Raising the Stones (supposedly very different), is in the making.

The music

The first track is rather a surprise. It is almost a-capella, and of course as always reminds a lot of Genesis. This is no surprise as Citizen Cain mimicks their big example quite well and throroughly. Remember though that it is not so that you hear parts of Genesis songs here, but mostly it's the way it is played and sung that remind us of the Gabriel era. So, the first track is very nice and moves into End of All Songs with a crash, but for the most part it is not so busy. Only later in the track does it become a little more involved and intricate. There are some very tricky breaks in there and the song isn't very easy to follow. Still, there's enough melody and structure to satisfy everyone, but some parts are definitely non-Genesis, especially the guitarsolo at the end, which by the way strikes me as going nowhere much.

Question of Sport reminds me more of Marillion. The song has a slow build-up with acoustic guitar, but becomes more rocky at the end. An ethereal track though, with percolative guitarplaying.

State of Confusion is not so good I think. The vocal melody is not that good and not so neatly done.

Missionary Position starts out bouncy as well. The vocals are rather high (Phil Collins in the higher regions) here and sound kind of strange. When the singer sings somwehat lower we can appreciate how much he sounds like Peter Gabriel. Then we get into one of those repetitive guitarriffs with interesting some signatures.

The title track has a rather medieval approach and reminds me more of Gentle Giant, also in view of the way it is sung. A driven song especially because of the drumming. It is also a bit chaotic.

Tabernacle of Hands is more or less in the same line: drumming against the grain and lots of guitar, but not very melodic. On this track the vocal melody is not so good however.

Unspoken Words is rather up-tempo and busy with lots of good drumming. The playing here also reminds me of Yes. This is mostly because Yes always played a less accessible version of progressive rock than the more melodic Genesis. Especially the guitarriffs can be non-melodious.

Conclusion

Well, for such old recordings, the sound is quite good, thought there might be a little hiss there. The music is driven, well executed, the singing is good (a la Gabriel) and the melodies are usually good, but sometimes the band seem to loose track inside a song. It is not all Genesis here, although the band is known as a Genesis copycat and some tracks remind me of Yes (the less melodic parts) and others even of the elusive Gentle Giant (because of the vocals). The keyboards are not so pronounced on this album and its mostly the guitar and the drums speaking and also then in this respect do they sound not like there forefathers before them. Strange, actually that should sound more like Genesis on what seems to be their later album Somewhere but Yesterday (chronologically that is, at least I do think the music on this CD is older, but if anyone knows better ....) For an album that seems to be meant to clear up some old material this is certainly not bad and I enjoyed it much. I'm curious to hear their new stuff.
