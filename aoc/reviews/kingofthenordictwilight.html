Summary

The guitarist of Rhapsody on his solo album like Rhapsody seemingly in the fantasy metal style.

The music

The album opens with bombastic operatic singing in the short intro To Magic Horizons. In the next one, Black Dragon the metal sets in: the music reminds me, as always, of Yngwie Malmsteen but is more bombastic and less focused on guitar. A nice ingredient is the intermezzo with its very fast paced violin sounding almost circus like. The metal part is not so great though. I find the music lacking in interesting melodies. Legend Of Steel is a baroque piece (a la Bach) with cembalo and flute. Then of course the bombasm sets in. This first instrumental part is quite okay, but then the vocal parts turns out to be less appealing. Lord Of The Winter Snow is a bit better, especially the metallic sounding keyboards in this track give it something extra. The singing is quite quick here and lends some urgency to the music. The chorus is a bit of the singalong type, a type that I tend to spot quite often on this album. A nice feature is the crazy-merry-go-round like part in the middle. The circus is back in town. Princess Aurora is quite a beautiful ballad sung by Rannveig Sif Sigurdardottir. A Sweeping, melodic track, a bit melodramatic perhaps and sung with quite an accent, but nonetheless quite appealing. Of course, this has to be thoroughly compensated for with a high paced nervous track such as The Ancient Forest Of Elves. Well fantasy all over and musically a folk-metal approach. The music again shows to be very much in the style of Rhapsody, although I have to say that the quality of Rhapsody is much higher. Still, not a bad one. Quite catchy and it works. The short Thorn Of Ice is next with its Latin operatic vocals. Nice melodies, but its too short to be considered a real song. The driven Where Heroes Lie is next up. Some good melodies in here, especially in the chorus and also featuring some nice violin. Warrior's Pride is a ballad as sung by a travelling singer in medieval days. The cembalo is the prominent instrument here with its choirs and good melody a sweeping but melodramatic track. In the choir parts I often have to think of Russia and cosacks. Just a remark, but also the quicker parts fit in with this. Now we come to the longest track on the album which is the title track. After some piano we get a powerful part sung in Latin. Again lots of choir singing which lends the music on this album its greatest "novelty" compared to Rhapsody's music; the singing lends some real drama to the music. The ordinary metal vocal part is up-tempo, but not imposing although it has its moments even then. The album should have closed with the long titletrack, but we have another of those ghost tracks right here. This time it is quite short: a beautifully sung a-capella piece.

Like with Rhapsody this is a continuing story. However, for the story one should not listen to this music. The story however is not very interesting and full of cliches.

Conclusion

The music is like is said on the cover: Epic Fantasy Metal. The music is not up to par with Rhapsody's latest album, but will help some to at leats pass the time until they see fit to release some new stuff. The music is of course quite bombastic, with quite a lot of operatic singing. Strongest negative point I feel to be the often weak melodies in the early parts of the album. Also later this sometimes occurs, but it turns out that especially the choir work is quite nice, and they are more prominent there. Newcomers to the style, over-the-top-bombastic-speed-metal with classical/folk influences, I would advise to buy Symphony Of The Enchanted Lands.
