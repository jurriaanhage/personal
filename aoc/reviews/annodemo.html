Jacula - Anno Demoni

Artist: Jacula
Title: Anno Demoni
Label: Mellow Records 118
Length(s): 33 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1992
Month of review: 02/1996

Line up

Antonio Bartoccetti - vocals, guitars, bass
Doris Norton - vocal, synths
Albert Goodman - drums
Charles Tiring - church organ, piano

Tracks

1) Anno Demoni 11.58
2) Missanigra 4.52
3) Jacula The Witch 2.46
4) Ego Sum Qui Sum 7.40
5) Soul Satan 5.40

Summary

Jacula is a strange band. The music is said to evolve during seances. Lead by Antonio Bartoccetti, they released one album in 1972. This album contains rest material from the period 1969-1979. Some of it seems to be live, although you can't tell from the music. I think everybody during that concert in Brno was told to shut up or otherwise... One might gather from the titles, that this album is rather in the satanistic vein. I too think it is, but don't let that bother you, because...

The music

the music is good, surprisingly good, although not overly varied. I think the best way to think of this, rather short, album is as an album by Tangerine Dream, having being banned to hell and asked to give a concert there (Froese or otherwise will have to learn to talk Latin first, however, but maybe a crash course can be provided). Differences are that it has a lot less tempo than the average TD release and they probably don't use sequencers, but do use a large amount of organ and church organ (very fitting!).

Jacula the Witch is vocalized by a clear female voice. Of the songs it's the most accessible and is actually quite peaceable. If this is a witch, it can't be all that bad.

4 is the most sinister track on the album, sounding at first like there's a few demons singing a chorus. We gte a sort of slow motion interplay between deformed vocals, church organ and violin.

The last track is the only rock that might qualify as progressive ROCK. The song is melody is played on the bass(which is rather unusual) and some background synth are added. The vocalist however does try his best to make his performance sound as, well, unpolished as possible. It's sort of funky, with a little guitar, but like I said mostly and the vocals are sung like the track is a joke. Terrible.

Some people might not want to like this music purely on the basis of the satanism involved, and of course they can if they like. Fact is this is a good album, with only one bummer: the last track. The vocals on this track are loud (contrary to the subdued, sinister vocals or friendly female vocals on the rest of the album).

Conclusion

A good album, not for everyone, but lovers of dark atmospheres a la TD's OST Sorcerer or some Pink Floyd or better yet Pulsar stuff should certainly give them a try, although the album is very short (bummer, bummer). Funny thing about this album is the recurring synth sound of a small bird.
