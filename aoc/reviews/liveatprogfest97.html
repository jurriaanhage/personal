Summary

Strange record this but as it was later explained to me, the second disc is the original slightly longer recording of Aurum Nostrum. The album itself is a live concert from Progfest and the label thought it would be nice to add the second disc (for free). The differences with the Aurum Nostrum released on Cyclops last year is not large. Still the band now has three rather full discs with only five different songs. Long ones, but still. Also with this disc come the lyrics of the songs in English. They're still sung in Swedish though.

The music

One might also look up the review of Aurum Nostrum that I made when that came out. Nothing much has changed. The longingly sounding Manuel is still my favourite track with a haunting melody on the flute. Agren (Mr. Agony) is typified by a much heavier sound and lots of organ contrasted and combined with folklike passages and a great melodic part at the end. The new track on the album is Jag, Anglamarks Bane (I, Ripper Of Nature). I guess this is a new track, at least I had never heard it. After a slow plodding beginning we come to a more abstract part with organ and highpitched guitar. The song brims with organ and is less sweet than the previous tracks, quite menacing in fact. Snalblast (Bitter Wind) starts out melodically again but soon the cold blowing winds take over. The song also has some merry lapses as well. Of all the tracks on this album I think I like this one the least. The last track Attestupan sounds a bit superficial and constructed. The melodies are also less appealing.

Conclusion

The music contains lots of mellotron (a la early King Crimson), but also obvious pointers to Genesis and with the flute also to Camel and Jethro Tull. There is nothing amiss with the performance of the band on the live disc and they make the music come out well. Differences with the studio album are not that strong, live the music is a little more dynamic and brimming but also at times a bit messier, but I guess a number of people will still find the music a bit slow moving (a la Fruitcake). The songs are often quite sad although sometimes a merry melody pops up. Having grown accustomed to the band and the slowly evolving music, the band can evoke the right emotions and paint the right pictures, but sometimes I still get a little lost in a composition like AEttestupan.
