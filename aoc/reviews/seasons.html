Summary

Nothing known about them here. Well, they did Lillywhite Lilith on the Genesis Tribute and Lizard Play on Eyewitness. This albums also contains I know what I like. Well, they probably recorded it for the tribute and then it was decided not to put it on.

The music

What about the rest of the album then. Well, again a band with many a face. Most of the music is in the progressive pop vein, but the influences are diverse and that's what makes it progressive.

First of all they can rock like Hogarth era Marillion (this is meant as a complement and I'm not saying that Fish can't rock either) This also has to dowith the slight ressemblance of the vocals of this band with said singer. Anyway it starts out all mysteriously with acoustic guitar and then percussion comes through giving rise later on to a rather frolic track with accented but certainly acceptable singing. Later in the track the song becomes more powered and it ends in world music. Well this just about shows you what this band can pour onto its unaware listener. The next track is more or less in the same vein. I do not mean it's a copy, but rather extended parts of the first one. In a sense every so song is grouped with a few others to comprise a new song (although this entirely my idea). Anyway the second is far more rock and can be compared to Paper Lies with a little more risk.

Still however is really Still with some accordeon added for flavour. Anyway, Reflections is a lot more playful, while the next track is very interesting and fresh soounding. Very percussive.

Mist Secret is a misty track (really!) and is an introduction to the rather rocky follow-up. The basswork is appreciated, but as a whole the track is a little too simple. The threesome is concluded by the melancholy Voice of the Snow.

The next threesome is started of by a simple, folky tune. The next track isn't that good, a bit in the vein of the new Fish sound (i.e. a bit boring). Fortunately it gets more powerful along the way. The threesome closes with a colourful ballad.

Well, I know what I like is...well, Genesis right? Rather danceable this version.

In Nagual there's a lot happening and it's hard to say what. Hope that helps, but I guess it doesn't.

Aaah, the next track is very definitely like Fish's Big Wedge. Definitely.

The albums closes with the recurrence of the first some tracks (as an afterlude of some sort). Anyway it's quite in the Holidays in Eden style of Marillion.

Conclusion

If there's one thing that characterizes this band then it's obstinacy, but in the good sense of the word. Well, hmm, don't know if you can take this word the right way, but in my language you can, so there. They certainly have a way of combining worldlike influences, plain rock (with some Marillion touches), accordeon and some quiet and also some varied pieces. Rather hard to pinpoint the best track, but the last one and the second one would do fine as a try out. A colourful try, and there's potential there certainly. The album does lack a bit of fire all over.

Oh yes, the cover. Well I'm not going to scan the insides of this CD, so to see it, you should buy it yourself. But I told you they were obstinate.
