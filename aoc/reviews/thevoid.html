Summary

Wih already four albums to his name, this is my first encounter with Bjorn. The artwork is very SF, and very nice by Tobias Richter reachable at tobias@lightworks.com.

The music

The music of Lynne is rather electronic. It also has some progressive rock touches, but the keyboards and synthesizers are by far the prominent instruments on this instrumental CD. I tend to compare Lynne with someone like Steve Hillman, also because of their use of the guitar while the approach to the music is more like EM. The links with Hillman are for instance very apparent in the first track following the cosmic intro. A rather rocking track, with the theme played on guitar and both cosmic and percolating synth in the back. Nice atmospheres. The keyboard parts are rather dark a la TD, while the guitar parts are high and clear.

While All Life is One part 3 is played I can tell you a little of what the album is in fact about, because it's another of those concept albums (no offence Bjorn, you should know we wallow in them). It's about this person in a faster than light ship heading out for the voi dnot being able to stop, because of technical failure. He knows he will see many things before his death, but also knows that he will never be able to tell anyone. After his death he knows the ship wil fly on. For ever?

Now to continue with the music All Life... started all friendly and downbeat, but at some point percussion sets in and the song becomes more involved. Again, this is one of those albums with programmed percussion and infact a lot of track have percussion and I do think it attracts too much attention. At the end the track takes a turn for the (melo)dramatic.

The EM played on Electroglow is rather updated binding a strong rhythm to cosmic synth tapestries. The "hup" sounds in this track give it a sort of ambient house atmosphere and in fact one might actually say that it is. On the whole it kind of goes on and nothing much happens melodically. Now and then a melody pops up, but not for long.

Dar-Shan has a rather prominent melody that is somewhat reminiscent of Gandalf with his albums Fantasy and Tales from a Longforgotten Kingdom. Again, what strikes me is the prominent percussion. The heavy drums are nice and in fact the melodies of this track are rather good and the track shows a lot of variation.

On the Edge? is in fact on the edge between EM and house, Truth or Sanity? part 3 is a collection of sounds, while Relentless is a long bleeping track again rather reminiscent of house (or modern EM). The vocoded parts at the end sound like "She got me" to me.

Signals at first sounds like a collection of sounds, but after a while it turns out to have a melody as well and also the guitar rears its ugly frets again.

Who Knows is a rather flowing track although with percussion. The song is kind of emotive and maybe a little melodramatic.

The closer The Nothing starts very moody. Slow percussives and a desolate melody played first on piano and later on organ. Also, the music is accompanied by many spacey sounds and after a while the music becomes at first up beat and hen after a few minutes the melodies return on synth and organ and then the music takes a turns for the majestic. A track that makes for contemplation.

Conclusion

This album is not so progressive in the strict way. It's mostly electronic, but rather percussive and at times it approaches ambient house. IMO there is nothing wrong with that. The melodies (if there are any) are usually nice and the music reminds me mostly of Steve Hillman (also on Cyclops) and a little of TD. The drumming on this album is automated and as you might know I'm not too fond of that, especially if it is as prominent as it is.
