Rolf Munkes Band - Demo

Artist: Rolf Munkes Band
Title: Demo
Label: no release as yet
Length(s): 12 minutes
Year(s) of release: 1999
Month of review: 09/1999

Line up

Rolf Munkes - guitars, bass
Gerald Kloos - drums

Tracks

1) Confuse Them All 3.53
2) That's New 4.05
3) Tap That Thing 4.00

Summary

Rolf Munkes and Gerald Kloos played together in the German band Fallacy between 1989 and 1991. This band supposedly made progressive rock with a classical tinge. Having received many awards for his playing, it still remains to be seen whether the compositions of the man are as gratifying.

The music

Being a prerelease demo CD, it only contains three rather short tracks. The music can be quite complex and varied, but not lacking in melody. What strikes me most is the energetic and the fast, clean playing. The drumming is also quite involved and loud at times, but for proggers a bit too uneventful. There are some breaks to try and keep it a bit interesting, but later that happens less and less. The sound of the drums is not how I would like it, a bit too dry. The music is a strongly rocking variant of jazzrock rather like Steve Vai, with plenty of breaks and fortunately some good melodies (such as in the opening of both the second and the third track). With regards to the first track I can remark that some parts of it feel a bit unnatural and the song does not flow well enough. The second track is a bit better: a nice drive and a good riff. The track contains at least a few worthwhile riffs and licks that hold each other nicely in balance, while the free drumming does something to accentuate. The third track is also strongly riff based, but there are even some bouncy up-tempo parts which one would not expect in his music.

Conclusion

Decent instrumental rock with jazzrock leanings. The production could improve a little, integrating the drums better with the rest of the music. One of the problems with this kind of, often self indulgent, music lies in the compositions, which more often than not get snowed under. The strong melodic content of the guitar playing helps here, but some of the music sounds a bit fragmentary: stringing themes and riffs together in a way that doesn't strike one as obvious or natural (very subjective, I agree, and in this case I refer to the opening track only). Still, there are strong implications that these are exactly the things that can and will become better as the band progresses to the full length album stage.
