Summary

After the classic previous album, Creazione, the band is finally back with a new album, Eclissi (Eclipse).

The music

opens speedy with fast rhythm guitar on Esodo. The powerful guitars are still present as well as the dark atmosphere that was also present on Creazione. The drummer does not sing anymore, a job that is now done by newcomer Mirko Andreasi. Maybe good for the live performance, but I think the drummer handled the job very well. Mirko is okay, but not noticeably better. This opener is a very strong track, coming close to progressive metal and as usual with a lot of breaks and recurring passages and at times almost filmic (not quiet, but in the sense that it seems it was written to go with a film). L'Inganno continues, more or less, on the same heavy footing, with more grooving, riffbased (vs. melodic) guitar with also some bluesy guitar work. Again, the tempo is very high, with some "inaccessibilities" added for good measure. In fact, the song seems like a freighttrain on the loose. A driven track, but a departure from Creazione. Le Amanti is something else again. Percolating guitar and keywork, more melodic and lyrical. The melodies are very nice and the song is more atmospheric than the previous ones. Some very nice progressions on this one and on the whole a great track. Senza Ritorno opens organically and bombastically with everything seemingly playing what at that moment he would like to play. The singer sings quite loud here, wanting to be heard I suppose. This is something I can't remember Bighetti doing on Creazione and maybe they wanted this more powerful voice. The longer Reverendo opens very moodily with lots of tensionbuilding dissonant piano and consists of melodic singing, percussive piano and again a large dose of heavy, raunchy guitar and in the softer regions, some moody bass playing. Temporale adds to the sound of A Piedi Nudi, lone horn playing, weird backing vocals, a neurotic part on the keys. The music of this band is like an ocean: waves spilling over you, immersing, drowning you, but always the water disappears, before it can actually do so, making it for many people at least, not a present experience to listen to this band. Of L'Infidele I'd like to say that it is enlivened by the injections of seventies inspired organ and it has a lush ending with keyboards and free horn playing. After another energy draining beginning, Amici D'Infanzia, which contains some soft, classical piano and some nice atmosphere changes, the album closes down with the title track. I could say that this song "includes all the band has to offer" but that holds for most of the other tracks as well. The song does contain some Arabic stylings and opens with acoustic guitar and on the whole the track builds up quite nicely with nice, Arabic styled percussion and horn. Like on the previous album it seems we are going to end softly, but wait....the guitar comes back with a vengeance.

The artwork is VERY nice with rather confronting paintings inside explaining more or less the Italian lyrics. Very dark and somber.

Conclusion

After the first two tracks the band seemed to have moved from the atmospheric side to the more hard rocking side: heavy, fast playing on mostly the guitar and lots of riffing, instead of melodic solos. This does not mean the tracks are bad, but I prefer to hear some variation in the tracks. Fortunately this variation is more present in the later tracks, although the band certainly has become a heavier band than on Creazione. The dark and somber atmosphere is still present and the singing seems to be more loud and prominent on this album and this might take some getting used to. A recommended though energy draining album by this Italian band.
