Summary

This is one sampler that will get many affirmative nods. The Japanese progressive scene has stayed for many prog listener outside of scope. Since Musea obtained the rights to release many of the albums in Europe the bands have become more well-known, but my idea is that still only die-hards will involve themselves with these bands. Not rightly so I think and this sampler with almost every band of note present and with both somewhat older (the last six) and newer songs (the first seven).

The music

The female rockers of Ars Nova open with their key-riddled brand of ELPish progressive. Bands like Mastermind and even more, Cairo, come to mind with an occasional nod to UK, but instrumental and more orchestral. Some clavecimbel on this fast moving track that might even remind some of Ekseption. Large variations in mood, tempo and melody on this track. The band does take on a lot on this track. A little too much I feel.

Maria by Teru Symphonia I remember a being quite a weak track. The song combines neo-progressive guitarrock with orchestral bombasm and female Japanese vocals. It is hard to point to direct references (Landmarq maybe?), but fact is that the band walks a fine line between kitsch and art.

Motoi Sakuraba performs his Tides Of History from Force Of Light. One of the better melodies on this one, played beautifully on flute with (soft) choirs and percussive piano. The song is not very bombastic which is a relief. A subtle piece of work, but with a slight bite towards the end.

Wappa Gappa is one band I am familiar with having seen them play live at Progworld 99. Floating Ice opens rather quietly. I even remember them playing the song, since it has quite a recognizable melody in the chorus. The band itself plays a diverse kind of music varying from these quiet mellow pieces to something akin to jazzrock. This is one of the nicer tracks in my opinion, having a certain kind of identity and good melodies.

Gerard you might know from my review of Meridian. Chaos is Gerard at their freaky best.

Side Steps sounds more like one of those soft fusion outfits than a progressive one. A very dreamy track, but also one that I can easily fall asleep to, and those handclappings certainly will not help keeping me awake. Later on the song becomes more percussive and contains a lot of acoustic guitar. The melody is sometimes quite nice even, but the listless arrangements make it all go to waste.

The last one from the "new" songs is by Makoto Kitayama with Shingetsu Project. The intro promises a dissonant piece of work, but this short track is dominated by sleepy Japanese vocals, a softly wailing violin and piano. I wonder why they gave this song an English title?

The last six tracks are from rereleases on Musea. First we get Magdalena with their short Left Alone. A classical piece this with lots of sounds, but strangely sounding far away. The vocal parts are again too mellow for my tastes. It seems the Japanese are not rock singers.

Bellaphon's Labyrinth was originally from 1987. The song was rerecorded in 1998, making this some kind of new song. It struck me first that the recording quality was less than with the other '98 songs. An instrumental, thoroughly melodic work with playful basswork, sweeping melodies on the keys and plenty of drumming. The song does lack real tension and when the song goes into easy jazzrock at the end, this certainly does not help. For mellotromaniacs: listen to the last part.

Mugen plays Venezia from Sinfonia Della Luna. Comparatively quite a lot of noise on this one (it IS the oldest track). The song is a rather quiet one and includes quite a lot of classical influences.

Outer Limits opens with Spanish guitar, which is not strange since the title of the track is Spanish Labyrinth. The violin on this track lends a certain tension to the music. The middle part is more melodic while the continuation touches upon Ravels Bolero. Not your typical Japanese prog, being more in line with Spanish flamenco progressive, but certainly not bad.

Deja Vu's Prelude is coming a bit late so at the end of this compilation disc. Not a very long track it does sound like one of those typical orchestral intro's to concept albums.

The album does close with Pageants Epilogue. The vocals of the singer sound longing, the piano lays the basis, while the flute adds to the spectrum. It all sounds a bit like Quidam, but more potent. The guitar solo (the first on the album?) is very good. An emotional track and one of the better, if not the best. It does sound a bit familiar though.

Conclusion

For the price alone a worthy thing to buy giving you access to many newcomers and classic Japanese acts from the eighties. A critical note against virtually all the songs here is the lack of tension and tightness in the compositions. The instrumentals are far too often strings of (often beautiful) themes, but lack bite and direction. There are a few exception, such as Gerard and Pageant, but on the whole the Japanese bands seems very directed towards theme.
