The music

The album opens lightly with The Sound Scary. Here I hear something akin a fugue with bass and acoustic guitar. After this rather quiet introduction the band bursts loose with Choke. Not a very melodic track, but more groovy and rocking. The vocals are varied and both male and female. The verse reminds me of Paul Simon's Sixty (was it?) Ways To Leave Your Lover. Ostrich sounds rather spaceous, yet not spacey. Wah-wah guitar, All About Evish vocals, and again quite groovy with involved percussion and bass playing. The openess of the music, especially in the guitar sound, continues into Foreshadow. This style is continued into Neurotic Heart. Huck Friend is quite different: in this rather up-tempo acoustic instrumental the band combines country music with some good melodic material. Later the electric guitar sets in and a jazzrock mood is evoked. Tribal Spirit is quite a melodic track, but with, like most other tracks here, a definite groove. The music does sound a bit down and to some will sound at times more "alternative" than "progressive", but I guess this also has to do with the European point of view on progressive. We Don't See has two faces: the face of the vocal tracks, but also later something akin to Gordian Knot's cover of the Bach track. In Thicker the name Lives And Times pops into my mind. To be honest, the music of Die A Tribe sounds better, rocks, grooves and is more varied, but I do see a danger in the overal selfsimilarity of the music. Mighty Simba (The Lion King?) is slightly Arabic, and the harmonies are not very strong. Fear reminds me of a number of things, which I cannot name. It is quite experimental and contains plenty of different moods and styles. A particularly interesting track. Closer is Love's Majestic Promise that has that Arabic influence again. The vocals are also somewhat Arabic, but here it is also that the music becomes somewhat more alternative than progressive, the band seems to hang in between.

Conclusion

Groovy and very much directed towards the guitar is the music of this band. The vocals are good, but the songs are a bit chaotic, particularly because of the varied percussion. The band seems interesting to people who would enjoy Echolyn, Finneus Gauge (without the harmonies) and Gordian Knot, although admittedly they not (yet) of that level, but coming close nonetheless. One might say that Die A Tribe lacks the sophistication, but still many of their melodies are worthwhile and the music is certainly not easy. The band also adds quite a lot of groove to their music, which is a good thing. I do have to warn them however that their music is particularly well-suited to be selfsimilar.
