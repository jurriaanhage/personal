Juno - The Magic Lantern

Artist: Juno
Title: The Magic Lantern
Label: self produced
Length(s): minutes
Year(s) of release: 1996
Month of review: 05/1998

Line up

Bas van Boven - drums
Joost Doesburg - bass, add. keyboards, vocals
Bas Jansen - keyboards, vocals
Maaike Roosendaal - vocals
Paul Willemsen - guitar, add. keyboards, vocals

Tracks

1) Wake Up And Dream On
2) The Old Days
3) Petty Please
4) Magic Of Love
5) The Fair
6) The Magic Lantern
7) Justification (Just A Vacation)

Summary

After going through a phase playing all kinds of music, the Dutch band Juno has embraced progressive rock as their vehicle. Although the line-up is not stable yet (soon to come?) and this demo tape is already quite old I still thought I'd take some time to review it. Although they have another demo out of which the music on this new demo is part, this is the only "progressive" on that first demo and I believe it is not officially obtainable.

The music

Wake Up And Dream opens the demo and has a certain Cantebury feel with Fender Rhodes. The vocals (the voice is good, but on a CD more attention must be given to them) are also slightly jazzy and quirky in the way that the vocals of other more prominent Cantebury bands generally are. The atmosphere of the track is rather laid-back. In the middle there's a rather heavy-on-the-hand organ part. Very dark. The Old Days reminds more of Yes of the first two albums, sounding quite uplifting with a slight sixties feel. The intermezzo is a rather complex one, while this part also contains some Giantesque harmony vocals (okay only two but you have to start somewhere). The guitar solo is quite slow and again the presence of Hammond organ is felt. To my feeling the singer still sings a little too reservedly and I would appreciate some more emotions put into the music. Now it seems she's just singing the lyrics. Petty Please is a jazz tinged track with jazzy drumming and varied vocals. Non-straightforward signatures abound and there's even a slight step into psych here. Very playful track. Magic Of Love is a soothing love song, a bit dreamy, with the male vocals sometimes a bit weak. This acoustic song more or less jostles along and we move right into the bouncy The Fair, that is sung by Maaike again. Here the Cardiacs come to mind, but a LOT slower. The Hammond is quite prominent again, and the interplay between the bass and the keyboards is also nice. The title track does not offer much new, but it does strike me that the music could use an infusion of energy here. Justification is one of the better tracks on this album opening with a wailing guitar and some nice mood changes throughout. Again the lack of energy wreaks itself and on the whole a Renaissance atsmophere is created throughout this track.

Conclusion

This tape probably has not been produced and in fact the music sounds quite live to me. The music of this band certainly holds a promise if they can get it together, but I would advise them to put more energy and emotion into their music. It all sounds quite laid-back now. Although this tape should give a good impression of what they can do with good melodies and nice combinations of ideas, they should also give some thought to their eventual sound, since it will be hard, I think, to make a consistent album with influences coming from the Cardiacs, Yes, Gentle Giant and the Canterbury school. Appealing music, now a record company and technical know-how to make it all come out.
