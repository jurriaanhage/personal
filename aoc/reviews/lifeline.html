Summary

George Konings used to be George Velvet of which a Dutch review can be found on my review site. In this review I state that I wasn't really impressed, although the singing and melodies were alright, but it just didn't captivate. The music was then melodic rock with many solos. Look what became of it.

The music

The booklet starts with a story containing all the titles of the album. The story being rather vague and philosphical does not however reflect the contents of the album. The House of Love in the story is where one can share, hold, touch and reach the oneness of body, mind, spirit and soul, but in the lyrics it only sex he's talking about and a 'supersexy lady'. I mean it's just like the covers of those video games you used to buy in the shops: the promise a lot, but on your screen it looks like shit.

Well, now the music: The already mentioned 'supersexy lady' and also 'all i wanna do is have some fun' (sounds familiar) do not sound not very inspirational, but the song containing the latter does seem to be good. A large change with respect to the previous CD is that the sound is more diverse (less concentration on guitar and more on the song) resulting in some quite good songmaterial as evident from 2 (although it contains 'All i want to do is have some fun'), 3 (very melancholy. Partly reminds me of the soft sad vocals in The Cask of Amontillado). The fourth track is not satisfactory, although I like the part where the vocals are different. The fifth track is more or less a ballad and I like it very much. Very emotive singing and playing. The guitar playing has a definite Egdon Heath sound.

The title track has a good melody but it never really gets started, while next is boring overall. The next track is better with some Duran Duran influences here in the vocals. The refrain is especially good and to the point.

The 'magnum opus' of the albums is the almost instrumental From Hell to Heaven. The track starts out very slowly and has a bluesy feel over it with a moaning guitar. A very good track played with feeling although it might be a trifle too long.

Conclusion

Melodic rock. Can be trite at times, but the melodies are usually good and the meaningless meandering has given in to better songwriting. At first I didn't think much of it and of course it still holds that loads of music like this has been made, but not when released privately. The second listen I rather enjoyed, but still, it's melodic rock, so the avid prog fan needn't really bother. Fans of melodic rock might check it out when they can find it. You could do worse.

BTW it's interesting how all the pages are backed by photographs of dunes, while the page before last is backed by what seems to be a Giger drawing.
