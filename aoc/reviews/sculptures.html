Summary

The music

My, my, life remains challenging. This album contains material that has influences in rock, pop, wave, progressive and probably a few veins more. Guitars tend to be riffy, vocals sound weird pretty often, with strange vocal bends that regularly remind me of the earlier solo works of Godley & Creme (squeaky voices, as well as near angelic harmonies). The rhythm section tends to rumble, sounding far from tight. This keeps the boredom away, but does raise the brows at times. As the album moves the tracks tend to sound more sparse, with dominant guitar riffing, at times bordering on hardrock, and the vocals sounding more and more shrill, as well as insecure.

Conclusion

The straight guitar dominated sound supported by non too challenging bass and drum can wear out pretty soon. Whereas this might be compensated for by the interesting and somewhat weird vocals, the insecurity and wavering quality, at times bordering on hysteria (like The Cure's Robert Smith after going REAlly over the edge), completely nullifies this possible effect. The result is an album that sounds more absent minded, abstract even, than it does sound experimental. If you also consider the raw, punky sound of the production, the evidence piles up. Sculptures hardly makes for a nice listening experience.
