Summary

For the first time the complete recording of Mouse available. This will especially be good news for people outside the Netherlands, since within the Netherlands a number of people could obtain Mouse then released on SI Music. As you all know, the company folded quickly after and only now is it available. An added bonus is that it also includes the Meeting (Act V) earlier on released as a mini. Note that I did take the trouble this time to type out all the titles. They also changed the artwork, for the better I think and the thick booklet includes all the lyrics and texts.

The music

The Dark is typical of Aragons instrumentals. Dark, melodic and even sinister. This continues on into the rocking A Private Matter. Hard riffs, high tones on the keyboards like bells and percussion (hard to tell they are programmed) now dominate. Waiting On A Life is the first vocal track. Well if you haven't heard them earlier, the vocals of Les Dougan may take some getting used to. They can be grating and harsh, almost growling, but also friendly. If you are looking for comparisons I think Marillion's Brave comes closest musically -- yet Brave is more songdirected and Mouse sound and story directed --, and vocally, well nothing I guess. Sometimes a passage sounds very familiar, and I think this is not just due to knowing the music on these albums, but also because some parts seem to be based on an earlier album Don't Bring The Rain. In The Gate the tempo goes up again. Already in Act 2, The Faceless Face Of The Faceless Majority, we start with End Of The Line. Dark ponderous percussion evoking a deserted plant. Untying The Knot is a weird one reminding me of Japans Obscure Alternatives. The next part of this track is quite different, screaming claustrophobic rock, while the last part is quite friendly again. Cold In A Warm Place is a slow, bluesy piece with Floydian guitar, The Gathering a restful piece, like the lapping of water and the light tinkling of Under The Eye. In Act 4 follows the confrontation with cold vocoded vocals provoking Mouse into a decision about his future. Now we come to the Meeting, the mini album that comprises Act 5. The first track is called The Meeting is a regular song in the style of Don't Bring The Rain. Many of the pieces on the album are just fragments that strung together make up this concept album, but this act has some more elaborate songs that can very well stand on their own. (Of course, it doesn't really matter if you cut up a song of 6 minutes into 4 pieces if the music stays the same.) The Chant is a powerful one, with lots of heavy percussion; contrast that with the acoustics of the Midsummer's Nights Dream. Tugging At The Heartstring enjoys a memorable powerful chorus while The Changeling is with 9 minutes by far the longest song on the album. It even features slide guitar. The last track on the first disc is On The Edge, an emotive piece featuring saxophone.

On disc 2 we continue with the last three acts. Musically I guess I have more or less indicated what you can expect. Let me point some more things: the neo-progressive guitar in Shocked, the fragile Waiting For The Big One, the plodding aggressive Brave New World, the acoustic The Switch and the uplifting The Cross. The album closes with Auld Lang Syne.

After this we get a 12 minute interview about the making of Mouse. It strikes me that one of the interviewed sounds like Alan Reed of Pallas.

About the story: it is quite a long one with much more story than lyrics. Mouse is a person who lives in a dark and somber world "growing up" as one might say encountering various people and situations, most of them not pleasant. In a sense I think the story has some parallels with The Lamb, growing up and maturing, but more serious and modern one might say.

Conclusion

Updated and completed this album is something that the selfrespecting progressive lover will not want to miss. Good melodies, good playing (I even do not mind the drum machine!), interesting story, good production and sound quality, and overall a well-crafted atmosphere make this one of the future classics of prog. This may sound pretentious, but let's just assume I know what I'm talking about. In quality it is on the level with IQs Subterranea and Ayreon's Into The Electric Castle. The music on this album can in not be compared to Aragons previous album Mr. Angel on the same label that contained quite light songs.
