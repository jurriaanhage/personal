Summary

Single known release of Damon Lewis Shulman, son of one of the members of Gentle Giant.

The music

All songs written by Shulman. The music is well-played, well-produced and well-sung. Shulman has quite a good voice. When he sings he sounds like he's singing in a wide open space (eg. Rain).

The band has been named after a song by Steve Reich but that doesn't help you with their the music. The music isn't very much in the progressive vein in the strictest sense (maybe a bit RIO). The music is at times jazzy, funky and freaky. The lyrics are all well thought through (including the manifesto (though he doesn't think it to be one) in Swim Against The Tide in which he talks and talks about his ideas (life how it is). Most songs have a short prelude containing strange sounds and weird noises that are supposed to be introductory.

The songs on this album can be divided among three categories: the melodic songs which include Dust In The Wind (though it's a bit jumpy through the overt percussion), In My House, Rain (one of the best songs on the album with freaky intermezzo, but closes down with a good guitar solo) and lastly To Be Continued (synthesizertune). The other category contains the jazzy or funky songs, and ranks among it This is life (one of the weaker songs), Work, Sweet children (with Ambient intro). The other short songs are just plain weird ditties while the thrird categorie is formed by songs that defy categorization: Swim Against The Tide contains talk and some background music for the first eight minutes and then moves into guitardominated jazzrock. The Tired Tide the prelude to the former is kind of Gentle Giant like with the vocals used. The last song on the album exemplifies the album best: Cruel Trick has Latin influences for the first five minutes, but very repetitive. Then we get two minutes flute/glockenspiel after which we get plain jazzrock with some organ (like begin seventies). At the end we have a keyboardsolo to round it all off.

Conclusion

An interesting album but musically not very interesting to fan of melodic progressive rock. It might be intersting though for people interested in the more complex kinds of this type of music and then mostly the jazzy and freaky side of it. Someone that comes to mind (and to whom this album has been dedicated) is Frank Zappa. Though the influences of Mr. Shulman are more easily heard, he is a bit less mature, the freakiness of some of his recordings might be due to a love for Mr. Zappa. I was also able to draw parallels with the peculiar side of a band like 10cc. They also worked some strange ideas into their songs, while keeping the songs themselves sounding accessible. The album is meant for open-minded people I guess. Am I open-minded? Well I thought so, but that doesn't mean that I like this album. So do I like this album? Well, it has it's charms and is quite nice to listen to at times, but not something that I would play very often (I like Zappa but do not play his stuff very often as well). The best song on the album is Rain IMO, but the song most exemplifying the band is Cruel Trick. I really wouldn't want to conclude this way but: this just might be an album that you should listen to yourself.
