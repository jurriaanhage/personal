The music

Ryusuke and Source K. Adachi are the instrumentalist on this combined Musea/Poseidon release. They are well-versed on the acoustic guitar, adding only some vocals to the proceedings. The album contains eleven tracks, open with the longest and fast-paced tune. The development in the songs is often along the lines of classical music, and certainly not devoid of tension or melody. The sound is very clear and up-front, and almost as if performed live. Slow Poison is indeed a much slower track, somewhat folk tinged in outset, harkening back I guess to the acoustic albums of Steve Hackett. On the next one, the duo goes all out, in a complex arrangement of fast guitar runs, all intertwining and playing against each other. Chance Meeting is a Bryan Ferry cover. If Ferry has any trademark (and I think he does), then it has been fully washed away by the duo, that is, until the vocals start. The vocal part is indeed very much Ferry, and Source is not a bad singer, having only a slight accent. The sound of the track is quite gypsy tinged and as usual fluent and complex. Towards the end we find a well-known traditional, but also a vocal Tull track, Wond'ring Aloud. I guess if you happen to collect the albums of Anthony Phillips, the acoustic works of Steve Hackett and people like Gordon Giltrap, then this might well be for you. Adachi Kyodai goes a bit further and especially the speed at which they play, and the energy that it generates can be quite large. Some people might find this monotonous, but there is so much to discover beneath these tunes, that it could be well worth the investigation. Contrary to my expectations I liked it.
