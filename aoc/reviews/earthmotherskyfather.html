Summary

Although on Poseidon, this band surprisingly hails from Hungary.

The music

Introduction is the short opener, which has some vague ethereal singing. The singing is more up front on Were I Able To Fly. Notwithstanding the titles in English, the vocals are in Hungarian. The music here is a combination of the pop music of The Police, Hungarian folk with a slight psyche edge. There is also some tension in this song, notwithstanding the optimistic overall sound. The intermezzo is rather experimental.

Szürgündejnem contains in this vein: slow, rhythmic psychedelic pop with Hungarian vocals and folk elements (well that is how they sound to my untrained ears). The vocals sound a bit Arabic as well to me. The intermezzo is less experimental this time, with the trumpet taking a slow moody lead. Shikadam however opens with organ and more pace and tension. Notwithstanding the somewhat experimental, folky character of the music, it must be said that Kampec Dolores writes real songs, with good melodies too. This song tends to be somewhat repetitive, but for a reason. And the organ is nice to have. The instrumental solo is for the guitar this time, which plays in a somewhat bluesy and experimental style.

With Kalimba we have arrived at the lengthier songs on this album. The opening is quite percussive. This is a relatively slowly developing song with chanted vocals (almost), followed by a long stretch of playful guitar soloing. Drums and trumpet give the music a jazzy flavour. The jazziness also pervades the bass driven, relaxed I'll Run So. However, in the middle part the band really goes deep to bring us a chaotic piece of avant-garde. The guitar also has some Crimsonesque elements. The second half of the song is repetitive and pacey.

Night Train has the chug of a slow train while the vocals indeed evoke the night. The music here can likened to Jon Hassell, indeed the softer side of the band generally does, and the wind instruments help although they are not as prominent here.

Mountains Should Move Though is very relaxed, and shows a bit of an Ozrics influence, although the vocals are quite distinctive (especially on this song, where they happen to be more avant-garde than usual (in places)). The bass is quite pronounced.

Fifteen is the longest song and closes the album. The vocals play a large role on this one, with many vocal lines intertwining and the vocals themselves somewhat experimentally sounding. Still, it all sounds surprisingly natural and easy going. The middle part is for the bass player as he gives plays excellently low. The world music feel is now at its strongest and likeness to the work of Tony Levin (solo and in some of his projects) is quite strong here.

Conclusion

Notwithstanding the pop like structures to the songs, Kampec Dolores harbours quite a bit of the exotic, enough to make the music interesting by itself. The female vocals are in Hungarian, and they have appeal, there is room for typical folk instruments, some experimental edges due to, mainly, the guitar play, so that the music does not become too easy on the ears. On the other hand, the music is not pure experiment, indeed the songs are really songs often with catchy melodies, and quite funky/rhythmic in places, but with just the necessary amount of challenge. Overall, the listener can expect something akin Jon Hassell or the solo works of Tony Levin, but some new elements such a funky pieces of pop and experimental excursions on guitar, as well as some psyche influences.
