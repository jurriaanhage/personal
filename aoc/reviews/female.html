Summary

From Sweden these guys run their homepage (so take a look) and I put their link on my page and vice versa and so we came to the point that they wanted me to review their CD, so here is.

The music

The hard thing about "progressive metal" is that it is hard to tell one from the other. Any kind of metal works with breaks, but in my opinion this shouldn't make them progressive. It is important though that the melody is on the front, but of course some people seem to think that progressiveness is measured in the number of different time signatures, but I wouldn't go much further than saying that there is a strong positive correlation.

Anyway, apart from this, the music on this CD "klinkt als een klok" as we tend to say in Holland, or to translate literally "sound like a bell". We mean that the music kind of jumps from my speakers into my room and ears and well it sounds right. Starting with Female Impersonator, the music is very loud and on the heavy side, to say the least. The vocals remind me at times of Michael Patton in this song, with that whine at the end of a sentence. All in all a fast paced track with a string melody, with a toe in seventies rock, but not derivative. The next track starts out vaguely progressive with a some repetitive patterns after which a heavy rhythm guitar takes over. The vocals are a little hard to understand on this one and go in the direction of Danzig (if I'm not mistaken, this is also on the clothes of one of the bandmembers). Although the drumming is interesting and involved and the vocal melody is important, I'd more easily connect it to Soundgarden Badmotorfinger era, than for instance Queensryche or Dream Theater. One thought struck me in that the vocal melodies are all very good and in style they remind me a little of a band like Jadis, so there you might have that progressive link that was to be looked for.

Conclusion

More or less for lovers of Faith no More (but this is heavier) or Living Colour (but more concentrated on metal), but also some nostalgic looks back to the seventies, but of course with the sound of the nineties. Good melodies and interplay with energetic drumming and an allround heavy sound, with every possible corner in the soundspectrum thoroughly filled with drums, bass, guitar and vocals. If there were any keyboards, which I very much doubt, then they have been snowed under by aforementioned instruments. If you are into heavy stuff certainly worth while to check it out, but Genesis, Yes or even Queensryche are in no way related, file under alternative metal. This does not mean that their music has no future, also in view of the positive reactions they got from all around the world ,and in fact the market being the way it is and being able to continue the way they are now moving (cause be honest a mini is certainly something else than a full length CD) they might even go the big way. Stil, these CDs make you think of the difference between various styles of music and the question how different they really are. What should count more of course is how good the music is, but we also have to remember that because of the large amount of music released everyday it is good to know that there can be made a differentation in style so that you need not go through an immense amount of pop to find that one progressive release. Besides that: the music on this CD is good.
