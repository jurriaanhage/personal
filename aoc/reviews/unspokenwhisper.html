Summary

The one time I saw them, in 1997 in Zwolle I was not impressed. In fact, the band they replaced, Ritual, was one of the reasons for going there and their absence was not soothed much by the tame music of FH. I heard however that since then the band has improved greatly and I was hoping to confirm this here.

The music

opens with playing children. The following bombastic part on keyboards promises a lot, but usually this stops at the vocals. Here however the band have chosen to first move into a (Floydish) guitar solo (the early vocal parts Hey You also remind of Floyd), and a good one at that. Some people may think of Collage on Moonshine here (also very loud and full, but the vocals on Moonshine are certainly not approached here), but on the whole, because of the large keyboard presence in this song (mostly in the background though) and the guitar playing in a rhythmic style, while giving the okay vocals some space to move in, I'm also thinking of the full sound of Egdon Heath, something that can also be heard in the closing vocals. After a pianic intermission we get a terrific all-out keyboardsolo in the style of Mark Kelly after which the piano returns. Despite all the names I've mentioned this is a great track with lots of melodic and atmosphere shifts, recurring themes and an appealing full sound. The next track, the instrumental Wolves At War, is no worse: powerful rhythm guitar, an exciting wailing guitar solo, quickfingered keyboards playing along, this is pure progressive rock and its over before you know it. Childscream is a vocal track opening with soft keyboards. The opening guitar melody is a bit too mellow and easy for my tastes. Since this melody is also used for the vocal part and sounds reminiscent of Ywis/Timelock I'm not all too satisfied with this part. Although the organ and guitar power up the chorus and the instrumental inermezzo is quite nice with hammered playing on the guitar, piano, fluting keyboards and nicely rolling drums, very much in the Egdon Heath style, I'm not that impressed with this track. The title track opens promising with piano in the Genesis vein, after which the powerful chords of the guitar set in. The vocal melody of this song sounds Dutch in a way, but its a lot better than the one of the previous track. After a rather rather long vocal accompanied by piano, we get some ascending chords, but soon after we come back to the easy, melodic vocal part. The vocalist sing "I'm not the only one" but I tend to hear "I'm nutty only one", which of course makes no sense, but it does indicate that the pronounciation could be better. The tense instrumental passage yields an question/answer game between the keys and the guitar. At the end the keys are allowed to build up, driven by the drums, to a funky part on the bass. After a some military drums and some acoustic guitar we return to the first verse and the Genesis atmosphere. Legend Of The Old Man's Tree is a varied instrumental with lots of different passages including ELPish keys, a full organ sound, but mostly a variety of guitarpassage, generally quite loud and powerful (Rush), but also moody and tasteful in the middle. Still, more a showase of musicianship than one of composition. An ode to the progressive radio program Xymphonia is up next. Opening nicely with synthetic strings. Then the guitar enters the scene and we continue in the laid-back Pendragon style with some screaming guitars thrown in for diversity. The comparatively short vocal part is not that strong, the musical part is, but it owes a lot to Pendragon. Closing the album is the instrumental Heroes with a large Saga component (opening guitars). A powerful track in all with some more softer interludes, but generally this is fullsounding track with plenty of solos.

Conclusion

I wonder how a band can arrive at such a full sound on their very first album? The music on this album is very melodic with lots of keyboard (there are two players), but the prize goes to Cents for his melodic, but also rhythmic guitar work. The vocals are okay, but I hear this typical Dutchness in there somewhere, that is also part of the vocals of early Egdon Heath and I'm afraid this is very much the weak point of this band. This also goes for the lyrics that sometimes do not seem very natural to me (in my opinion, this was also largely the problem of Cliffhanger on their first album). Notwithstanding all the names I've mentioned I think the music on this album has a lot of merit and the mix of influences is it seems their own. If one is fond of loud, modern progressive, say in the style of Marillion to Egdon Heath, with some Saga and Rush thrown in, taking a listen is advised. This being their first offering, I'm realy curious about what will be up next.
