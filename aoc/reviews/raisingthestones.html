Summary

These Genesis epigones promised us an un-Genesis like album. This should be it. Also, only two people of the band are left at this time.

The music

It seems these guys recorded some long tracks and then scattered the part throughout the album, at least judging by the names of the tracks.

A question we might ask about this band: can a band sound not like Genesis, while the singer DOES sound like Gabriel. Hard, very hard, and I'm trying to discount it here. After a very nice opening with synthetic choir we get a vocal part. Here, and also judging by the artwork, it turns out that the band still has a liking for the mythical and the mystical. The music has some echoes of Genesis especially in the easily recognized acoustic parts although the band had an "older" sound with some clavecimbel in the second part of Last Days of Cain. In the next part there's a nice flowing instrumental part with flute and keyboards. The vocal parts however all sound very much alike and this is of course due to the oh so recognizable vocals. The rather joyous piano at the end of this part leads us into Ixion's Conclusion which is quite up-tempo and features a nicely ascending keyboarsolo with numerous breaks that end in a sort of cacophony of horns (well not really) that remind me of the Cardiacs. I'm not altogether satisfied with this part however; it is sometimes a little too farfetched and "constructed".

The intro to Bad Karma is complex with various melodies intertwined and certainly not like Genesis would make it. The music has some Arabic overtones. After this rather hectic part we get the ballad like vocal parts. Later on we get rather dated sounding keyboards, but with a nice vocal melody and after a while a dark intermezzo with bass and flute. This is more typically Genesis, although playing scales on a piano certainly is not, and this what they do next. The song ends majestically although they do fiddle around a little even here.

The next track is a sectioned one with the first section being a moody almost instrumental with nice sound effects and voices. The track end with Ghosts of Jericho part 2, Looking Heaven In The Face. This is a rather pumping track with operatic keyboard sounds.

Corcyra - The Suppliants stands on its own and opens with lush keyboards, but on the whole of it, this is a rather busy track with quick pacing and lots of break in the middle and again a classical sound to the music. The vocal melody is okay, but the lyrics are not all too pleasant. After the lush outro we come to the five sectioned song with no real name. After an intro with numerous signature changes and Arabic stylings we get an up-tempo vocal part in the Citizen Cain style and then we get an Arabic sounding percussive intermezzo (Variations?) that reaches a climax. After the up-tempo The Blood Plains Of Hev-Hem the song closes in a melancholy mood with Forever and Aborted. Very nice.

The Last Supper and In Deep Waters form the next track of this album. A short one in fact with synthetic strings and sounding classical all the way. A terrific melody by the way.

In the next track mythology gets mixed up a bit with Hercules and Jericho. This track is the first part of Ghosts of Jericho (spelled Ghost's of Jericho on the CD). The second part of the track I Spy With My Little Life (some wordplay can be spotted in the titles, also see Will-o'-the Wish) enjoys some nice piano.

Black Rain and Webs form the penultimate track. The first part of this sounds like what we've heard although the strings towards the end are very nice.

Silently Seeking Euridice (or was it Susan) opens orchestrally and the first part A Lover's Tale is quite a good one. The next one, The Stalker's Dance is a little too bouncy for my tastes. Still, the next parts reveal that this is one of the better tracks of the album with some very nice melodies.

Conclusion

Well although the albums contains some nice and "new" parts and it is by far more experimental than their previous efforts with the introduction of Arabic and classical stylings I'm not overly impressed. Especially the less melodic parts full of breaks have an air of directionlessness about them and with an album of such length it might be nice to introduce more variegated vocals or sing a little less. Okay, the singer can't help his vocals, but these vocals do make the music sound self similar. I do not want to advise them to go back to wrk on the old footing, but hopefully the next effort sounds more....meaningful?
