Summary

Again a rerelease from Cooking Vinyl, hard busy to rerelease the Ubu albums. This is the follow up to Dub Housing from 1979.

The music

It's him again, David Thomas with his insane vocalizations. On this chaotic opener, The Fabulous Sequel, everyone more or less seems to do whatever he likes. The differences with Dub Housing are not that strong. 48 Guitars & One Girl contains quite some pointers to more "ordinary" American music. Thomas rants on in his highpitched freewheeling way. A dissonant piece. Bird whistles and more or less ocassional percussion and piano opens A Small Dark Cloud. Slow and ponderous the piece proceeds. Like on Dub Housing I'm somewhat reminded of Talking Heads, but going on where that band stopped. Small Was Fast harbours all the typical ingredients from loud claustrophobic rock to squeaking bath ducks finally derailing into All The Dogs Are Barking, which has that lone desert feel to it, with its twanging guitar. Skipping now to the melody and paradoxically monotony of Goodbye. A ponderous piece with something akin to church organ sounds incorporated into it. Even with all that melody, the band never omits to put in some dissonance. The Voice Of The Sand is a short experimental interlude after which we conclude with Kingdom Come. A hazy vibrant piece, that shortly becomes a bit more percussive. A rather "light" closer.

Conclusion

Compared to Dub Housing there seems to be even a bit more experimentation here and more of a variety in moods. On Dub Housing there were some danceable pieces even (although strange ones), but that has gone on this album making it a more definite statement than the previous. The style however has not intrinsically changed that much however.
