Summary

The follow-up to Fresh Blood, also reviewed on this page.

The music

The album opens with The Watcher and it shows that nothing much has changed with respect to their previous album. In this song the meandering violin alternates with Crimsonesque guitar playing. The rhythm section is rather unobtrusive. An open calm and soothing piece. In Mutoid Man the bass takes on a more prominent character and some keyboard experimentation as well and organ to boot. I do wonder where these sounds come from. Again, the music has a likeness to King Crimson and the music becomes positively driving at some point. Very good. The music obviously also refers to Jean-Luc Ponty, but not forgetting the KC influences. On Flood the soft repetitive guitar is the most striking feature. The violin solos over this, but one of the problems with this kind of music, is that the melodies are often not very explicit, or one might say explicitly appealing. Then the song becomes a bit heavier and again something like an organ sets in. It is probably not an organ, but a bass. Backbone Of Night has a strong ethnic feel to it and explains the long list of percussive instruments with the name of Francis Dyer. The violin is not played in a terribly exciting manner generally, a bit subdued and sad in fact and this gives the music a somewhat Balkanic/Gipsy feel and later some Arabic influences are added. In Mazeppa (Me Zappa?) the repetitive guitar playing is very much like Frippertronics. However, the song also rocks and contains a bluesy guitar solo. Melodically it is not so interesting. With Diamond Eye we crosss the halfway line. A rather complicated sounding piece with lots of things going on and a lot of rhythmic variation. After the short melodic The Real Thing with acoustic guitar we come to The Getaway which like its title may imply is a rather driven piece. Moss on the other hand is again back to the ethereal violin playing on a repetitive background of acoustic guitar. In Circus After Hours the pace returns to the music and the drumming is again excellent. After Dusk Creatures which reminds me of Fripp soundscapes, but with accompaniment, we close with Broquen. The guitar on this track is quite prominent with its bluesy sound and a bluesy pace to match. The latter part goes very much in the Fripp direction once again.

Conclusion

A very nice album with good playing, a good sound and interesting compositions. It seems the band has won a little in variation, but I feel still more can be done. As always the violin is quite prominent and as such can remind people of Curved Air (without vocals) or Ponty, although the music is certainly not jazzy. Compositionally the greatest influence seems to be King Crimson/Fripp, but with aggression replaced by something akin to sadness. I like it when the stick comes into play with it distinctive sound. A satisfying album, but not imposing.
