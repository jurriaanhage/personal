Summary

Hailing from Brazil and two albums and a number of EPs to their name this is the new one from prog metalband Angra. No artwork when I got this, and the last info I can give you that even a single has been released, Lisbon, with unreleased versions of Make Believe and Angels Cry.

The music

Wings Of Reality opens this album. After a bombastic opening follows a quick-paced, pumping part, very melodic and not that interesting. The instrumental intermezzo is quite nice however, while the vocal part is replayed afterward. The song is overall quite bombastic with lots of synth strings and because of this "classicalness" reminiscent of eveyrthing from Malmsteen up to Rhapsody. Petrified Eyes opens with bluesy guitarwork. What follows is rather straightforward, quite up-tempo, an acoustic intermezzo, and some nice rhythmic changes, but not overly complicated. Like many of the melodic metal bands of today, the music goes in quite easily. Lisbon, the single, opens classically and is very melodic. The strings seem to be real ones here. The (slighty accented) singing of Matos is quite melodramtic in this song and remind me of some rock ballad. In addition to a driving part (but still melodic), the "Skies Are Falling Down" part is very nice. This part is allowed to build up and fade til end. Metal Icarus, what to think of a title like that? Well, it's a pumping song with double bassdrums, but on the whole not very interesting, too metallic. Paradise opens a lot better with a good guitar solo and a layer of keyboards in the back. The vocal part is rather dark and driving, while the chorus is as always very melodic. This song contains some strong (vocal) melodies and also as a composition is one of the more variegated ones. Both Mysteru Machine and the title track are, the intro to the latter aside, not overly interesting. I mean the melodies are sometimes nice, but it is also quite straightforward and a little too much of the singalong type. The same holds for Extreme Dream that has an uninspired guitar solo. Gentle Change is a slightly jumpy, quite rhythmic and it sounds a bit sunny. It's not really a ballad, but just a happy song. The album closes with Speed continuing the metallic style of this album.

Conclusion

There can be variation on this album, but I hesitate to call this progressive metal. Just somewhat varied, melodic metal I would say with Lisbon and Paradise being the most interesting for proggers. For metal this album is not bad, but it's a long way from contemporary progmetal albums like Tyranny or Wake of Magellan.
