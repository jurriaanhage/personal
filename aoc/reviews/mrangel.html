Summary

Happens to some bands. Happens all the time. The record company goes bankrupt and this is even worse when you've just delivered your conceptual masterpiece such as Aragon did. Mouse is still hard to find these days, but relatively quick (for them) the band Aragon is back, and again on a Dutch label. After the first release by co-owner Marc Brasse', Aragon is the second band to release an album on the LaBraD'Or label.

And guys it is plain iO Pages and not I.O. Pages.

The music

Anyway, starting of with Surface Tension, you might be getting scared when I note that this song sounds rather like Bryan Adams' Run To You. Do'n be too afraid howevre, since not only is this one of the man's best tracks, but they add some nice stuff on the keys as well. The song is quite poppy, but as one the few bands around, Aragon can make good poppy tracks. The biting singing of Dougan are still present, probably a reason why chances that this album is picked by the main public is about zero. I heard their reasons for not adding a drummer: the alchemy of the current band is not to be disturbed, but listening with the headphones the drumming is certainly the weakest part of the band. I hope one fhte current members decides to learn to drum. The beginning of the Art Gallery gives us the other face of Aragon: mysterious and intimate. The basswork is very nice on this song by the way. The bell-like keyboards from the first album also return here. On the whole indeed, the sound of this album can be very well compared with Don't Bring The Rain, although without an epic such as the Crucifixion. Here It Comes Again is again up-tempo with some rather frolic keyboards, quite bouncy in fact with pronounced bass but also some introverted passages. The Crying Game is a rather desperate track with high vocals of Dougan and soft keyboards. A sense of drama is evoked very well here and this must be one of the high points on the album, as Dougan winds his way through the meandering vocal melody. As one might expect, the songs closes with a emotional guitarsolo (think of Sorrow here). Wings Of Heaven is a mid-tempo track with quite a lot of keyboard (the importance of the guitar is rather low on the entire album and is used more for the powerful accents). The Haunting is the shortest track one this album. Pleasant tinklings on the keys, military ruffles, but the vocal melody is not so great this time. One More Angry Moment is a heavy guitaristic track. The end though is quite soft. I Don't Want To Know opens wonderfully, with very (in)tense basswork. Then the vocals, then the fluting keyboards and then the rather powerful chorus, after which the guitar adds its tension to the next verse. The lyrics are about a love broken, and the protagonist not being able to cope yet with talking about the subject, certainly not with the "other one". Here In My Heart is a very sweet ballad, a little too sweet maybe for some. Musically the track is quite laidback, but it has a nice build-up with incidental percussion and ends very well with a great melody played on the keyboards. The closing vocals I'm not fond of. Acoustic guitar and a bouncy rhythm characterize A Little Bit Of Love. This song can be compared with that frolic ditty Solstice from the first album and I wasn't fond of that one either. The closer Mr Angel is also not a high flyer, again being a little too jumpy for my tastes.

Conclusion

Melodically, the music on this album is strong and the strength of the band is hightened by the fact that this band can make contagious music without getting too easy and simple for the prog fan to take. The music refers mostly to the more thoughtful parts of Don't Bring The Rain (The Cradle, the first part of the Wolves track and then some). The instrumentation is focues very much on the keyboards playing the most of the melodies, the basswork is sometimes quite pronounced and then very good, while the guitar is allowed to rip only once in a while, but when it does, it does so with a vengeance. I'm still sorry they do not use a live drummer, and for the next album we'll just have to see what they will offer, both instrumentally and musically. For now, it's time to enjoy this not very typically progressive, but still widely appealing album, but which to my tastes ended not as strongly as is could have.
