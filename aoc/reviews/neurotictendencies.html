Summary

A do-it-mostly-yourself musician from the States who recently got to promoting and rereleasing his music.

The music

The rather short Rodent Man opens the album. Lots of different guitar parts on this one, playing in various combinations. The drumming seems programmed and this is quite audible, making for a rather dull drum sound. Focus however should be on the guitar playing which is both melodic and meandering. GuiatrBy's Sandwich is similar in style: guitar pyrotechnics, but, this has to be said, Ray has something to say with his guitar. It's not just technique. Tempation Is Here is the first vocal track. As on Atomic Soldiers the vocals are rather flat and most people will not like them much. The way they are produced they sound a bit hollow and full of echo. The overall production is a bit muddy (probably a budget thing). This is a much more catchy track with a recognizable vocal melody. Ray then plays around a bit with effects. Nothing Is, Nothing Was is also a vocal track. Again the vocals are detrimental to the track. Ray does use vocoding here and that is better. This is a rather relaxed track, with a few pacey parts. The album has quite a level of noise when the sound is gone as we move to track number five, Contortion Drive. The vocals here are really depressing of the type that Floyd used to have (used to?). The song gets to be more instrumental later on with plenty of fingerquick playing, which is by the ears of it, the most prominent part of the music of Rick Ray. The instrumental parts remind me of Camels jazzrock instrumentals of the seventies. Forgotten Dreams is a good track with both good guitarplaying but also good structure and vocal melody. Divine Wind is a weird one with a kind of fifties jingle like theme. I don't like it. More funny than anything else. The next part features the clarinet of Rick Schultz, but to me the track is ruined already. Actually the continuation is very unstructured and does not do anything for me at all. My Time Is Coming is back to the vocal tracks. Neurology III however is a rather potent piece at first, with some frolic contributions by Rick Schultz on clarinet. Later the track slows down a bit with hazy vocals and guitar solo's backed by slow clarinet. Quite psychedelic this. I Can't Stand it continues along the same road. A bluesy track in a way with some Hendrix leanings (how many guitar players are not influenced by him?) Some powerful chords in here. With All Tomorrows brings more of the same (and I do not mean this in a negative way). Money Up Your Nose is a relaxed track. The vocal part is not that strong and more or less based on a single focal line. No One Knows Your Name is the final track, a slowish, ploding one. Not very uplifting. By the way, the vocals here (and now I think of it also earlier on the album) remind me of the singer of Fruitcake, the band on Cyclops. The final track is a weird ghost track with some oddball sounds.

Conclusion

More or less of the same type as the Atomic Soldiers album (though I rate it compositionally higher) this is guitar rock with a difference. The production is not good, neither are the vocals that suffer from this production. However the type of music does not demand this, but it will certainly put some people off. It is too bad the drumming is not live, that would certainly add to the music. The melodic parts are usally good, with some good vocal melodies and also the instrumental guitar parts can usually hold my attention quite well.
