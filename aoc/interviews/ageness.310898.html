Ageness interview August 31th 1998

Introduction

The following interview was done with Tommy Eriksson of Ageness in a bar in Helsinki on the 31st of August 1998. The time was 21.30 when he picked us up from the hotel and we followed him to a bar where we could talk. We is Nik'e van Vugt and myself. There Tommy and I got to talking about the past, present and future of Ageness, his way of working and Colossus, the prog "movement" in Helsinki.

The interview

When I looked at the bio on your webpage it seemed to me that your first album was quite a success here.

Well the first album was limited to Helsinki mostly. At first we were shocked and surprised that was there something like a prog movement outside Finland. We got very good reviews saying that we sounded like mid-period Genesis. The ironic thing was that the second album didn't sound much like Genesis and therefore we got bad reviews!? The difference was however that the second album had mostly newer songs on it. This second album Rituals was meant for a Finnish record company. They offered us a lousy deal and so we skipped it. For our latest album, Imageness, Musea suggested that we work together and the good thing is that we do not need to spend money on printing the discs anymore, since Musea does this. So, we could pay more attention to and spend more money on recording and we didn't have to rush anything as on the previous disc, which lacked a guitarsolo and so the engineer played it. Imageness contains only new songs. We did have some old songs and in fact the artwork for it were based on those older songs, but I did not put them on.

Will Musea also release the older albums?

Well they did also release Rituals, but not the older one, Showing Paces, yet. We will certainly do so and remaster it and add some bonusmaterial. I would also like to remix it.

And what about a next album?

Musea is certainly willing to do another album.

What one often sees on Finnish records is a thank you to some cultural institute. What is this?

In Finland a band can apply to a foundation for money to record an album. For this they can get money and if you come back and show them you're still busy, you get more every time, although this is not true for all bands. Also, if somebody in Holland wants to get us to play in Holland, they can apply for money with the Finnish consul in Holland.

How do you write songs?

Well, I tend to write them in parts and put together the parts that feel right together. For a period I put everything I do into a computer and it turns out that bad ideas for some songs work well somewhere else. The lyrics sometimes come together with the song or they are added later and in some cases even earlier. Lyrics are only meant to have the right feeling and thus are rather intuitive. Something new on this album that I did, was that a song or idea had to be there entirely before I picked up an instrument. Otherwise, all music evolves from your own limitations.

You still write all the songs?

Yes, I write the songs, record them as demo's and we discuss them together. I do have a veto right. Sometimes I do record songs that I do not like, but that the others do want to record. But not on this one! For this album I did not look at the limitations of the players, but just wrote what I wanted to hear myself and let them learn to play it.

How do you record the music?

I have a small studio at home, where I work. I have no drums there and no grand piano and I also recorded some organ in studio. In sound, a big difference between our earlier albums and the latest one is that the sounds of the keyboards are exactly what they were when I played it. What often happens is that people record keyboards with some setting and they change the settings later so the music is the same, but it sounds different. On this album I did not do this.

Imageness starts with more straightforward shorter songs and becomes more complex only later on. Any special reason?

We put the short songs first on purpose to get some airplay and the long songs are then put at the end. We do in fact get quite a lot of airplay.

There's one thing I wondered about: how did you get to those opening vocals in Sequels?

At first I only wanted a choir boy on this one, but later we added more. The idea for doing that came from Nightmare On Elm Street. The subject of the lyrics in Sequels is that everything in these modern times is questionable, in doubt, even with people from The Salvation Army.

How is the prog scene in Finland?

In Finland there is a better progscene than in Englang. I lived there for a year and I can tell. We have quite a good following here, although that is also true worldwide.

What about this Colossus?

Well it's a Finnish progressive movement that exists since December 1995. Two of us are actively involved with it. They release a magazine, seven issues up to now, and hold monthly club evenings. There are twenty to thirty progressive bands in Helsinki and the number is still growing. Five or six bands have actually recorded CDs. One of the active labels for prog here is Metamorphosis.

I also heard something about videos. Explain.

The Finnish tv recorded a video/movie for Imageness including the songs Line Of Fire and Metamorphosis with Scandinavian nurses and live footage. Unfortunately we cannot use this movie whenever we want to, because they have to copyright, so far.... We did make a video for Fear which was even aired in North Carolina and in Finland of course. Musea is planning to release a compilation video of some of their bands and it could be included there. We will also be taping our coming concert with John Wetton (took place in September 1998) and we might release this ourselves, but that takes money (later info indicates that this will not be the case and it will in all probability be limited to a video of two songs).

What about this concert? A good opportunity to play with somebody well-known in the scene and meet a new audience?

Yes I guess it is. We usually play for the same 200 people here in Helsinki. The gig with Wetton and Ritual will probably draw something like 300 people (it turned out there were 400). We do not play that often, something ten times per year, but even in the smallest villages we get a lot of support, for instance from the local press. In one small town called Kotka we got very much attention in the newspapers there and seventy progheads showed up who went nuts.
