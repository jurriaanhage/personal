Pain Of Salvation interview November 14 of 1999

Introduction

The following interview is done with progmetal band Pain Of Salvation during the Progpower festival held on November 14 of 1999 in 013, Tilburg. Answers to my questions were given by one of three members (Daniel, Kristoffer, Johan) of Pain Of Salvation I happened to interview. As we sat down on the floor in one of the fortunately empty, because not too spacey, corridors of 013, we had a little chat.

The interview

First of all, I'd like to compliment you with your latest record, One Hour By The Concrete Lake. The bio with the album stated that your debut album would be released in April/May 1999. I haven't seen it though?

It was the Entropia album. It was released on September I think.

According to the bio you are a combination of Yes and Faith No More. Now, I couldn't really hear this in your music at all.

Who does? Well they is not our words. Eveybody says different things.

What do YOU say?

Ehm, that is a good one. It is hard to say, because we all listen to very different kinds of music. Even though Daniel is the one making all the music we all approach our instruments, the way we play in our own way. Everyone of us has a different musical education. We can not say: we like this and this band, we want to play music like that. We play what we think sounds good to us. We do not mean that what we do is unique, because it is rather the opposite. We take impressions, influences from many things, not only music.

It is hard to say actually, but everybody wants to hear a comparison between two maybe three bands, like X meets Y or something. We try to make the music we want to listen to ourselves and which we feel is not on the market today. If we would really make something meets something, then I guess we would not make something that is in any way special.

If you say we have here a mix between Frank Sinatra and Queensryche, then I guess you have something pretty unique.

Yeah, that is true.

Often we are compared to bands we have never heard of, or just never heard.

I think there are comparisons with bands as regards their musical approach. I think one band that is similar in their musical approach is the band in the early seventies, named Focus. The have this approach that they are playing WITH music, if you know what I mean. I, Johan, was the last one to come into this band and I may see the music of the band a bit differently, more from a distance.

As a matter of fact, up to now, I've never heard of Focus. But that's me, because I do not know many bands.

In the quieter parts you remind me of Enchant, also a band on Inside Out, but the reference to Yes and Faith No More has to come from somewhere. Now, the latter I can here in say the vocal style of Inside, but for Yes...

A lot of people have mentioned Faith No More as one of the influences. It is one of the names that in fact occurred several times. Apart from that I think most comparisons are I think one of a kind. I think that what they want to try to express is that Faith No More is kind of a modern sounding band and they mix a lot of influences and the vocalist uses his voice more or less like an instrument. And then they probably feel we also have some sort of seventies feel, so we are a mix between a modern and a seventies band. Now, we seldom have heard of the seventies band. Johan has listened to a lot of seventies' bands, but we was in fact the last one to come into the band. I'd have to say that when we actually composed most of the material, none of us had very much listened to anything seventies or progressive.

Nowadays we do not listen to our kind of music at all. I do not listen to progmetal or things like that. No one in the band does.

If you play it yourself, I can very well imagine this. The only reason for the Yes reference I can see is that your music is at times rather complex. Lots of breaks, especially in the beginning of the album. I also heard some Kansas there somewhere in New Year's Eve.

Yes, Kansas was also mentioned a few times. This is the third time I have heard their name. I still have not heard any Kansas song.

Well, Kansas plays a different kind of music, but often I hear resemblances in the vocal melodies and this is actually the comparison I tend to make for you: only in the vocal parts. You have been and are labelled as a progmetal band. For me, compared to most of those you are not only a bit on the epic side, but also dark. Do you also feel this?

In a way, I suppose so, but not in the way you might think when you hear the album.

Did you hear Entropia?

No

Well, One Hour By The Concrete Lake is much darker. Entropia is much lighter and funkier.

It is not light.

I think we move around to suit the concepts, the lyrics.

Does that mean you started with the lyrics?

No, we didn't. We first write the songs and the lyrics, but you can always do something in your approach, so things like the sound of the keyboards you can always change.

The mood is something that comes later as it were.

Yes.

Well Daniel always has the mood of the music in his mind. Then he tries to explain it to us and this takes a lot of time. I don't know if it comes out the way he thinks, but I think it is similar.

Yes and he can think of music polyphonically. The bastard.

So you always have to come down to their level?

Well, it takes time to indoctrinate people. That's the problem for me. But seriously, as Kristoffer said, for the concept of the Concrete Lake album I had an idea and we talked about it a lot. We had the concept in our head but we didn't have the lyrics. But we still had a kind of mood that we wanted the album we had. It is a bit of a mystery.

What is the story about anyway? Promo CDs often do not have lyrics attached to them, even if the albums happen to be concept albums.

I've said this a few times, but I'll do it again.

You'll have to.

Yeah, okay. I started to do peace work at the university of Gothenburg and then I wrote an essay on environmental pollution, the nuclear industry, the weapon industry and war, the dispossession of indigenous people such as Indians in the United States. Also, global water consumption. I think that was it. I tried to emphasize the connections between these issues and why they relate to each other, and why they form a circle that influences everything. And then I felt that this would be a good thing to do a concept album about. So I suggested this to the rest of the band, gave them the essay and a lot of material and we talked a lot about it. So it is kind of based on that essay and a lot of reading. The problem was that we had a lot of facts and figures, but that doesn't make a great concept. It would be rather boring. So we had to bring it down to a personal level and it starts out with this man working in the weapon industry. It is obvious that he has these inner doubts and he does not feel very comfortable in his situation. On New Year's Eve he decides that he will go and see what his lifestyle, his actions, how they changed the world. Chapter two of the concept is when he travels around the world and makes stops at different places, different problems and to emphasize the connections between them. In the third chapter he ends up in former U.S.S.R., in a place in the Easturalian mountains, at a lake there, where they dumped so much waste in the lake since the nineteenforties so that in 1988, the radiation of this lake was 2.5 times that of the Tschernobyl accident. If you would stand for one hour at this lake, then you would die within two weeks from radiation. They covered this lake with concrete in order to block the radiation. I started some radiation physics and nuclear physics at the university to get to know the face of the enemy as it were. I reckon you'd have to have this concrete shell on the lake for 24000 years or so. But it had cracks and leaks within ten years.

I read this year, that radioactivity has spread by underground rivers, so the problem is certainly not solved, by putting concrete on the lake. There have been many cases of this kind. In places in Canada for instance, really big lakes have been destroyed as well in the same way.

And plains, and mountains, not just lakes. At Black Hill in Canada, the Canadian Indians actually own the ground, but the "white men" as it were have dumped nuclear waste and they have mined uranium on that location even though the Indians won the case in court. It doesn't seem to matter. The Indians have 82 percent mortality with newborn children, which is really absurd. But anyway, at this lake is where this man ends up and he contemplates his position and being part of the big machinery and what that means to him.

Was the final track on the album Inside Out a way to gain access to the label?

Ha, ha, no. The song was made before we signed there. In fact, we had heard a European record company was interested and when we finally met we heard they were called Inside Out. So it seems like a sign.

Maybe we should have called the song EMI?

How popular are you in Scandinavia?

We do not hear much about hard rock or metal bands in Scandinavia. In Sweden anyway.

Sweden exports a lot of music, but it is not hard rock, it is pop music.

When I went to Finland, I saw a lot of hard music there. Also in Sweden and Norway there seem to be many death metal bands and such.

Well there are many such bands, but they are not heard in the media. That is the thing. You have a place to rehearse and you find that half the bands play some kind of heavy music. Death metal or trash or ordinary metal. We probably do not even have a Swedish metal magazine. I can not think of one. We have Close-Up, but that is concerned with the demo and underground scene.

Maybe the big countries for the type of music are Italy and Germany, that is where most of the bands come from it seems.

Well Holland was during our tour in March the greatest place to play. For us anyway. The audience knew the songs and sang along with the lyrics, and we never expected this, being the opening act. We were figuring that people came to see Threshold and that maybe someone has heard our record. It turned out that many people came to see us and that happened especially in Holland.

How did you feel about touring.

Well the best thing about it was in fact that we were touring at all. To have that experience. It was our first tour ever. I was a bit surprised that the stages were very different. In Switzerland we had a huge stage, but in France one time we played in a pub or something. That was really confusing. Italy was a nice country, but very few people came to concerts and most seemed to be family of Eldritch. But we liked the environments. In France it was even hot, in middle of March.

Well, it does not surprise me that much, because most sounds I hear about you are very positive.

Up to now, we've had only one negative review from someone in a small town in Sweden. The best remark in that review was "They must have a lot of money, or else they would never have the opportunity to record an album". That is very true of course. Extremely bizarre. But anyway, we had many great reviews and now we also would like to sell many albums, that would also be great. The problem is to get the album.

It does seem to be difficult to get in the shops. Which is a bit strange, because Inside Out is usually quite easily available. In a limited amount of shops of course, but everyone has some kind of shop nearby where he can get it here in Holland.

In Sweden also. People want to get the album, but they can not find it.

We have limited distribution by a small company in Sweden and almost no promotion. The market is not big enough for that.

Albums that are wanted are sometimes not available and vice versa. I guess in the end, luck is very important here. That must be frustrating though.

But we'll spread the word right here tonight.

Now we have to hope people well sell some records here as well.

Well, that is a problem. There was a break-in into our van last night and they stole all our new t-shirts. We had paid for them with our own money. The albums we wanted to sell were ordered, but did not arrive so I think we have twenty or so Concrete Lake albums in the merchandise stand.

Things like these happen. At Progfarm one of the bands was playing but their distributor had left all their CDs at home. And they hadn't taken anything along themselves. But what music do you like?

Kristoffer: I like jazz and fusion, mostly fusion, because it is more rock 'n' roll. Weather Report for instance. I listen to bass players a lot. Jaco Pastorius. For the rest, Tori Amos, Seal. Music can be good, but what makes it very good is the production. If something has a bad sound, then I can not stand it. A good production makes it all for me. I'm a bit of sound engineer.

Johan: I listen to classical music, choral music, and some idols such as Stevie Wonder, the Beatles. I like most music.

Daniel: although I'm like Johan not genre-fixed, I have to say that I really like Faith No More. One of the influences that I have to mention. I listen a lot to the Abbey Road album. I'm influenced by Jesus Christ Superstar which may account for the seventies' influences. More modern music I like is Dalbello and ofcourse The Gathering, which you should know. I liked the album Nighttime Birds, especially the title track. But that is with most things, you have one, two, three favourite songs.

What are you doing at this moment, besides playing here of course.

We are making a third album at the moment which we hope to record between February and April next year. We had a break during the summer. I had some problems with my voice after the tour, I got married in July. So we had a break of a few months and now we are getting a grip on the new material. Basically we have more than an album in material. We'll have to move it together.

Will it be a collection of songs or a concept again?

For myself, I find it strange to have a song about one thing and a song about something else entirely on the same album.

But you can have this by having them relate in some or other way, without having a specific concept or story.

I think it will be a concept album. It seems to fall in with our approach to music. That's the way we all feel about it. And it is either a concept album or it is not. There are albums where the first song is related to songs at the end of the album, but not those in between, like with the first King Diamond album. For us that is impossible. We always try to say something and the more interesting things are the big topics. The big topics are very hard to deal with within a single song.

also the impact of an album is larger than of a single song.

Yes, you have more playground for bigger issues and you can view it from a number of sides. I feel that as the lyric writer there are some things, emotions that have to be dealt with and I need more songs to deal with it. And to put a song on one album, one on the other to get it out of my system, I prefer to have it on one album.

With the Concrete Lake album we also put some facts in between the lyrics in the booklet and also poems.

Source material, so if people want to know more about this, then that is where they can find it.

In fact, in our magazine, iO Pages, somebody asked for an interview to be done with you, because it seemed to him, you had something to say.

We did get e-mails of people telling us that they changed their lifestyle after listening to our album. That is both very frightening and very flattering.

I do hope they do not misinterpret things we say, because that could be disastrous.

That is one of the problems with albums such as these: by writing about it you get a kind of responsibility.

Most artists do not feel this responsibility. There are so many artists today who write a lot of nonsense while their main goal is to sell albums. I feel that if you are to release albums and write lyrics, you should think of what you say.

Hmm, but it is also a good way of getting paranoid, paying attention to what you say all the time.

For us it is important to have something to say.

On the other hand, there will be tendency then to be precise in your lyrics. You want to say how it is, not some generalization. If you put all the detail in your lyrics however they become cluttered. A way around this was found for The Lamb by Genesis. They had a story, but only included parts of the story in the lyrics themselves.

That is also what we like to do. You can put a lot in the poems and such in the booklet. On the internet there were some discussions about our lyrics and people were saying that we could not prove this and this and this. But the goal is not to write some down as a monument, something that is absolute truth. We can not back up everything we say. We can only say what we have read and what our opinions are. It is then up to the listener to form his own opinion. We do not say we have the answer and it is on the album.
