No Name interview November 7th 1998

Introduction

This interview was held at the Progfarm festival in Bakkeveen, the Netherlands. The date is the 7th of November as Jurriaan Hage and Roberto Lambooy interviewed the bassplayer Gilles Loes and the keyboard player Alex Rukavina.

The interview

You are from Luxembourg. There are not many prog bands that originate there, are there.

Alex: No, no, there is a band that calls itself an experimental rockband, but we are the only progressive band. Oh, no we do have a rival now, it is called Suppers Ready. They have been doing covers for years, very quietly, but now they have made a demo cd. So we are not the only ones anymore.

How did you get that name of yours?

Alex: Many years ago we were just playing together, but we never wanted to play concerts. After a year were asking us to play some concerts. We told them we had no name, so we had 'no name' for that concert and it remained this way. It is not a very original name.

I do not know any other bands with this name.

Gilles: Well, I heard of a Dutch band (BZN?, JH) and a German band with that name.

Will your new album be released by Musea like we first heard?

Alex: Musea is not very interested, because the music is too loud for them. They prefer music which is more melodic. Our music is more powerful; it is more neo-progressive than progressive. So it was better to go to Angular and they have a contract with SPV and they are a big distributor within Europe. Musea still distributes part of it, but we are not linked to them anymore. I mean they are fine people, and perhaps our next CD will be more in their direction and then they might be able to do something with it. But for us it does not really matter who sells it for us. Of course, we do not know yet how the new distribution will work out, we will see this next February. The last CD sold 3000 copies and that is good enough for us. We hope the next one will sell about 5000.

Since we are not familiar with your CDs we really cannot ask a lot about them. This long song during the concert about Phineas Fogg was from the last CD wasn't it?

Alex: Yes, and also the song about the Orient Express is also from the last CD. And there was one other long track September. This one was also from the last CD. We haven't played that one in a long time. We played it here especially.

During Orient Express we were reminded of Led Zeppelins Kashmir.

Alex: Yes, a short part of the song is from that song. Since we really love IQ, we have written a song that is dedicated to IQ. The song could an IQ song, but with another voice. The rest of it is very close to the music of IQ. It was quite difficult to find a way to copy a band to thank it. We think IQ is a real band, it sounds like a band. Many of the other bands sound like a guitar and a keyboard, they do not have the sound of a band.

You mean IQ is more coherent.

Alex: Yes. IQ is for me the finest example of a prog band.

IQ told us some time ago that what they do is arrange pop songs in a progressive way. Do you regard yourselves as a pop band in this way.

Alex: No.

Gilles: Today we playd an unusually heavy set. But usually, like the Follow The Signs song, we play some more poppy songs. On the new CD there are two or three lighter tracks. Also on the last CD. On our first CDs there was always a track in Luxembourgish. That was to sell the CD in our own country. This time however there was no track for which Patrick (Kiefer, the singer) could sing in Luxembourgish. So on this CD there is nothing in Luxembourgish, but there are a few lighter tracks. We do not see ourselves as a pop band, but we certainly do have elements of a pop band. We are not from the die-hard Genesis school.

During the 80 days around the world song, you played a long instrumental keyboard solo. How did you get to play this well?

Alex: Well, my father teaches piano at the conservatory. I have taken five or six years teaching piano at the conservatory, not from my father though. After that I did not feel like it anymore, and I said goodbye to the piano. When I was eightteen some guys asked me to come and play keyboards in their band. I didn't have a keyboard, so I bought a small one. Then we started to play and like with the name, we never wanted to do anything but play together. More and more we became a band as members were changing. Then we found we had a good band, all members lived within a short distance of eachother. So we can call eachother easily and arrange to play together.

You do sound like you play often together.

Alex: Well not as much as we used to. Next week we have two gigs and then we do not know.

Gilles: After the two concerts next week, we will only play again after the album comes out in February. Wait, there might a birthday concert, since this December we will be together for ten years. We will hold this in our hometown. We are doing some gigs now in November since we were expecting the album to be released on November the 16th. There were some problems with the deadline. We made some mistakes, the record company made some and finally it was set to February first. This is mostly due to the Christmas coming up and people only buy well known titles at Christmas. The policy of SPV is to put CDs in store for two months and then to see what happened. If too few were sold, the CDs are taken out of the shops. To get all these CDs back costs a lot of money, so they told us they would avoid this period. Tehre were four gigs scheduled for the release of the CD and we didn't want to cancel them. So we thought, let's do something special for the public. The album was first scheduled for September, then for October, then for November and then February. We wanted to compensate our listeners and so took a song from the album and make a shorter version of it and put it with two compilation disc tracks and a demo version of some older song onto a CD single, to have something to offer.

I saw it lying about, and one song on that CD single was called De Verstand. Is that Luxembourgish?

Gilles: Yes. It was a song from a compilation disc in Luxembourg. De Verstand means 'the mind'.

Although we call it Het Verstand, it means the same thing in Dutch.

Gilles: It was never on an official disc, only on a sampler. There is always a big festival in the city of Luxembourg each year. We played there once and they always put two of your songs on a compilation. Now, there are no compilations discs left and if people wanted to have the song, they could't get it. So that's why we put it on.

The only track we knew from your band we heard on the Empire and Sisyphus compilation discs. Do you know whether they helped you?

Alex: We do not know. We do not know how much were sold, or anything at all about it actually. We only gave them the song and never heard from it again.

Gilles: There will probably be a song from the next album on the next Sisyphus sampler. We do rarely get feedback about these songs.

Alex: We never got an e-mail or letter about these songs on those discs, although our address is included.

Gilles: Maybe people heard form us through this compilation, but they never told us.

Alex: I think that these compilations do now show enough of the band to show the listener what kind of music you play. They say that the songs have to four minutes of five minutes, but with our music that is a short time to explain the music. In ten minutes you can show them at least something. We have made a few four minute songs. Very pop, not very progressive. The other bands here are much more progressive. We try to play songs, with a voice and that people can hear that voice. We are there to provide a background for the voice and to add some special effects to the song. We are not just playing and playing so there are no peaks in the music. For me the voice is very important.

Big Big Train also had a lot of vocals. Did you hear them?

Alex: Yes, but he just stands there and he's more speaking than singing. I like it too, but it is not my kind of music, the music I would make. I prefer power.

Well since we do not really know your music we have little else to ask. Do you want to tell our readers something?

Alex: Buy a lot of CDs!

You mean in this case mostly yours?

Alex: Only! Well you could tell the new CD called The Other Side will be out next Februari and it will feature a thirty six minute song called Ten Eiben?

Gilles: It it divided into six sections. We played parts 3, 4 and 5 today.

Alex: The new songs are still quite hard to play for us. WE have played them in the studio, but live it is difficult to play them still. With the older songs there's no such problem. You could notice from the audience that we had a little trouble playing it, they could hear it didn't go off quite well.

Gilles: We hope that we can play some more gigs in the Netherlands.

Alex: Amsterdam.

Well, there are not that many concerts there. Most of it happens in the eastern part of the country, funnily enough.

Alex: Utrecht.

Utrecht is certainly central to the country.

Alex: Coming here was quite a trip, 600 kilometers.

De Nieuwe Pul in Uden is quite popular. And it is also quite close.

Alex: We were also very tired today. Last night we slept here and it was freezing since there was no heating.

Gilles: Nevertheless we were thankful to have been asked to play here but we could not play full strength. It would really be nice to play here some more in front of this Dutch prog scene. In Luxembourg there is no such scene. Luxembourg is so small that with two gigs you cover the entire country. It is difficult to play in other countries, because you need the contacts. We had a lot of luck in playing here since Edo contacted us, but is very difficult to say "come on let's tour the Netherlands".

Arie Verstegen does this. He also organizes our up and coming concert. He also organizes the Pul festival in May or June. At the moment there are quite many festivals here since they seem the best way to get some people without having the really big names. They seem well suited for new bands to make a name of some kind. Or you should support a bigger band.

Gilles: We would like to do that. We supported Arena in Luxembourg and it was great. In September we played with Marillion in Cologne at the fanclub meeting. That was the best gig we ever played. They were already screaming before we got on stage. There were a 1000 people there. Noorderligt is a good venue. We saw IQ there with Subterannea. We also went to London, but the sound in Tilburh was better. The nice thing about London was that the whole prog scene was there: Arena, Jadis, Pendragon, except Marillion, everyone. If you would have dropped a bomb there there would have been no prog scene in England anymore.

Do you play a lot in Luxembourg?

Gilles: Something like ten times a year.

But you play as a rock band I guess, not as a typical prog band?

Gilles: We are playing and people are coming, but not because we are a prog band. We do have some fans from Germany that are coming over. They drive 400 kilometeres to see our gigs. We do not mind. Last year we did play at that large festival in Germany with Saga, Asia, Galleon and many more.

Oh yes, here in Holland we only heard about it afterwards.

Gilles: Yes, the promotion was so bad. There was a big stage, a big venue. We were opening the second day. We were playing at three o'clock. There were three or four hundred people there. But still it was good to play there.
