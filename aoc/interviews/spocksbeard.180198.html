Spock's Beard interview January 18th 1998

Introduction

This is the transcript of an interview held in Tivoli, Utrecht, the Netherlands with Neal Morse, composer, lead singer and keyboardist of Spock's Beard. The interview was held on the 18th of January 1998, just before the concert that turned out to be truly marvellous. Some additions to this interview were made based on e-mail exchange. The interview was done by Jurriaan Hage and Roberto Lambooy and written by Jurriaan Hage.

The interview

In an interview (Dave Meros in iO Pages) it was said that Spock's Beard might play in Europe if Beware of Darkness would sell well there. Now, you're here, so I conclude it sold well.

Yeah it sold pretty well. Upwards of fifteen thousand. We are grateful to everyone at GEP for putting this thing together and for doing such a great job. People are coming to the concerts and they know the words; it's great.

How big is this tour? Where have you been?

Well, let's see. We've been in Germany, Goettingen and Karlsruhe and we did Paris last night. Now we are here and then we have three more dates in Germany with Hamburg being the last. That's it.

Tough schedule?

A gig every night? Well, it's the first time that I've had to carry a full concert night after night vocall, so it was kind of scary. I'm not that worried now, since I know now that my voice is going to hold. Some of the high falsetto notes are not really there. The rest of the gig is strong enough so that's okay. I'm getting more relaxed now and I think that's a good sign.

Isn't it strange to do a tour here just BEFORE the album is released?

Well, it sort of worked out that way. You see, originally they wanted us in December, but for various scheduling reasons this was not possible. So we are doing it now. Also, I thought the record was to be released on the first of January, but it got pushed back for various business reasons that I do not know.

It's probably because after New Year it takes a little time for things to get moving again.

Yeah, that's probably right.

You have not played here to promote either The Light or Beware Of Darkness. The new one is out soon, so I wonder what you will be playing.

We'll be playing two songs of the Light (because of the extra encore they played three), we're doing four things from the new album and we are doing something like five tracks from Beware of Darkness. It's kind of an even mix.

I'm not familiar with the Light, but I read that it's more classic, standard progressive.

Yeah, it's the proggiest. It has the most time changes and so on. Beware Of Darkness is also very proggy with exception of Waste Away.

But do you play less of The Light, because it's the oldest album, or because it's not really your style anymore?

No, we play less of the Light, because we saved On The Edge for an encore, but in fact we haven't actually been playing it. We've been doing for an encore...can I say this...oh why not?...we've been doing the Light and then we've been doing... because the first night in Goettingen there was a bunch of requests for Waste Away -- what kind of surprised me -- and so we did Waste Away instead and we kind of stuck with that. (In fact, the band played both Waste Away, The Light and On The Edge as encores in Tivoli.) We didn't do The Water, because it is so long and if we did that then there wouldn't be room for anything else. The Water live is about 25 minutes long. We just looked at the material we had and picked a bunch of things and it worked out pretty well. [without a warning the drummer Nick D'Virgillio is standing right behind us.]

I just got your new CD to play and we've managed to play it a few times. Of the second album there was quite a lot of material left I heard.

Yeah, there's always quite a lot of material left. We have an overage of stuff and we sort of pick along the way we go. But, excepting Harms Way all of the material on Kindness of Strangers is rather new.

Are you that productive?

Well, I guess I am pretty productive. One of the things is that the business side is pretty far behind the writing side. The stuff for the Light album was written early on and before The Light came out in 95 or 96, I was writing Walking On The Wind and all that stuff (that came on the Beware Of Darkness album). The stuff that I write now will be released literally in the year 2000. So it's about three years behind, because we have to record it, overdub it, mix it and then the company has to get the artwork and everything has to get together and a year has gone around. So it's a year from recording to release and I'm writing the stuff way before that.

What if you don't like your music in 2000 anymore, but do now. Isn't that a waste? Doesn't that make you panic or something?]

Well, I'm always a bit worried about whether people will like whatever we do, but, if we don't like it, then we won't record it. We look at each album individually, and I suppose, if we didn't have enough material that everyone liked we would wait 'til we did. But so far we've been into whatever songs we've had.

Does this all mean that you were recording the new album, Kindness Of Strangers, right about when Beware Of Darkness came out?

I guess so, yes. We did the basic tracks a year ago.

That's when Beware Of Darkness came out, over here.

You have to be, since it's quite a long time before it really comes out.

Still, a year is quite short for a progressive band.

It isn't for me, you know. I'm in a hurry. I'm not getting any younger. I'm 37. Life is short. My plane could crash. I want to get out all the music I can when I'm alive. I'm really hyper that way.

Did you always have this? I can imagine that Kevin Gilbert's death had an influence here.

No, I've always felt that way. Before I wasn't so worried about death, it was being too old to be a pop star, that was my biggest fear. Now I am too old to be a pop star, and it doesn't matter. I'm happier and more productive than ever. And who knows, maybe we'll still pull this thing off, doing music we love. How's that for a happy ending?! On the other hand I have to try and calm down and be patient and let things flow the way that they want to. When I tried to force things, it pisses people off and it doesn't forward things anyway.

I didn't check on the last album, but are you still the sole composer?

Yes. With the exception of the last thing, Flow, of which some of the lyrics were written by a guy called Tony Ray. I wrote that when I was sixteen. I was really into jazz and I was writing progressive, but I didn't think that I could write lyrics. This guy that I hung out with and partied with told me "hey man, I'm a poet, check this out". The original lyrics to the song started with "Sunlight gliding, children siding". This guy was out there. A lot of that was written when I was sixteen. Also the end part "Go forth..." is also like it was, though originally it was "unto the source" instead of "into the source". I haven't seen this guy since I was seventeen years old.

Does he know this then?

I don't know. I don't think so. I've talked to a few old friends and asked them whether they knew what happened to Tony Ray and nobody knows. Maybe someday I'll get the call from the guy?

Saying "Give me my money".

Yeah right. Such as it is. He should like get an eighth of just that track and no, he wouldn't get much.

Well, if you sell an awful lot...

Well, why not. You really can't tell, about sales. We've been surprised all along. I can definitely stand for this thing to become really huge. We can really cross over. People that have all kinds of different tastes, not prog, sometimes still like us. We can really have a wide audience, but whether we will or not, remains to be seen.

That fifteen you just mentioned, was that world wide?

Thomas (Waber), how many copies did we sell of Beware Of Darkness in Europe?

Thomas: Twelve thousand

And then six or seven thousand in Japan and two or three in the States. So about twenty. We are really proud of that.

You should be. Not many amateur prog bands are able to do that. [sorry about this Neal, but amateur in Dutch means both non-professional as non-vocational. I knew you were, at least until recently, not making a living off this band, so there you go. Maybe I should change my vocabulary here since this is the second run in I had (the other one with IQ, also GEP)]

Amateur prog band?

I mean non-vocational or something like that. Like that you have side jobs.

Oh yes, we do have other projects. We have other things we do. But Spock's Beard is something we are all focused on. We all want to see this succeed. This is our love thing.

So the rest is more or less to bring in the money and pay the rent.

Yeah, sort of. I mean we enjoy our other gigs. No matter what was going on with Spock's Beard, if Tears For Fears or Genesis calls Nick again, he'd go do that, because that is a great thing. But that doesn't mean that Spock's Beard isn't the focal point of our music lives.

So, it's at least more than a hobby.

Certainly. Much more. For me, it's almost a full-time job now, since I also run Radiant Records, which sells our records in the States. Now I'm gone, my wife is doing the shipping. I just hired a PR person. I'm doing all kinds of things. It's a big thing.

Now about the music on the last album. I liked Beware Of Darkness very much, one of the best of 1997, in progressive. When I listened to your last album for the first time, I kind of missed the prog elements. With BoD you hear parts of Yes, Gentle Giant (like the harmonies in Thoughts) and now with the new album, I hear a lot less of that. Almost as if your sound has become more integrated.

The last album is more song oriented. There are less fiddly bits on this album than on other albums. But I still think there's still a lot of it. But that was where the band was at, that's what the band wanted to do. As a matter fact, Strange World didn't actually get my vote. I didn't think it was much, but for Nick and Al it's the best song on the album. Go figure.

In an interview with Dave Meros, I read that your new album might move more into the alternative rock scene. Progressive, but with influences from that side.

It'd be nice...ehm, as long as we maintain the integrity of the recording process. As long as the criteria among the band is: do we like it, do we love it, then whatever makes it on any market, doesn't really matter. I mean it would be really nice, and we would all really love to be hugely succesful. I'd love to be succesful in the alternative market. We'd love to break out big. I would define success as success in the business end (seperate from the artistic end) as becoming known and selling at least a gold album. Having alot of people buy your stuff, that's basically it.

I would imagine so. The direct influences on your new album seem to lessen a bit.

I suppose that's good.

I think so too. We might call it maturation.

Cool.

I thought you'd like that.

As far as that goes, everybody has his opinion on what we sound like. We have been reviewed as sounding as the Eagles. [laughter]

Well, we missed that reference.

There's people that say we sound a lot like a band we've never heard of.

That might be because you both get your influences from the same source. Like if you compare Spock's Beard with Epidermis, people that do not know Gentle Giant, will think that Spock's Beard is sometimes like Epidermis.

Yeah, that's right. That makes sense. So it's all where you are coming from and what your musical orientation is.

A striking reference I found was Electric Light Orchestra, the earlier one.

Really? Don't know it.

It's when they have this rather dark sound with cello and string bass.

Oh right, they were into the Beatles right?

Yes, I think it's what we talked of earlier, a common influence. Funny that both Beatles influences work out "the same".

Well, I don't actually know any other prog bands that have a pronounced Beatles influence. The Beatles were my first love, in popular music. I used to sound so much like John Lennon, that when I listen to old demoes I say to myself that people should've told me to stop doing this Lennon impression. I used to be really over the top on my demoes from the Eighties and stuff. Amazing.

What kind of music was this then?

Singer-songwriter pop stuff. Eighties pop mostly.

Having so much music left over, can we see another album coming within a year or so?

We're going for a record a year. That's what I want to do. I'm not in control of that, though. If Thomas and Michael from GEP, and Martin, sorry Martin, and Lawrence and Mike, I don't wanna leave anyone out. If the label does not want to put it out, I can't make them.

Another label might.

Even with another label....it's like if you made movies for a studio....they put the things out when they think it will sell the most...you can kick and scream or whatever but they'll do it when they think it's best. Can't blame them really. We basically deliver them the masters and the artwork and they say well, we'll put it out right then. I think Thomas already said he didn't want to do an album before spring 1999. So now I'm going "Ooh man, do I have to wait that long?".

On the third album we did think that we spotted less hammond and more guitar.

Oh really?

On BoD there is a lot of organ.

I think that on KoS there's more edgy guitars. It's probably because of what Al did and because of the engineer we used. He was more a sort of Zeppelin guy and you can hear that well in the drums. So this has a lot to do with how the album sounds in the end, the people you choose. So it's Rich Mouser, it's Rich Mouser's fault, man.

We don't say it's a fault.

I know, just teasing.

But it does make BoD sound more bombastic.

A lot of BoD was mixed by the late Kevin Gilbert and I think his mixes were really...you see Kevin had a strong prog background. Rich as well. He's a really big Yes fan, but he's more into the rock side. I wanted it this way you know. I wanted to have cymbals that were like those on the old Zeppelin and Beatles and Yes too ....pussssh. If you listen to The Light, they sound very limb. That's all I wanted, to have compressed cymbals. That was my whole focus, all I cared about.

Yeah, who cares about composition?

Right. Somebody gave me a tape with the Beatles anthology things and I heard the basic tracks of Good Morning: "Nothing to do to save his life, call his wife and pussssh" and it kicked my ass. So I think, why can't we do that? They did that. There are certain things in recording that you want to do, and you never quite ...and that was great about working with Kevin. Cause I didn't know how Yes got those mellotrons to fucking get you in the gut. And when he did that mellotron swell in Walking On The Wind, just before the guitar solo, he just put the mellotron into an echo chamber and he flooded it with sound and it's just magical. So I learned a lot from Kevin and in fact I learn a lot from every engineer I work with. I'm learning more and more about how to get what I want. [after a small interruption of a domestic kind we continue]

Live on the road, right?

Yeah well, we have a crew that takes pretty good care of us.

When you're big they will do everything for you.

Yeah, we can get bread in the right shape. This is from Spinal Tap you know, the classic parody of a rock band.

Could you explain some of the lyrics. In Time Has Come I hear something of homelessness.

You mean "If you leave here we will hate you" or the trashman thing? You know that last one was partly based on the trashman character in Stephen King's The Stand. Funny thing is that I'd forgotten all this, but when people asked me, I thought "oh yeah". I imagine this guy siting at the junkyard and saying all this stuff. There was something cool about it, you know. he middle part is complete gibberish, sort of like a dream sequence. The next part, I moved into Nashville at that point, that would have been 95. I was feeling this sort of friction from my parents, just a little weirdness on the phone and I was feeling they were kind of mad that I left. That's where the "We will hate you if you leave here" comes from. And the end part is about the trashman as an old man. But you wanted to hear about the new album right? In The Mouth Of Madness is about how spooky, and completely cool, it would be to go completely nuts and float around in space. Cakewalk On Easy Street is because of my wife. She always tells me that I should be happy since I at least got my arms.

Like in piece of cake?

A cakewalk is an expression, meaning piece of cake. It's sort of funny since this guy is in really bad shape. His legs have fallen off, he's having a really hard time he's got stuff up his butt, but he's happy since at least he still got his arms.

To push along the cart?

Right. June is about the demise of Spock's Beard before we even happened.

It sounds a bit nostalgic.

Yeah, it's sort of like we had only one good season and all of it then goes to hell and we can never get in tune again. And all the gigs suck. It would be like writing about this kind of life, after it.

About how your living right now?

No we don't have telephones or private rooms. Maybe the next tour. It might not ever happen. It really shows what pessimist I am. Harms Way pretty much felt the right thing to say. It sort of came out of my mouth and it sounded good. I guess it would be about wanting to help people and putting your ass on the line instead of staying out of Harms Way.

What about Strange World. I mean who is Mary?

No one. I just thought it sounded cool. I think I was thinking of Bowie's 'Rock and Roll Star'. "Bevin went to fight in Belfast - Judy stayed at home to starve" That's not the exact lyric but...I don't know...it had something to do with it. We already talked about Flow and the lyric to that.

Are many of your lyrics intuitive?

Yeah, sure. They're pretty spontaneous. If it feels good, sounds cool and fits with the music, I just kind of go with it, whether it makes any sense or not. It's very freeing, saying whatever you want.

Another advantage is that a lot of people might be able to relate to that.

Yeah like Gabriel said on what the Lamb Lies Down On Broadway mean: "Whatever it means to you". That's the thing. As long as it means something to the listener, then it's okay.

The intensity of your concert was almost punk-like. Where do you get this energy. Is that the hyper you were talking of? Can you explain a bit how concerts go.

That's just who we are. We're very excited about this music and we get to get some aggresions out.

Was it as good for you as it was for us? (sic)

We loved it! As a matter of fact Al just mentioned to me that that was his favorite gig of the tour!

Was talking to a real trekkie yesterday and he told me there exists a Star Trek episode where Spock has a beard. Is this a coincidence or might this be where the bandname is from? It is an episode where the federation is bad instead of good.

Yes, this music is from a parrellel universe....you know...like...where Spock has a beard.....

Do you know anything about the success of the album?

It's sold at least 11,000 so far. The most in Europe. It's doing really well.
